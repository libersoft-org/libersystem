   Towards the consumer the plan says only that the stream [carries a `state` for every change](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0192.md:36), and the [causes of a closed stream](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0192.md:149) do not include a slow reader. InputService's key and contact streams [close on the first frame they cannot send](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/input_service.rs:283). A tool [redrawn in place as states arrive](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0192.md:147), behind a stick that reports every change, fills 64 frames and is disconnected under that precedent; an implementer who drops frames instead loses releases. The [connection-time STATE](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0192.md:86) is also sent at boot before any report, because [a new connection is answered before the controller is polled](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/drivers/core/src/common.rs:621), and a zero-initialised hat then reads north ([0 is north](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0192.md:30)). No proof has a reader that falls behind.

   **Correct the publisher's rules and the stream-order item.** A STATE that cannot be sent marks its gamepad unsent, and the publisher sends that gamepad's current state once the connection has room, on a short retry deadline while any gamepad is unsent. InputService keeps at most one pending `state` per gamepad for a reader whose channel is full, replaces it with the latest and sends it when there is room; it closes a stream only for an undeliverable `present`, `arrived`, `departed` or release. State the state before the first report: no buttons, hats centred, and a stated value for each axis. The services test fills a stream without reading it, then reads it, and asserts that the stream is still open and ends on each gamepad's current state.

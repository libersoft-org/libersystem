AUDITOR'S REVIEW OF PLAN P02M0202 (2026-09-25T22:57:24Z):

**Rating: 4/10.** UCSI is the right destination for P02M0099's requirement, but the plan does not say which process publishes what or in which vocabulary, gives UCSI a policy role the interface does not grant, promises deliveries nothing can receive, and adds a full Power Delivery engine for hardware the system does not run on.

Reviewed [the plan](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md) and its [originating requirement](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:253). Also reviewed the plans it builds on: [P02M0196](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md), [P02M0195](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md) and [P02M0181](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md). On the code side: the provider catalogue, the power vocabulary and its normalisation leaf, the display contracts, the USB drivers and the supported platforms, all at commit `07371c44af82a11c1d275b0cbd832899f712e62a`. Plans P02M0197 to P02M0202 exist only in the working tree (they are not yet committed), and P02M0190, P02M0196 and P02M0099 carry uncommitted edits; all were read as they stand there, and line numbers refer to the working tree. These were checked against the UCSI interface as Linux's `ucsi` driver implements it and the Power Delivery timers Linux uses. The plan is a checklist written before implementation, so the findings concern decisions it needs, not the expected absence of feature code.

1. **High - The plan does not say which process publishes the Power Delivery contract, and `liber:power@1` has no way to carry one.**

   [The vocabulary item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:17) creates a `typec` service. [The delivery item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:21) sends "a power contract as a power source to P02M0181 ('USB-C, 20 V, 3.25 A')".

   Only a driver that holds a claim can publish. DeviceManager [publishes what a binding offered](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:2762), the [catalogue](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:360) has no other publish path, and P02M0181 requires that [a driver publishes through its authorized binding and never receives a writable power-service connection](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:40). The UCSI device's claim is [a platform device's](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:29), and a TCPCI driver's is [one I2C address's](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md:26). So a separate `typec` service can publish nothing. Nor is there a provider kind between those drivers and the service in [the closed vocabulary](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:154).

   The destination has no place for a contract either:
   - [`source-kind`](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/power.lsidl:18) is AC, battery, UPS, load or thermal zone.
   - [Voltage and current](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/power.lsidl:161) are measurements, with current signed into or out of storage. "20 V, 3.25 A" is a negotiated limit, not a measurement.
   - Nothing holds what the partner offered.
   - Producers must convert through [the `power-model` leaf's adapters](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:77), and those exist for ACPI and HID only.

   On a laptop, the [ACPI AC adapter that P02M0196 delivers to P02M0181](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:50) already reports the same charger. A second source for it needs a stated relation to the first.

   An implementer would have to invent the process graph, a provider kind, an IDL extension and a normalisation rule. Publishing a limit in a measurement field would also misreport the source.

   **Correct the vocabulary and delivery items** by naming the processes:
   - the UCSI driver, holding the `PNP0CA0` claim;
   - the TCPCI driver, holding its device claim;
   - where the policy engine runs;
   - the `typec` service, consuming an append-only connector provider kind, with its manifest declarations.

   Have the driver that holds the claim publish `power-source`. Extend `liber:power@1`, append-only, with either a USB PD source kind or a contract record (negotiated voltage and current limit, offered PDOs), with its `power-model` validation. State how that source relates to the ACPI AC source for the same charger.

2. **High - The TCPCI part adds a full USB PD 3.1 policy engine that no requirement asks for and no supported platform has hardware for, and the milestone cannot close without it.**

   [P02M0202c](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:41) specifies TCPCI with the Type-C and PD 3.1 engines: Extended Power Range, role swaps, discovery and DisplayPort VDMs. [Its verification](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:58) repeats "every scenario above" on that engine.

   Neither the requirement nor the platforms call for it:
   - The originating requirement is [UCSI](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:6890), and TCPCI appears nowhere in P02M0099.
   - Every supported profile is [QEMU `q35` or `virt`](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:16), [no physical machine is qualified](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:17), and none of them has a port controller.
   - Where hardware cannot be emulated, the project defers it: [P02M0195's DesignWare controllers](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md:14) and [P02M0200's SBSA watchdog](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0200.md:61).

   Nor can it be proved:
   - The only oracle would be a harness port controller and PD partner written from the same specification.
   - That fixture is not ["P02M0195's fixture"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:58), which [plays a touchpad](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md:48).
   - PD's timers are tens of milliseconds, [tSenderResponse 24-30 ms](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/include/linux/usb/pd.h). The kernel tick is [100 Hz](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/kernel/arch/common/time.rs:12), and the plan states no timing budget for an engine reached over IPC and I2C.
   - The [safety checks are deferred](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:47) to a later plan.

   This is the largest and most dangerous part of the milestone, and it can be proved only against a model written by the same hand. Carrying it keeps the milestone open for hardware the system does not run on.

   **Correct P02M0202c and its verification** by moving TCPCI and the PD engine to a milestone of its own that waits for a named board, as the precedents above do, so that this milestone closes on UCSI. If the owner wants it kept here, cut it to a sink-only engine within the standard power range and drop EPR, swaps and DisplayPort VDMs. Make the safety invariants and a timing budget acceptance criteria, and name the new fixture.

3. **Medium - The policy item assumes decisions that UCSI leaves to the platform, and the UCSI command list omits the commands the promised requests need.**

   [The policy defaults](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:25) are: accept the best offer, answer a partner's swap requests, enter DisplayPort, and refuse unknown modes. Under UCSI these are the PPM's own decisions; the operating system configures and asks. Linux's driver [can enter or configure an alternate mode only when the PPM advertises alternate-mode override, and otherwise the firmware enters modes itself](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/displayport.c).

   [The command list](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:32) misses commands from [the UCSI command set](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi.h):
   - no `ACK_CC_CI`, which acknowledges every command completion and connector change;
   - no `SET_NEW_CAM`, which enters or leaves a mode;
   - no role configuration: `SET_UOM` (whose later form is `SET_CCOM`) and `SET_PDM`, through which the system tells the PPM which roles and swaps to accept.

   It also names a single `_DSM` call, while [the ACPI binding uses two](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi_acpi.c): function 1 after writing the mailbox, and function 2, which has the platform refresh the mailbox before it is read.

   The owner would confirm defaults that the UCSI path cannot enforce. A harness PPM written to this plan would also accept requests that real PPMs refuse.

   **Correct the policy and UCSI items** by splitting the policy by transport. On UCSI the system configures acceptance and requests swaps and modes, and enters a mode only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises override. The full policy applies only on the system's own engine. Add the missing commands and both `_DSM` functions. Give the harness a PPM without override, whose refusal `typec` reports.

4. **Medium - Data-role and DisplayPort facts are promised to consumers that have no interface for them, and the gate checks a DisplayService hot-plug that cannot exist.**

   [The delivery item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:22) sends the data role "to the USB stack", and DisplayPort hot-plug and lane assignment "to DisplayService, for whichever display controller drives it". [The UCSI gate](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:56) expects "the hot-plug in DisplayService".

   Neither consumer can receive these facts:
   - The [display-device contract](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/display-device.lsidl:71) carries only resizes and replaced backings.
   - DisplayService [shows at most one surface](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/display_service.rs:8), and has no connector or hot-plug notion.
   - [A display controller that can reach a monitor's bus is still unowned](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:271).
   - The USB drivers have no notion of a data role; [OTG descriptors are skipped](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/drivers/core/src/usb_function.rs:175).
   - Nothing maps a Type-C connector to a root-hub port, and the plan [excludes a device-mode controller](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:65).

   So of the three facts the plan delivers "where they belong", two have no receiver, no mapping and no oracle.

   **Correct the delivery item and the UCSI gate.** Report the data role and the DisplayPort state in `typec` only. Leave delivery to the USB stack and to a display controller to the milestones that create a connector-aware display controller and a port-to-connector mapping, or plan those interfaces here as items. Remove "the hot-plug in DisplayService" from the gate.

Validation was plan, source, and Linux UCSI and Power Delivery source inspection only. No plan or source code was modified, and no build or guest execution was performed for this audit.

1. **ACCEPTED - Who publishes, and where a contract goes.** The first item of P02M0202a now names the processes. `ucsi-acpi` holds the `PNP0CA0` claim and reaches `_DSM` and `Notify` through the node-scoped ACPI channel that comes with the claim. `tcpci` binds as P02M0195b's child binding. It gets an address-scoped I2C connection and a line-scoped GPIO connection, minted through each controller's scoped `CONNECT`. Those connections are requirements naming both controllers, so the driver stops as a lost dependency when either controller ends. The sink engine runs inside `tcpci`. Each driver publishes `power-source` and a new `typec-connector` kind, appended to `provider-kind`, with their manifest `provides` rows. TypeCService holds no claim: it consumes `typec-connector` through a kind-scoped catalogue connection and serves `liber:typec@1`'s read and operator roots, with its manifest row stated. Of the audit's two options for power, the plan takes the SOURCE KIND and declines the contract record. `usb-c` is appended to `source-kind`. `present`, `online` and the MEASURED VBUS voltage, current and power are the only values a `usb-c` source carries, the measurements coming from UCSI 2.1 readings or a port controller's register, with the existing signs. A reported `source-fault` covers a VBUS fault. The negotiated contract is a limit and never goes into the measurement fields: the contract and the offers live in `liber:typec@1`'s connector record. The contract record was declined because adding a field to `source-state` breaks the record's positional wire layout (LSIDL section 8). It would also duplicate the connector record, and a second stream would race it. The enum append is the only wire change. `power-model` gains a `usbc` adapter, and `canon::validate` gains a rule refusing a `usb-c` record with fields the kind lacks or a known voltage above 48 V. The plan now states how the source relates to the ACPI AC adapter. They are two reports and firmware describes no join between them, so PowerService merges nothing and readers must not add them. A TCPCI board has the `usb-c` source alone.

2. **ACCEPTED - The Power Delivery engine on TCPCI.** Applied as coordination decision D18 settles it. Moving TCPCI to a milestone of its own was declined because D18 is final for this round. The audit's alternative was taken in full. P02M0202c is now a SINK-ONLY engine in the Standard Power Range that also accepts revision 2.0 partners. Fixed offers only, and the connector must be described as a sink: a connector described as a source or dual-role is refused at bind, and a missing description means Type-C current only. The selection rule is proposed and asked when the part starts. Seven numbered SAFETY INVARIANTS are acceptance criteria, each with a host test and a gate case: request bounds; Fixed offers only; latest-capabilities positions only; nothing above vSafe5V before PS_RDY; the sink path disabled and the contract voided FIRST on detach, Hard Reset, error recovery or an alarm; VBUS alarms; no belief in unchecked messages. The TIMING BUDGET is also an acceptance criterion. Timers never expire before their minimum at the kernel's granule (ceil(min/granule)+1). The one timer whose maximum this exceeds, SenderResponseTimer, is named. The alert-to-Request path must never reach 24 ms and must stay at or under 15 ms at the 99th percentile, over 200 negotiations under KVM. A miss leaves the part open. A bind that finds VBUS already present, including a rebind after a lost dependency, renegotiates through Soft_Reset without dropping VBUS; this has a host test and a gate case. The fixture is named: `tcpc-partner`, a device model added to P02M0195's `vhost-i2c-gpio.py` (D8). It runs on the default translated machine, using the vhost-user IOTLB P02M0195a adds. Its source partner is written from the specification's source-side state machines and shares no code with the engine. The TCPCI cases no longer repeat the UCSI scenarios. EPR, the source role, swaps, structured VDMs, DisplayPort, PPS/AVS and a charger driver are in EXCLUDES until a board is named.

3. **ACCEPTED - UCSI policy and commands.** The policy item is split by transport. On UCSI the PPM negotiates, answers swaps and, without override, enters modes. The system configures swap acceptance (the accept bits of `SET_UOR`/`SET_PDR`) and the operation and power-direction modes (`SET_CCOM`/`SET_UOM`, `SET_PDM`, only where advertised). It forwards an operator's role requests, and sends `SET_NEW_CAM` only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises override; otherwise the request is refused before any command and `typec` reports why. The system never enters a mode on its own, since nothing consumes DisplayPort. The full policy (selection, refusing swaps and VDMs) applies only to the system's own TCPCI engine. P02M0202b now has four items. The transport item covers the version-dependent mailbox layout, the `_DSM` UUID, function 1 after every write, function 2 before every read, and `Notify(0x80)`. The command-discipline item covers one outstanding command, length and connector checks, `ACK_CC_CI` for every completion and connector change, and BUSY, ERROR and NOT SUPPORTED. The command list separates mandatory commands from commands used only where advertised, and states that no power-changing UCSI command is issued. The recovery item has five-second bounds. The harness PPM has two profiles, one of them WITHOUT override, whose refusal `typec` reports. Firmware-like staging behind `_DSM` means a driver that skips either function fails the gate, and the PPM's command log checks the discipline.

4. **ACCEPTED - Facts with no receiver.** The delivery item is replaced: the data role and DisplayPort state are reported in `typec` and nowhere else, and the item says why. There is no data-role notion in the USB drivers, no connector-to-root-port map, one surface and no hot-plug in the display contract, and no owned display controller that reaches a monitor's bus. Delivery waits for the milestones that create those. It is in EXCLUDES, and a device-role connector is still shown. "The hot-plug in DisplayService" is removed from the UCSI gate. The gate now checks `typec`, PowerService's `usb-c` source beside the fixture's own AC adapter, and the PPM's command log. Planning the missing interfaces here was declined as outside this milestone's scope.

1. **High - The plan does not say which process publishes the Power Delivery contract, and `liber:power@1` has no way to carry one.**

   [The vocabulary item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:17) creates a `typec` service. [The delivery item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:21) sends "a power contract as a power source to P02M0181 ('USB-C, 20 V, 3.25 A')".

   Only a driver that holds a claim can publish. DeviceManager [publishes what a binding offered](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:2762), the [catalogue](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:360) has no other publish path, and P02M0181 requires that [a driver publishes through its authorized binding and never receives a writable power-service connection](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:40). The UCSI device's claim is [a platform device's](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:29), and a TCPCI driver's is [one I2C address's](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md:26). So a separate `typec` service can publish nothing. Nor is there a provider kind between those drivers and the service in [the closed vocabulary](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:154).

   The destination has no place for a contract either:
   - [`source-kind`](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/power.lsidl:18) is AC, battery, UPS, load or thermal zone.
   - [Voltage and current](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/power.lsidl:161) are measurements, with current signed into or out of storage. "20 V, 3.25 A" is a negotiated limit, not a measurement.
   - Nothing holds what the partner offered.
   - Producers must convert through [the `power-model` leaf's adapters](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:77), and those exist for ACPI and HID only.

   On a laptop, the [ACPI AC adapter that P02M0196 delivers to P02M0181](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:50) already reports the same charger. A second source for it needs a stated relation to the first.

   An implementer would have to invent the process graph, a provider kind, an IDL extension and a normalisation rule. Publishing a limit in a measurement field would also misreport the source.

   **Correct the vocabulary and delivery items** by naming the processes:
   - the UCSI driver, holding the `PNP0CA0` claim;
   - the TCPCI driver, holding its device claim;
   - where the policy engine runs;
   - the `typec` service, consuming an append-only connector provider kind, with its manifest declarations.

   Have the driver that holds the claim publish `power-source`. Extend `liber:power@1`, append-only, with either a USB PD source kind or a contract record (negotiated voltage and current limit, offered PDOs), with its `power-model` validation. State how that source relates to the ACPI AC source for the same charger.

2. **High - The TCPCI part adds a full USB PD 3.1 policy engine that no requirement asks for and no supported platform has hardware for, and the milestone cannot close without it.**

   - Every supported profile is [QEMU `q35` or `virt`](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:16), [no physical machine is qualified](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/ARCHITECTURES.md:17), and none of them has a port controller.

   Nor can it be proved:
   - The only oracle would be a harness port controller and PD partner written from the same specification.
   - That fixture is not ["P02M0195's fixture"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:58), which [plays a touchpad](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0195.md:48).
   - PD's timers are tens of milliseconds, [tSenderResponse 24-30 ms](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/include/linux/usb/pd.h). The kernel tick is [100 Hz](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/kernel/arch/common/time.rs:12), and the plan states no timing budget for an engine reached over IPC and I2C.
   - The [safety checks are deferred](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:47) to a later plan.

   This is the largest and most dangerous part of the milestone, and it can be proved only against a model written by the same hand. Carrying it keeps the milestone open for hardware the system does not run on.

3. **Medium - The policy item assumes decisions that UCSI leaves to the platform, and the UCSI command list omits the commands the promised requests need.**

   [The policy defaults](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:25) are: accept the best offer, answer a partner's swap requests, enter DisplayPort, and refuse unknown modes. Under UCSI these are the PPM's own decisions; the operating system configures and asks. Linux's driver [can enter or configure an alternate mode only when the PPM advertises alternate-mode override, and otherwise the firmware enters modes itself](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/displayport.c).

   [The command list](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:32) misses commands from [the UCSI command set](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi.h):
   - no `ACK_CC_CI`, which acknowledges every command completion and connector change;
   - no `SET_NEW_CAM`, which enters or leaves a mode;
   - no role configuration: `SET_UOM` (whose later form is `SET_CCOM`) and `SET_PDM`, through which the system tells the PPM which roles and swaps to accept.

   It also names a single `_DSM` call, while [the ACPI binding uses two](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi_acpi.c): function 1 after writing the mailbox, and function 2, which has the platform refresh the mailbox before it is read.

   The owner would confirm defaults that the UCSI path cannot enforce. A harness PPM written to this plan would also accept requests that real PPMs refuse.

   **Correct the policy and UCSI items** by splitting the policy by transport. On UCSI the system configures acceptance and requests swaps and modes, and enters a mode only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises override. The full policy applies only on the system's own engine. Add the missing commands and both `_DSM` functions. Give the harness a PPM without override, whose refusal `typec` reports.

4. **Medium - Data-role and DisplayPort facts are promised to consumers that have no interface for them, and the gate checks a DisplayService hot-plug that cannot exist.**

   [The delivery item](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:22) sends the data role "to the USB stack", and DisplayPort hot-plug and lane assignment "to DisplayService, for whichever display controller drives it". [The UCSI gate](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:56) expects "the hot-plug in DisplayService".

   Neither consumer can receive these facts:
   - The [display-device contract](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/display-device.lsidl:71) carries only resizes and replaced backings.
   - DisplayService [shows at most one surface](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/display_service.rs:8), and has no connector or hot-plug notion.
   - [A display controller that can reach a monitor's bus is still unowned](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:271).
   - The USB drivers have no notion of a data role; [OTG descriptors are skipped](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/drivers/core/src/usb_function.rs:175).
   - Nothing maps a Type-C connector to a root-hub port, and the plan [excludes a device-mode controller](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:65).

   So of the three facts the plan delivers "where they belong", two have no receiver, no mapping and no oracle.

   **Correct the delivery item and the UCSI gate.** Report the data role and the DisplayPort state in `typec` only. Leave delivery to the USB stack and to a display controller to the milestones that create a connector-aware display controller and a port-to-connector mapping, or plan those interfaces here as items. Remove "the hot-plug in DisplayService" from the gate.

1. **High - The UCSI transport needs the firmware's own AML to read and write the mailbox the driver claims, and P02M0196b as written refuses exactly that, on hardware and in the fixture.**

   The plan makes the mailbox [the claim's `_CRS` range](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:121), ["on hardware, firmware-reserved memory"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:122). It calls [function 1 after every write and function 2 before every read](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:126). The fixture's `_DSM` ["copies CONTROL and MESSAGE_OUT into an outbound staging area ... function 2 copies an inbound staging area into CCI and MESSAGE_IN"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:206). So the `_DSM`, which runs in the ACPI service, must read and write the mailbox itself.

   Real firmware does the same. Intel's reference design (https://www.intel.com/content/dam/www/public/us/en/documents/white-papers/bios-implementation-of-ucsi.pdf, sections 2.2.3 to 2.2.5) says the ACPI code "defines a shared mailbox in main memory to share with the OPM driver" as `OperationRegion (USBC, SystemMemory, ...)`. Its `_DSM` copies between that region and the EC. Its SCI handler "updates the OPM-BIOS mailbox ... and then notifies the OPM". A shipping Lenovo SSDT declares `OperationRegion (USBC, SystemMemory, UBCB, 0x38)` over the base its `_CRS` returns. It reads the mailbox in `ECWR`, writes it in `ECRD`, and runs `ECRD` before `Notify (\_SB.UBTC, 0x80)` (https://github.com/tylernguyen/x1c6-hackintosh/blob/main/ACPI/Disassembled%20ACPI/BIOS-v1.34/SSDT-7-UsbCTabl.dsl, lines 74-79, 94, 148-238). Linux maps the same range as write-back RAM with `devm_memremap(..., MEMREMAP_WB)` (https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi_acpi.c).

   P02M0196b refuses both halves:
   - A SystemMemory region over ["a claimed device's ranges"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:144) is refused. So once `ucsi-acpi` holds the claim, every access by the node's own `_DSM` and notification code is refused.
   - The fixture carve-out admits the harness mailbox for the claim and ["carved out of what the service maps"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:163). So the fixture's `_DSM` cannot copy anything.
   - `_CRS`-derived MMIO must be ["not over RAM"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:157), while the mailbox is main memory. P02M0196b names no admission for firmware-reserved memory, although the plan says that check admits it.

   The planner's re-check asked P02M0196 only to authorise the range "for the `PNP0CA0` claim". The carve-out that answered that request removed the range from the one process that runs the `_DSM`. So function 1 and function 2 fail on every laptop and in the fixture, and the gate cannot pass. This continues original finding 3.

   One gate check also cannot run. The command log is to fail the gate on ["a read not preceded by function 2"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:218). The driver's reads of the ivshmem [`memory-backend-file`](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0196.md:312) are plain memory reads that the harness never sees. Only the stale-data effect [the staging gives](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:208) is observable.

   **Correct the transport item and the UCSI gate.** State that the mailbox is shared: the claim maps it, and the claimed node's own AML reads and writes it through a SystemMemory region over the same range, inside `_DSM` and before `Notify`, with one memory type for both mappings. State the two admissions P02M0196b must carry as this plan's requirements on it: a `_CRS` range over firmware-reserved or ACPI NVS memory for a namespace device, and a SystemMemory region over a claimed range when the region belongs to the claimed node itself. The fixture carve-out must stay reachable by the UCSI node's `_DSM`. Replace the unobservable read check with a gate assertion on the stale-data effect.

   The state machine says ["detach on VBUS removal or on CC open for tPDDebounce"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:164). The fixture's partner is [written from the specification's source-side state machines](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:224). A source answers a Hard Reset by discharging VBUS to vSafe0V and then restoring it (TCPCI R2.0 v1.3, section 4.4.18.2: "A TCPC acting as a Source shall always discharge to vSafe0V upon a disconnect, Hard Reset, or Power Role Swap", https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB_TCPCI_R2_V1p3_30122_CLEAN.pdf).

   Type-C R2.0 section 4.5.2.2.5.2 limits the VBUS exit to a port that "is not in the process of a USB PD PR_Swap or a USB PD Hard Reset or a USB PD FR_Swap" (https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/USB%20Type-C%20Spec%20R2.0%20-%20August%202019.pdf). Linux's `tcpm` ignores VBUS-off in its hard-reset states ("Do nothing, its OK to receive vbus off events"). It treats VBUS as detached only if VBUS does not return within `PD_T_SRC_RECOVER_MAX + PD_T_SRC_TURN_ON` (https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/tcpm/tcpm.c, `_tcpm_pd_vbus_off`, `SNK_HARD_RESET_WAIT_VBUS`).

   As written, every Hard Reset - after a VBUS alarm (invariant 6), a missing PS_RDY or a silent source - passes through Unattached.SNK and a new tCCDebounce attach. Each time, `typec` and the `usb-c` source report a partner that never left as gone and back. The gate case ["no Source_Capabilities (... then the Type-C current after the hard-reset count)"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:233) then depends on whether the hard-reset count survives that detach, which the plan does not say. This continues original finding 2.

   **Correct the state-machine item.** Exit Attached.SNK on VBUS removal only outside a Hard Reset, as Type-C section 4.5.2.2.5.2 says. During a Hard Reset, wait for VBUS to return (tSafe0V, tSrcRecover, tSrcTurnOn) and detach only if it does not. Keep the hard-reset count across the reset. Add a gate case in which the partner cycles VBUS on a Hard Reset and `typec` reports no detach.

4. **Medium - Safety invariants 1 and 6 are undefined where they bind: invariant 1 does not bound a request by the board's described sink PDOs, and invariant 6 does not say where the alarms sit while VBUS moves between contracts or after a bind that trusts no contract.**

   Invariant 1 bounds a request only by ["the board's highest described sink voltage"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:176) and ["the board's sink current"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:177). The plan itself says ["the board's described sink capabilities are what bound a request here"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:258). The device-tree binding defines `sink-pdos` as each "supported power sink data object" (https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/plain/Documentation/devicetree/bindings/connector/usb-connector.yaml). Linux's `tcpm_pd_select_pdo` requests only an offer that fits inside one described sink PDO, and bounds the current by that PDO (`tcpm.c`, URL above). So invariant 1 admits a Fixed 12 V request on a board that describes Fixed 5 V and 15 V. And "the board's sink current" has as many values as the board has sink PDOs.

   Invariant 6 sets the alarms ["around the contract's voltage"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:187). Between Accept and PS_RDY the contract is ["in transition"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:182), and after a bind that finds VBUS present it is ["reported unknown"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:157). TCPCI's alarms are level-triggered and re-asserted while the condition prevails (TCPCI section 4.4.18.3, URL above). So on a 5 V to 15 V transition, alarms around either voltage fire on every voltage change and send a Hard Reset. Alarms switched off leave the ramp unprotected. Two implementers would choose differently, and the gate's [3.1 charger case with its 15 V contract](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:230) and its [rebind during a 15 V contract](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:239) pass or fail by that choice. This continues original finding 2.

   **Correct invariants 1 and 6.** Invariant 1: a request's voltage lies inside one described sink PDO, and its current is at or below that PDO's current. Invariant 6: between Accept and PS_RDY the alarms span the old and the new voltage with the specification's tolerance and narrow at PS_RDY. State where the alarms sit after a bind that finds VBUS present. Give each rule a host test and a gate case, including one transition that stays inside the envelope and one that overshoots it.

5. **Medium - The five-second PPM bounds are shorter than real UCSI firmware needs, so the driver would reset a slow PPM and end its binding.**

   The plan treats a command or acknowledgement not completed [within five seconds](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:144) as a silent PPM. It polls the reset [for five seconds](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:145), and [two failed resets end the binding](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:147).

   Linux raised both bounds to ten seconds because of field failures:
   - https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/bf4f9ae1cb08ccaafbe6874be6c46f59b83ae778: "the initial PPM reset may take up to ~8000-10000ms on some Lenovo laptops".
   - https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/eb4573cf2fd860b20adfae050c3f6ec6ddc3abdb: "commands sent after the PPM reset, such as SET_NOTIFICATION_ENABLE and GET_CAPABILITY, can exceed 5 seconds".

   On those machines the plan's driver declares a slow PPM silent, resets it, and ends the binding after two resets. DeviceManager then has [at most three automatic attempts](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:57), [100 ms and 200 ms apart](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:149), to recover it. The UCSI fixture has no slow profile, so no gate would show this. This continues original finding 3.

   **Correct the recovery item.** Use bounds that cover the ten seconds Linux measured for the first reset and for the commands after it. A longer bound for the first reset at bind than in operation is one way. Add a fixture PPM whose first reset and first commands take longer than five seconds.

6. **Medium - The operator grant for the four requests rests on an "administrative session" that PermissionManager does not have, and `power-control`, named as the precedent, is held by no shipping program.**

   The plan gives the `typec` tool [the four requests](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:116) but only the read grant. The operator grant goes ["only to an administrative session, as `power-control`'s is"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:117). PermissionManager's policy is a per-program table. `btctl` holds both Bluetooth authorities, and the table calls it ["the one shipping row that grants the operator authority"](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/permission_manager.rs:328). `power-control` goes only to the development probe `powercheck`, ["the one holder of the control authority"](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/permission_manager.rs:367). P02M0181 says the control endpoint is minted for ["an explicitly authorized administrative component"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:57), not a session. "Administrative session" appears nowhere else in the plans or the code.

   As written, no shipping program can issue the requests. The gate's ["data-role swap requested through `typec`"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:213) needs a holder the plan does not name. One implementer would grant the tool both authorities; another would build a session mechanism.

   **Correct the `typec` tool item.** Name the holder of `typec-control`. Either the `typec` row grants both authorities, as `btctl`'s does, which is an owner decision because it would be the second shipping row granting an operator authority. Or no shipping program holds it, and the gate's requests come from a development probe. Drop "administrative session".

7. **Medium - Two `usb-c` bounds collide with PowerService's rule that one refused record ends the whole provider: connector numbers as local ids overflow at the sixteenth connector, and the 48 V check refuses in-tolerance readings on a 48 V contract.**

   The plan makes ["the local id the connector's number, at most 16"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:43) and promises that a larger PPM ["is served for the first 16"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:46). UCSI numbers connectors from 1 (`connector[i].num = i + 1` in https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/typec/ucsi/ucsi.c). PowerService's registry takes 16 as [the range of local identities](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/logic/src/power_registry.rs:34). It refuses [a local id of 16 or more](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/logic/src/power_registry.rs:302), and [that refusal ends the provider](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/logic/src/power_registry.rs:294).

   The plan also has `canon::validate` refuse [a known voltage above 48 V](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:88), while carrying [UCSI 2.1 VBUS readings](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:80). PowerService ends a provider whose [record is not canonical](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/core/src/power_service.rs:329). The TCPCI specification's own worked example takes a 20 V contract's VBUS to 21.5 V, "20V + 5% + vSrcValid(max)" (section 4.4.5.4.2, URL above). So a correct reading on a 48 V EPR contract is above 48 V.

   Either case removes every connector's source of that driver from PowerService. This continues original finding 1.

   **Correct the processes and power items.** Use the connector number less one as the local id, or serve at most 15 connectors. Set the voltage refusal above the highest EPR voltage plus its tolerance, with a host test for a 48 V contract reading 50 V.

8. **Low - The timing budget expects a finer deadline granule "once P02M0198a lands", but P02M0198a keeps every deadline in 10 ms ticks.**

   The budget says ["one tick, 10 ms, today; finer once P02M0198a lands"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:192). The host suite tests every timer ["at 10 ms and at 1 ms granules"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0202.md:246). P02M0198a says ["TICKS STAY THE ABI UNIT"](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0198.md:22) and that every deadline is still an absolute tick. The ABI states that every deadline argument [is an absolute value on the tick counter](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/abi/src/lib.rs:224), with [`TICKS_PER_SECOND` = 100](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/abi/src/lib.rs:233). No planned milestone provides the 1 ms granule, and SenderResponseTimer's 30 to 40 ms expiry is permanent, not temporary. This continues original finding 2.

   **Correct the timing budget item.** State the granule as the 10 ms tick with no finer deadline planned. Drop the 1 ms granule from the host suite, or name the plan that would make it real.

AUDITOR'S REVIEW OF PLAN P02M0181 (2026-09-21T14:09:05Z):

**Rating: 4/10.** The intended service boundary is sensible, but the document is still a requirements outline. Its completion dependency is unresolved, and the publication, normalization and authority contracts need decisions before implementation. The findings below concern those missing decisions, not the expected absence of the new service's code.

Reviewed the plan, its originating HID Power Device and ACPI requirements, current ACPI parsing, provider lifecycle, power authority, service wiring and guest fixtures at commit `467f761df5a27b5409ffa3cd54e942485b4cf078`.

1. **High - The completion condition depends on the drivers this milestone is supposed to unblock, including two additional ACPI prerequisites.**

   **What is wrong:** The definition of done requires an HID source and an ACPI source to publish through the service, while the final section describes this milestone as their prerequisite. The HID driver explicitly waits for P02M0181. The ACPI classes additionally require firmware-node device identity and an AML interpreter. Neither prerequisite is scheduled here. The current ACPI crate is a bounded table parser and explicitly publishes no devices; it cannot provide battery or thermal namespace values. See [plan completion condition](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:42), [HID dependency](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:5691), [ACPI blockers](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:4035), and [current ACPI scope](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/acpi/src/lib.rs:1).

   **Why it matters:** An implementer cannot complete this as a service-only change with actual HID and ACPI producers. They must either absorb substantial blocked platform work or substitute synthetic data while claiming the two-source condition is met. The latter would not establish the advertised hardware integration.

   **Required correction:** Separate landing the service/provider contract from completing real-source integration. Name the driver and ACPI prerequisites, their ordering and which milestone owns each integration step. If a service-first gate uses injected HID/ACPI-shaped fixtures, identify that scope explicitly and retain an open completion condition for actual providers. Specify the guest fixture and its observable assertions; the existing USB harness does not automatically attach a UPS ([attachment path](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/harness/qemu-run.sh:868), [supported gadget kinds](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/harness/usb-gadget.sh:73)).

2. **High - The plan defines read/control clients but no authorized producer boundary.**

   **What is wrong:** The stated problem is that drivers lack a destination, but the proposed interface only divides authority into STATE and CONTROL. It never defines how a driver registers a source, publishes readings, identifies its sources, or loses publication authority when its binding ends. The project's provider catalogue has a closed kind set without power, manager-owned publication generations, and an explicit withdrawal mechanism. These are real integration requirements, not an existing generic power publication API. See [interface and publication requirement](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:21), [provider kinds and identity](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:151), [withdrawal semantics](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:193), and [catalogue connection contract](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:309).

   **Why it matters:** A client API alone leaves the original driver blocker unresolved. An improvised shared publish endpoint can also let a reader fabricate another source's measurements, or leave an unplugged UPS's last healthy state visible indefinitely.

   **Required correction:** Define the bounded provider-to-service contract and its authority separately from public readers and operators. Choose the catalogue/bootstrap integration, assign source identity and generation ownership, restrict each publisher to its own sources, and define withdrawal, disconnect and restart behavior. Identify the corresponding provider-kind, wire/registry and service-manifest changes, with tests for unauthorized publication and provider loss. Reuse the existing binding lifetime model rather than inventing an unrelated device identity.

3. **High - CONTROL has no operation set or route to the existing power authority.**

   **What is wrong:** The plan promises a separate control capability but names no control verbs, targets, grant owner or dispatch boundary. The upstream HID item specifically requires capability-gated output switching and shutdown commands. Machine reboot/power-off already have a deliberately narrow interface whose authority remains with SystemManager; DeviceManager receives a `SYSPOWER` client through the service manifest. The plan does not say how these existing machine operations relate to per-device UPS controls. See [control requirement](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:21), [HID output controls](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0099.md:5694), [existing machine-power boundary](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/process.lsidl:133), and [existing capability delivery](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/user/services/manifest.toml:2725).

   **Why it matters:** Implementers can produce incompatible meanings of the same control grant: an empty placeholder, authority over one device, or authority over platform-wide power operations. The plan does not choose among them, so neither callers nor tests can determine what a grant authorizes. Moving root authority into the new service would also undo an existing security boundary unnecessarily.

   **Required correction:** Specify the supported control operations and choose whether a grant authorizes one device or platform-wide control, together with its minting/grant policy and the path to the responsible existing service or provider. Keep suspend excluded as the plan already requires. State whether machine power is reached through the current narrow interface or is outside the new interface. Test that read clients cannot invoke controls and that control requests respect the declared grant scope; this does not require per-device attenuation if an explicitly authorized platform-wide operator endpoint is the chosen design.

4. **High - The normalized vocabulary omits the distinctions needed to normalize real battery values correctly.**

   **What is wrong:** The plan lists capacity, voltage, current and runtime, but freezes no units, representation of unavailable values, conversion rules or ownership of normalization. This is more than choosing integer widths. ACPI can report capacity in either mWh or mAh and uses an explicit unknown sentinel; these cannot all be treated as one unqualified numeric capacity. See [planned vocabulary and tests](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:24) and the primary [ACPI power-source specification, battery information and status fields](https://uefi.org/specs/ACPI/6.6/10_Power_Source_and_Power_Meter_Devices.html).

   **Why it matters:** Two sources can appear to publish one schema while reporting different physical quantities. Treating unknown as zero or deriving unavailable runtime as a number produces false empty-battery or alarm decisions, which defeats the principal reason for this service.

   **Required correction:** Add a compact field/units contract for each source type, including energy versus charge capacity, missing/unknown/invalid readings, sign and direction, supported precision and checked conversion behavior. Assign normalization to one layer and define how source alarms and thermal thresholds are represented without introducing policy actions. Use cross-source fixtures with independently stated expected values, including unknowns, overflow and conversions that cannot be performed from the available data.

5. **Medium - Bounded, coalesced subscriptions lack a coherent snapshot and loss contract.**

   **What is wrong:** Enumeration and subscription are both completion requirements, but the plan does not define their ordering, the initial subscription state, or what happens when queued changes are coalesced or overflow. The repository already addresses the enumeration/subscription race by returning the current provider set and subsequent changes through one subscription operation. See [subscription requirement](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:30), [completion requirement](/data/yellow/libersystem/docs/todo/P02M0181.md:42), and [existing snapshot-plus-live convention](/data/yellow/libersystem/src/idl/device.lsidl:309).

   **Why it matters:** A battery can disappear or change between enumeration and subscription, leaving a client with a permanently incorrect view. Unspecified coalescing also leaves it unclear whether alarm transitions are intentionally lossy, preserved, or recoverable; a bounded queue alone cannot establish the promised observable state.

   **Required correction:** Define an atomic initial snapshot plus subsequent updates, or an equivalent revision/resynchronization protocol. State the per-source coalescing and overflow policy, including removals and alarm changes, and the bounds on subscriber resources. Add focused tests for a change during subscription, a slow reader, overflow and source removal, and make the guest oracle assert the resulting client-visible state.

Validation was static source, plan and primary protocol specification inspection. No build or guest execution was performed. No plan or source code was modified.

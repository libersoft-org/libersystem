# WHY A STAGED DRIVER OR SERVICE HAS NO ORACLE THAT NAMES IT.
#
# `component<TAB>reason`. Read by check-component-oracles.sh, which refuses a staged driver or
# service that has neither a covering test nor a line here.
#
# A staged executable is not evidence that a kernel-test fixture starts it. Functional claims
# belong only on tests that exercise the real component; development checks are named here because
# this census reads kernel declarations only.

dev_channel	its oracle is a DEV-GUEST check, not a kernel test: dev-selftest.py drives publication, refusal and rollback through this driver, and that guest cannot share a boot with the suite - qemu-run.sh refuses DEV_PROFILE together with TEST. Covered, elsewhere.
gamepad_fixture	its oracle is the qemu-gamepad-tool GATE, not a kernel test: the gate boots the development image with the fixture's QEMU test device, and `gamepadcheck` reads the `gamepad` tool's own lines for every pad the fixture plays - a press, a range's ends, a hat, a departure and a replug. This census reads kernel declarations only. Covered, elsewhere.
dev_agent	the same: dev-selftest.py IS the oracle for this agent and runs on the development guest. The check above ranges over kernel tests and cannot see a dev-guest one.
session_service	NO ORACLE. Nothing in the suite fails when this service breaks. It is staged, it starts, and no test asserts an effect of it - so a change to it selects builds and nothing that would catch a regression. This line is the record of that, not a decision that it needs none.
time_service	NO ORACLE. The RTC tests assert what the KERNEL reads off the clock hardware; nothing asserts what this service answers to a client. Same shape as session_service and recorded for the same reason.
virtio_input	NO ORACLE. This line used to name `kernel.services.input_service_streams_pointer_events`, and that test does not exercise this driver: the pointer device is interactive-only, so the test spawns InputService and - in its own words - "plays the driver itself" by sending raw events on a producer end it keeps. Breaking `virtio_input` cannot make it fail, and nothing else in the suite asserts an effect of it. A change to it selects builds and a smoke boot. Recorded as the gap it is rather than as the coverage it is not.

virtio_gpu	Its real runtime oracle is dev.gpu-restart, which checks the translated driver after disable/rebind and a displayed frame. The kernel DisplayService restoration fixture supplies its own scanout and does not execute this driver; its former driver covers claim was false.
virtio_console	NO FUNCTIONAL ORACLE. The ConsoleService channel test exercises ConsoleService and DisplayService with a synthetic scanout; it never starts this driver. The boot observes driver readiness but asserts no virtio-console queue effect, so that is not claimed as functional coverage.
system_graph_service	NO FUNCTIONAL ORACLE. kernel.kernel.system_graph_reflects_live_state tests kernel graph::collect_from directly and never launches SystemGraphService. Boot readiness alone does not assert the service's generated interface or replies.

virtio_rng	NO ORACLE. Nothing in the suite starts this driver. The secure-random test branches on whether the pool happens to be seeded rather than requiring THIS driver to have seeded it, so breaking the driver cannot make it fail; the boot observes readiness and asserts no queue effect. A change to it selects builds and a smoke boot. It is one of the phased-backlog virtio devices rather than part of the running set, and this line records the gap as the gap it is rather than as coverage it does not have.
power_service	its oracle is the power-service GATE, not a kernel test: the gate boots the development image with the in-guest power fixture and asserts the canonical units, the subscriptions, the controls and the grant boundaries through `powercheck` and `powerread`; the `acpi` and `power-ups` gates read the real producers through it. This census reads kernel declarations only. Covered, elsewhere.
power_fixture	its oracle is the power-service GATE: the fixture's publications are what that gate's probes read through PowerService, value by value. Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_service	its oracle is the acpi GATE, not a kernel test: the gate boots QEMU's DSDT with the fixture SSDT and asserts every node accounted for, the node channels and grants handed through DeviceManager, the fixture driver's probes, a restart of the service and `_OSC` granting and refusing; the processor-power gate reads the processor objects it serves. Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_fixture	its oracle is the acpi GATE: its probes - the region reads and writes, the refusals, the line field, `_DSD`, `_DSM` and `_INI` refused - are the lines that gate requires. Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_power	its oracle is the acpi GATE (the fixture's battery, adapter and zone read through PowerService in exact units, updated at the power line, a storm coalesced) and the processor-power GATE (the zone's cooling half: `_TMP` sampled every `_TSP`, and past `_CRT` the forced power-off armed by this driver with the policy stopped, QEMU gone at the bound). Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_button	its oracle is the sleep GATE: the fixture's lid, power button and sleep button raised by the harness, the lid's close suspending the machine and the last sleep's record naming the lid and the sleep button. Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_tad	its oracle is the sleep GATE's platform boot: the CMOS RTC named absent, the guest's wall clock within two seconds of the TAD's clock the harness runs five years ahead, a file stamped with it, the TAD's power state through each sleep, and a suspend to RAM's timed wake on its timer. Covered, elsewhere.
sleep_fixture	its oracle is the sleep GATE: a suspend this fixture's binding refuses, named in the refusal and unwound to a running system. Covered, elsewhere.
acpi_fan	its oracle is the processor-power GATE (past `_AC0` FAN0's `_FSL` level read from the fixture's pages, FAN1 following the default curve) and the sleep GATE's fan case (the level cleared by the harness during S3 and commanded again after the resume). Covered, elsewhere.
processor_power_service	its oracle is the processor-power GATE, not a kernel test: the registers the kernel writes for the tables this service installs are read by the host from the fixture's region - at rest, under load, per profile, under `_PPC` with `_OST` - the service killed and relaunched with every table installed again, and the zone heated through the relaunched instance to `_CRT`. Covered, elsewhere.
hibernation_service	its oracle is the hibernate GATE: the image written, QEMU's `SUSPEND_DISK` on a held QMP connection, the restore by a new QEMU on the same disk and TPM with the counter going on, and the modified, foreign-machine and foreign-image refusals. Covered, elsewhere.
tpm_driver	its oracle is the qemu-tpm-tool GATE: behind both of QEMU's front-ends, the row, draws, PCR extends checked by SHA-256 on the host, a seal, the driver disabled and enabled under a held connection, and a quote OpenSSL verifies. Covered, elsewhere.
tpm_service	its oracle is the qemu-tpm-tool GATE: the grants' refusals (`not-granted` at the service, `other-component`), TpmService stopped and started, and the quote verified on the host. Covered, elsewhere.
i2c_hid	its oracle is the i2c-hid GATE: the touchpad's and touchscreen's reports reaching a live InputService client in order, and the storm and reset cases. Covered, elsewhere.
uart16550	its oracle is the serial-handoff GATE: COM1 handed to this driver and taken back with the kernel's own accesses counted at zero, and a panic on the wire while it holds the port. Covered, elsewhere.
i6300esb	its oracle is the watchdog GATE: QEMU's run state under `-action watchdog=pause` stays `running` through healthy timeouts, a restarted service and a rebound driver, and reaches `watchdog` when the liveness answer stops; the reset run names its watchdog as the cause. Covered, elsewhere.
tco	its oracle is the watchdog GATE: the TCO armed alone with No-Reboot cleared and read back, three timeouts `running`, and the silence ending in `watchdog`. Covered, elsewhere.
wdat	its oracle is the watchdog GATE: a WDAT over the TCO's registers loaded with `-acpitable`, driven by this driver to the same `running` and `watchdog` states. Covered, elsewhere.
watchdog_service	its oracle is the watchdog GATE: the device the policy names armed and the others disarmed, the service killed and its replacement arming again, and the orderly reboot's notice answered before the first service stops. Covered, elsewhere.
ipmi	its oracle is the ipmi GATE: the five system interfaces each answering its own BMC's product ID, the sensors and FRU as the harness files hold them, a scripted temperature reaching a PowerService subscriber, and an answer past the bound refused. Covered, elsewhere.
smbus_ich9	its oracle is the ipmi GATE: the SSIF interface is reached through this controller, and its BMC answers with the files' sensors and FRU. Covered, elsewhere.
bmc_service	its oracle is the ipmi and qemu-ipmi-admin GATES: the BMCs reported once per GUID, the boot event written once across a restart, events followed, and the two administrative actions through the protected screen. Covered, elsewhere.
ucsi_acpi	its oracle is the typec-ucsi GATE: four PPM profiles - contracts, role swaps accepted and refused, DisplayPort entry, a silent PPM reported and recovered - read through `typec` and PowerService. Covered, elsewhere.
tcpci	its oracle is the typec-tcpci GATE: a Power Delivery source written from the source side negotiating with this driver's sink - the contract, the transition envelope, the refused swaps, malformed messages not believed, the controller cycled without VBUS dropping and 200 negotiations timed. Covered, elsewhere.
typec_service	its oracle is the typec-ucsi and typec-tcpci GATES: every connector's state, contract and partner read through `typec` and `typeccheck`, and the `usb-c` source in PowerService. Covered, elsewhere.

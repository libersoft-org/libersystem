# P02M0113 - LiberFS: what a mount may not do, and what a checksum does not prove

Status: COMPLETE. An outside audit of LiberFS, verified claim by claim against the code.

Two sentences hold the whole milestone. **A mount may not repair what it cannot read** - because a
failure it cannot distinguish from an empty disk becomes a format. And **a checksum proves the bytes
are the ones that were written, not that they mean anything** - so a structure that is internally
consistent and impossible is accepted by every check that only verifies CRCs.

*Condensed 2026-08-08: findings stated once, with their resolutions folded in.*

## The mount

**Any mount failure formatted the volume.** `LiberFs::mount` returned `Option`, so an unreadable
superblock, an I/O error and a genuinely blank disk were the same answer - and the service formatted
on all three. Everything else in this milestone is downstream of that: an answer meaning "I could not
tell" may never be read as "there is nothing here".

**The superblock was checked for form, not for sense**: magic, version, flags, algorithms and CRC,
none of them against each other or against the medium. **The label was not the UTF-8 it claimed to
be** - truncation avoided splitting a sequence but nothing validated what was read back. **The free
bitmap was sized through an unchecked `u64 → usize` cast.**

## What a checksum does not prove

- **A raw extent whose `length` outruns its `store_len` got blocks nobody marked.**
- **Nothing detected two owners of one block.** Marking is idempotent by construction, so setting a
  bit twice is invisible - which means a cross-linked structure passes.
- **The free map was derived from metadata nobody verified.**
- **A CRC-consistent self-fanning tree made `fsck` exponential.** The marking walk carried no visited
  set, so a tree that points back into itself is a denial of service that verifies perfectly.
- **A directory hash-collision group could be split across two leaves.**
- **Deleting a damaged "empty" directory stranded its children**, because emptiness was
  `inode.size == 0` rather than an actual absence of entries.
- **`fsck` was a checksum scrubber, not a structural verifier.**

## Snapshots and transactions

- **The previous generation's snapshot table was not protected.**
- **Snapshot records from disk were trusted more than the ones being written.**
- **A failed transaction kept a compression change it did not commit.**
- **A named snapshot mount answered from the live generation's caches** - not in the audit, found
  while verifying it.
- **A truncated compressed extent poisoned the cache for the snapshot that still held it.**
- **An invalid compressed stream read as short data plus zeros** rather than as an error.

## Errors that became other answers

- **`lookup` collapsed six answers into `None`** - `NotFound`, an I/O error and a type mismatch all
  arrived as "no".
- **A listing hid an I/O error as a missing file.**
- **A whole-file overwrite dropped `owner_tag`.**
- **I/O at the very top of the address space overflowed** its own guard.
- **Inode numbers are `u32`, monotonic and never reused**, so a long-lived volume eventually refuses
  to create anything. Recorded as a bound rather than fixed.

# P02M0164 - One NIC, one disk, one screen: a second of anything has nowhere to go

Status: PLANNED AFTER P02M0163. PROVIDERS AND DEPENDENCIES. FIFTH OF SEVEN - see P02M0098.

## Goal

Let a driver publish typed provider instances that services subscribe to, so a machine can have two
disks, two NICs or two sound devices - and let a registry entry wait for something it needs instead
of failing because it was tried too early.

## What is there now

- **PROVIDERS ARE HANDLES PASSED ONCE AT BOOT.** A driver's report carries a channel and DeviceManager
  routes it to the one service that owns that kind: the block channel to StorageService, the frame
  channel to NetworkService, and so on. It works, and it works for exactly one instance of each.
- **THE MACHINE THIS TREE BOOTS HAS FOUR VIRTIO-BLK FUNCTIONS.** They are told apart in the boot
  report because the boot-report milestone put addresses on the lines - not because anything in the
  system holds four block providers.
- **A DRIVER IS ONLINE OR IT IS NOT.** There is no state between "the process started" and "the
  device is usable", so a controller with a slow child is either reported up before it is or not
  reported at all.
- **NOTHING WAITS FOR ANYTHING.** A driver that needs another provider present is launched anyway and
  fails.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - a driver publishes zero or more typed providers.** `block`, `frame`, `display`, `input`,
  `serial`, `pcm` - each with its own identity and generation, added and removed while the driver
  runs. Zero is a legal number: a controller with no child is online because it is bound.

- [ ] **M2 - services subscribe instead of being handed one.** A service asks for providers of a kind
  and receives add and remove events. Removing one provider does not disturb the others, and a
  service started later sees what is already there.

- [ ] **M3 - driver readiness and provider readiness are separate.** `READY` means the process is
  initialised and supervised. A provider appears when its own probe is complete. One child failing
  does not take a healthy controller or its siblings down.

- [ ] **M4 - provider removal completes in-flight work or says it could not.** A consumer acknowledges
  a removal before the channel is revoked, under a bounded deadline, after which the revocation is
  forced and reported as forced. A clean removal never loses a request that had been accepted.

- [ ] **M5 - `requires` and `provides` in the registry.** An entry may declare what it needs - a bus,
  a provider kind, a firmware transport - and is retried when that arrives. Cycles and unsatisfiable
  boot-critical chains are refused when the registry is built, which is where the system-volume cycle
  is caught as one instance of a general rule rather than by its own checker.

- [ ] **M6 - the tests are about the second instance.** Two disks, two NICs, one removed while the
  other is in use, a provider that arrives late and releases a waiting bind, a consumer that does not
  acknowledge a removal.

## Definition of done

- Two devices of one kind produce two independent providers, and removing one leaves the other
  working, under test.
- A service subscribing after boot sees the providers that already exist.
- A driver can be online with no providers and add one later.
- A registry entry waits for what it declared and binds when it arrives; a cycle is refused at
  registry-build time.
- A forced removal is reported as forced and never claims the in-flight work completed.

## What this milestone refuses, and why

- **A general publish/subscribe bus.** Providers are typed, few, and owned by a binding. A message
  broker would be a larger thing than the problem.
- **Reworking the services that consume them.** Each service changes at one seam - where it used to
  receive a handle, it subscribes. What a service does with a disk is not this milestone's.
- **Multi-path or failover policy.** Two providers of a kind is what this makes possible; deciding
  which one something should use is a policy question and belongs to whoever has the use case.

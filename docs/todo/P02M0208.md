# P02M0208 - A driver's answer is timed by when it arrived, not by when DeviceManager read it

Status: PLANNED (2026-11-08) - the owner's decision is recorded below; nothing is implemented yet, by the owner's
instruction ("zatim nerealizuj").

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-11-08). The question came out of P02M0207. The tenth sealed run's `system-suspend-riscv64`
failure had three defects behind it; P02M0207 fixed two. This is the third. P02M0207's fix of the recovery window took
it out of that gate's way without removing it. Asked whether to fix it there, give it a milestone of its own or leave
it, the owner chose a milestone of its own, planned and not implemented: "OK, zaloz tedy na to ten milestone, ale
zatim nerealizuj".

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (read from the tree on 2026-11-08, at 1f54d157):
  - THE RULE IS RIGHT; ITS CLOCK IS NOT. DeviceManager's deadlines are about silence. `drain_channel` says so ("A
    DEADLINE IS ABOUT SILENCE, AND THERE IS NO SILENCE WHEN THE ANSWER HAS ARRIVED"), and `check-driver-lifecycle.py`
    holds it (`ready_receipt_deadline`: a `READY` taken at 99 against a deadline of 100 is timely). But the time an
    answer is given is `last_frame_at = clock()`, taken when DeviceManager READS the frame. The kernel keeps no time on
    a queued message: `Message` (`object/channel/mod.rs`) carries an id, the bytes, the capabilities and their
    accounting. So "arrived" is approximated by "read", which holds only while DeviceManager reads at once.
  - DEVICEMANAGER DOES NOT ALWAYS READ AT ONCE. It is one thread, and its work between two waits is synchronous:
    `begin_bind` starts a driver's executable (`spawn_in`), attaches its device to an IOMMU domain and sends `BIND`,
    and a teardown is followed to its confirmations. Meanwhile every other driver's answers wait unread in their
    channels.
  - THREE DEADLINES ARE JUDGED BY THE READ:
    1. THE BIND HANDSHAKE. `tick_handshakes` queues `TimedOut` for every handshake whose deadline `clock()` has passed
       before any channel is read - at the top of a pass of `pump`, and of the loop that stands between the two
       phases - and `drain_frames` refuses a `READY` read after the deadline (`handshake_expired(..,
       node.last_frame_at)`).
    2. THE HEARTBEAT. `drain_channel` expires a ping whose deadline has passed (`expire_heartbeat(node, now)`) before
       it reads the answer that may be waiting in the channel.
    3. A PLANNED STOP. `expire_planned_stop(node, now)`, the same way, before the `STOPPED`.
  - MEASURED (2026-11-08, the riscv64 fixture boot of `system-suspend-riscv64`, in place, before P02M0207 fixed the
    recovery window; the times are the host's stamps on the serial log). After a real suspend to RAM, virtio-net
    printed `online` - the line a driver prints just before its offers and its `READY`. DeviceManager was then binding
    virtio-console (its device attached to an IOMMU domain 0.33 s later) and virtio-snd (1.00 s later), and settling
    xhci's teardown (1.63 s later). It read virtio-net's `READY` about 1.7 s after it was sent, three ticks past the
    deadline: "virtio-net did not report in inside its share of the boot window", with "last opcode 4 3 tick(s) ago"
    (opcode 4 is `READY`). The cause it gave was wrong as well: "it never answered its bind at all". The driver was
    torn down and the network did not come back.
  - WHY IT STILL MATTERS AFTER P02M0207. A recovery now gets its whole window (133 s on riscv64), so that gate no
    longer meets the defect. Any deadline shorter than one of DeviceManager's busy spells still does: the last seconds
    of the boot window, a heartbeat, a planned stop. The slower the machine, the longer the spells.

THE DESIGN: THE KERNEL STAMPS EACH MESSAGE WITH ITS ARRIVAL, AND DEVICEMANAGER JUDGES EACH FRAME BY ITS STAMP.
  - THE STAMP. A message records the tick at which its send put it on the receiver's queue - the monotonic clock the
    runtime's `clock()` reads. It is the kernel's, so a driver cannot move it.
  - A message that goes back to the head after a failed copy (`return_to_head`) keeps its stamp.
  - THE RECEIVE THAT RETURNS IT. The receive DeviceManager uses (`recv_message_caps`, `SYS_CHANNEL_RECV_CAPS`) gets a
    stamped counterpart in the kernel and the runtime. Every existing receive stays as it is. Whether the counterpart
    is a new syscall or an optional out-word on the existing one is settled at implementation, against the ABI's
    rules.
  - DEVICEMANAGER:
    - A frame's time is its stamp: `last_frame_at` is when the frame arrived.
    - An expiry is judged only after the node's channel has been read. `tick_handshakes`, `expire_heartbeat` and
      `expire_planned_stop` judge a node after its frames, not before them. An answer that arrived before its deadline
      is timely however late it is read, and one that arrived after it is late however soon it is read.
    - THE ORDER MAKES IT DECIDABLE. A channel is first in, first out, so the expiry is decided at the first frame
      stamped after the deadline, or at an empty channel. The bound of 64 frames a pass stays. It spreads the reading
      over passes, and the channel's depth bounds how many. A frame stamped after the deadline cannot answer it, so
      traffic cannot buy a driver time.
    - A LATE ANSWER IS CALLED LATE. A refused `READY` says how late it arrived ("answered N tick(s) after its
      deadline"), not "it never answered its bind at all".
    - THE WAIT IS PRINTED. The `bind window` line gains how long the answer waited to be read ("..., read M tick(s)
      after it arrived"). This is DeviceManager's own lateness, measured on every bind of every boot.
  - WHAT DOES NOT CHANGE: every window and its length - the boot window, P02M0207's chains, the attempt and heartbeat
    deadlines; the bound of 64 frames a pass; what any other service receives.

WHAT IT COSTS:
  - Eight bytes on every queued message, and one read of the tick counter on every send.
  - One receive more in the ABI, on three targets.
  - The runtime changes, so the audit link (`foreign-audit-link.py --check`) and the dynamic report (`./check.sh
    --refresh dynamic-report`) are redone, as for every runtime change.
  - DeviceManager's `bind window` and timeout lines change. Every gate that reads them is checked against the new
    lines; `check-typec-ucsi.sh` matches the `bind window` line's beginning, which stays.

WEIGHED AND DROPPED:
  - A time written into `READY` by the driver: the driver could move it. A deadline that a slow or hostile driver can
    talk its way past is no deadline.
  - Reading every channel between two pieces of synchronous work: it shortens the wait and cannot end it, because the
    piece of work itself is the wait.
  - Lengthening a deadline by the time DeviceManager was busy: a correction for one cause of lateness that the stamp
    makes unnecessary, and a window whose length would depend on what else happened.
  - A delay built into DeviceManager's test build, to stage the race in QEMU: a supervisor whose test build behaves
    unlike its shipping one tests a different program. The host oracle runs DeviceManager's own code.

ORDER. Independent of P02M0203 to P02M0205, which change the same file for other rules.

- [ ] 1. THE KERNEL'S STAMP. `Message` records the tick of its enqueue, `return_to_head` keeps it, and the stamped
  receive returns it. Tests in the test kernel, on all three targets:
  - a message sent at tick T and received k ticks later returns T;
  - a failed copy (a buffer too small, a bad address) and the receive after it return the same stamp;
  - two messages keep their order, and their stamps do not decrease;
  - a message that was queued before a `wait_any` began keeps the stamp of its send.
- [ ] 2. THE RUNTIME. The stamped receive beside `try_recv_caps`; the audit link and the dynamic report redone.
- [ ] 3. DEVICEMANAGER:
  - frames judged by their stamps;
  - the three expiries judged after the node's frames;
  - a late answer called late;
  - the `bind window` line's wait.
- [ ] 4. THE HOST ORACLE. `check-driver-lifecycle.py` runs DeviceManager's own `drain_channel` against a fake clock
  and a fake channel. Its fake receive gains stamps, and:
  - `ready_receipt_deadline` gains the case this milestone is for: a `READY` that arrived at 99 and is read at 140,
    against a deadline of 100, is timely. Today's code refuses it, and the case is watched failing that way first. One
    that arrived at 100 is late, however early it is read;
  - the same pair for a heartbeat's answer and for a planned stop's `STOPPED`;
  - a pass that starts past a deadline with a timely `READY` waiting does not queue `TimedOut` (`tick_handshakes`);
  - the gate's mutations keep their meaning ("late READY admitted" now admits a `READY` stamped at the deadline), and
    gain one: the stamp replaced by the read time, which the new cases must catch.
- [ ] 5. IN QEMU:
  - the kernel suite's new cases (item 1), on all three targets;
  - `system-suspend-riscv64`, where the defect was measured: its riscv64 fixture boot asserts the new `bind window`
    clause, and that no driver whose `READY` arrived inside its window was refused;
  - the gates that read DeviceManager's lines, run once against the new ones.

EXCLUDES: making DeviceManager's binds asynchronous or faster; the length of any window; other services' receives,
which can use the stamp later.

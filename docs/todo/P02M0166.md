# P02M0166 - Everything that went wrong is reported as `failed`

Status: PLANNED AFTER P02M0165. OBSERVABILITY, TYPED FAILURES AND OPERATOR POLICY. LAST OF SEVEN - see P02M0098.

## Goal

Make the state of every binding readable from outside, with one vocabulary for why something is not
running, and let an operator choose among the drivers a device already declared - without being able
to widen what any of them may do.

## What is there now

- **TWO CAUSES CAN BE TOLD APART AND THE REST CANNOT.** `STATE_UNBOUND` and `STATE_DRIVER_MISSING`
  were separated under P02M0098 on 2026-08-12, and they are DeviceManager's own states, printed.
  Everything else - a claim that failed, a protocol mismatch, a dependency not met, a driver that
  hung - has nowhere to be said.
- **`lsdev` AND THE SYSTEM GRAPH SHOW WHAT WAS TRUE AT BOOT.** They do not show the selected artifact,
  the match rule, the generation, the granted resources or the published providers.
- **THERE IS NO OPERATOR CONTROL AT ALL.** Which driver binds a device is decided entirely by the
  compiled registry.
- **THE BOOT VOLUME IS FOUND BY LOOKING.** The loader picks the first LiberFS volume, or the one the
  medium's pairing names. A second identical disk is told apart by the pairing file and by nothing
  else, and `DeviceManager` reconstruction has no locator to reproduce.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - one typed failure vocabulary, rendered the same everywhere.** `unbound`, `claimed`,
  `removed`, `driver-missing`, `artifact-mismatch`, `protocol-mismatch`, `dependency-pending`,
  `iommu-required`, `resource-exhausted`, `hung`, `quarantined`. Every surface renders the same cause
  without collapsing it, and adding a cause means adding it once.

- [ ] **M2 - `lsdev` shows the binding.** Per node: the selected artifact, the match rule that chose
  it, the binding unit, the generation, the state and restart count, the granted resources and the
  published providers. A controller's health is its own: an xHCI with only a keyboard on it is online.

- [ ] **M3 - the System Graph and the log agree with it.** One state, three renderings, and a test
  that compares them rather than a convention that they should match.

- [ ] **M4 - operator policy, bounded by the registry.** An operator may disable a device, retry a
  bind, or select among the candidates the registry already declared for it. Selecting something the
  registry did not declare is refused: policy narrows, never widens. The policy is capability-gated
  and survives a reboot.

- [ ] **M5 - the boot volume is named, not found.** The boot protocol and the image metadata carry a
  root-device locator - a firmware path or a BDF chain plus the volume UUID - and DeviceManager
  resolves exactly that node. A second matching disk becomes an ordinary provider and cannot take the
  system role by enumerating first. Missing, duplicate or ambiguous is a typed boot failure, and a
  reconstruction selects the same backing or refuses.

- [ ] **M6 - a bounded diagnostic snapshot.** What is captured when a binding fails or hangs is
  declared, bounded and free of device payload. It survives when persistent logging does not, because
  the case it exists for is the one where logging is what broke.

## Definition of done

- Every cause a binding can fail for has a name in one vocabulary, and `lsdev`, the System Graph and
  the log render the same one.
- `lsdev` shows the artifact, rule, unit, generation, state, resources and providers of every binding.
- An operator can disable, retry and choose among declared candidates, and cannot grant a driver
  anything the registry did not.
- The system volume is resolved from an explicit locator, and an ambiguous one fails loudly rather
  than picking.
- A failed or hung binding leaves a bounded snapshot that does not depend on the log surviving.

## What this milestone refuses, and why

- **A monitoring or metrics subsystem.** These are three existing surfaces showing state that already
  exists in one place.
- **An operator policy language.** Disable, retry and choose-among-declared is the whole of it. A
  policy that can express more than the registry declared is a way to widen authority from a
  configuration file.
- **Persistent history.** The snapshot is about the failure that just happened. Keeping a record over
  time is a logging question and this tree has one.

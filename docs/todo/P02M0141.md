# P02M0141 - ManagedReferenceVM v1

Status: PLANNED. SYSTEM-ARCHITECTURE AND PRODUCT-PROFILE GATE. This milestone qualifies one
bounded, host-managed QEMU/KVM reference VM. It does not create a general server, autonomous edge
appliance, physical-hardware or desktop platform.

## Goal

Turn the current reference VM into one coherent managed product without building abstractions for
products that do not exist yet.

LiberSystem already has the foundations to preserve: capability authority, a small mechanism-led
kernel, LSIDL contracts, process-isolated services and drivers, explicit volumes, a data-driven
artifact/service manifest and QEMU verification. This milestone closes only the composition layer
needed by the first managed VM:

- one exact statement of what the guest and its trusted host provide;
- one canonical description of the shipped guest composition;
- typed bootstrap, lifecycle, authority, Domain, state and resource declarations;
- fail-closed service and control-plane recovery with reboot as a valid outcome;
- one signed immutable guest image and a host-managed replacement/rollback procedure;
- local, freshness-bearing health and lifecycle evidence; and
- system scenarios proving those specific promises.

The work is deliberately incremental. Extend the existing manifest and runtime mechanisms; do not
replace working subsystem-owned registries, protocols or verification models with a new universal
framework.

## Product boundary fixed here

`ManagedReferenceVM v1` has the following non-negotiable boundary:

- The only Tier-1 machine is x86_64 QEMU/KVM with OVMF/UEFI and the exact virtio device set frozen
  by its `MachineProfile`. AArch64 and RISC-V remain development and portability targets until a
  later profile qualifies them independently.
- The shipped system is one lockstep, immutable whole image containing trusted first-party system
  components. Image-internal system services are not independently upgraded or version-mixed.
- The VM host is part of the deployment and recovery contract. It owns image installation, atomic
  replacement, rollback/snapshot policy, console and log capture, and a watchdog able to restart an
  unresponsive guest. It must fence the prior QEMU process and hold one exclusive launch/data lock
  before attaching the writable persistent volume to a new VM epoch.
- The trusted computing boundary includes the host supervisor, host kernel/filesystem, QEMU/KVM,
  OVMF and image store. The guest makes no confidentiality or integrity claim against a malicious
  host.
- Persistent application and service data live outside the immutable system image. Their schemas
  must be compatible with a candidate image before that image may replace the current one.
- SystemManager remains resident and supervises the control-plane owner. Explicitly restartable
  services may be replaced in-guest; loss or wedging of the control plane may instead request or
  trigger a bounded VM reboot. In-place resurrection of SystemManager and workload adoption are not
  promises of this profile.
- A service name is not authority. Initial authority is a generated, auditable projection of the
  composition, while runtime access still requires actual kernel capabilities.
- Service replacement creates a new instance epoch. Generic file, socket, watch, session or stream
  resurrection is not promised: unless a service-specific contract says otherwise, outstanding
  subresources fail and the client must reopen or the guest must restart.
- Operations are local or host-mediated. This profile has no in-guest RemoteGateway, network PKI,
  device enrollment or general remote-principal model. A dedicated bounded host/guest status
  protocol reports health; parsing human console text is not that protocol.

## Explicitly deferred

The following work needs a new product contract and must not be smuggled into this milestone as a
"future-proof" abstraction:

- guest-managed A/B slots, recovery image, Secure Boot policy, anti-rollback counter and autonomous
  field update;
- device/operator enrollment, credential recovery and a network RemoteGateway;
- dynamic or alternative service-provider selection, mixed-version system services and rolling
  stateful upgrade;
- generic lease/revocation, resource-resume or cross-volume transaction frameworks;
- hostile multi-tenancy, workload adoption, high-availability control-plane replacement and
  distributed placement;
- removable-media discovery as a general Volume Registry, physical-hardware breadth, desktop and
  application-ecosystem policy.

These remain possible future directions in `docs/CONCEPT_EN.md`; they are not active milestone
dependencies and their seams are preserved only where the current product already needs one.

## Relationship to existing work

- P02M0079 and the current `manifest.toml` remain the seed and source owner for artifact staging,
  service rows and generated lifecycle data. `SystemSpec` below is a compatible extension, not a
  parallel manifest.
- P02M0098 remains the owner of the driver/provider lifecycle. This profile consumes only the
  fixed virtio devices selected by its MachineProfile; unselected multi-instance and hotplug breadth
  does not become a hidden completion prerequisite.
- P02M0104 and P02M0118 remain the owners of repeatable QEMU execution and attributable verification.
- Existing kernel, loader, ABI, filesystem, security, resource, System Graph and service milestones
  remain the owners of their implementations. This milestone composes their proven contracts and
  does not reopen them merely to rename a type.
- Local parser, kernel, driver, filesystem or codec defects found by a system scenario are recorded
  against the owning subsystem. The architecture gate remains the system invariant exercised by
  that scenario.

## Work

- [ ] **M1.1 - Freeze the managed-VM product, host/guest boundary and architectural decisions.**
  - Publish the exact supported user and operator journeys, trusted-host assumptions, security
    boundary, data ownership and explicit non-goals of `ManagedReferenceVM v1`.
  - Record the exact x86_64 QEMU/KVM, OVMF and virtio support tier; do not turn ISA bring-up into a
    claim about arbitrary machines or all emulator configurations.
  - Name the host supervisor, QEMU/KVM, OVMF, host kernel/filesystem and image store as explicit
    deployment dependencies and TCB assumptions. State that guest secrets are not protected from
    the trusted host.
  - Record concise ADRs for capability authority, the small kernel boundary, whole-image internal
    compatibility, explicit volumes, the native/LSIDL/current Liber-Wasm application seams and
    node-local Channels.
  - Classify every boundary as guest image-internal, persistent-state, host/guest operational or
    deliberately unsupported. Link audit findings to their owner rather than copying the audit into
    a second closure registry.
  - Completion is a reviewed, versioned `ManagedReferenceVM Product Contract v1`, not a broad
    catalog of hypothetical server, desktop or hardware products.

- [ ] **M1.2 - Extend the existing manifest into the minimal canonical `SystemSpec v1`.**
  - Preserve existing artifact/program/service rows and add only the fields consumed by this
    profile: stable artifact/deployment/service identity, boot and instance epoch rules, start
    dependency, bootstrap roles, Domain/failure parent, static resource profile, state class,
    config schema reference, fixed volume role and release inclusion.
  - Distinguish a shipped program from a desired deployment and a live process instance without
    introducing unused tenant, placement, alternative-provider or session-template machinery.
  - Compose owned fragments plus the product and MachineProfile overlay into one deterministic,
    versioned representation and digest. Reject duplicate identities, missing providers/artifacts,
    cycles, unknown required fields and invalid parents.
  - Use simple exact versions for image-internal contracts. Schema evolution must fail closed, but
    version ranges and runtime negotiation are not required for a lockstep image.
  - Adding an ordinary static service must require its owned fragment and implementation, not a new
    handwritten branch in the central bootstrap router.

- [ ] **M1.3 - Generate only the missing composition projections and remove their duplicates.**
  - Keep existing generated build/staging projections. Add the concrete start plan, typed bootstrap
    roles, initial grant plan, Domain/failure/resource placement, desired System Graph identity and
    release inventory from `SystemSpec`.
  - Migrate one consumer at a time in shadow mode, comparing generated and legacy output on the
    actual image before switching it and deleting the corresponding manual table.
  - Gate duplicate identity, missing grant target, invalid Domain parent, generated drift and a
    shipped artifact or deployment absent from the release inventory.
  - Authority and trust inventories are useful audit inputs, not a claim that one generated list is
    a complete property-independent TCB proof.
  - Do not absorb LSIDL schemas, driver matching, filesystem policy or the verification model into
    `SystemSpec`; keep their current owners and connect them through stable identities.

- [ ] **M1.4 - Finish one typed, versioned bootstrap and control contract.**
  - Replace remaining positional or numeric-tag conventions with a generated launch context naming
    component/service identity, instance epoch, typed capability roles, config version and declared
    Domain/failure parent.
  - Reuse the existing `CapSet`, generated grant sender and typed lifecycle foundations. Do not add
    a second universal envelope beside them or replace device-specific lifecycle owned by P02M0098.
  - Define readiness, health, stop, quiesce and disconnect messages with bounded deadlines and
    explicit failure results. A started process is not automatically Ready or Healthy.
  - Reject missing or duplicate required roles, unexpected authority-bearing handles, wrong role
    types and unsupported versions deterministically. Field order is not semantic.
  - A component receives only its declared capabilities; the common control format is not a reason
    to hand every component a resolver, manager or root capability.

- [ ] **M1.5 - Define the small lifecycle, state and fault contract actually promised by the VM.**
  - Use a bounded lifecycle vocabulary for absent, starting, ready, degraded, failed, stopping and
    replaced, while keeping desired and observed state distinct.
  - Classify every long-lived deployment as ephemeral, reconstructible or durable. Name the durable
    owner, storage location, schema version and behavior across service epoch, guest reboot and
    image replacement.
  - Mark only selected services restartable. Give them stable `ServiceId`, changing
    `InstanceEpoch`, readiness/health criteria and a finite retry/backoff policy; all others declare
    reboot or terminal failure as their recovery result.
  - Define the default client result on provider loss as an explicit failure. Add reopen, resume,
    replay or gap semantics only to a protocol that demonstrably needs and implements them.
  - Publish a finite fault matrix covering service crash/hang, ServiceManager/control-plane loss,
    SystemManager or guest hang, resource exhaustion, fixed-device loss, persistent-state refusal,
    power interruption and failed image replacement. Each row names detector, response, preserved
    state, recovery-time objective (RTO) and recovery-point objective (RPO); reboot, host rollback
    or operator recovery are valid honest outcomes.

- [ ] **M1.6 - Make SystemManager resident and execute the generated plan fail-closed.**
  - Keep SystemManager alive as the minimal owner/monitor of one `system@BootId` Domain and the
    ServiceManager instance executing the generated desired-state plan.
  - Align supervision, failure and resource parents for the profile's control plane, data services,
    fixed-device drivers and workloads. Every exception is explicit in `SystemSpec`.
  - Restart only services classified as restartable and reconstructible in M1.5. Replacement gets a
    new epoch and stale endpoints cannot be mistaken for the new instance.
  - On ServiceManager/control-plane loss, destroy what can be safely destroyed and request a bounded
    reboot. If SystemManager or the guest wedges, the host watchdog restarts the VM. Do not add an
    in-kernel first-userspace resurrection protocol or adopt processes across the failed epoch.
  - Prove that each recovery row reaches Ready again, a clean reboot/rollback, or a declared terminal
    refusal without silently continuing with an ownerless userspace subtree.

- [ ] **M1.7 - Define and enforce bounded authority, resource, configuration and volume semantics.**
  - Every initial grant names a real source grantor, capability/interface class, rights and target;
    generated boot holdings may be no broader than the plan. Dynamic grants remain attributable to
    specialized grantors that actually hold the parent capability.
  - M1.3 is the only generator of grant, Domain and resource projections. This task defines their
    semantics and makes the runtime enforce and report the generated result; it does not compile a
    parallel plan.
  - Split reboot, poweroff and host-requested image transition into a narrow `SystemPower`
    capability. Reacting to a power event must not grant root-Domain management.
  - Give each long-lived deployment an explicit or inherited static profile for memory, handles,
    threads, queued IPC, connections/admission and bounded work. Reserve enough progress for health,
    shutdown and reboot paths; advanced priority donation and interrupt accounting are deferred
    until a measured workload requires them.
  - Type and version only supported product configuration. Each exposed key names owner, default,
    validation, persistence and whether it applies live or after restart; add multi-key transactions
    only for a concrete group that requires atomicity.
  - Distinguish immutable release configuration, host-supplied deployment configuration and guest
    persistent state so image replacement cannot silently overwrite or reinterpret the wrong class.
  - Bind fixed roles such as immutable system image and persistent data to explicit MachineProfile
    identities rather than enumeration order. Refuse missing, duplicate or swapped roles before a
    writable mount. A general removable-media registry and cross-volume coordinator are outside
    this profile.

- [ ] **M1.8 - Freeze the MachineProfile and signed host-managed release contract.**
  - Specify exact QEMU/KVM and OVMF version policy, CPU/SMP and memory envelope, boot method, virtio
    device set/features, storage layout, display/console, timer/interrupt and power/watchdog
    semantics. Bind the profile identity and digest into the image and evidence.
  - Produce a publisher-signed `ReleaseManifest` for immutable OVMF code and variable-policy
    template, loader, kernel, read-only system image, `SystemSpec`, policy, MachineProfile
    constraints, state-compatibility range, provenance and shipped software inventory. Signing
    identifies exact immutable bytes and publisher authority, not merely a source revision or
    corruption checksum; the host operator pins the release verification key.
  - Keep deployment-specific facts in a separate host-owned durable `DeploymentRecord`: resolved
    QEMU arguments and virtio topology, OVMF variable-instance identity, persistent-volume UUID and
    snapshot, active/candidate/last-known-good `ReleaseId`, current boot attempt and terminal state.
    A publisher cannot sign facts that exist only after deployment.
  - Implement one small reference host supervisor: verify the release, acquire the exclusive
    VM/data lock, fence any old QEMU process, select the candidate, start it, wait for the bounded
    health result and atomically commit or restore the prior image. It is not a general daemon,
    package manager or cloud control plane.
  - Atomically update the `DeploymentRecord`. Supervisor crash recovery must never infer active,
    candidate, snapshot or boot-attempt state from a partially updated filename set.
  - Bound candidate and last-known-good retry counts. If both fail, release the VM cleanly and enter
    an operator-visible `FAILED/NEEDS_OPERATOR` state instead of rebooting forever.
  - Automatic rollback is allowed only when the prior image can read all state the candidate may
    have written, or when the host atomically restores a verified pre-candidate data snapshot. The
    v1 reference path performs no irreversible migration; an incompatible candidate is refused.
  - Do not build guest A/B slots, an in-guest recovery image, device PKI or anti-rollback hardware
    assumptions. A later autonomous-appliance profile must choose and test those mechanisms.

- [ ] **M1.9 - Project lifecycle and health into existing local/host operations.**
  - Extend the existing System Graph, logs and counters with the same deployment/service identity,
    boot/instance epoch, desired/observed state, transition reason and freshness used by
    `SystemSpec` and M1.5.
  - Distinguish unknown, stale, down and degraded. A one-time successful bootstrap message is not a
    continuing health result.
  - Expose the bounded profile's health, logs, release identity and update/recovery result through
    the local console and captured log format. Add a dedicated bounded `ManagedVmStatus v1`
    host/guest channel carrying `ReleaseId`, `BootId`, `STARTING`/`READY`/`DEGRADED`/`FATAL`, a
    monotonic sequence and heartbeat; the host must not infer health by scraping console prose.
  - Make SystemManager emit READY only after the declared critical service set is ready. Process
    liveness alone is not health, and a stale heartbeat must trigger the M1.5 recovery row.
  - Accept READY only when its `ReleaseId` equals the host-verified candidate and its `BootId`
    belongs to the current launch attempt. A mismatch, replayed prior READY or non-monotonic status
    sequence is `FATAL` and cannot commit the candidate.
  - Keep the observation path read-only and narrow. This single-operator profile does not require a
    general remote-principal, tenant-redaction or capability-topology discovery service.
  - Document property-specific trust assumptions where relevant, but do not add a runtime framework
    that claims to calculate a complete TCB automatically.

- [ ] **M1.10 - Migrate the shipped VM and close the profile with watched system scenarios.**
  - Account for every shipped artifact, deployment, initial grant, Domain/resource profile, exposed
    config key, fixed volume role and durable state owner in the canonical model. Remove temporary
    shadow tables before closure.
  - Qualify clean boot and shutdown, one restartable-service crash/hang, a non-restartable or
    control-plane failure leading to reboot, a guest hang handled by the host watchdog, overload,
    fixed-device/data-volume loss and persistent-state incompatibility.
  - Qualify signed candidate installation, successful health acceptance, pre-health failure and
    compatible rollback/restore. An incompatible state transition must be refused or enter the
    documented host-recovery path rather than pretend rollback is safe.
  - Hard-kill the guest during a persistent write and during candidate activation; verify the
    declared filesystem recovery, snapshot/rollback result and RPO rather than relying on graceful
    shutdown-only evidence.
  - Prove that a modified signed release or inconsistent `DeploymentRecord` is refused, the system
    image stays read-only, fixed volume roles cannot be swapped, two VM epochs cannot attach the same
    writable data volume, a stale/mismatched READY cannot commit, and exhausted restart attempts
    reach `FAILED/NEEDS_OPERATOR`.
  - Kill the host supervisor after candidate selection, after QEMU start but before READY, after
    READY but before commit and during rollback. On recovery it must use the durable record, fence
    any old QEMU process and reach one unambiguous state without attaching the data volume twice.
  - Capture exact image, SystemSpec and MachineProfile identity, lifecycle epochs, recovery result,
    RTO/RPO and relevant authority/resource evidence for each scenario.
  - Publish the support matrix, host deployment/update procedure, state backup/recovery procedure
    and known limitations. Only this bounded profile may then be called `ManagedReferenceVM v1`;
    completion makes no autonomous-edge, server, physical-hardware or desktop claim.

## Definition of done

- One deterministic, versioned extension of the current manifest accounts for the exact shipped
  guest and generates its start/bootstrap, initial-grant, Domain/resource, desired-observability and
  release projections without a parallel hand-maintained table for the same fact.
- The remaining bootstrap/control path is typed and versioned; each service receives only declared
  capabilities and reports bounded readiness, health and shutdown outcomes.
- Every long-lived deployment has an honest state class and recovery result. Selected restartable
  services receive new epochs; other failures reach bounded reboot, host rollback/restart or a
  declared refusal instead of unsupported transparent recovery.
- SystemManager remains resident, ServiceManager executes the generated plan, and control-plane or
  guest failure cannot leave the VM advertised as healthy while an ownerless subtree continues.
- Static authority, resource/config profiles and fixed system/persistent volume roles match the
  observed guest. `SystemPower` does not imply root-Domain management.
- The exact x86_64 QEMU/KVM/OVMF/virtio MachineProfile is bound to a signed immutable image and its
  verification evidence. AArch64, RISC-V and unspecified emulator/hardware configurations are not
  covered by this product claim.
- The trusted-host installation workflow safely accepts or restores an image according to the
  persistent-state compatibility contract. Its exclusive lock and fencing prevent two VM epochs
  from writing one data volume; bounded retry ends visibly if neither candidate nor last-known-good
  can boot.
- The dedicated host/guest status channel reports exact release/boot identity and fresh lifecycle
  health without parsing console text, and the host watchdog recovers an unresponsive guest.
- Local/host operations expose fresh health, lifecycle and release identity sufficient to execute
  the documented deployment, update and recovery procedures.
- Every M1.10 scenario passes for the exact image and MachineProfile, with recorded evidence and no
  unexplained broader boot capability or duplicated source of composition truth.
- At minimum, implementation adds and passes repository gates equivalent to:

```sh
./check.sh --gate system-spec
./check.sh --gate machine-profile
./check.sh --gate managed-vm-release
./check.sh --gate managed-vm-scenarios
git diff --check
```

The concrete gate names may be settled while implementing M1.2-M1.3. Closure records the exact
commands, image/profile digests and watched-scenario results in dated evidence sections in this
file. A green unit-test collection without the host-managed update, reboot/watchdog and state-
compatibility scenarios is not sufficient evidence.

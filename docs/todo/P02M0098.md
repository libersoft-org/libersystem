# P02M0098 - A device claim is a count, so two drivers can own one device

Status: COMPLETE (2026-08-27). KERNEL DEVICE AUTHORITY. THE FIRST OF SEVEN SLICES OF THE
DRIVER-BINDING WORK.

The claim is the kernel's: exclusive, generation-stamped, and every capability derived from it dies
with it - including the MAPPING, which is the half a driver would otherwise still be holding. Proved
by nine tests in `object/claim/tests.rs` and two in `object/channel/tests.rs`, each watched to fail
before it passed, on a green 354-test x86_64 run. The `u64` generation is the only one: the
virtio-iommu backend's parallel `set_generation` is gone, and the test that used to check it now
checks the domain's, which is the one the kernel mints.

THIS MILESTONE WAS A PROGRAMME AND IS NOW A SLICE. It carried thirty-one items - the whole of
declarative binding, the driver protocol, transactional bind, discovery breadth, providers,
supervision, observability and operator policy - behind one completion gate, and blocked every
concrete driver in P02M0099 until all of it passed. Each of those items is the size of an ordinary
milestone here; its Definition of done was one sentence of some sixty clauses, which is a gate nobody
can evaluate and therefore a gate that gets declared met. The DMA/IOVA half had already been carved
out into P02M0153 for the same reason, and that one piece filled a milestone on its own and still
needed reopening afterwards.

So the work is now seven milestones, this one first, and P02M0099 is blocked on the first three
rather than on all of it - those are what a new driver actually needs. What each slice owns:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **P02M0098** (here) | the kernel owns the claim: exclusive, generation-stamped, and every resource capability dies with it |
| P02M0161 | one versioned driver protocol, with the generation on every message |
| P02M0162 | bind and unbind are transactional, and each binding has one ordered state machine |
| P02M0163 | discovery covers every function, and binding selects on standards identity |
| P02M0164 | typed providers, multiple instances, and the dependency graph |
| P02M0165 | supervision: hung drivers, planned stop, accounting, recovery |
| P02M0166 | observability, one typed failure vocabulary, operator policy |

## Goal

Make "who owns this device" a fact the kernel holds, so that nothing else has to be trusted to
respect it - and so that taking it away really takes away everything derived from it.

Three properties, and no more than three:

1. **One owner.** A device is claimed by exactly one holder. A second claim fails.
2. **Every derived capability dies with the claim.** MMIO, interrupt and DMA authority granted under a
   claim stop working when it ends, without the holder's cooperation.
3. **A claim has a generation.** A new claim of the same device is a NEW binding, never continuity
   with the last one, and anything stamped with an old generation is refused.

## What is there now

Measured against the tree, not against the intention.

- **THE CLAIM IS A REFERENCE COUNT.** `device::acquire_bus_master` increments `OWNERS[index]` and
  turns bus mastering on at the 0 -> 1 transition. Nothing refuses a second acquisition, so two
  drivers naming the same index both get a `DeviceMemory` for its BAR and both drive it. Exclusivity
  is not enforced anywhere; it holds today because `DeviceManager` happens to launch one driver per
  device.
- **THE AUTHORITY GATE EXISTS AND IS THE RIGHT SHAPE.** `sys_device_acquire` refuses a caller without
  the `DeviceManager` privilege, so an ordinary process cannot mint a device capability by naming an
  index. That is the half of this milestone that is already true, and the reason the rest is a
  tightening rather than a new mechanism.
- **THERE IS NO GENERATION ANYWHERE ON THE PATH.** `src/kernel/device.rs` has none.
  `iommu::attach_for` passes the constant `1` and says so in its own comment: *"Binding identity is
  the driver lifecycle's to own and this does not recreate it."* P02M0153 left that placeholder
  deliberately, waiting for this.
- **A DERIVED CAPABILITY IS TRANSFERABLE AND OUTLIVES NOTHING.** `sys_device_acquire` installs the
  `DeviceMemory` with `Rights::TRANSFER`, so a driver may hand its device's BAR to another process,
  and revoking the claim would not reach it.

## Work

- [x] **M1 - one claim, and a second one fails.** `acquire_bus_master`'s count becomes an owner: the
  first claim succeeds and any other is refused with a reason that says the device is claimed. The
  count itself does not survive: a device with two owners is the state this milestone exists to make
  impossible, and a count is a way of representing it.

- [x] **M2 - the claim carries a generation, and it is the one the IOMMU already asks for.** A new
  claim of the same device gets a new generation. `iommu::attach_for` stops passing `1` and passes
  this, which is what makes a mapping from a previous binding refusable by arithmetic rather than by
  bookkeeping - the property P02M0153 wrote its `StaleGeneration` fault for and could not reach.

- [x] **M3 - every capability derived from a claim ends with it.** MMIO, interrupt and DMA authority
  granted under a claim stop working when the claim ends, whether it ended cleanly or because the
  holder died. Revocation does not ask the holder for anything.

- [x] **M4 - a device capability reaches its holder and stops there, through an attenuating send.**
  `sys_channel_send` moves a handle with the rights it already has; there is no way today to hand
  something over with less than you hold, so "arrives without `TRANSFER`" is not a property of the
  existing primitive and has to be one. THERE IS NO ROOM FOR ONE MORE ARGUMENT, which is what this
  item said first and is wrong: the syscall ABI carries exactly four. `syscall(number, a0, a1, a2,
  a3)` on all three ports and `syscall_dispatch(num, a0, a1, a2, a3)` in the kernel, and
  `sys_channel_send(ch, bytes_ptr, bytes_len, xfer)` already spends all four. A fifth would be an ABI
  change to every architecture for one call.

  So it is a new call whose fourth argument is a POINTER rather than a handle:
  `sys_channel_send_attenuated(ch, bytes_ptr, bytes_len, transfer_ptr)`, where `transfer_ptr` names a
  `{ handle, rights_mask }` in the caller's memory, read with the same `read_bytes`/`user_buf_ok`
  path every other pointer argument uses. Inside, it is the SAME transactional transfer the existing
  send performs - this is a mask applied to an existing move, not a second way of moving a
  capability. The receiver gets the INTERSECTION of the capability's rights and the mask; a mask
  naming a right the capability does not hold is not an error, because an intersection cannot widen;
  and a send that fails leaves the sender's capability and its rights exactly as they were. It is minted for the broker
  WITH `TRANSFER` and arrives at the driver WITHOUT it - one attenuating move, and no second one.
  Minting it without `TRANSFER` outright, which this item said first, would have broken the boot on
  the first try: `launch_one` calls `device_acquire` and then `send_blocking(dm_side, .., cap)`, so
  DeviceManager is the one that hands the capability over and cannot do that with a capability it
  may not move. The rule the item is for - a driver cannot pass its device on - is about the HOLDER,
  and attenuating on the way to the holder is what states it. What a driver may do for a CHILD is not
  answered here and has nowhere to be answered yet: process-level child binding is refused by this
  milestone and by P02M0163, so there is no child to grant anything to.

- [x] **M5 - a forced release proves the device is quiet before anything is reused.** Bus mastering
  off, interrupts masked, and the IOMMU teardown confirmed - in that order, and completed - before a
  frame or a vector goes back into circulation. Most of this exists: `release_bus_master` already
  turns off bus mastering before detaching, and P02M0153 made the unmap confirm. What is missing is
  that it happens on a FORCED release too, not only when a cooperative driver drops its handles.

- [x] **M6 - a claim is a key, every device capability carries it, AND THE HOLDER CAN BE TOLD WHAT IT
  IS.** `ClaimKey { device_index, generation }` is what makes "everything derived from the previous
  claim" a set that can be identified - and today there is no way for DeviceManager to learn one or
  to act on it. `device_acquire(index, privilege)` returns a handle and nothing else, and the only
  release in the tree happens when the capability is DROPPED, which is the cooperative path this
  milestone exists to stop depending on. `ObjectInfo.generation` is not it either, though it is closer
  than it looks: it is a `u32` REVOCATION generation on the object - capabilities snapshot it at mint
  time and lookup compares, so one bump invalidates every capability to that object. That is a real
  mechanism and the wrong subject. It answers "has this object been revoked", per object; a claim
  needs "which binding of this DEVICE is this", per device and across the objects a binding derives.

  So the claim needs one small lifecycle surface, and M3, M5 and M8 are unimplementable without it:
  the acquisition ANSWERS with the key, a privileged release TAKES the whole key, and the outcome is
  a state a caller can read - the SAME four M8 defines, `Free | Claimed | Releasing | Quarantined`,
  and not a second list; this item first gave three of them and left `Claimed` out, which is the one
  a caller sees most. Reading it is not enough either: P02M0162's manager is one `wait_any` loop and
  cannot spin on a status - so the acquisition has to leave the MANAGER holding something waitable,
  and today it does not: `device_acquire` returns one `DeviceMemory` handle and that handle is
  precisely what gets sent on to the driver, so after a successful bind the manager holds nothing
  about the claim at all.

  The split, named, because P02M0162's rollback cannot be written until it is settled: the
  acquisition answers with the `ClaimKey` AND a claim handle that STAYS with the manager - staying
  is a property it has to be GIVEN, not one it has by being called that: minted without `TRANSFER`
  and without `DUPLICATE`, carrying only what reading its state and waiting on it need. A claim
  handle that can be moved can leave the manager's Domain, survive its killing, and hold the forced
  release off exactly when the machine most needs it - which is the same argument M4 makes about the
  device capability, one level up. There is a test that its transfer is refused. The `DeviceMemory`
  and the other resource capabilities are what travel to the driver.

  THAT ACQUISITION IS ONE OPERATION OR NONE OF IT. It now installs two handles and copies a key out,
  and each of those can fail: a partial success leaves a claim alive that its would-be owner never
  learned the name of, which is a device nothing can release and nothing can rebind. The whole of it
  rolls back the way `sys_channel_create` was made to - close both handles, leave the caller where it
  started. Two tests, because the item names three failures and one test covers one of them: the
  copy-out fails on purpose, and separately the SECOND handle install fails after the first has
  succeeded - which is the case that can leave a live claim whose owner never learned its name, and
  the one an implementation is most likely to get half right.

  AND THE LAST CLOSE OF THE CLAIM HANDLE IS A FORCED RELEASE. Not a leak, not a silent orphan: the
  same teardown M5 describes, started by the kernel, because a DeviceManager that DIED is exactly the
  case P02M0165's cold reconstruction has to survive. LOGICAL close, not the drop of the last
  internal `Arc`: `Process::terminate()` closes a handle table synchronously, so the release starts
  when the last table entry goes rather than whenever the final reference happens to be dropped -
  which is what makes the timing something a test can pin down. And it survives it by finding devices that are
  `Free`, `Releasing` or `Quarantined` rather than claimed by a process that no longer exists. With a
  test that closes the manager's last claim handle while the DRIVER still holds its `DeviceMemory`,
  and requires the device to reach `Free` or `Quarantined` and every capability derived from that
  claim to have stopped working - the ordinary revocation of M3, reached by a route nobody chose. The terminal
  result of a release - `Free`, or `Quarantined` where the teardown could not be confirmed - arrives
  on that claim handle, which sits in the manager's wait set beside the driver's process handle.
  Nothing here is a numeric ABI choice; it is which of two objects each side keeps.

  A release naming a stale generation is refused rather than applied
  to whoever holds the device now, which is the same arithmetic M2 gives the IOMMU. Today `DeviceMemory` carries the device index, `DmaBuffer` carries `device: Option<u32>`,
  and `sys_device_msix_acquire(index, privilege)` takes an index plus an ambient DeviceManager
  privilege - so nothing anywhere names WHICH claim it belongs to, and M3's revocation has no way to
  ask. The key goes on all three before M2 and M3 can be written.

- [x] **M7 - one generation, not two, and it is a `u64` that does not wrap.** The claim's generation is the only one. `attach_for` passes
  a hardcoded `1`, and the virtio-iommu backend separately stamps `self.generation` - a
  controller-wide value with its own `set_generation` setter - onto every row of `attached`. Two
  parallel notions of "which binding is this" cannot both be the answer to a stale-generation
  refusal. The claim's value is passed into the backend's `attach` and the parallel setter goes.
  It advances with `checked_add`; a slot that runs out of generations is retired for the life of the
  boot rather than wrapped onto a number a dead handle still names - the rule `StaleHandlesStayDead`
  already states for handle slots.

- [x] **M8 - the claim table has states, and a second claim has an error of its own.**
  `Free | Claimed | Releasing | Quarantined` - the same four M6 names, because two lists is how a
  state ends up meaning different things in two places. A new claim does not begin while the previous one is releasing, and it
  waits on bus mastering, interrupts and the IOMMU teardown having actually completed rather than
  having been asked for - unconfirmed resources stay quarantined. `AlreadyClaimed` is its own errno
  rather than the `ERR_INVALID` everything currently collapses into, because a caller that cannot
  tell "somebody else has it" from "you passed nonsense" cannot retry correctly.

- [x] **M9 - the tests are about what a hostile or dead holder cannot do.** A second claim is refused;
  a revoked MMIO capability faults rather than reading the BAR - reached through the raw virtual
  address the driver had already mapped, because a handle that refuses is not evidence that the
  MAPPING is gone; a revoked DMA address does not
  translate; a stale-generation mapping is refused; a forced release leaves no usable mapping or
  vector. Host tests where the fake backend allows it, and one x86_64 QEMU case for the parts that
  need a real device.
  NAMED, AND UNABLE TO SKIP ITSELF GREEN. The nearest thing in the tree today - the hardware suite's
  bus-master check - looks for a device nobody is driving and returns quietly when every device on
  the machine is claimed, which on a healthy boot is most of them. A gate whose subject can vanish is
  a gate that passes when there was nothing to test. The claim table's own host test has no such
  escape, and the QEMU case names the device it uses rather than searching for a free one.

## Order across the seven

M0160 is independent of all of this and can be done at any time. Then: this milestone, P02M0161,
P02M0162, the PCI-only P02M0163, P02M0164, P02M0165 and P02M0166 - in that order, because each one
is what makes the next one's subject exist.

Two things moved between them after the 2026-08-26 audit, and both moved because they could not be
written where they were. The binding record's shape - a state and an optional cause - is defined in
P02M0162 rather than in P02M0166, since five milestones would otherwise each invent their own. And
the system-volume locator is an item in P02M0164 rather than a milestone of its own, because
P02M0150 already put the paired volume's identity in the boot medium's signed manifest this round -
and it sits in P02M0164 rather than P02M0165 because that is where the four hardcoded block slots are
deleted, which is the moment "which disk is the root" stops having an answer by accident.

## Dependency order

M1 and M2 are one change to the same table, and M6 is what makes M2 and M3 expressible at all - a
claim key has to exist before "everything from the previous claim" is a set. M7 goes with M2 for the
same reason: two generations cannot both be the answer. M3 depends on M1 having something to end and on
nothing else: revocation walks the set M6's `ClaimKey` names, so it reaches every capability derived
from that claim WHEREVER it ended up, however many processes it was passed through. M4 bounds how far
it can be passed, which is a narrower property and a separate one - the two can be written in either
order. M5 is the order the teardown happens in and depends on M3. M8 is the table's
own bookkeeping and can be written any time after M1. M9 is last.

## Definition of done

- A second claim of a device that is already claimed is refused, with `AlreadyClaimed` and not with
  the general invalid-argument error, and no path anywhere increments a count instead.
- Every capability derived from a claim stops working when the claim ends - MMIO reached through the
  raw virtual address the driver had already mapped, the interrupt vector, and the DMA translation -
  whether the holder cooperated, was killed, or is still running and does not know.
- One generation answers "which binding is this". It is a `u64` advanced with `checked_add`, a slot
  that runs out is retired for the boot rather than wrapped, and a mapping stamped with a previous
  generation is refused by arithmetic rather than by bookkeeping.
- A device capability arrives at its holder without `TRANSFER`, through one attenuating send, and the
  holder cannot pass it on.
- An attenuating send that FAILS leaves the sender exactly where it was - the handle still open, its
  rights unchanged - and two threads naming one handle in two concurrent attenuating sends do not
  both succeed. That second case is the one the ordinary send was already fixed for: it used to look
  up, clone and send, so one handle became two capabilities without `DUPLICATE`, and a new call that
  reimplemented the move rather than reusing the transaction would reintroduce it.
- A forced release leaves bus mastering off, interrupts masked and the IOMMU teardown CONFIRMED
  before any frame or vector is reused; a teardown that was not confirmed leaves the resources
  quarantined and the claim not `Free`.
- Every one of these has a test that names the device it uses, and none can pass by finding nothing
  to test.

Child bindings and per-interface authority are NOT part of this and are not P02M0163's either -
that milestone refuses them, because in this tree HID and mass storage are code inside the xHCI
driver rather than processes, so splitting them is a driver rewrite and not discovery plumbing.

## HISTORY - the registry, as it was specified before it was built (2026-08-12)

**NOTHING BELOW THIS LINE IS WORK.** It is the specification the registry was written from, kept
because the reasoning is still the reasoning and the three items above it that are ticked were done
against it. It describes a milestone of twenty-seven items and a `driver_for` function; neither
exists. This milestone is nine items (see the head), and `driver_for` was deleted when the registry
replaced it - the only mentions left in the tree are comments saying so. An implementer reads the
head; this is here for whoever asks why the registry has the shape it has.

The reasoning as it stood: every item presupposed a checked declarative registry, generated from the
artifact manifest, that DeviceManager consumes instead of choosing drivers in a `match`. Nothing else
could be built first without being built twice. What follows is that first slice specified against
the tree as it stood, so writing it was mechanical and so the parts it deliberately did not cover
were visible rather than discovered later.

### What is there today, exactly

- **The match table is a function.** `driver_for(&DeviceInfo)` at
  `src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:670` returns a program name from `info.device_type`,
  with one hardcoded PCI address (`DEV_CHANNEL_BUS`/`DEV_CHANNEL_DEV`) for the development console.
  Seven arms, virtio type numbers plus `DEVICE_TYPE_XHCI`, `_ => b""` for everything else.
- **The supervisor's driver list is a literal.** `service_manager.rs:428` builds
  `[(&'static [u8], bool); 6]` by naming each driver and pairing it with "did its client channel
  arrive". Adding a driver means editing an array and its arity.
- **The discovery vocabulary is one struct.** `abi::DeviceInfo` carries `device_type`, the MMIO
  window, the virtio structure offsets and `bus`/`dev`/`func`. There is no PCI class/subclass/
  interface, no USB interface descriptor, no FDT compatible string - so the binding rules this
  milestone asks for cannot be expressed against it yet. That is a discovery change (item 4), and
  the registry must be shaped so it does not have to be redesigned when discovery grows.
- **The manifest is already parsed by a shared crate.** `src/tools/system-manifest` produces
  `Program { name, owner, role, linkage, stage, destination, providers, development }` and is read
  by `mkpackages`, `kernel/build.rs` and `verify-model`. Driver programs are ordinary programs with
  `role = "driver"` and `providers = []`.
- **Generation into a consumer already has a pattern.** `kernel/build.rs` renders match arms from
  the manifest into `OUT_DIR`, and `component_host` includes a generated `program_paths.rs`. The
  registry follows it rather than inventing a second mechanism.

### The manifest schema the registry is generated from

A `[programs.driver]` sub-table on a `role = "driver"` program, absent for every other role and
REQUIRED for that one - a driver with no binding rules is a driver nothing can ever select, which is
a manifest error rather than a driver that never binds:

```toml
[[programs]]
name = "virtio_blk"
role = "driver"
stage = "volume"
destination = "drivers/virtio_blk.lsexe"

[programs.driver]
lifecycle = "controller"          # boot-critical | controller | function | interface
match = [{ virtio-type = 2 }]     # one or more rules; see below
priority = "generic"              # exact | generic | quirk
providers = ["block"]             # typed provider kinds this driver may publish
```

Match rules are a CLOSED tagged set, not free-form key/value pairs, so a rule that discovery cannot
answer fails at generation instead of never matching at runtime. The set opens with what the current
`DeviceInfo` can answer and is extended as item 4 extends discovery:

- `{ virtio-type = N }` - what `driver_for` matches today;
- `{ pci-class = C, pci-subclass = S, pci-interface = I }` - the standards path, with subclass and
  interface optional, once discovery reports them;
- `{ pci-address = { bus, dev, func } }` - what the development console needs, and the only rule
  that names a location rather than a kind;
- `{ device-type = N }` - the non-virtio types the kernel already reports (`DEVICE_TYPE_XHCI`).

`priority` is a three-value enum rather than a number, because the milestone's arbitration rule is
about KIND and not about magnitude: an exact standardised match beats a broader class match, and an
explicit tested quirk beats the generic path only when it matches at all. Numbers invite tie-breaks
nobody can justify.

### What generation checks, and refuses

At registry-build time, in `system-manifest` beside the validation that is already there:

1. **Duplicate identity** - two entries naming the same executable, or one program with two driver
   tables.
2. **Ambiguous equal-rank match** - two entries whose rules can match one node at the same
   `priority`. Decidable because the rule set is closed and finite: two rules overlap when every
   field either agrees or is absent on one side.
3. **A staged path that does not exist** - the `destination` must be a declared artifact, which the
   manifest already knows; the digest and ABI checks of the artifact-integrity item come later and
   plug in here.
4. **An undeclared provider kind** - `providers` must name kinds from the same closed set the
   provider-routing item uses, so a driver cannot publish something no consumer can subscribe to.
5. **A boot-critical driver staged outside `init.pkg`** - the bootstrap-exception item's rule,
   checkable statically and exactly the self-hosting cycle it names.

Each refusal names the entry and the rule. A manifest that passes generates a table; a manifest that
does not fails the build.

### What DeviceManager does with it

`driver_for` is deleted. In its place, a generated `DRIVER_REGISTRY: &[RegistryEntry]` and one
selection function that walks it, collects every entry whose rules match the node, and picks by
`priority` - refusing to choose when two survive at the same rank, because the generation check
above proves that cannot happen and an assertion is how it stays proven. The result is the entry,
not just a name: the lifecycle class, the declared providers and the restart policy come with it,
which is what items 6, 8 and 12 need and what a `&'static [u8]` cannot carry.

`service_manager.rs`'s `[(&'static [u8], bool); 6]` becomes a derivation over the same registry, so
adding a driver is a manifest row and nothing else.

### What this slice deliberately does NOT do

Said here so the next reader does not mistake a first slice for the milestone:

- it does not change the kernel's device authority (`SYS_DEVICE_ACQUIRE` stays ambient and
  index-addressed; `DeviceRoot`/`Binding` is its own item and the largest one);
- it does not add PCI class/subclass to discovery - the rules for it are specified above and
  unusable until item 4 lands;
- it does not make bind transactional, generation-tagged or supervised past bring-up;
- it does not touch the driver protocol, DMA, IOMMU or IRQ contracts.

What it does do is remove the two places where driver selection is written in Rust rather than
declared, which is the completion gate's first clause and the precondition for every item that has
to attach data to a binding.

- Concept: the DeviceManager capability/binding model, P02M0011/P02M0089 service supervision and
  resolver lifecycle, P02M0032/P02M0080 USB hotplug, P02M0079's data-driven artifact manifest, the
  ResourceManager accounting policy, and P02M0099 as the first consumer of the completed
  binder rather than another extension of today's hard-coded path.

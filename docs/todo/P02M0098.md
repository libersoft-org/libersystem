# P02M0098 - A device claim is a count, so two drivers can own one device

Status: PLANNED. KERNEL DEVICE AUTHORITY. THE FIRST OF SEVEN SLICES OF THE DRIVER-BINDING WORK.

THIS MILESTONE WAS A PROGRAMME AND IS NOW A SLICE. It carried thirty-one items - the whole of
declarative binding, the driver protocol, transactional bind, discovery breadth, providers,
supervision, observability and operator policy - behind one completion gate, and blocked every
concrete driver in P02M0099 until all of it passed. Each of those items is the size of an ordinary
milestone here; its Definition of done was one sentence of some sixty clauses, which is a gate nobody
can evaluate and therefore a gate that gets declared met. The DMA/IOVA half had already been carved
out into P02M0153 for the same reason, and that one piece filled a milestone on its own and still
needed reopening afterwards.

So the work is now seven milestones, this one first, and P02M0099 is blocked on the first three
rather than on all of it - those are what a new driver actually needs. What each slice owns:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **P02M0098** (here) | the kernel owns the claim: exclusive, generation-stamped, and every resource capability dies with it |
| P02M0161 | one versioned driver protocol, with the generation on every message |
| P02M0162 | bind and unbind are transactional, and each binding has one ordered state machine |
| P02M0163 | discovery covers every function, and binding selects on standards identity |
| P02M0164 | typed providers, multiple instances, and the dependency graph |
| P02M0165 | supervision: hung drivers, planned stop, accounting, recovery |
| P02M0166 | observability, one typed failure vocabulary, operator policy |

## Goal

Make "who owns this device" a fact the kernel holds, so that nothing else has to be trusted to
respect it - and so that taking it away really takes away everything derived from it.

Three properties, and no more than three:

1. **One owner.** A device is claimed by exactly one holder. A second claim fails.
2. **Every derived capability dies with the claim.** MMIO, interrupt and DMA authority granted under a
   claim stop working when it ends, without the holder's cooperation.
3. **A claim has a generation.** A new claim of the same device is a NEW binding, never continuity
   with the last one, and anything stamped with an old generation is refused.

## What is there now

Measured against the tree, not against the intention.

- **THE CLAIM IS A REFERENCE COUNT.** `device::acquire_bus_master` increments `OWNERS[index]` and
  turns bus mastering on at the 0 -> 1 transition. Nothing refuses a second acquisition, so two
  drivers naming the same index both get a `DeviceMemory` for its BAR and both drive it. Exclusivity
  is not enforced anywhere; it holds today because `DeviceManager` happens to launch one driver per
  device.
- **THE AUTHORITY GATE EXISTS AND IS THE RIGHT SHAPE.** `sys_device_acquire` refuses a caller without
  the `DeviceManager` privilege, so an ordinary process cannot mint a device capability by naming an
  index. That is the half of this milestone that is already true, and the reason the rest is a
  tightening rather than a new mechanism.
- **THERE IS NO GENERATION ANYWHERE ON THE PATH.** `src/kernel/device.rs` has none.
  `iommu::attach_for` passes the constant `1` and says so in its own comment: *"Binding identity is
  the driver lifecycle's to own and this does not recreate it."* P02M0153 left that placeholder
  deliberately, waiting for this.
- **A DERIVED CAPABILITY IS TRANSFERABLE AND OUTLIVES NOTHING.** `sys_device_acquire` installs the
  `DeviceMemory` with `Rights::TRANSFER`, so a driver may hand its device's BAR to another process,
  and revoking the claim would not reach it.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - one claim, and a second one fails.** `acquire_bus_master`'s count becomes an owner: the
  first claim succeeds and any other is refused with a reason that says the device is claimed. The
  count itself does not survive: a device with two owners is the state this milestone exists to make
  impossible, and a count is a way of representing it.

- [ ] **M2 - the claim carries a generation, and it is the one the IOMMU already asks for.** A new
  claim of the same device gets a new generation. `iommu::attach_for` stops passing `1` and passes
  this, which is what makes a mapping from a previous binding refusable by arithmetic rather than by
  bookkeeping - the property P02M0153 wrote its `StaleGeneration` fault for and could not reach.

- [ ] **M3 - every capability derived from a claim ends with it.** MMIO, interrupt and DMA authority
  granted under a claim stop working when the claim ends, whether it ended cleanly or because the
  holder died. Revocation does not ask the holder for anything.

- [ ] **M4 - a derived capability cannot be passed on.** A device capability is the holder's, so it is
  minted without `TRANSFER`. A driver that wants to give a child access asks for one for the child -
  which is P02M0163's binding-unit work and needs this to be true first.

- [ ] **M5 - a forced release proves the device is quiet before anything is reused.** Bus mastering
  off, interrupts masked, and the IOMMU teardown confirmed - in that order, and completed - before a
  frame or a vector goes back into circulation. Most of this exists: `release_bus_master` already
  turns off bus mastering before detaching, and P02M0153 made the unmap confirm. What is missing is
  that it happens on a FORCED release too, not only when a cooperative driver drops its handles.

- [ ] **M6 - the tests are about what a hostile or dead holder cannot do.** A second claim is refused;
  a revoked MMIO capability faults rather than reading the BAR; a revoked DMA address does not
  translate; a stale-generation mapping is refused; a forced release leaves no usable mapping or
  vector. Host tests where the fake backend allows it, and one x86_64 QEMU case for the parts that
  need a real device.

## Dependency order

M1 and M2 are one change to the same table. M3 depends on M1 having something to end. M4 is
independent. M5 depends on M3. M6 accompanies each rather than following them.

## Definition of done

- A device has at most one owner, and a second claim is refused with a typed reason rather than
  admitted.
- Every claim carries a generation, `iommu::attach_for` uses it, and a mapping made under a previous
  generation is refused.
- Ending a claim ends the MMIO, interrupt and DMA authority derived from it, without the holder's
  cooperation, and a test proves each of the three from the far side.
- A device capability cannot be transferred to another process.
- A forced release leaves the device not mastering the bus, its interrupts masked and its IOMMU
  mappings taken down, proved before any frame or vector is reused.

## What this milestone refuses, and why

- **A capability revocation mechanism for the whole kernel.** This needs device capabilities to die
  with their claim, which is a small, specific lifetime rule. A general revocation facility is a much
  larger design and nothing here needs one.
- **Recursive revocation across child devices.** A USB interface handed out by a controller is
  P02M0163's binding unit, and it cannot be defined before there is a claim to attenuate. This
  milestone makes the parent's claim real; the child tree is the milestone that introduces children.
- **The full interrupt resource contract.** Sharing, level versus edge, multi-vector and storm
  behaviour are supervision's, in P02M0165. What this owes them is that an interrupt capability comes
  from a claim and dies with it.
- **Changing who may claim.** `sys_device_acquire`'s privilege gate is already right. Replacing it
  with a new authority object would be a rewrite of a thing that works.

## What was built under this number before it was split (2026-08-12)

Two items were completed while this milestone still carried everything, and they are kept here rather
than moved: done work belongs where it was done. Their subject - the generated registry and how a
driver is selected - is P02M0163's from here on, and that milestone starts from these.

- [x] **Driver presence is distinct from driver activation, and both are visible.**

  DONE 2026-08-12 for the observable half, which is the half that was missing.

  Loading was already demand-driven: DeviceManager walks discovery and reads an ELF only for a
  device that matched, so an absent device has always cost no process, no mapped ELF and no
  capability. What did not exist was the ability to SAY so. Three states answered two different
  questions with the same silence - a device no driver binds and a device whose artifact could not
  be read were both simply absent from the summary, so "this system does not support that card" and
  "the image is missing a file" looked identical to anyone reading the boot.

  `STATE_UNBOUND` is present-and-unsupported: nothing is wrong and nothing is missing.
  `STATE_DRIVER_MISSING` is present, matched, and the artifact the registry names is not on the
  volume - the image disagreeing with its own registry, which is a packaging fault an operator can
  act on rather than a bug. `report_state` says both, beside the online count rather than folded
  into it: `2 of 5 device(s) online, 1 unbound, 1 driver-missing`.

  A controller with no child is untouched by this and stays online, because it is bound - it owns
  discovery and must observe later hotplug, which is the item's last sentence and was already true.

  MEASURED, and honestly: the x86_64 guest reports `8 of 8 device(s) online` - no unbound, no
  driver-missing. `STATE_UNBOUND` cannot fire in this configuration at all, and the reason is worth
  writing down rather than leaving as an untested branch: the kernel's `DEVICES` table holds only
  the device kinds it knows how to resolve - virtio functions and xHCI - and every one of them has a
  driver in the image. An unsupported device is not absent from the summary because the state is
  broken; it is absent because discovery never records one. The state becomes reachable when
  discovery broadens to every PCI function, which is the item above it and is not done.

  What is still coarse: these are DeviceManager's own states, printed. Carrying them into `lsdev`,
  the System Graph and the log is the observability item further down, and the shared typed failure
  vocabulary - `claimed`, `removed`, `artifact-mismatch`, `iommu-required`, `hung`, `quarantined` -
  is the identity item at the end. These two names are the two that can be distinguished today.
- [x] **The checked declarative driver registry, generated from the artifact manifest.**

  DONE 2026-08-12 for the part that exists today, and the parts that do not are named below rather
  than pretended.

  `[programs.driver]` on a `role = "driver"` row - required there and refused everywhere else,
  because a driver with no rules is one DeviceManager can never select and rules on a service are
  rules nothing consults. It carries `lifecycle` (`boot-critical` / `controller` / `function` /
  `interface`), `match`, and `priority` (`generic` / `exact` / `quirk`). `build.rs` renders the
  eight rows into `DRIVER_REGISTRY`, and `driver_for` is DELETED - the completion gate's first
  clause. ServiceManager's `[(&'static [u8], bool); 6]` is derived from the same rows, and it was
  already wrong: `virtio_console` and `dev_channel` are drivers in the image and were missing from
  it, which nothing could detect because the list and the image had no common source.

  A RULE IS A CONJUNCTION OF PREDICATES and a driver's `match` list is the disjunction. That shape
  was arrived at by getting it wrong: the first version was a tagged enum of single questions, which
  can only OR them - and the development console's selection is `device_type == console AND the
  pinned PCI address`. OR-ing those would bind ANY device at that address to the console driver.

  THE AMBIGUITY CHECK REFUSED THE REAL MANIFEST ON ITS FIRST RUN, and it was right. The vocabulary
  had `virtio-type` and `device-type` as separate predicates for what is one field - `DeviceInfo`
  carries a single `device_type` and the virtio kinds share its number space with xHCI - so
  `virtio-type = 2` and `device-type = 256` looked like statements about different things and
  therefore compatible. Two names for one field cannot be compared, which is a defect in the
  vocabulary rather than in the manifest. One predicate now.

  Eight refusals, each watched to say no, in `the_driver_registry_refuses_what_it_says_it_refuses`:
  a driver with no rules, an empty match list, a rule with no predicates (it would select every
  device), a rule naming something discovery cannot answer (it fails to PARSE - that is what the
  closed set buys), a boot-critical driver staged on the volume it exists to mount, a non-boot
  driver pinned into `init.pkg`, two entries that can match one node at the same priority, and
  binding rules on a program that is not a driver. Plus two positives: a quirk beating a generic
  entry is arbitration rather than ambiguity, and a narrower conjunction at a higher priority is
  too.

  Two things fell out of it that the item did not ask for and the tree needed:

  - the bootstrap exception is now the `boot-critical` LIFECYCLE rather than the string
    `virtio_blk` written into DeviceManager's phase-one loop, and `system-manifest` refuses a
    boot-critical driver staged anywhere but `init.pkg` - the self-hosting cycle as a rule;
  - a development-only driver is absent from a shipping registry, which the deleted
    `#[cfg(feature = "development")]` used to do and a generated table would have lost. Without it
    a shipped image would match the second virtio-console to a driver it does not contain.

  WHAT IS NOT DONE, and belongs to the items that own it: `required resources`, `provided typed
  contracts` and `restart policy` are not in the entry, because nothing consumes them yet - the
  resource contract, the provider routing and the supervision policy are three separate items below,
  and a field no code reads is a declaration that drifts. `pci-class` / `pci-subclass` /
  `pci-interface` are declarable and never match, because `DeviceInfo` does not carry them; that is
  the discovery item, and the shape is fixed now so it will not change when it lands.
- [x] **Deterministic match arbitration and fallback.**

  DONE 2026-08-12.

  `registry_candidates` returns every entry whose rules match, sorted most specific first -
  `quirk` over `exact` over `generic`. The ORDER is the arbitration and the LIST is the fallback,
  which is why they are one function: a selector that picks a winner and throws the rest away cannot
  fall back, and one that keeps them unordered falls back by enumeration order.

  Never by enumeration order: a tie is REFUSED, not broken. `system-manifest` proves at
  registry-build time that two entries cannot overlap at one priority, and the check stays in the
  selector anyway - a proof nothing checks is a comment.

  Metadata before process creation: nothing is launched to discover whether it fits. The choice is
  made from `DeviceInfo` alone, which is the item's "rather than launching every possible driver to
  see which one succeeds".

  The next candidate runs only after the last is gone. The ELF is unmapped and its handle closed at
  the bottom of every attempt whatever the outcome, so a fallback never runs beside the process it
  replaces - and the loop breaks the moment one binds, so nothing below a successful candidate is
  tried.

  Every rejection is said, with which driver and why: an artifact the registry names and the volume
  does not have, or a driver that loaded and did not bind. A fallback that quietly succeeds hides
  that the preferred driver did not, which is how a machine comes to run on its second choice for
  months with nobody aware of it.

  What "fully torn down" covers today is the LOAD: the mapping and the file handle. A driver that
  reached `launch_one` and failed part-way leaves its process teardown to the transactional-bind
  item, which owns rollback across the claim, the resources and the accounting. This item owns the
  ORDER, and the order is now right.

## The registry, specified before it is written (2026-08-12)

This milestone is twenty-seven items and every one of them presupposes the third: a checked
declarative registry, generated from the artifact manifest, that DeviceManager consumes instead of
choosing drivers in a `match`. Nothing else can be built first without being built twice. What
follows is that first slice specified against the tree as it stands, so writing it is mechanical and
so the parts it deliberately does not cover are visible rather than discovered later.

### What is there today, exactly

- **The match table is a function.** `driver_for(&DeviceInfo)` at
  `src/user/services/core/src/device_manager.rs:670` returns a program name from `info.device_type`,
  with one hardcoded PCI address (`DEV_CHANNEL_BUS`/`DEV_CHANNEL_DEV`) for the development console.
  Seven arms, virtio type numbers plus `DEVICE_TYPE_XHCI`, `_ => b""` for everything else.
- **The supervisor's driver list is a literal.** `service_manager.rs:428` builds
  `[(&'static [u8], bool); 6]` by naming each driver and pairing it with "did its client channel
  arrive". Adding a driver means editing an array and its arity.
- **The discovery vocabulary is one struct.** `abi::DeviceInfo` carries `device_type`, the MMIO
  window, the virtio structure offsets and `bus`/`dev`/`func`. There is no PCI class/subclass/
  interface, no USB interface descriptor, no FDT compatible string - so the binding rules this
  milestone asks for cannot be expressed against it yet. That is a discovery change (item 4), and
  the registry must be shaped so it does not have to be redesigned when discovery grows.
- **The manifest is already parsed by a shared crate.** `src/tools/system-manifest` produces
  `Program { name, owner, role, linkage, stage, destination, providers, development }` and is read
  by `mkpackages`, `kernel/build.rs` and `verify-model`. Driver programs are ordinary programs with
  `role = "driver"` and `providers = []`.
- **Generation into a consumer already has a pattern.** `kernel/build.rs` renders match arms from
  the manifest into `OUT_DIR`, and `component_host` includes a generated `program_paths.rs`. The
  registry follows it rather than inventing a second mechanism.

### The manifest schema the registry is generated from

A `[programs.driver]` sub-table on a `role = "driver"` program, absent for every other role and
REQUIRED for that one - a driver with no binding rules is a driver nothing can ever select, which is
a manifest error rather than a driver that never binds:

```toml
[[programs]]
name = "virtio_blk"
role = "driver"
stage = "volume"
destination = "drivers/virtio_blk.lsexe"

[programs.driver]
lifecycle = "controller"          # boot-critical | controller | function | interface
match = [{ virtio-type = 2 }]     # one or more rules; see below
priority = "generic"              # exact | generic | quirk
providers = ["block"]             # typed provider kinds this driver may publish
```

Match rules are a CLOSED tagged set, not free-form key/value pairs, so a rule that discovery cannot
answer fails at generation instead of never matching at runtime. The set opens with what the current
`DeviceInfo` can answer and is extended as item 4 extends discovery:

- `{ virtio-type = N }` - what `driver_for` matches today;
- `{ pci-class = C, pci-subclass = S, pci-interface = I }` - the standards path, with subclass and
  interface optional, once discovery reports them;
- `{ pci-address = { bus, dev, func } }` - what the development console needs, and the only rule
  that names a location rather than a kind;
- `{ device-type = N }` - the non-virtio types the kernel already reports (`DEVICE_TYPE_XHCI`).

`priority` is a three-value enum rather than a number, because the milestone's arbitration rule is
about KIND and not about magnitude: an exact standardised match beats a broader class match, and an
explicit tested quirk beats the generic path only when it matches at all. Numbers invite tie-breaks
nobody can justify.

### What generation checks, and refuses

At registry-build time, in `system-manifest` beside the validation that is already there:

1. **Duplicate identity** - two entries naming the same executable, or one program with two driver
   tables.
2. **Ambiguous equal-rank match** - two entries whose rules can match one node at the same
   `priority`. Decidable because the rule set is closed and finite: two rules overlap when every
   field either agrees or is absent on one side.
3. **A staged path that does not exist** - the `destination` must be a declared artifact, which the
   manifest already knows; the digest and ABI checks of the artifact-integrity item come later and
   plug in here.
4. **An undeclared provider kind** - `providers` must name kinds from the same closed set the
   provider-routing item uses, so a driver cannot publish something no consumer can subscribe to.
5. **A boot-critical driver staged outside `init.pkg`** - the bootstrap-exception item's rule,
   checkable statically and exactly the self-hosting cycle it names.

Each refusal names the entry and the rule. A manifest that passes generates a table; a manifest that
does not fails the build.

### What DeviceManager does with it

`driver_for` is deleted. In its place, a generated `DRIVER_REGISTRY: &[RegistryEntry]` and one
selection function that walks it, collects every entry whose rules match the node, and picks by
`priority` - refusing to choose when two survive at the same rank, because the generation check
above proves that cannot happen and an assertion is how it stays proven. The result is the entry,
not just a name: the lifecycle class, the declared providers and the restart policy come with it,
which is what items 6, 8 and 12 need and what a `&'static [u8]` cannot carry.

`service_manager.rs`'s `[(&'static [u8], bool); 6]` becomes a derivation over the same registry, so
adding a driver is a manifest row and nothing else.

### What this slice deliberately does NOT do

Said here so the next reader does not mistake a first slice for the milestone:

- it does not change the kernel's device authority (`SYS_DEVICE_ACQUIRE` stays ambient and
  index-addressed; `DeviceRoot`/`Binding` is its own item and the largest one);
- it does not add PCI class/subclass to discovery - the rules for it are specified above and
  unusable until item 4 lands;
- it does not make bind transactional, generation-tagged or supervised past bring-up;
- it does not touch the driver protocol, DMA, IOMMU or IRQ contracts.

What it does do is remove the two places where driver selection is written in Rust rather than
declared, which is the completion gate's first clause and the precondition for every item that has
to attach data to a binding.

- Concept: the DeviceManager capability/binding model, P02M0011/P02M0089 service supervision and
  resolver lifecycle, P02M0032/P02M0080 USB hotplug, P02M0079's data-driven artifact manifest, the
  ResourceManager accounting policy, and P02M0099 as the first consumer of the completed
  binder rather than another extension of today's hard-coded path.

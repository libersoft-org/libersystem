# P02M0099 - Universal standards-based driver set

Status: PLANNED AFTER P02M0098, P02M0161 AND P02M0162, WHICH ARE THE HARD GATE. This milestone adds
every approved driver candidate whose controller, protocol or device-class interface is standardized
across vendors. It does not promise support for arbitrary real machines: PCI/USB/ACPI/
FDT discovery, IRQ/DMA attachment, clocks, resets, pinmux and power can still require
platform glue, while Phase 4 owns drivers and quirks for concrete chips, products, SoCs
and boards.

THE GATE IS THREE SLICES, NOT FORTY-ONE ITEMS. P02M0098 was one milestone carrying the whole of the
driver-binding work, and this one waited on all of it - so no driver could be written until
observability, operator policy and power lifecycle were finished, none of which a driver needs. That
milestone is now seven (see its head for the split), and what a new driver actually requires is the
first three TOGETHER - no one of them is "the foundation": the kernel-owned claim, its generation and
its revocation (P02M0098), the start-and-report handshake (P02M0161), and a bind that rolls back when
it fails (P02M0162). NOT the stop: `STOP`/`STOPPED` is P02M0165's, whole, and P02M0161 says so in its
own goal.

The four that follow - discovery breadth, providers, supervision, observability - are what the driver
SET needs to be usable rather than what any one driver needs to exist, and they may land beside the
drivers rather than in front of them. A driver written here still consumes the registry, the claim,
the transactional bind and the generation, and none may add a private launch path, singleton service
handoff, source `match` arm or product-ID-first binding shortcut.

AND THIS IS A ROADMAP, NOT ONE PIECE OF WORK. Forty-one driver candidates across three groups plus
one prerequisite that is not a driver - forty-two checkboxes - and several of the candidates are
families rather than single drivers, so "done when every item is done" states a direction and not
something anybody finishes in one go.

SO IT IS A CROSS-PHASE UMBRELLA AND NOT A PHASE-2 GATE, and it is listed under Phase 2 because that
is where its first items belong, not because Phase 2 waits for its last one. Phase 2's own definition
of done names the capability-scoped appliance and the QEMU suite on three targets; nothing in it
requires NVMe, TPM or UVC. STARTING WORK HERE MEANS PICKING ONE ITEM, not starting "M0099": the item
states what it requires, what it owns and its one integration gate, and it closes on its own.

AND THE HARD GATE IS COMPLETE WITH TWO VERIFICATIONS OUTSTANDING, which the first driver inherits
rather than discovers. P02M0161 says so itself - *"only x86_64 has BOOTED the new handshake"*, with
the aarch64 and riscv64 in-kernel suites not yet run against it - and P02M0162 hands on three bind
windows that are round counts being read as ticks, unmeasured by its own statement. Neither blocks
writing a driver; both block ACCEPTING one on three architectures, which is what the integration gate
below asks for. Each item is closable on its own and ships on its own, the
milestone closes when its items do, and nothing in it waits for the whole of it. The groups fix
PRIORITY, not permission: an item in group 3 whose prerequisites are met is not held by an unfinished
item in group 1 it does not need.

## What the binder does not reach yet, and who extends it

Measured 2026-08-28, because three of the four things groups 2 and 3 assume were explicitly REFUSED
by [P02M0163](P02M0163.md) and are not arriving from anywhere else.

- **A claimable device exists only where the kernel already resolves one.** `device.rs` builds a
  `DeviceEntry` from the virtio scan and from xHCI, and from nothing else. NVMe, AHCI and HDA are
  visible to `lspci` and have no entry, so there is nothing to hand any of them a BAR/IRQ resource
  profile through.
- **There is no firmware inventory.** P02M0163 refuses ACPI and FDT in as many words: the FDT parser
  produces a `BootInfo` - RAM banks, CPU ids, controller addresses - not an inventory with
  `compatible` strings a rule could match, and there is no AML namespace on x86_64 at all.
- **The binding unit is a PCI function.** `BindingId` is bus, device, function and generation. A USB
  interface, an NVMe namespace and a hotplug slot are not expressible in it.
- **There is no arrival or removal event.** The PCI scan runs once at boot. P02M0163 refuses hotplug
  because a milestone promising it would have had to build the mechanism as well.

- **A PROVIDER CAN BE WITHDRAWN; NOBODY IS TOLD, AND NO DRIVER DOES IT.** An earlier draft of this
  line said withdrawal did not exist, and that was wrong: `Withdraw` is opcode 6 of the driver
  protocol - *"the provider this driver published under that token is going away"* - and
  DeviceManager handles `BindingEvent::Withdrawn` by calling `catalogue.withdraw` and saying so on
  the console. TWO THINGS ARE ACTUALLY MISSING. The catalogue's `subscribe` is defined as everything
  of that kind published right now and then the live stream, and that stream carries additions with
  no withdrawal event - so a consumer holding one is never told a provider went away. And no driver
  sends the message: xHCI creates its block channel unconditionally, "Always created" by its own
  comment, answers with an error status when no stick is attached, and on a detach drops its storage
  state and leaves the publication standing.

- **THE ONE RULE THIS MILESTONE STATES IS BROKEN BY THE MECHANISM EVERY NEW DRIVER MUST USE.** The
  head says no driver may add a "source `match` arm", and resources are handed out by exactly that:
  a literal name list - `driver_name == b"virtio_input" || driver_name == b"virtio_net" || ...` -
  decides which drivers get an MSI-X vector. A new driver that needs an interrupt has no way to say
  so except by being added to it, so the rule describes an intention rather than a constraint.

THE RULE, AND IT IS THE WHOLE ANSWER: the FIRST driver of a family that needs one of these owns the
NARROW extension it needs and says so in its own bullet. Concretely, and so it is not left abstract:
**NVMe** owns the first plain-PCI resource profile, since it is the first item that needs a claimable
device the kernel does not already resolve. **ARM PL011** is the first firmware-bound driver - it binds
"through ACPI/FDT compatible identity and supplied clock/IRQ data, not a QEMU address constant", and
it stands eighty lines ahead of HID over I2C - so it owns a stable firmware-node identity and one
claimable resource row for it. HID over I2C then uses what PL011 built. There is no `USB hotplug` checkbox and two
rounds have now named a wrong owner for it. "The first group-3 item written" is wrong for the reason
this same file states two hundred lines earlier: group 3 contains HID over I2C, which is not USB and
attaches nothing to a USB bus. The catalogue subscribe, an `OFFER` on attach, a `WITHDRAW` and a
closed child server on detach, and a new provider generation on reattach are owned by **THE FIRST
ITEM THAT ACTUALLY ATTACHES A USB CHILD AT RUNTIME** - the USB execution-model prerequisite if it
lands first, otherwise the first USB class slice. A sub-function binding unit arrives with the first driver that is actually split. And the DECLARATIVE
RESOURCE PROFILE - the registry entry says what the driver needs and the manager reads it - is owned
by THE FIRST NEW DRIVER THAT WOULD OTHERWISE REQUIRE A CHANGE TO THE NAME LIST, which is a wider
trigger than the one written here first. "A shape the list cannot express" would have let a driver
with an ordinary MSI-X need be handled by appending one more name, which is exactly the edit the
head's rule forbids - so the rule would have been broken by every driver that obeyed the trigger. What is refused is a universal discovery framework
built ahead of the drivers that would show what it has to hold - which is the shape P02M0163 declined
for exactly this reason.

## 1. virtio devices

This is the hardware this system actually runs on, in every developer run and every CI boot, so it is
where a driver is cheapest to write and hardest to leave broken. virtio-iommu leads because it is the
isolation every other DMA driver here is measured against and its backend already exists.

- [ ] **virtio-iommu**: [P02M0153](P02M0153.md) owns the DMA/IOVA contract, its kernel-owned backend
  interface, its host fake and the IOMMU-absent policy - they MOVED there from P02M0098 - and it
  implements the backend and closes the first x86_64 EDU + virtio-net security slice. WHAT IS LEFT
  HERE IS A ROLLOUT AND IT IS NOT YET WORK: "the remaining drivers and architectures" is not a list,
  and an item without one cannot be picked up. Whoever starts it names the drivers, the targets and
  the gate for each - or the item is closed as belonging to P02M0153 and the per-driver attachment
  moves into the driver items that need it. Device DMA remains denied until
  attachment succeeds; driver crash tears mappings down. Native VT-d, AMD-Vi and ARM
  SMMU implementations are separate Phase-4 backends to the same contract.
- [ ] **virtio-rng**: add a bounded entropy-source driver and a typed kernel/security
  ingestion contract that credits source quality conservatively, mixes rather than
  exposes raw host bytes, handles short/failing requests and never treats one VM source
  as proof of cryptographic health. Cover deterministic test injection, startup without
  entropy and driver restart without repeating previously delivered output.
- [ ] **virtio-serial multiport**: generalize `virtio-console` from one debug console to
  dynamically named, hotpluggable ports with bounded control messages, per-port queues,
  open/close state and independent capability-scoped byte streams. Reserve the emergency
  console path, but let provisioning/diagnostic agents use separate ports instead of
  multiplexing an ad-hoc protocol over console bytes. Reuse P02M0104's specification-compliant
  static named development-port foundation; this task retains ownership of general
  dynamic discovery, hotplug and provisioning/diagnostic ports.
- [ ] **virtio-vsock**: implement bounded host/guest stream connections with negotiated
  CID, port capability, credit accounting, reset/shutdown and connection limits. Expose
  it as an explicit local transport for provisioning, diagnostics and agents, not as an
  ambient bypass around NetworkService, PermissionManager or authenticated remote-admin
  policy. Test hostile credit updates, peer reset and host disappearance.
- [ ] **virtio-scsi**: negotiate transport features, enumerate bounded targets/LUNs,
  support read/write/flush and hotplug behind the canonical block contract, and cap
  queues, CDB/sense lengths and outstanding tags. AND IT OWNS THE TWO SHARED CONTRACTS IT
  WOULD OTHERWISE ASSUME: there is no common SCSI command/sense layer - SCSI lives inside
  `usb_storage.rs` - and no canonical block wire, which is copied by hand into three
  places. This slice extracts a bounded SCSI command/sense core out of the USB BOT code
  and states the block wire once. NVMe, AHCI and UAS then USE them rather than each
  finding out.
  It complements rather than replaces `virtio-blk`; unsupported passthrough commands are
  not exposed to ordinary storage clients.
- [ ] **virtio-crypto**: negotiate only explicitly supported algorithms and create
  bounded session/key handles for selected symmetric/hash operations through a typed
  crypto-provider contract. Secret key material is transferred once under a narrow
  capability, never readable back, zeroized on teardown and not assumed safer than the
  host. Keep software cryptography as the correctness baseline and reject migration/
  replay-sensitive offload until its threat model is written.
- [ ] **virtio-balloon and virtio-mem**: add separate policy-controlled VM memory
  providers only after ResourceManager has explicit reclaim/hotplug contracts and the
  kernel can safely offline, pin and return pages - NEITHER OF WHICH HAS AN OWNER, here or
  anywhere else, which makes this item's precondition a wait with nothing at the end of it.
  This item owns them or it does not start: reclaim and hotplug in ResourceManager, and the
  kernel side of offlining a page, are its first work rather than its assumption. Bound request batches, never reclaim
  DMA/mapped/unevictable pages, survive host cancellation and report pressure without
  letting the host directly choose a victim Domain. Keep both protocols distinct.
- [ ] **virtio-fs** as an explicit development-only integration backend: run it behind
  StorageService's capability-scoped Volume API, require an opt-in QEMU/developer flag,
  bound requests/names/xattrs and prevent path traversal or host-node identity leakage.
  It is never the system-volume format, production default or an ambient host-filesystem
  mount; LiberFS and the ordinary package/staging pipeline remain canonical. P02M0104 does
  not depend on this backend and continues to publish executable generations through its
  validated artifact protocol after virtio-fs exists. Test that a process without the
  granted volume cannot observe the host share.

## 2. Generic and universal controllers

Standardized silicon interfaces: one driver per specification, every vendor's part behind it. Ordered
by what a real machine cannot be used without - storage and console first, because a machine that
cannot read its disk or print a line cannot be debugged - with server and legacy compatibility paths
at the end.

- [ ] **NVMe** generic PCIe storage controller: controller reset/enable, admin plus I/O
  queues, identify controller/namespaces, PRP transfers, flush, read/write, status and
  timeout recovery behind the existing block contract used by `virtio-blk` and USB
  storage. Bound queue depth and transfer size, validate every completion ID/phase,
  support multiple namespaces/controllers and cleanly fail unsupported metadata,
  protection, zoned or vendor commands rather than partially interpreting them.
- [ ] **AHCI/SATA** standard HBA: enumerate implemented ports, identify SATA devices,
  issue bounded DMA read/write/flush commands, handle NCQ only after the single-command
  path, detect hotplug and recover a failed port without resetting unrelated ports.
  Serve the existing block contract and reject unsupported ATAPI/port-multiplier paths
  explicitly. Keep controller quirks and non-AHCI vendor modes in Phase 4.
- [ ] **UEFI GOP / simple-framebuffer handoff**: formalize the already implemented boot
  framebuffer discovery as one architecture-neutral immutable descriptor and a generic
  early-display provider with checked geometry/pixel masks/cache policy. Define the
  ownership handoff to DisplayService/real GPU drivers, retain console output when no
  GPU binds and test x86_64/aarch64/riscv64 UEFI plus direct-boot simple-framebuffer
  paths. This is consolidation of existing paths, not a second framebuffer stack.
- [ ] **16550 UART**: retain the minimal existing kernel early-console path, then hand a
  discovered standard UART's MMIO/PIO and IRQ capabilities to a restartable userspace
  serial driver after DeviceManager is online. Support FIFO, RX/TX interrupt flow and
  bounded buffering behind the canonical serial contract; isolate baud/clock/platform
  description from the common 16550 register engine and preserve panic output fallback.
  AND THE SERIAL CONTRACT HAS THREE CLAIMANTS AND NO DEFINITION. "The canonical serial contract" does
  not exist. USB CDC-ACM defines "one typed serial/byte-stream contract" a hundred and twenty lines
  further down; virtio-serial multiport, in GROUP 1 and therefore ahead of both, gives its ports
  "independent capability-scoped byte streams". Whichever of the three is written first defines it
  and the other two use it - and the first one has to say whether a virtio port, a UART and a USB
  adapter are one contract, or whether the byte stream is the base and the line-coding and control
  lines are a serial layer on top. That question is answered once, by whoever gets there first, and
  none of the three may assume it was.
- [ ] **ARM PL011 UART**: perform the same early-console -> userspace handoff for the
  existing PL011 path, sharing the serial contract, buffering, lifecycle and tests with
  16550 while retaining its distinct register/interrupt implementation. Bind through
  ACPI/FDT compatible identity and supplied clock/IRQ data, not a QEMU address constant.
- [ ] **SDHCI** standard SD/eMMC host controller: command/data inhibit handling,
  capabilities, voltage/clock negotiation, card identification, block addressing,
  PIO first and bounded ADMA only after the simple path is proven; expose removable
  media through the existing block contract. Keep the SD protocol core independent of
  PCI/ACPI/FDT attachment so board-specific clock, reset, regulator and pinmux glue can
  remain outside the universal driver. Cover insertion/removal and read-only cards.
- [ ] **EDID/DDC display discovery**: add one bounded EDID parser and typed monitor mode/
  physical-size/capability vocabulary shared by simple framebuffer, virtio-gpu and later
  real display drivers. Verify header/checksums, cap extension blocks and reject malformed
  timings before arithmetic. DDC/I2C/AUX are transport backends supplied by the bound
  display controller; EDID is a helper/provider contract, not an independent process
  poking arbitrary buses.
- [ ] **PCIe hotplug, AER and PME** shared bus infrastructure: process standardized slot
  notifications, enumerate/remove functions dynamically, surface corrected/uncorrected
  Advanced Error Reporting records and power-management events, and coordinate safe
  driver stop before resource removal. Bound capability walks and event storms; a fatal
  error quarantines the affected function/subtree rather than resetting unrelated PCIe
  devices. Native platform slot controllers remain firmware glue behind this contract.
  THIS ITEM OWNS THE LIFECYCLE IT NEEDS, because nothing else does: `BindingState` has no
  `Removed` and cannot, since the inventory is boot-only. So this item adds the removal
  event, the transition through `Stopping`, and a terminal `Removed`. TERMINAL AND
  REPLUGGABLE ARE NOT A CONTRADICTION, AND THE MODEL HAS TO SAY WHY: the slot outlives the
  device as a tombstone, `Removed` is terminal FOR THAT BINDING, and only a NEW ARRIVAL on
  that slot opens a new one - whose claim mints the next generation, so a message stamped
  with the old one is refused by arithmetic rather than by anyone remembering. A removal
  whose teardown was NOT confirmed ends in `Quarantined`, not `Removed`: `Removed` says the
  device is gone and nothing is owed, which is the one thing an unconfirmed teardown cannot
  say. It also reaches into two neighbours and says so rather than assuming: P02M0165 gains
  a device-removed stop intent, and P02M0166 gains a rendering for `Removed`. A USB or SD
  child disappearing is NOT any of this: it withdraws a provider and its parent controller
  stays `Online`.
- [ ] **TPM 2.0 TIS/CRB**: discover either standardized transport, serialize bounded TPM
  commands, enforce locality/timeout/cancel, validate response headers and expose only
  typed operations needed for random data, PCR measurement/quote and sealed keys.
  Applications never receive raw MMIO or unrestricted command passthrough; integrate
  with future measured/verified boot and identity policy without making TPM presence a
  boot requirement. Vendor firmware and proprietary security processors remain Phase 4.
- [ ] **ACPI power button, battery and thermal classes**: parse only the required bounded
  AML namespace/resources behind a platform service, publish typed power-button,
  battery/AC and thermal-zone state, and capability-gate shutdown/suspend requests.
  THIS IS THREE ITEMS AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS A DRIVER. The power button is a fixed-hardware
  PM1a event named by the FADT and needs no interpreter. Battery and thermal are `_BST`,
  `_BIF` and `_TMP` - CONTROL METHODS - and this kernel has no AML interpreter and no ACPI
  namespace at all: it reads `APIC`, `SRAT` and `SLIT` and nothing else. So those two halves
  wait on an interpreter, which is its own milestone and is not this one; splitting them off
  is the first thing whoever picks this up has to do.
  Validate package/object depth and event storms; keep board EC methods, fan curves and
  vendor power policy in Phase 4. FDT platforms use equivalent typed providers rather
  than pretending ACPI is universal across firmware ecosystems.
- [ ] **High Definition Audio (HDA)** standard PCI controller: reset and enumerate
  codecs/function groups/widgets, build a minimal deterministic pin -> converter route,
  configure stream descriptor/BDL/rate/format and expose playback/capture through the
  same PCM contract as `virtio-snd` and USB Audio. Bound CORB/RIRB and unsolicited
  responses, isolate codec/controller failure, and leave vendor DSP firmware, jack
  policy and machine-specific routing quirks to Phase 4.
- [ ] **USB Type-C Connector System Software Interface (UCSI)**: NEEDS AN AML INTERPRETER on
  x86, where the PPM interface is reached through ACPI - see the power-button item; the
  transport-based variants are a different item. Then: discover firmware-
  described connectors, consume bounded notifications and expose connector, data/power
  role, orientation, alternate-mode and charging state through a typed Type-C service.
  Capability-gate role swaps and power-policy changes; keep USB-PD policy in the owning
  service and vendor retimers/EC transports in Phase 4. Test notification storms,
  connector removal and firmware timeouts.
- [ ] **ACPI WDAT hardware watchdog**: parse the bounded watchdog action table, validate
  register regions/instruction sequences, expose arm/pet/disarm/status through a narrow
  watchdog service and integrate ownership with ServiceManager's unattended recovery
  policy. Never execute arbitrary AML or table writes from an untrusted client; prove
  expiry/reset in a fake platform and preserve a clean no-watchdog boot path.
- [ ] **ACPI Time and Alarm Device**: NEEDS AN AML INTERPRETER - ACPI000E is a namespace
  device with control methods, and this kernel reads static tables only. Then: bind it, expose
  bounded read/set/alarm operations as one optional TimeService source and preserve RTC/
  NTP fallback. Validate GAS/register widths and alarm ranges, capability-gate clock and
  wake changes, and keep vendor RTC/EC implementations in Phase 4. Absence never blocks
  boot or wall-clock service.
- [ ] **EHCI** standard USB 2 host controller as a lower-priority compatibility path:
  reuse USB enumeration/class drivers above one host-controller interface, implement
  bounded async/periodic schedules, split transactions and root-hub events, and prove
  HID/storage/class parity with xHCI. It must not fork USB descriptors, class state or
  policy. Add only after current xHCI is the measured reference implementation.
- [ ] **OHCI and UHCI** legacy USB 1.x host controllers as the final compatibility wave:
  separate controller engines share the same USB core/class contracts, bounded transfer
  ownership and hotplug semantics. Require QEMU fixture coverage and a demonstrated
  target need before enabling either by default; no legacy polling path may weaken IRQ,
  DMA cleanup or controller-process isolation.
- [ ] **IPMI KCS, BT and SSIF transports**: implement the standardized host-to-BMC
  transports behind one bounded IPMI message contract, with request serialization,
  sequence/timeout recovery and explicit BMC disappearance. A higher management service
  owns sensors, SEL, chassis and authentication policy; raw commands require an admin
  capability. Prefer this in the server deployment wave and leave vendor BMC extensions
  unsupported by default.
- [ ] **ACPI NFIT / NVDIMM**: parse bounded SPA ranges, region mappings, flush hints and
  health records, expose persistent-memory namespaces as typed block or direct-access
  providers only after persistence ordering and poison handling are defined. Validate
  overlap/alignment against the physical map and never map firmware control regions into
  clients. This is a server-priority universal path; vendor management commands and
  platform-specific interleave quirks remain Phase 4.

## 3. USB device classes and the remainder

Most of these sit on the xHCI stack that already exists, so each is a class driver rather than new
controller machinery, which is why the group follows the controllers. HID over I2C is the exception
and is here for its class rather than its transport: it reuses the HID parser and reaches its device
over a firmware-described I2C controller, so it is also the first item in this group that needs the
firmware identity named above. Ordered by what a person sitting at the machine notices, with the
mutating and niche transports last.

- [ ] **THE EXECUTION MODEL FOR THIS GROUP IS DECIDED ONCE, BEFORE THE FIRST ITEM IN IT.** Today HID
  and mass storage are MODULES INSIDE the xHCI process, and the controller holds a single
  `Option<(UsbDevice, Storage)>` - one storage device, in one Domain, with one claim. Every bullet
  below asks for a registry entry, a claim, a transactional bind and DeviceManager binding per
  driver, and neither shape gives it that. Two answers, and this item is choosing between them:
  class drivers stay inside the xHCI Domain, in which case the per-driver registry, claim and bind
  requirements do not apply to them and this document says so; or one USB-interface binding unit
  exists, with endpoint capabilities, its own generation and composite devices, in which case it is
  the narrow extension the rule above describes and this item owns it. P02M0163 refused the second
  as "splitting a driver into several and giving each its own authority" and said the milestone that
  needs it builds it. This is that milestone, and the choice is its first item rather than a thing
  each class driver rediscovers.

- [ ] **Generic USB HID expansion** beyond the implemented keyboard and pointer paths:
  extend the shared report-descriptor parser and InputService vocabulary for gamepads,
  joysticks, consumer controls, tablets and multi-touch collections. Preserve usage
  page/id, logical ranges, units, contact identity and simultaneous controls instead of
  flattening every device into keys or a mouse. Bound report sizes, collection depth,
  contact count and malformed descriptors; existing keyboard/pointer behavior must not
  regress.
- [ ] **USB CDC-ACM** serial class: bind communication/data interfaces and alternate
  settings, parse functional descriptors, serve bounded RX/TX with line-coding and
  control-line operations, propagate disconnects, stalls and short packets, and define
  one typed serial/byte-stream contract reusable by console tools, MCU links and modems.
  Exercise composite devices and several simultaneous adapters; do not expose a global
  ambient `/dev/tty*` namespace.
- [ ] **USB Attached SCSI (UAS)**: reuse the bounded SCSI command/sense layer from BOT
  while implementing UAS command/status/data pipes, stream IDs, tagged concurrency,
  task management, timeout and endpoint recovery. Expose the same block contract and
  retain BOT as a negotiated fallback for devices that offer both. Cap outstanding
  commands and reject duplicate/unknown tags or inconsistent residue deterministically.
- [ ] **USB CDC-ECM and CDC-NCM** network classes: share descriptor/control plumbing but
  keep their frame formats separate; validate MAC/MTU/filter descriptors, bound NCM
  NTB/datagram tables and malformed offsets, and feed the same capability-scoped frame
  transport NetworkService already consumes from `virtio-net`. Cover link transitions,
  zero-length/short transfers, disconnect and backpressure without a second network
  stack inside the driver.
- [ ] **HID over I2C**: AND IT OWNS THE I2C CONTRACT, because there is none. No I2C controller driver
  exists, `ProviderKindName` has no I2C kind, and "the bound I2C controller supplies the transport"
  names a provider nothing publishes. AN EARLIER ROUND LEFT THIS AS A CHOICE AND THAT WAS WRONG:
  "waits for the controller item" waits for an item this roadmap does not contain, so only one of the
  two answers exists. THIS ITEM OWNS a bounded I2C bus contract and its host fake, and its bind half
  is hardware-deferred - there is no I2C HID model in QEMU either, so the descriptor and protocol
  halves close on host tests and the end-to-end bind waits for a machine that has one. Otherwise:
  reuse the generic HID report parser and expanded InputService
  vocabulary over the standardized HID-I2C descriptor/register protocol. Keep the HID
  transport independent of concrete I2C controllers; ACPI/FDT attachment supplies only
  the bus capability, address, IRQ/reset and descriptor location. Bound report lengths,
  power/reset transitions and interrupt storms; support touchpads/touchscreens without
  product-ID bindings.
- [ ] **USB Audio Class 1/2**: parse audio-control topology and streaming alternate
  settings, choose only explicitly supported PCM formats/rates/channels, schedule
  bounded isochronous transfers and adapt clock/feedback endpoints without unbounded
  drift. Present the same PCM transport contract as `virtio-snd` to AudioService;
  support playback and capture independently, propagate unplug/xrun, and reject DSP,
  MIDI and vendor extensions until their own vocabularies exist.
- [ ] **USB Bluetooth HCI transport**: bind the standard USB Bluetooth class endpoints,
  carry bounded HCI command/event/ACL/ISO packets over a capability-scoped controller
  transport and recover stalls/unplug. The driver stops at HCI: pairing, keys, L2CAP,
  profiles and radio policy belong to a separately sandboxed Bluetooth service, whose
  size/risk does not leak into xHCI or the transport process.
- [ ] **USB HID Power Device class**: extend the common HID usage/value machinery for
  UPS, battery, load, voltage, runtime and alarm reports, publishing normalized state to
  the platform power service. Capability-gate output switching and shutdown commands;
  bound collection/report complexity and preserve unknown vendor usages without
  interpreting them. Reuse generic HID transport instead of adding a UPS-specific USB
  parser.
- [ ] **USB CCID** smart-card class: parse class/slot capabilities, power and negotiate
  card parameters, exchange bounded APDUs with timeout/abort and surface insertion/
  removal through a capability-scoped smart-card service. Do not place PIN handling or
  authentication policy inside the transport driver; malformed lengths, sequence IDs
  and unsolicited slot changes get deterministic tests.
- [ ] **USB CDC-MBIM**: bind the standard mobile-broadband control/data interfaces,
  validate fragmented control messages and session/NDP tables, model SIM/network/link
  state through a typed modem service and pass IP datagrams to NetworkService under an
  explicit grant. Bound outstanding transactions and datagrams; no modem-management
  command or subscription identity becomes ambient authority.
- [ ] **USB Video Class (UVC)**: first add a bounded camera vocabulary for format/frame
  enumeration, negotiated stream parameters, timestamps and capability-scoped frame
  buffers; do not tunnel video through DisplayService. Then parse UVC control/streaming
  descriptors, negotiate uncompressed or already supported compressed formats, assemble
  payload headers into bounded frames over isochronous/bulk endpoints, and recover from
  frame loss, malformed FID/EOF sequences and unplug. No image decoder is duplicated in
  the driver; consumers use the P02M0093/P02M0096 codec leaves.
- [ ] **USB MIDI**: support MIDI 1.0 event packets and the standardized USB MIDI 2.0/UMP
  path only after a shared bounded event/timestamp vocabulary exists. Preserve cable/
  group identity, ordering and timestamps, cap queues and SysEx size, recover from

## Where each item's specification and reference implementation come from

Written down because "is this implementable" was asked and the answer is not uniform, and because
two of the three obstacles have nothing to do with hardware.

THE READY-MADE DRIVER IS ALMOST ALWAYS LINUX AND ALMOST ALWAYS GPL-2.0, which cannot be copied into
this tree without changing what this tree is. Linux stays useful as a description of what real
hardware DOES - ordering, quirks, the register write that has to come second - and not as code. What
follows names permissively licensed references instead, because those are the ones that can be read
with a clear head:

- **`virtio-drivers`** (rust-vmm), MIT / Apache-2.0 - `no_std` Rust, the whole of group 1's shape.
- **FreeBSD / NetBSD / OpenBSD**, BSD-2/3 - the widest coverage of group 2 and much of group 3.
- **EDK2 / TianoCore**, BSD-2-Clause-Patent - NVMe, AHCI, all four USB controllers, USB mass storage
  and HID, SD/MMC, TPM2, ACPI table parsing. Firmware-shaped, so often the smallest correct example.
- **Redox OS**, MIT - NVMe, AHCI, xHCI, virtio, HDA, in Rust.

AND FOUR ITEMS ARE BLOCKED ON SOMETHING THIS SECTION EXISTS TO MAKE VISIBLE: they need an AML
interpreter and an ACPI namespace, and this kernel has neither. It reads `APIC`, `SRAT` and `SLIT` -
static tables - and nothing else. `_BST`, `_BIF`, `_TMP` and the UCSI PPM interface are control
methods, so those items are not "waiting for a laptop", they are waiting for a bytecode interpreter
the size of the rest of this milestone. Marked **AML** below. The power button is NOT among them: on
a fixed-hardware platform it is a PM1a event named by the FADT, and WDAT and NFIT are static tables.

## Order, gates and completion

- [ ] Land the three groups in this order as a PRIORITY, not as a gate. The first group is what this
  system runs on today; the second is what a real machine needs before anything can be installed on
  it; the third is breadth on a USB stack that already exists. Within a group the order is by value
  rather than by cost, except that a cheap item unblocking an expensive one earns its place ahead of
  it. A later item never blocks shipping or testing an earlier finished one - AND AN EARLIER ITEM
  BLOCKS A LATER ONE ONLY WHERE IT IS AN ACTUAL PREREQUISITE. Development-only virtio-fs does not
  hold up NVMe and NFIT does not hold up CDC-ACM; reading the group order as permission would make
  every unfinished item in group 1 a gate on forty others for no technical reason.
- [ ] Each item states three things and they fit on one line each: what it REQUIRES that already
  exists, what it OWNS that does not yet, and the one named integration gate that proves it. THREE
  SHARED CONTRACTS HAVE NO OWNER TODAY and will be discovered by whoever writes the driver that needs
  them: there is no common SCSI layer - SCSI lives inside `usb_storage.rs` - the block wire format is
  copied by hand into StorageService, virtio-blk and USB storage, and the provider vocabulary is a
  closed set of eight kinds in `system-manifest`, which the first item needing a ninth extends.
  P02M0164 catalogues more providers than the services consume; which service consumes which is the
  consuming item's, stated in its own bullet. On a
  target without an enforcing IOMMU every DMA driver in every group still follows P02M0153's explicit
  `iommu-required` versus audited trusted-untranslated policy: putting virtio-iommu first does not
  pretend it protects a native platform where that paravirtual provider is absent.
- [ ] Conformance and hostile-device gates for every family: pure descriptor/register/
  ring parsers get host tests and deterministic malformed mutations before MMIO/DMA;
  fake-controller tests cover completion ordering, timeout, reset, disconnect and

## Fourth audit (2026-08-16): `AI/audit/drivers.md`, and `AI/audit/wire.md` WIRE-002

Sixteen driver findings, ten of them High. The first is reported independently by two audits, which
is why it is first: `AI/audit/wire.md` reaches the same code from the `Buffer` contract's side.

THIS DEBT BELONGS TO THE FAMILIES IT TOUCHES, NOT TO THE MILESTONE AS A WHOLE. It is not in the
ordering above and it is not in the Definition of done, and that was right: making sixteen findings
about today's drivers a gate on forty new ones would block every item on defects in code most of them
never touch. Each finding is owned by the family whose driver it is in, and is closed when that
family's next item is - which for the ones below means block storage, USB, virtio sound, virtio GPU
and HID. AND THE FINDINGS ARE RE-VERIFIED BEFORE THEY ARE ACTED ON: they were written on
2026-08-16 and the tree has moved, as the first bullet's correction shows.

- [ ] **A block write maps a transferred object and copies `count * 512` bytes out of it without
  ever asking how big it is (DRV-003 / WIRE-002, both High).** VERIFIED IN BOTH DRIVERS.
  `virtio_blk::serve_write` and `usb_storage::serve_write` each take a caller-supplied `count`,
  compute `bytes = count as u64 * SECTOR as u64`, call `map_object(src_handle)`, and then
  `core::ptr::copy_nonoverlapping(src as *const u8, dst, bytes as usize)`. The bound that IS checked
  - `span.fit(bytes)` and `st.fit_data(bytes)` - bounds the DESTINATION DMA span, not the source
  object. A client that sends `count = 1000` with a 512-byte MemoryObject drives a 512 kB read out
  of a 512-byte mapping: either a fault that kills the isolated driver, or adjacent mapped memory
  written to the disk. `rt::object_info` already reports a memory object's byte size, so the check
  is available today. RE-VERIFIED 2026-08-28 AND NARROWED TWICE: the original finding said object
  type and rights were unchecked on the same path. `sys_memory_map` goes through
  `current_typed::<MemoryObject>(handle, ObjectType::MemoryObject, Rights::MAP)`, so the TYPE and the
  `MAP` right are checked - which is not the same as "rights are checked", and the distinction is the
  finding: `READ` is not asked for on an object the driver reads OUT of. What is missing is that and
  the SIZE, and the size is the whole of the copy defect. The 512 kB example is virtio-blk's exactly;
  USB storage clamps its count, so its over-read is smaller and still an over-read.
- [ ] **Virtqueue size, used IDs, used lengths and used-index movement are not validated centrally
  (DRV-001, High).** A device - or anything that can act as one - controls those fields.
- [ ] **Block drivers silently clamp counts and never validate LBA ranges, and USB truncates the LBA
  to 32 bits (DRV-002, High).** A silent clamp turns a wrong request into a wrong write.
- [ ] **USB flush converts every repeated failure into success without reading sense data
  (DRV-005, High).** A durability barrier that reports success on failure is worse than none.
- [ ] **USB Bulk-Only Transport ignores actual transfer lengths and CSW residue (DRV-006, High).**
  A short transfer is then read as a complete one.
- [ ] **xHCI loses DMA handles and slot ownership across hotplug and partial failure, and its
  synchronous waits consume and discard port-status events (DRV-007, -008, High).**
- [ ] **Virtio sound treats any interrupt as TX completion, ignores status, reuses stale PCM and
  always replies success (DRV-009, High).**
- [ ] **Virtio GPU trusts unbounded dimensions and uses unchecked framebuffer and rectangle
  arithmetic (DRV-010, High).**
- [ ] **USB configuration walkers read fixed fields outside descriptor records and accept short
  descriptor transfers (DRV-012, High).**
- [ ] **HID arithmetic can overflow before or during report decoding; a short report leaves the
  previous-report tail stale; left and right modifiers share one boolean and release each other
  (DRV-011, -013, -014).**
- [ ] **Unexpected transferred handles are ignored and leaked across several service protocols
  (DRV-004).**
- [ ] **Roughly 5,700 lines of unsafe device logic have no direct tests (DRV-016).** This is the one
  that makes the others expensive to fix safely, so it is worth doing first for whichever driver is
  touched first.
- [ ] **Without an IOMMU a compromised driver or device can DMA outside its process-owned buffers
  (DRV-015, design).** Same boundary as KERN-CORE-020; state it as a threat-model limit if it is not
  being closed.

AND SIX OF THESE HAVE NO FAMILY ITEM TO RIDE ON, so each is given a closure trigger here rather than
a milestone of its own. DRV-009 (virtio sound) and DRV-010 (virtio GPU) were given one in an earlier
round that named items THIS ROADMAP DOES NOT HAVE: there is no `virtio-sound` checkbox and no
`virtio-gpu` checkbox, only references to those drivers from inside other bullets. They are defects
in drivers this system ALREADY SHIPS, and a roadmap of drivers to ADD has nowhere to put them. So
they close where those drivers are next changed, AND THE FIX IS PART OF THAT ITEM'S GATE RATHER THAN
ADJACENT TO IT - which is the difference between a trigger and a hope. Writing HDA does not oblige
anybody to fix `virtio_snd`'s interrupt handling, and writing an EDID parser does not oblige anybody
to bound `virtio_gpu`'s rectangle arithmetic; so DRV-009 is named in the gate of whichever of HDA and
USB Audio is written first, and DRV-010 in EDID/DDC's. A finding that merely rides along beside an
item is a finding nobody has to close.

AND DRV-001 HAS NO OWNER AT ALL. Central virtqueue validation - size, used ids, used lengths, index
movement - lives in code every virtio driver shares, so no single driver family's item contains it
and none of the triggers above reaches it. It is owned by the FIRST VIRTIO ITEM in group 1 to be
written, whichever that turns out to be, because that is the first time somebody is in that code with
a gate attached.

The four that are cross-cutting close on a stated event instead: DRV-004, unexpected
transferred handles leaked across service protocols, closes with the first item that changes a
service protocol's handle handling; DRV-015 is a THREAT-MODEL LIMIT and closes by being written into
the concept's threat model, not by a driver; DRV-016, the untested unsafe device logic, closes per
family as each family's item lands, which is what its own text already says - "worth doing first for
whichever driver is touched first" - and is recorded here so it is not read as one debt anybody could
pay in one go.

# P02M0196 - Devices the firmware describes: identity, static tables, the device tree and ACPI's AML

Status: OPEN - APPROVED BY THE OWNER, WHOLE (2026-09-25): "yes, do all of it; where a milestone is missing,
create it". Nothing is started: the owner asked for plans only until he says otherwise. P02M0190 (TPM) and
P02M0195 (I2C) depend on it, and the milestones created with it for what it feeds - P02M0197 to P02M0202 -
are listed at the end.

WHAT THE PROBLEM IS. A driver in this system is an ordinary process, and it reaches hardware only through a
device DeviceManager has handed it: the claim is what gives it the device's registers and interrupts. That
works for every device that ANNOUNCES itself - PCI functions and USB devices answer "who is there?" with a
vendor, a product and an address. It does not work for the devices soldered to the board that announce
nothing, which the firmware alone knows about and describes in a map it hands the operating system at boot:
  - on PCs, ACPI - partly as TABLES of fixed shape ("the TPM is at 0xFED40000", "the debug serial port is
    here") and mostly as a PROGRAM in AML bytecode ("to read the battery, run method `_BST`"; "the touchpad
    is at address 0x2C on I2C controller 1, interrupt on GPIO pin 17");
  - on ARM and RISC-V boards, the DEVICE TREE - a plain tree of named nodes with addresses and interrupts.
This system reads that map only for what the kernel needs to boot - the FADT, the MADT, SRAT and SLIT, and
the device tree's memory, CPUs, interrupt controller and PCI host - and has NO IDENTITY for any other device
it describes: no name DeviceManager lists it under, no set of resources that belong to it, no rule for who may
claim it. So none of them can have a driver.

WHAT DEPENDS ON IT: the TPM driver (P02M0190), the ARM PL011 and 16550 UARTs, the ACPI battery, AC adapter and
thermal zones (P02M0181's real producers), HID over I2C (P02M0195), and - through the milestones listed at the
end - the lid and buttons, the ACPI time-and-alarm device, system sleep, processor power, backlight, the WDAT
watchdog, IPMI and UCSI.

WHAT EXISTS TO BUILD ON, AND WHY IT IS NOT ENOUGH. Every layer of today's claim is PCI-shaped: a kernel device
row holds one BAR and a bus/device/function, `SYS_DEVICE_CLAIM` mints one `DeviceMemory` for that BAR, DMA
admission and IOMMU attach are by bus/device/function, and `BindingId`, `provider-info` and the manifest's two
transports are PCI's - P02M0099's identity section says the non-PCI identity is a prerequisite with its own
owner, and this milestone is that owner. No wired interrupt is scoped to a claim on any port. `src/acpi` parses
the FADT (GPE blocks included, used by nothing) and the MADT's source overrides; `src/fdt` parses memory,
CPUs, interrupt controllers, PCI hosts and INTx maps; `src/tpm` finds a TPM from its table.

## P02M0196a - Step 1: identity, the claim, and the devices static descriptions name

- [ ] A PLATFORM DEVICE ROW in the kernel's device table, DISCRIMINATED from a PCI row rather than squeezed
  into one: its identity, the match ids it answers to, a resource set - several MMIO ranges, port ranges
  (P02M0191's `PortRange`), declared registers (P02M0200's, for WDAT), wired interrupt lines with trigger and
  polarity, connections (GPIO lines, serial-bus addresses) and an optional DMA stream id - and a state:
  CLAIMABLE, KERNEL-HELD, FIRMWARE-HELD or RESERVATION (step 2's `PNP0C01`/`PNP0C02`, never claimable).
  `SYS_DEVICE_COUNT` and `SYS_DEVICE_INFO` answer platform rows beside PCI ones, the information record
  gaining the discriminant and a platform part, appended; nothing is versioned.
- [ ] IDENTITY FORMS, one per source, each a stable bounded name: `acpi:` the namespace path, with `_HID`,
  `_CID` and `_UID` as match ids - and for a `PRP0001` node its `_DSD` `compatible` strings too, so a driver
  matches the same way on ACPI and the tree; `dt:` the node path, with its `compatible` list and unit address;
  `table:` a static table's signature and instance (`table:TPM2#0`, `table:WDAT#0`, a DBG2 entry by its
  index); `kernel:` a device the kernel declares because it already drives it (`kernel:com1`, match id
  `PNP0501`, 0x3F8 and IRQ 4). An SMBIOS IPMI record is NO row's identity: `smbios:38#n` is a match id that
  the `IPI0001` row it agrees with gains (the static sources below).
- [ ] ONE DEVICE PER RESOURCE SET. Rows are published in a fixed order - kernel-declared, static tables, then
  the tree or the namespace - and a later description whose ranges overlap a row's is MERGED into it when both
  START AT THE SAME BASE and one contains the other: its identity and match ids join the row's, the row keeps
  the FIRST description's resources, and a live claim is never changed. Any other overlap is REFUSED at
  publication and reported; a reservation's overlaps follow step 2's reservation rule instead. So the `TPM2`
  table and `MSFT0101` are one device whose claim stays the table's one page though QEMU's `_CRS` names 0x1000
  (CRB) or 0x5000 (TIS); and SPCR or DBG2 and the UART's node, the `HPET` table and `PNP0103`, and COM1 and
  its `PNP0501` node are each one device.
- [ ] THE KERNEL-HELD SET, published so every device is accounted for, and never claimable: on x86_64 the PIC,
  the PIT, the speaker port, the CMOS clock, the HPET, fw_cfg, the ISA DMA controller (a bus master nothing
  translates), the I/O APICs, IOMMU units and PCI host bridges; on aarch64 the GIC with its ITS and v2m frames,
  the timer, the PL031 clock, fw_cfg and the PCIe host; on riscv64 the APLIC, the IMSIC, the ACLINT, the
  goldfish clock, fw_cfg, the PCIe host and the `sifive,test` reset device (whose authority is
  `system-power`'s). The kernel's CONSOLE UART is kernel-held until a named HANDOFF item releases it: COM1
  through P02M0191, aarch64's PL011 through P02M0099's PL011 item and riscv64's 16550 through P02M0099's 16550
  item, both of which already require that handoff. THE CLOCKS STAY THE KERNEL'S: it reads them for the wall
  clock and P02M0197 arms their alarms, so no driver is wanted for them and none gets a handoff.
- [ ] THE CLAIM MINTS EVERY RESOURCE, AND RELEASE REVOKES EVERY ONE. A platform claim is taken exactly like a
  PCI claim - the same key, generation, quarantine and teardown - and DeviceManager then mints each resource
  from the claim by kind and index: a `DeviceMemory` per MMIO range, a `PortRange` per port range, an
  `Interrupt` per wired line, each registered as derived from the claim before it is handed out, as the BAR's
  memory is today. That mint call is introduced by whichever of this step and P02M0191 lands first, since both
  use it; the port half waits for P02M0191's object, MMIO and interrupts do not. The `kernel:com1` claim also
  carries P02M0191's `ConsoleTap` kind (the driver's drain of the kernel's console ring), and claiming it
  performs P02M0191's handoff.
- [ ] CLAIM-SCOPED WIRED INTERRUPTS ON ALL THREE ARCHITECTURES. Today none is scoped to a claim: x86_64 binds
  only the fifteen legacy vectors behind the DeviceManager privilege, through one I/O APIC at a fixed base
  whose MADT entries are never read, and aarch64 and riscv64 refuse outright.
  - x86_64: every I/O APIC enumerated from the MADT with its GSI base; source overrides applied; a GSI above
    15 given a vector from a wired pool rather than `IRQ_BASE + GSI`; trigger and polarity from the `_CRS`
    descriptor or the override.
  - aarch64: GIC SPIs configured - edge or level, routed to a core, enabled - from the tree's `interrupts`.
  - riscv64: APLIC wired sources in the MSI delivery mode this port runs (`aia=aplic-imsic`), sense mode and
    target from the tree. This kernel drives no PLIC, so a machine without AIA refuses wired claims by name.
  - MASK UNTIL ACKNOWLEDGED: a LEVEL line is disabled at its controller in the dispatch path, before the end
    of interrupt, and enabled again by the driver's existing `SYS_INTERRUPT_ACK` - today's x86_64 dispatch
    signals and sends EOI without masking, so a level source still asserted re-fires until the driver runs.
    Release and a kill mask the line and give back its vector or identity. The legacy `SYS_INTERRUPT_BIND`
    path, which nothing in userspace calls, is retired.
- [ ] DMA FOR NON-PCI ENDPOINTS: a platform claim is NON-MASTERING - DMA policy `none`, no DMA buffer for it -
  unless its description names a stream id (a tree's `iommus`, IORT, DMAR's namespace-device entries), in which
  case it is attached like a PCI endpoint where this kernel's IOMMU driver serves that controller, and refused
  by name where it does not. No device this milestone's gates claim masters the bus.
- [ ] BINDING, ORIGIN, MATCH AND DIAGNOSTICS, changed together: `BindingId` becomes PCI (bus, device,
  function) or PLATFORM (the kernel's platform number), with its generation; `provider-info` gains the same
  discriminant, appended; the manifest's match vocabulary gains `transport = "platform"` with `hid`, `cid`,
  `compatible`, `table` and `class` (step 2's method-only classes) predicates, so drivers match firmware ids
  the way they match PCI ids today; policy keys use the stable identity name, never the number; `lsdev` shows
  each platform row's source, identity, resources and state, and each PCI function's companion node.
- [ ] THE STATIC SOURCES, read by the kernel with no interpreter, each into what it is:
  - `TPM2` -> a device with ONE MMIO range, the 4 KiB locality-0 page: 0xFED40000 for start method 6 (FIFO),
    the control area's address less 0x40 for start method 7 (CRB); no interrupt (the transport polls), no DMA;
    any other start method is logged by number and creates nothing (P02M0190). x86_64 only - the other two
    loaders pass no RSDP;
  - `SPCR`, `DBG2` -> the UART descriptions they are, merged with the console's row or the namespace node;
  - `HPET` -> merged into the kernel-held HPET;
  - `WDAT` -> a device whose resources are the registers its instruction entries name (P02M0200): system I/O
    registers as `PortRange`s after the reserved-set and claim checks, system memory registers NOT as
    `DeviceMemory` but as P02M0200's kernel-accessed declared registers (one register, its exact width,
    write-masked), because a page such as ICH9's GCS also holds interrupt-routing registers;
  - `BGRT` -> a firmware fact (where the boot logo is), not a device and never claimable;
  - SMBIOS type 38 -> NO ROW: a cross-check for IPMI (P02M0201). When step 2 publishes an `IPI0001` row, the
    record that agrees with it is attached as that row's `smbios:38#n` match id - the record's interface type
    equal to the node's `_IFT`, which the service reports with the node, and its address equal to the node's:
    for KCS and BT the record's base decoded (its low bit marks I/O space, the modifier byte gives address bit
    0) equal to the base of the node's `_CRS` range; for SSIF the base shifted right by one - the seven-bit
    address - equal to the `I2cSerialBusV2` address. A record no node takes, or one that disagrees, is
    reported and changes nothing; the node is used either way.
  The kernel's table lookup gains instances; today it returns the first table of a signature.
- [ ] SMBIOS REACHES THE SYSTEM, which nothing hands over today: the loader on each port reads the UEFI
  configuration table for SMBIOS 3.x (the 64-bit entry point), else 2.x, and passes the entry point's physical
  address in a field appended to `BootInfo` (zero when absent; no version change); the kernel reads it with a
  bounded, host-tested parser - entry-point checksum, structure-table length, each structure's formatted
  length and string set bounded - and names the system's type-1 identity on the boot log. A direct device-tree
  boot has no SMBIOS and says so.
- [ ] THE DEVICE TREE: every node with a `compatible` and resources and not `status = "disabled"` published,
  the kernel-held nodes marked. `reg` is translated through the parents' `ranges` (a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` node
  sits under QEMU's platform-bus `simple-bus`); below an I2C controller's node it is an address on that bus -
  a connection (step 2), never MMIO. A row carries its translated `reg` whole, except a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio`
  node's, which is ONE MMIO range - the 4 KiB locality-0 page at its translated `reg` base - and no interrupt,
  exactly as the `TPM2` row is (QEMU's node names 0x5000, all five localities). An interrupt specifier -
  `interrupts-extended`, or `interrupts` with `interrupt-parent`, through any `interrupt-map` - is a WIRED
  line where its parent is the interrupt controller the kernel drives, and a LINE CONNECTION where its parent
  node is both `gpio-controller` and `interrupt-controller` (the virtio-gpio function's `virtio,device29`
  node): the line its first cell and the trigger its second, made by the companion join and never a wired
  interrupt. The node's property block, its CHILD NODES and their properties included (P02M0202c's
  `usb-c-connector` child), reaches the driver through its claim (bounded), with `fixed-clock` references
  resolved to a frequency - the "supplied clock data" P02M0099's PL011 item asks for - and any other clock,
  reset, regulator or pinctrl reference, or an interrupt whose parent is neither, listed as unresolved, so a
  driver refuses rather than guesses. The tree's PCI child nodes are joined to their functions by the kernel
  (see the companion join in step 2).
- [ ] WHAT STEP 1 UNBLOCKS: the TPM driver (P02M0190) on x86_64 through `TPM2` and on aarch64 and riscv64
  through the tree's `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` node; the WDAT watchdog (P02M0200); the SMBIOS records IPMI's
  cross-check reads (P02M0201), attached once step 2 publishes `IPI0001`; PL011 and 16550 through their
  handoffs; every simple tree-described device on a board.

## P02M0196b - Step 2: AML and the ACPI namespace

- [ ] WHERE AML RUNS, DECIDED: in a userspace ACPI SERVICE, never in the kernel - it is firmware code and it
  can be wrong, and a defect in it must cost a restart rather than the machine. The service holds a new
  privilege, `FirmwareInterpreter`, which ServiceManager gives to it alone, and every authority it has is
  minted by the kernel under the policy below.
  ITS STAGE, AND WHAT FOLLOWS FROM IT. The service is VOLUME-STAGED and `transparent`: launched through
  ProcessService once that is up - its dependencies are LogService and ProcessService - and restarted by
  ServiceManager's existing ladder, which relaunches from the volume and from nothing else, so a pinned
  service could not be restarted at all. DeviceManager is pinned and runs both of its bind rounds before
  ProcessService starts - phase one to mount the volume, phase two while ServiceManager waits on its answer
  to `DRIVERS` - so:
  - DeviceManager holds NO MANIFEST ROLE on the service, since a role's provider must be a declared
    dependency and would have to start before it; ServiceManager hands DeviceManager a client of each
    instance on its control channel as that instance comes up, a restarted one included - the late hand-off
    the development agent's launcher already takes, and the ladder's step for this one service, as it has
    one for ConfigService's storage - through which DeviceManager asks for node channels and hands over the
    connections the service holds (below);
  - NO BIND ROUND WAITS FOR THE NAMESPACE: the namespace's platform devices are bound as their rows arrive,
    as a hot-plugged function is; a companion joined after its function's driver bound is attached then, and
    that driver asks for the node channel as it does after a restart; a node-channel request that reaches
    DeviceManager before an instance's report is answered at the report;
  - a device the firmware describes ONLY in AML cannot hold the system volume (EXCLUDES).
  A crashed service is restarted with a fresh namespace: the kernel disables every GPE it had enabled and
  revokes every region it had mapped when it dies, the node channels it minted close, so each driver asks
  DeviceManager again, and its connections close with it and are granted again to the new instance. EACH
  INSTANCE REPORTS "NAMESPACE LOADED" once its walk is published, through the kernel, which passes the
  report to DeviceManager on its device-event channel after every row that walk published, naming the
  instance.
- [ ] THE POLICY THE KERNEL ENFORCES, whatever AML asks for:
  - SYSTEM MEMORY regions map ACPI NVS, ACPI-reclaimable and firmware-reserved ranges of the boot memory map,
    and MMIO outside RAM that no kernel-held range, PCI BAR or window, and no claim covers. REFUSED: RAM of
    every other type, kernel-held MMIO (LAPIC, I/O APICs, IOMMU units, ECAM, HPET, the interrupt
    controllers), any BAR or window, and a claimed device's ranges - and a claim is refused, and reported,
    while a region another node declares maps any part of its ranges, so the rule holds whichever comes
    first. TWO ADMISSIONS. FOR FIRMWARE THAT SHARES A DEVICE'S MEMORY WITH ITS DRIVER: a region that a claimed
    namespace device's OWN node declares, inside that device's own `_CRS` memory range, stays mappable while
    the device is claimed - the driver and the node's own methods (`_DSM`, the code that runs before its
    `Notify`) share that memory, as UCSI's mailbox is shared; a region any other node declares over a claimed
    range stays refused. FOR FIRMWARE THAT OWNS A PCI FUNCTION: a BAR of an UNCLAIMED function the service
    names as the companion of the node declaring the region. The kernel then records that function
    FIRMWARE-HELD - a claim held by the service, shown by `lsdev`, that no driver can take - and refuses if a
    driver holds it already. ONE MEMORY TYPE PER RANGE, for the service's mappings and a claim's alike:
    write-back for ACPI NVS, ACPI-reclaimable and firmware-reserved memory, uncached for MMIO - which the
    loader then keeps as a boot-memory-map kind of its own, appended, instead of folding it into reserved as
    it does today.
  - SYSTEM I/O regions get P02M0191 `PortRange`s, refused over P02M0191's reserved set (fixed, FADT-derived and
    run-time additions - the fixed part holding the ISA DMA controllers' channel and control registers,
    `0x00..0x1F` and `0xC0..0xDF`, so no region reaches the kernel-held controller above and starts a transfer
    nothing translates, while its page registers from `0x81`, and a POST-code region at `0x80`, stay
    mintable) and inside a live claim's range, and minted over its terminal-path category as that milestone
    allows. KERNEL-MEDIATED instead: a write to the SMI command port (the FADT's ACPI-disable value
    refused), CMOS NVRAM bytes from 0x0E up for `SystemCMOS` regions, under the kernel's CMOS lock (the clock
    registers stay the kernel's), and PM timer reads.
  - PCI CONFIGURATION regions become kernel-performed accesses by segment, bus, device and function: reads of
    any function; writes refused below offset 0x40, inside MSI and MSI-X capabilities, to a function a driver
    holds, and to the registers the kernel's chipset rows name as its own (the ECAM base, the PM and GPE
    bases). The configuration ports themselves are in P02M0191's reserved set.
  - `_CRS`-DERIVED RESOURCES are checked before a row is published: MMIO not over RAM - ACPI NVS and
    firmware-reserved memory excepted, since firmware places mailboxes there - kernel-held ranges or PCI BARs
    and windows; ports not in the reserved set or a PCI I/O BAR or window; a wired line not one the kernel
    uses (timer, SCI, IPIs, a kernel-held console's line); no overlap with another row except by step 1's
    merge rule and the reservation rule below. A description merged into an earlier row adds no resource, so
    these checks do not refuse it - the `PNP0501` node over the kernel's COM1, whose ports are in the reserved
    set, among them - and A RESERVATION MINTS NOTHING, so they do not refuse one either: its overlaps with rows
    follow the reservation rule, and a reservation over kernel-held MMIO or reserved ports - q35's `DRAC`
    (`PNP0C01`) over the MCFG window, the ECAM; an ICH9 board's `LDRC` over port `0x61`, the SMI command port
    and the PM block - is recorded as one. The service DESCRIBES; the kernel decides what is minted.
  - THE FIXTURE CARVE-OUT, compiled into the development build (`LIBER_DEVELOPMENT=1`) only and never into the
    shipping set, admits two things inside the BAR of a firmware-held `ivshmem-plain` function (1af4:1110): a
    `_CRS` memory range, for a platform device's claim - a harness-played UCSI mailbox, for instance - which
    that device's own node still reaches under the first admission above; and the SystemMemory registers of a
    processor table P02M0198's install check names, for the kernel's install - the service's firmware-held
    claim does not count, for them, as a claim - carved out of what the service maps: no region of the
    service's covers them while the table stands. Every shipping build refuses both, and everywhere else the
    BAR rule holds.
  For the BAR and window checks the boot scan records EVERY function's BARs; today a function outside the
  resolved families has a row with none.
- [ ] THE NEW CALLS, all behind `FirmwareInterpreter`: read an ACPI table by signature and instance (every
  SSDT, and `LoadTable`'s by OEM ids); map a SystemMemory range; mint a SystemIO `PortRange`; a mediated access
  (SMI command, CMOS NVRAM, PM timer); a PCI configuration access; report and withdraw a namespace platform
  device or reservation with its `_CRS` resources; report a companion (node -> segment, bus, device,
  function) and, with a GPIO or serial-bus controller's companion or row, the `_AEI` lines and the lines and
  addresses the service's own fields name; report a host bridge's `_OSC` answer; report the namespace loaded;
  attach the service's event channel; GPE control (enable and disable for runtime, set and clear for wake,
  clear-and-enable after the method) and release of the global lock. The privilege also admits P02M0197b's
  sleep-type registration call.
- [ ] THE INTERPRETER: the DSDT and every SSDT in one namespace; `Load` and `LoadTable` supported, a loaded
  table checked like any other and counted against the bounds, `Unload` refused; method execution BOUNDED -
  time, recursion, memory, loop iterations, package depth - and a method past a bound aborted and reported,
  not retried; `Mutex`, `Event`, `Sleep`, `Stall` and the global lock. Operation regions in system memory,
  system I/O, PCI configuration and `SystemCMOS` under the policy above; `EmbeddedControl` through the EC
  transport below; `GenericSerialBus` through an address-scoped connection (a protocol the controller does
  not declare refused), and `GeneralPurposeIo` FOR INPUT READS ONLY through a line-scoped connection - a write
  is refused by name and reported - each to the controller the field's connection names and obtained like
  the `_AEI` lines below, a read of a line the service already holds for `_AEI` being served on that
  connection, since a controller grants each line once; any other space refused at access and reported.
  `Notify` is delivered to the node's channel.
- [ ] WHAT THE OS SAYS IT IS, DECIDED, because firmware gates its paths on it: `_OSI` answers true for the
  Windows strings through the newest this interpreter is tested against and for the feature strings it
  implements, false for "Linux", "Darwin" and anything else - firmware is validated against Windows, and
  machines otherwise hide batteries, touchpads or processor states; `\_OS` is "Microsoft Windows NT"; `_REV`
  is 2. THE HANDSHAKES, each before what it grants is used:
  - every PCI Express host bridge's `_OSC` requests native hot-plug, AER, PME, capability-structure and LTR
    control, and ON AN ACPI MACHINE the kernel arms its native hot-plug slots and AER handling only for what
    `_OSC` grants (a bridge without `_OSC` grants nothing), leaving firmware-owned slots unarmed and saying
    so - today it arms them at the boot scan; device-tree machines keep native control as today. The
    harness's `acpi-pci-hotplug-with-bridge-support=off` is what makes q35's `_OSC` grant native hot-plug;
  - the platform-wide `\_SB._OSC` requests the bits this system implements: CPPC and CPPC v2 (P02M0198),
    `_OST`, power resources, platform-coordinated `_LPI`;
  - each processor's `_OSC` - `_PDC` where there is none - declares exactly the forms P02M0198's kernel
    executes (system I/O and system memory registers, x86 MWAIT hints; no model-specific-register forms), so
    firmware never hands over a table the kernel refuses; it runs before `_PSS`, `_CST` or `_CPC` is read,
    which is when Intel firmware `Load`s its processor SSDTs.
- [ ] NAMESPACE DEVICES walked with `_STA` and `_INI` in the specification's order; each present device
  reported with `_HID`, `_CID`, `_UID` and `_CRS` (`_PRS`/`_SRS` unused - resources are taken as the firmware
  set them), and an `IPI0001` node with its `_IFT` for step 1's SMBIOS cross-check; a device-check or eject
  `Notify` re-walks the subtree and withdraws what left. Each node kind is said, not guessed: `PNP0C01` and
  `PNP0C02` are RESERVATIONS - rows in that state, listed by `lsdev`, never claimable and never merged,
  reported before the other devices of each walk. A reservation keeps every NEW row published after it (and
  BAR placement, where the kernel places BARs) out of its ranges, and leaves alone the rows published before
  it and the descriptions merged into them; it never enters P02M0191's reserved set, and a mint inside it -
  the TCO sub-range, a WDAT port range, an AML region - stays governed by the reserved set, claims and the
  policy above, as Linux records these ranges without making them busy. Host bridges are the roots
  companions are found under; `ACPI0007` and `Processor` objects are the service's own, NOT published as
  devices (step 3); `ThermalZone` objects are published under the id `THERMALZONE`; PCI interrupt links are
  unused (INTx is disabled); the embedded controller is the service's own.
- [ ] THE COMPANION JOIN. A node with `_ADR` under a host bridge is resolved - with the bridge's `_SEG` and
  `_BBN` and the secondary bus numbers of the bridges between, which the kernel's scan knows - to the PCI
  function it describes, and ATTACHED to that function's row as firmware data (the kernel stores the node
  path, DeviceManager reads it with the row), NOT published as a second claimable device; the tree's PCI child
  nodes (`reg`) are joined the same way. The function's driver receives the node's channel with its claim:
  `_DSD`, `_DSM`, device power methods and `Notify`. A node below a companion that is not itself a PCI
  function is a platform device of its own, never a part of the function: its `_CRS` resources are its row's,
  as any namespace device's are, and it is METHOD-ONLY when they name no memory, port or wired line - an ACPI
  video OUTPUT, whose `_ADR` is a display id, or a HID-over-I2C or SSIF device, whose resources are
  connections - its claim then carrying the channel and its connections. Its identity carries the parent
  companion's PCI function, resolved whether or not a driver binds that function (a laptop's
  boot-framebuffer GPU has none). Such a node with no `_HID` MATCHES BY A CLASS the enumeration assigns from a
  rule table, one row per class: the first is P02M0199's `video-output` - a child of a companion that has
  `_DOD` or `_DOS`, itself having `_BCL` and `_BCM`. CONNECTIONS: an `I2cSerialBusV2`, a `GpioInt` or an
  input `GpioIo` resource source (a `GpioIo` restricted to output is refused by name), and on a tree an I2C
  parent or an interrupt whose parent node is both `gpio-controller` and `interrupt-controller` (step 1's
  line connection), resolves to the row of the controller (a platform device, or a PCI function through its
  companion); the child's row lists each connection naming that row, and DeviceManager binds the child
  through P02M0195's scoped connections - an address- or line-scoped connection minted through the
  controller's `CONNECT`, the child a dependant of each controller. A GPIO controller's `_AEI` line list
  reaches DeviceManager with its companion or row.
- [ ] THE NODE-SCOPED CHANNEL: with every claim of a namespace device, or of a PCI function with a companion,
  DeviceManager asks the service for a channel scoped to that node (and to its children where the class says
  so): evaluate a named method or object there with bounded arguments and results; `_DSD` decoded (device
  properties and hierarchical data); `_DSM` by UUID, revision and function; the node's `Notify` values. A
  class row may also name methods on the PARENT node its driver may evaluate - the first is `_DOS` on the video
  adapter, for P02M0199's backlight driver. Platform methods (`_INI`, `_REG`, `_OSC`, `_PTS`, `_WAK`, `\_Sx`)
  and every other node are refused. Revoked at release.
- [ ] GENERAL-PURPOSE EVENTS, SPLIT BETWEEN THE KERNEL AND THE SERVICE. The kernel keeps the SCI - P02M0099's
  decision stands - and its handler now reads the FADT's GPE0 and GPE1 blocks too: at init every enable bit is
  cleared and every status bit acknowledged before the SCI is routed; in the handler each asserted ENABLED GPE
  is masked, its number latched and delivered on the service's event channel (a channel of its own, carrying
  GPE numbers; the fixed buttons keep `platform_event`'s kinds). A GPE is enabled again only when the service
  has run `_Lxx` or `_Exx` (an edge GPE's status cleared before the method, a level GPE's after) and asks for
  the clear-and-enable. The kernel owns the runtime and WAKE enable masks and changes them only at the
  service's request; P02M0197 arms wake GPEs through that interface. STORMS ARE PER GPE: one that re-asserts
  past a bound in a window is left disabled and reported, the SCI and every other GPE unaffected. And the
  existing storm path is fixed: an SCI that decodes to nothing past its bound now MASKS its redirection entry,
  as its comment always said - today it clears only PM1 enable, and a still-asserted source keeps raising the
  line. PM1's `GBL_STS` is decoded and delivered, and `GBL_RLS` written at the service's request, for the
  global lock the EC and AML share with firmware.
- [ ] GPIO-SIGNALLED EVENTS, AND THE SERVICE'S OTHER CONNECTIONS: a GPIO controller's `_AEI` lines are the
  service's. When the controller's driver is bound, DeviceManager mints line-scoped connections for exactly
  the listed lines through the controller's `CONNECT` - the same mechanism a child device gets - and hands
  them to the service; it mints them AGAIN for each new instance of the service, on that instance's
  "namespace loaded" report, and after the controller rebinds. The lines and addresses the service's
  `GeneralPurposeIo` and `GenericSerialBus` fields name are granted the same way. On each edge or level the
  service runs `_Exx`/`_Lxx` (`_EVT` for a pin above 255) in the controller's scope. No other consumer is
  given those lines.
- [ ] THE EMBEDDED CONTROLLER TRANSPORT, INSIDE THE SERVICE, as the handler of `EmbeddedControl` regions, so
  `_REG` and ECDT-time accesses are ordered in one process: read, write, burst, the query command and `_Qxx`
  on `SCI_EVT`, every wait on IBF and OBF bounded; its GPE through the split above; `_GLK` honoured; its two
  ports minted as `PortRange`s from the ECDT for early access and from `PNP0C09`'s `_CRS` after, the `PNP0C09`
  device FIRMWARE-HELD by the service and never claimable. QEMU emulates no EC, so the protocol is a
  host-tested library against a register model (a stuck IBF, a flood of queries, a controller that vanishes),
  and its acceptance on hardware is a recorded run on a laptop the owner provides, asked when the part starts
  - the same standing as P02M0195's DesignWare controllers and P02M0200's SBSA watchdog.
- [ ] THE DEVICE CLASSES ARE THEIR OWNERS', each a driver bound to its node and built on this step's mechanism
  (the node channel, `Notify`, `_DSD`/`_DSM`, the EC): battery (`PNP0C0A`), AC adapter (`ACPI0003`) and
  thermal zones -> P02M0099's ACPI item, as P02M0181's producers; lid (`PNP0C0D`), control-method power and
  sleep buttons (`PNP0C0C`, `PNP0C0E`) and the Time and Alarm Device (`ACPI000E`) -> P02M0197; backlight
  (the `video-output` class) and the ambient-light sensor (`ACPI0008`) -> P02M0199; fans (`PNP0C0B`) and the
  thermal zones' cooling half -> P02M0198; UCSI (`PNP0CA0`) -> P02M0202. None of them is built here.
- [ ] WHAT STEP 2 UNBLOCKS: laptops - the classes above in their milestones, and the I2C devices of P02M0195.

## P02M0196c - Step 3: the ACPI half of power management

- [ ] THE PLATFORM HALF OF SLEEP, in the ACPI service; the entry itself - the FACS waking vector, the PM1
  writes, RTC_EN, SLP_TYP|SLP_EN and the return - is P02M0197b's kernel operation, not this milestone's. THIS
  ITEM LANDS TOGETHER WITH P02M0197a AND P02M0197b, since neither side works alone. The service evaluates the
  `\_S0`..`\_S5` packages (and reads whether the FADT says low-power S0 idle) and REGISTERS the sleep-type
  pairs with the kernel once, when it starts, through the registration call P02M0197b introduces and the
  `FirmwareInterpreter` privilege admits - which is also how `\_S5` reaches the kernel's power-off, today's
  fixed ports, PSCI and SBI staying the fallback. THE STEPS AROUND THE ENTRY, in P02M0197a's transaction,
  travel on the control channel ServiceManager holds for the service - the one its sleep notice travels on,
  which the service's manifest row declares - as a `platform-sleep` interface in `liber:process@1` beside
  `system-sleep`, served by the service there: `prepare(state, wake nodes)` evaluates `_PRW` (the wake GPE,
  the deepest state, the power resources, which it turns on) and `_DSW` or `_PSW` on those nodes, sets their
  wake GPEs through step 2's GPE interface, then runs `_PTS` and `_SST`; `wake(state)` runs `_WAK` and
  `_SST`, then disarms what `prepare` armed. The node-scoped channel still refuses `_PTS` and `_WAK`.
- [ ] DEVICE POWER STATES: `_PS0` to `_PS3`, power resources (`_PR0` to `_PR3`, with `_ON`, `_OFF` and `_STA`)
  reference-counted across the devices that share them, and `_SxD`/`_SxW` - run when a device's driver asks
  through its node channel or P02M0197's transaction asks.
- [ ] THE PLATFORM DATA OF PROCESSOR POWER, after the processor handshake and the tables it loads: each
  processor's `_PSS`, `_PCT`, `_PPC`, `_PSD`, `_CPC`, `_CST`, `_LPI`, `_PTC`, `_TSS`, `_TPC` and `_TSD`, and its
  `Notify` values 0x80, 0x81 and 0x82 with the `_OST` acknowledgement, over a contract the service serves to
  P02M0198's ProcessorPowerService through a manifest client role, each processor keyed by namespace path and
  `_UID` (matched to the MADT's processor UID). A thermal zone's cooling methods reach P02M0198 through the
  zone's own driver and its node channel, not from the service. P02M0198 owns the policy and the scheduling.
- [ ] WHAT STEP 3 UNBLOCKS: a laptop that sleeps and wakes, and a CPU that does not run at full clock while
  idle - each finished in its own milestone.

## What this milestone feeds, and the milestones created for it (2026-09-25)

The owner's "where a milestone is missing, create it" was applied to everything the firmware describes and
nothing yet consumes:
  - P02M0197 - sleep and resume: the system-wide suspend transaction, wake sources and their policy; step 3's
    platform half of sleep lands together with its parts a and b.
  - P02M0198 - processor and thermal power management: performance and idle states, cooling, fans.
  - P02M0199 - display brightness: the backlight control, its hotkeys and its tool.
  - P02M0200 - hardware watchdogs: the ACPI WDAT and the watchdog devices QEMU emulates.
  - P02M0201 - out-of-band management: IPMI's transports and a BMC service.
  - P02M0202 - USB Type-C connectors: UCSI, power roles, Power Delivery contracts, alternate modes.
Consumers that already had a milestone keep it: the TPM (P02M0190), the UARTs (P02M0099 with P02M0191),
battery, AC and thermal state (P02M0181 through P02M0099's ACPI item), and HID over I2C (P02M0195). The ACPI
Time and Alarm Device goes whole to P02M0197 - its alarms are wake sources, and on a machine whose FADT says
there is no CMOS clock its `_GRT` is registered behind the kernel's RTC read - because one device has one
owner; by the same rule the lid and the control-method buttons are P02M0197's, the backlight and the
ambient-light sensor P02M0199's, the fans P02M0198's and UCSI P02M0202's, each built on step 2's mechanism
rather than here.

# P02M0195 - I2C: the bus, its controllers and HID over I2C (touchpads and touchscreens)

Status: OPEN. It closes when every item below is done. NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for the plan
only (2026-09-25).

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-09-25): "yes, if we can emulate it". WE CAN, and this is how, checked against the
QEMU on this machine (10.0.11) rather than assumed:
  - QEMU has no HID-over-I2C device and none of the I2C controllers laptops actually carry.
  - It DOES have `vhost-user-i2c-pci` - a virtio-i2c controller whose DEVICE SIDE is an external process
    speaking the vhost-user protocol - and `vhost-user-gpio-pci`, the same for GPIO lines. That is the
    usbredir arrangement again: whatever answers on the other end of the socket IS the device as far as the
    guest can tell. So a harness program can play a HID-over-I2C touchpad at an I2C address AND the GPIO line
    it raises when it has a report, with nothing loaded into the host's kernel. The x86_64, aarch64 and
    riscv64 QEMUs all have both devices.
  - WHAT CANNOT BE EMULATED: the controllers real laptops use - Intel's and AMD's DesignWare-based I2C blocks.
    A driver for them could only be proved on a real laptop, so it is NOT in this milestone; it is a
    follow-up that needs hardware, put to the owner when there is a machine to prove it on.

WHAT A LAPTOP'S FIRMWARE SAYS, AND HOW THE GUEST SAYS THE SAME. An I2C device announces nothing: a laptop's
firmware says, in ACPI's AML, "a HID-over-I2C device (`PNP0C50`) at address 0x2C on controller I2C1, its
descriptor register at 0x20, its interrupt on GPIO pin 17". In the guest the same description is supplied
the standard way - an extra ACPI table QEMU loads with `-acpitable` on x86_64, and on aarch64 and riscv64 the
device tree the firmware is handed - so the VM exercises exactly the path a laptop will, not a shortcut
beside it.

TWO HALVES, AND THE ORDER AROUND P02M0196:
  - P02M0195a, THE BUS: the I2C/SMBus and GPIO contracts, their provider kinds and scoped connections, the
    virtio-i2c and virtio-gpio drivers, and the vhost-user backend with its IOMMU decision. It needs NOTHING
    from P02M0196 - the ACPI service's own connections are built and verified on it in P02M0196b and
    P02M0196d - and lands BEFORE P02M0196d's fixture, which raises its `_AEI` event on this half's GPIO
    line.
  - P02M0195b, HID OVER I2C: the child binding, the HID-over-I2C driver, and InputService following every
    pointer and touch provider. Its ACPI path waits for P02M0196b (namespace devices, `_DSM`, the node-scoped
    ACPI channel) and for P02M0196's companion join, which attaches a namespace or device-tree node to its
    PCI function and resolves `I2cSerialBusV2` and `GpioInt` resources - and, on a tree, an interrupt
    specifier whose parent node is a GPIO controller - to the binding and provider that serve them; its
    device-tree path waits for P02M0196a's tree devices and the same join. InputService's change waits for
    nothing. Nothing in P02M0196 waits for this half.

## P02M0195a - The bus: contracts, scoped connections and the two drivers

- [ ] THE I2C/SMBUS BUS CONTRACT - ONE contract with a FUNCTIONALITY SET, an IDL device contract
  (`i2c-device-proto`) like the other device contracts. The controller DECLARES which transactions it
  serves, and a consumer that needs one the controller lacks refuses it at bind instead of failing later:
    - PLAIN I2C: write, read, and write-then-read as ONE transfer with a repeated start, to one seven-bit
      address, each at most 2048 bytes (`hid-i2c`'s `MAX_TRANSFER`) or the controller's smaller declared
      maximum;
    - SMBUS: quick, send and receive byte, read and write byte and word data, block write, block read
      returning the count THE DEVICE sent (at most 32 bytes, SMBus 2.0's block), I2C block read after a
      one-byte command, and PEC on any of them where the controller declares it.
  The errors are `hid-i2c`'s `BusError` (no device, interrupted, too long, controller) plus `unsupported`
  (outside the declared set) and `pec` (a checksum that does not match). No clock rate, no bus number and no
  pin: a controller that has a clock takes it from its own firmware description. SMBus alert and host
  notify are not in it; its SMBus consumers poll. P02M0201's ICH9 SMBus driver declares the SMBus block
  transactions SSIF uses (block write, the block read with the device's count, PEC) and not plain I2C - the
  other SMBus transactions are declared when a consumer needs one - so a HID device is refused on it and
  SSIF is served by it.
  THE CLIENT SIDE: `hid_i2c::I2cBus` is implemented unchanged over an address-scoped connection (in the
  HID driver, on a thin wrapper around the contract's client, so the protocol library stays free of IPC; an
  address other than the connection's own is refused before it is sent), and the SMBus transactions are a
  second client type, the one P02M0201's SSIF uses.
- [ ] THE GPIO LINE CONTRACT (`gpio-device-proto`), input lines only: one line's level, and its interrupt
  configured as rising, falling or both edges, or high or low level. An event is delivered ONCE and the line
  stays MASKED until the consumer acknowledges it, so a level-triggered line that stays asserted cannot
  storm - it is delivered again after the acknowledgement, and not before. A connection scoped for level
  reads alone - an input `GpioIo`, a `GeneralPurposeIo` field's connection - answers the level and is
  delivered no event.
- [ ] SCOPED CONNECTIONS, AND NEVER THE WHOLE BUS. The provider kinds `i2c-bus` and `gpio-lines` are
  appended to every closed table of kinds (`provider-kind` in `device.lsidl`, `driver_protocol::provider`,
  the system manifest's kind names, DeviceManager's kind mappings) at the next free wire numbers when they
  land, and published like any other, but no role or permission row may name them (the manifest refuses
  one, as it refuses `fixture-control` in a shipping row) and the catalogue's `open` refuses them: the ONLY
  connections are those DeviceManager mints with a SCOPED `CONNECT` - the existing opcode with a scope after
  its token (one seven-bit address; one line with its trigger and polarity; or one line for level reads
  alone), an additive change that leaves the driver protocol's version as it is. The controller driver
  serves that endpoint for that address or line alone and enforces it; it holds each address and each line
  for ONE connection at a time, and answers a second one, or a scope it cannot serve, with a refusal on the
  minted connection itself, so `CONNECT` stays one-way.
  THE OFFERED ENDPOINT SERVES NOTHING. An `OFFER` carries exactly one endpoint, and for these two kinds it
  would be an unscoped connection to the whole controller: the controller driver keeps no end of it and
  reports no `DISCONNECT` for it, and DeviceManager closes it at publication without counting it, so only
  scoped connections count against `consumers`. Each is refunded by `DISCONNECT`, and the rows declare
  `consumers` for the connections a controller serves at once - `i2c-bus` 8, `gpio-lines` 16 (the `_AEI`
  lines and one per child) - within the 32 connections one driver serves. When the controller's binding
  ends, every connection it served closes with its process.
  WHO IS HANDED ONE, and nobody else is:
    - a child binding, with its claim (P02M0195b; P02M0201's SSIF and P02M0202's TCPCI bind the same way);
    - the ACPI service, whose grants are built and verified in P02M0196b and P02M0196d on this half's
      scoped `CONNECT`: each line a GPIO controller's companion node lists in `_AEI` - minted from the list
      the companion join gives DeviceManager when that controller publishes, again after it rebinds, and
      again for each new ACPI-service instance on its "namespace loaded" report, while what runs on an
      event (`_Exx`, `_Lxx`, `_EVT`) is P02M0196b's; the line a `GeneralPurposeIo` field's connection
      names, for level reads alone; and the address a `GenericSerialBus` field's connection names;
    - in the suite, the kernel test that plays DeviceManager's part.
- [ ] A virtio-i2c controller driver (virtio device 34) serving the contract. It declares plain I2C and the
  SMBus transactions that compose from I2C messages - quick (a zero-length request, which every virtio-i2c
  device must offer), byte, byte and word data, block write, I2C block read, PEC computed in software - and
  NOT the block read with the device's count: a virtio-i2c request fixes each read's length when it is queued,
  so the count byte cannot choose it. Write-then-read is two requests grouped with
  `VIRTIO_I2C_FLAGS_FAIL_NEXT`, which the device performs as one transfer with a repeated start. The device
  reports only success or failure, so every failure arrives as `interrupted`. Manifest row:
  `dma = "trusted-untranslated"` like the other virtio rows, `virtio-type = 34`,
  `provides = [{ kind = "i2c-bus", most = 1, consumers = 8 }]`.
- [ ] A virtio-gpio driver (virtio device 41) serving the line contract: the line names read at bind with
  the request queue's `GET_LINE_NAMES`; each line a connection is scoped to set to input with `SET_DIRECTION`
  and read with `GET_VALUE`, and for an interrupt scope armed with `SET_IRQ_TYPE` and given one event-queue
  buffer that is queued again only when the consumer acknowledges - which is the device's own mask; a line
  given back is disarmed (`SET_IRQ_TYPE` none) and deactivated (`SET_DIRECTION` none). Manifest row as
  above, with `virtio-type = 41` and `provides = [{ kind = "gpio-lines", most = 1, consumers = 16 }]`.

## P02M0195a - The fixture and the bus half's verification

- [ ] `vhost-i2c-gpio.py`: ONE harness program serving both vhost-user sockets, in Python like the harness's
  other peers (the memory table's descriptors arrive by `SCM_RIGHTS` and are mapped with `mmap`), started and
  reaped by the harness the way `vsock-echo.py` is. DEVICE MODELS sit behind it - here a register device at
  0x50 (writable registers, SMBus block answers carrying a device count, a PEC byte on request) and the lines
  it can raise; P02M0195b adds the touchpad and the touchscreen, and P02M0202 its TCPCI port controller - and
  a CONTROL SOCKET through which a gate or another fixture raises and lowers a line and picks a script
  (P02M0196d raises its `_AEI` line through it). The two devices are attached only when a run asks with
  `I2C_FIXTURE=bus` or `I2C_FIXTURE=hid`, the pattern `DMA_FIXTURE=1` already follows, at pinned slots, with
  the guest's RAM on a shared `memory-backend-memfd` on that run only.
  THE IOMMU DECISION: THE BACKEND SERVES THE vhost-user IOTLB, AND NO GATE NEEDS `--no-iommu`. Each arm of
  `qemu-run.sh` attaches the two devices with `iommu_platform=on` exactly when it built the virtio-iommu
  (x86_64 from its `iommu` decision, which is its `virtio_plain` string; aarch64 and riscv64 from
  `PORT_IOMMU`), and never with `disable-legacy`, which these modern-only devices do not have. So x86_64's
  `virtio_opts` and the ports' own option strings, which carry `disable-legacy=on`, are not used for them, nor
  is `qemu_virtio_opts` on a port, where it reads an `IOMMU` that `run.sh` exports only for `--no-iommu` and
  would leave `iommu_platform` off on a machine that has the controller. They therefore carry
  `iommu_platform=on` on every ordinary run and guest gate, and not in the untranslated suite. For that the
  backend offers `VIRTIO_F_ACCESS_PLATFORM` together with the reply-ack and backend-request protocol features
  (this QEMU refuses an IOMMU device whose backend lacks them: "IOMMU support requires reply-ack and
  backend-req protocol features."), translates every ring and descriptor address through the IOTLB entries
  QEMU sends (each update and invalidation acknowledged), and on a miss sends the request on the backend
  channel and serves the main channel until the update arrives. `ats` stays off: this QEMU's virtio-iommu
  refuses the device-IOTLB notifier ATS would select ("Virtio-iommu does not support dev-iotlb yet"). CHOSEN
  OVER RUNNING THESE GATES `--no-iommu` because P02M0196d's fixture and the gates of P02M0197 to P02M0202 ride
  this GPIO line, and each of them would otherwise move onto the degraded machine, with its second signed
  image and no network.
- [ ] Kernel hardware tests (tag `i2c`, run with `I2C_FIXTURE=bus`), the test playing DeviceManager's part as
  the existing driver tests do, the offered endpoint closed as DeviceManager closes it: both drivers bound; a
  scoped `CONNECT` for 0x50 writing, reading and write-then-reading its registers; every SMBus transaction
  virtio-i2c declares, with PEC right and wrong, and the block read with the device's count answered
  `unsupported`; an unoccupied address failing; a transfer over the bound refused; 0x51 refused on the 0x50
  connection, a second connection for 0x50 refused, and 0x50 served again after the first disconnects; the
  line names read; a line armed for each trigger, raised through the control socket, delivered once and
  silent until acknowledged, and a level line still asserted delivered again after the acknowledgement; a
  line scoped for level reads answering the level the control socket sets and delivered no event; a second
  connection for a held line refused; and a controller driver killed with its scoped connections closed.
  x86_64 in the suite; aarch64 and riscv64 in their emulated sweep.
- [ ] Gate `i2c-bus`: the same selection under `IOMMU=1`, the variable a gate that owns its profile sets
  (as `qemu-virtio-iommu-x86_64` does), so the IOTLB path is proved on x86_64 on the translated machine that
  the gates of this milestone and of the six that ride its line boot.
- [ ] Host suites: both contracts' codecs; the scope checks; SMBus composed from I2C messages and PEC's CRC-8
  against the SMBus specification's own examples; virtio-i2c request building; virtio-gpio's per-line event
  state; the backend's memory-table and IOTLB translation, misses and invalidations included; the system
  manifest refusing a role or permission row that names either kind; and DeviceManager's admission rule for
  the two kinds, as a pure function in `driver-binding`, where its catalogue arithmetic is host-tested because
  DeviceManager is a `no_std` binary no host drives - the offered endpoint closed and never counted, a scoped
  mint counted against `consumers` and refused past it, `DISCONNECT` refunding one, and `open` refused. WHAT
  A PURE FUNCTION CANNOT SHOW - that DeviceManager really closes the offered endpoint at publication, which
  today's `publish_all` does not do (it keeps the handle in the entry, and `outstanding` counts it) - is
  proved where the tree already proves such effects against real handles: a DEVELOPMENT-BOOT SELF-TEST
  beside `unopened_provider_withdrawal`, publishing an `i2c-bus` and a `gpio-lines` provider through the
  real `Catalogue` with a real channel, and finding the far end of each offered endpoint closed at
  publication and each entry counting no consumer. The `i2c-hid` gate's rebind case proves the rest end to
  end - the controller giving its lines back and DeviceManager minting again after a parent rebinds - and is
  not counted as a proof of the rule. Each test and gate registered in the verification model in the same
  change.

## P02M0195b - HID over I2C

- [ ] THE CHILD BINDING - how a device on another driver's bus gets a driver of its own. The `PNP0C50` or
  `ACPI0C50` node (or the tree's `hid-over-i2c` node) is a platform device of its own, claimed by the
  `i2c_hid` driver: the method-only platform device P02M0196's companion join describes for a node below a
  companion that names no registers of it. Its connections, which the join resolves to the controllers'
  bindings and providers, become REQUIREMENTS of the node beside its entry's `requires`, and each names the
  controller's FUNCTION, so a controller rebound under a new generation satisfies it again. DeviceManager's
  existing machinery does the rest: wait-then-bind keeps the node in `DependencyPending` until both providers
  are published, and the dependency-lost stop (`StopIntent::DependencyLost`, dependents first) stops it when
  EITHER parent's binding ends, spending none of its restart budget; it binds again when both are back. At
  bind DeviceManager mints the address-scoped connection (the `I2cSerialBusV2` address; ten-bit addressing
  refused) and the line-scoped connection (the `GpioInt` line, trigger and polarity) and hands them over as
  `RESOURCE` frames with the claim and P02M0196b's node-scoped ACPI channel; the child's binding ending closes
  both and frees the address and the line. A line the ACPI service holds for `_AEI` is not granted to a child.
  On a device tree the same device is a `hid-over-i2c` node (`reg`, `hid-descr-addr`, `interrupts-extended`)
  under the virtio-i2c function's `virtio,device22` node - the bindings Linux documents - and its interrupt is
  a LINE CONNECTION: an interrupt specifier (`interrupts-extended`, or `interrupts` with `interrupt-parent`)
  whose parent node is both `gpio-controller` and `interrupt-controller` - here the virtio-gpio function's
  `virtio,device29` node - names the line in its first cell and the trigger in its second (the tree's
  interrupt-type flags, whose values virtio-gpio's trigger types share), and the join makes it a connection,
  never a wired interrupt.
  WHY NOT A MODULE INSIDE THE CONTROLLER'S PROCESS, as the USB classes are: the interrupt is a line on
  ANOTHER controller, which a module inside the I2C controller would have to be granted across processes
  anyway; the device has a firmware identity of its own, which a USB interface does not; and SSIF and TCPCI
  have the same shape, so one mechanism serves all three.
- [ ] THE DRIVER, over the protocol layer that ALREADY EXISTS. `src/user/libs/driver/hid-i2c` - the HID
  descriptor and its refusals, the commands, input reports checked in both directions, `Device` - is closed
  under P02M0099 with `host.hid-i2c`'s 13 tests and is not planned again. The driver adds what the library
  deliberately leaves to its caller: the descriptor register from the node's `_DSM` (the HID-over-I2C GUID
  `3CDFF6F7-4267-4555-AD05-B30A3D8938DE`, function 1) or the tree's `hid-descr-addr`; the node's `_PS0`
  before power-on and `_PS3` after sleep where it has them; power on, RESET, and the reset indication
  awaited within a stated bound, retried once and then refused; the report descriptor handed to the generic
  HID parser `drivers::hid`; the report loop - on the line's event, read input reports until the line is
  quiet, then acknowledge - with an interrupt storm bounded (a line asserted with no report behind it
  resets the device once, and then fails the binding); and SET_POWER sleep on `STOP`. It links `hid-i2c`
  statically, so the library stays a source row; that row's manifest comment and the library's module
  comment are corrected in the same change, to name this consumer and to say that SSIF uses the contract's
  SMBus transactions and not `I2cBus`.
- [ ] WHAT A BINDING PUBLISHES, AND THE MODE A TOUCHPAD IS LEFT IN. The driver sends NO Input Mode or Device
  Mode feature report, so a precision touchpad stays in the mouse mode it powers on in, and returns to after
  every RESET, and reports through its mouse collection alone. CHOSEN over setting Input Mode 3, which moves
  it to its touch pad collection, because InputService derives no pointer from contacts: a switched touchpad
  would deliver contacts and move no cursor. The report descriptor's APPLICATION COLLECTIONS then decide: a
  Mouse or Pointer collection publishes `pointer`; a Touch Screen collection (digitizer usage 0x04), through
  which a touchscreen reports its contacts in the mode it starts in, publishes `touch`; a Touch Pad
  collection (0x05), silent without that switch, and any other collection publish nothing. Each input
  report is decoded by the collection its report id belongs to (`hid::fields` records it), so a
  touchscreen's contact axes are never folded into a pointer. The vocabulary is the one P02M0099's HID
  expansion already delivers for USB, so InputService reads these as it reads xHCI's once it follows them
  (below). Manifest row: `transport = "platform"`, matching `PNP0C50` and `ACPI0C50` as `_HID` or `_CID`
  and the compatible `hid-over-i2c`, `dma = "none"`, providing `pointer` and `touch` (`most = 1` each).
- [ ] INPUTSERVICE FOLLOWS EVERY POINTER AND TOUCH PROVIDER. Today it opens the first live provider of each
  kind in the snapshot its subscription holds at its start and closes the subscription, so this binding's
  `pointer` - a second one beside the `pointer` xHCI always publishes - would never be opened, nor anything
  published after a rebind. It KEEPS its `pointer` and `touch` subscriptions open, as MidiService keeps its
  own (two of the catalogue's 32 subscriber places, held for its life), and follows them: every live
  `pointer` provider up to FOUR is attached, their events merged into the one cursor as today's two slots
  are; ONE `touch` surface is attached at a time, because a contact event names no surface and each surface
  normalises its axes across itself, so two surfaces in one stream would be contacts in two coordinate
  spaces under colliding identities. A live provider past its kind's bound waits, logged once, and is
  attached when one of its kind is detached. A provider is detached on its withdrawal, or when its
  connection closes, and is not opened again until it is published again; a detached surface's contacts
  still down are lifted, as focus loss lifts them; and a re-publication is attached like any other. The
  `input` kind keeps its bootstrap-only discovery.

## P02M0195b - The fixture and the gates

- [ ] The backend's HID models (`I2C_FIXTURE=hid`), each with its own GPIO line, signalled level and active
  low, the reset indication, and a malformed descriptor on request:
    - A PRECISION TOUCHPAD at 0x2C, descriptor register 0x20: mouse, touch pad and configuration
      collections; in mouse mode at power-on and after every RESET, moved to its touch pad collection by
      Input Mode 3 and back by 0; its script - moves and a click - reported through the ONE collection its
      mode selects, so a driver that switched it would see no moves.
    - A TOUCHSCREEN at 0x10, descriptor register 0x01: a Touch Screen collection reporting its script - a
      two-finger contact and the lift - with no mode switch.
- [ ] The SSDT, with NO `iasl`: bytes from the in-tree AML emitter P02M0196 builds for its own fixtures, to
  which this milestone adds, with host tests against the specification's encodings, whatever of
  `I2cSerialBusV2`, `GpioInt` and a `_DSM` returning an integer it lacks. It describes the two functions at
  their pinned slots under `\_SB.PCI0` - by `Scope` into the node QEMU's own DSDT has for a present function
  where there is one, since two nodes with one `_ADR` would give the companion join two candidates - and,
  below the I2C controller's node, the two devices: each with a vendor `_HID` and `_CID` `PNP0C50` (the
  form laptops use, so the `_CID` match is what is proved), its `I2cSerialBusV2` address (0x2C, 0x10), a
  `GpioInt` (level, active low) naming its line on the GPIO controller's node, and a `_DSM` answering its
  descriptor register (0x20, 0x01). Loaded with `-acpitable`.
- [ ] The device tree, with NO `dtc`: the harness's pure-Python tree editor (`dma-mode-record.py`'s)
  extended to add the two PCI function nodes under the host bridge (`reg` from the pinned slots), their
  `virtio,device22` and `virtio,device29` children, and the two `hid-over-i2c` nodes (`reg` 0x2C and 0x10,
  `hid-descr-addr` 0x20 and 0x01) with `interrupts-extended` into the GPIO node. The ports boot through UEFI
  and the loader passes on the tree the firmware publishes, so the tree is dumped, edited and handed to QEMU
  with `-dtb` - the round trip the harness's independent-producer fixture already makes on both ports. The
  dump is taken without the two vhost-user devices, which add no node to QEMU's generated tree.
- [ ] Gate `i2c-hid`, a guest gate on the DEFAULT translated machine and the development image: both devices
  found through the firmware's description and bound as children, the touchpad's binding publishing
  `pointer` alone and the touchscreen's `touch` alone; their descriptors read; the touchpad's moves and
  click arriving in order as pointer events, and the touchscreen's two-finger contact and lift as contacts,
  at a development probe that is a live pointer client of InputService (as the Bluetooth gate's `btcheck`
  is) and holds a surface with input focus, whose proof opens the contact stream; the malformed descriptor
  refused, the binding failed and nothing published; each device surviving the RESET its driver makes when
  the control socket holds its line asserted with no report behind it, after which the moves still arrive
  as pointer events and the contact as contacts; and, beside the xHCI tablet, the virtio-i2c binding
  disabled and enabled through the device policy's verbs: both HID bindings stopped as lost dependencies
  with their connections closed and their providers detached while the tablet's stays attached, then bound
  again - on the GPIO controller that stayed bound, whose lines the first connections gave back - and their
  re-publications attached, the moves and the contact arriving again. x86_64 through the SSDT; aarch64 and
  riscv64 through the tree, in their emulated sweep.
- [ ] A kernel services test of InputService's following, tagged as the existing touch-surface test is,
  standing in for the drivers and the catalogue as every InputService test does, with a catalogue helper that
  keeps both subscriptions open and announces publications and withdrawals on them later: two pointer
  providers at start and a third published later, all moving the cursor; one withdrawn - its connection
  closed, the others still delivering - and its re-publication attached; a fifth waiting while four are
  attached and attached when one is withdrawn; a second surface waiting while the first is attached, the first
  withdrawn with a finger down - that contact lifted - and the second then attached and delivering. Every
  existing InputService test passes with the subscriptions kept open.
- [ ] Host suites for the driver's pure parts: `_CRS` and tree resources turned into scopes (a `GpioInt`'s
  trigger and polarity, and a tree specifier's trigger cell, into one line trigger), the reset bound, the
  storm rule, and the application collections turned into publications - a precision touchpad's descriptor
  to `pointer` alone, a touchscreen's to `touch` alone, each report decoded by the collection its id belongs
  to; the emitter's and the tree editor's additions. The protocol's suite is `host.hid-i2c` as it stands,
  extended only where the driver changes the library. Each registered in the verification model.

EXCLUDES: the DesignWare controllers of real laptops (see above), and with them a HID device whose interrupt
is a wired interrupt instead of a GPIO line, which only such a laptop can show; the Input Mode and Device Mode
switches, and with them a precision touchpad's touch pad collection (its multi-finger contacts and gestures)
until something turns contacts into pointer motion, and a touchscreen that reports contacts only after such a
switch; several touch surfaces in one contact stream, which needs a surface identity the contact event lacks;
I2C devices other than HID - sensors, EEPROMs, battery gauges - which each get an item when something consumes
them (SSIF is P02M0201's and TCPCI P02M0202's, both over this contract and this child binding); SMBus alert
and host notify (the consumers poll); GPIO output lines - P02M0196b refuses an AML `GeneralPurposeIo` write
and connects an input `GpioIo` alone - and with them reset lines a device tree names (`reset-gpios`); ten-bit
addresses and a second bus master.

# P02M0195 - I2C: the bus, its controllers and HID over I2C (touchpads and touchscreens)

Status: OPEN. It closes when every item below is done. NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for the plan
only (2026-09-25).

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-09-25): "yes, if we can emulate it". WE CAN, and this is how, checked against the
QEMU on this machine (10.0.11) rather than assumed:
  - QEMU has no HID-over-I2C device and none of the I2C controllers laptops actually carry.
  - It DOES have `vhost-user-i2c-pci` - a virtio-i2c controller whose DEVICE SIDE is an external process
    speaking the vhost-user protocol - and `vhost-user-gpio-pci`, the same for GPIO lines. That is the
    usbredir arrangement again: whatever answers on the other end of the socket IS the device as far as the
    guest can tell. So a harness program can play a HID-over-I2C touchpad at an I2C address AND the GPIO line
    it raises when it has a report, with nothing loaded into the host's kernel. The x86_64, aarch64 and
    riscv64 QEMUs all have both devices.
  - WHAT CANNOT BE EMULATED: the controllers real laptops use - Intel's and AMD's DesignWare-based I2C blocks.
    A driver for them could only be proved on a real laptop, so it is NOT in this milestone; it is a
    follow-up that needs hardware, put to the owner when there is a machine to prove it on.

WHAT A LAPTOP'S FIRMWARE SAYS, AND HOW THE GUEST SAYS THE SAME. An I2C device announces nothing: a laptop's
firmware says, in ACPI's AML, "a HID-over-I2C device (`PNP0C50`) at address 0x2C on controller I2C1, its
descriptor register at 0x20, its interrupt on GPIO pin 17". In the guest the same description is supplied
the standard way - an extra ACPI table QEMU loads with `-acpitable` on x86_64, and on aarch64 and riscv64 the
device tree the firmware is handed - so the VM exercises exactly the path a laptop will, not a shortcut
beside it.

TWO HALVES, AND THE ORDER AROUND P02M0196:
  - P02M0195a, THE BUS: the I2C/SMBus and GPIO contracts, their provider kinds and scoped connections, the
    virtio-i2c and virtio-gpio drivers, and the vhost-user backend with its IOMMU decision. It needs NOTHING
    from P02M0196 and lands BEFORE P02M0196d's fixture, which raises its `_AEI` event on this half's GPIO
    line.
  - P02M0195b, HID OVER I2C: the child binding and the HID-over-I2C driver. Its ACPI path waits for P02M0196b
    (namespace devices, `_DSM`, the node-scoped ACPI channel) and for P02M0196's companion join, which
    attaches a namespace or device-tree node to its PCI function and resolves `I2cSerialBusV2`, `GpioInt`
    and GPIO phandles to the binding and provider that serve them; its device-tree path waits for P02M0196a's
    tree devices and the same join. Nothing in P02M0196 waits for this half.

## P02M0195a - The bus: contracts, scoped connections and the two drivers

- [ ] THE I2C/SMBUS BUS CONTRACT - ONE contract with a FUNCTIONALITY SET, an IDL device contract
  (`i2c-device-proto`) like the other device contracts. The controller DECLARES which transactions it
  serves, and a consumer that needs one the controller lacks refuses it at bind instead of failing later:
    - PLAIN I2C: write, read, and write-then-read as ONE transfer with a repeated start, to one seven-bit
      address, each at most 2048 bytes (`hid-i2c`'s `MAX_TRANSFER`) or the controller's smaller declared
      maximum;
    - SMBUS: quick, send and receive byte, read and write byte and word data, block write, block read
      returning the count THE DEVICE sent (at most 32 bytes, SMBus 2.0's block), I2C block read after a
      one-byte command, and PEC on any of them where the controller declares it.
  The errors are `hid-i2c`'s `BusError` (no device, interrupted, too long, controller) plus `unsupported`
  (outside the declared set) and `pec` (a checksum that does not match). No clock rate, no bus number and no
  pin: a controller that has a clock takes it from its own firmware description. SMBus alert and host
  notify are not in it; its SMBus consumers poll. P02M0201's ICH9 SMBus driver declares the SMBus block
  transactions SSIF uses (block write, the block read with the device's count, PEC) and not plain I2C - the
  other SMBus transactions are declared when a consumer needs one - so a HID device is refused on it and
  SSIF is served by it.
  THE CLIENT SIDE: `hid_i2c::I2cBus` is implemented unchanged over an address-scoped connection (in the
  HID driver, on a thin wrapper around the contract's client, so the protocol library stays free of IPC; an
  address other than the connection's own is refused before it is sent), and the SMBus transactions are a
  second client type, the one P02M0201's SSIF uses.
- [ ] THE GPIO LINE CONTRACT (`gpio-device-proto`), input lines only: one line's level, and its interrupt
  configured as rising, falling or both edges, or high or low level. An event is delivered ONCE and the line
  stays MASKED until the consumer acknowledges it, so a level-triggered line that stays asserted cannot
  storm - it is delivered again after the acknowledgement, and not before.
- [ ] SCOPED CONNECTIONS, AND NEVER THE WHOLE BUS. The provider kinds `i2c-bus` and `gpio-lines` are
  published like any other, but no role or permission row may name them (the manifest refuses one, as it
  refuses `fixture-control` in a shipping row) and the catalogue's `open` refuses them: the ONLY connections
  are those DeviceManager mints with a SCOPED `CONNECT` - the existing opcode with a scope after its token
  (one seven-bit address, or one line with its trigger and polarity), an additive change that leaves the
  driver protocol's version as it is. The controller driver serves that endpoint for that address or line
  alone and enforces it; it holds each address and each line for ONE connection at a time, and answers a
  second one, or a scope it cannot serve, with a refusal on the minted connection itself, so `CONNECT` stays
  one-way. A scoped connection counts against the kind's declared `consumers` and is refunded by
  `DISCONNECT`; when the controller's binding ends, every connection it served closes with its process.
  WHO IS HANDED ONE, and nobody else is: a child binding, with its claim (P02M0195b; P02M0201's SSIF and
  P02M0202's TCPCI bind the same way); the ACPI service, for each line a GPIO controller's companion node
  lists in `_AEI` - DeviceManager mints them when that controller publishes, from the list the companion
  join gives it, and again after a rebind, while what runs on an event (`_Exx`, `_Lxx`, `_EVT`) is
  P02M0196b's - and for the address a `GenericSerialBus` operation region's connection names, if P02M0196b's
  interpreter serves one; and, in the suite, the kernel test that plays DeviceManager's part.
- [ ] A virtio-i2c controller driver (virtio device 34) serving the contract. It declares plain I2C and the
  SMBus transactions that compose from I2C messages - quick (on a device offering zero-length requests),
  byte, byte and word data, block write, I2C block read, PEC computed in software - and NOT the block read
  with the device's count: a virtio-i2c request fixes each read's length when it is queued, so the count
  byte cannot choose it. Write-then-read is two requests grouped with `VIRTIO_I2C_FLAGS_FAIL_NEXT`, which
  the device performs as one transfer with a repeated start. The device reports only success or failure,
  so every failure arrives as `interrupted`. Manifest row: `dma = "trusted-untranslated"` like the other
  virtio rows, `virtio-type = 34`, `provides = [{ kind = "i2c-bus", most = 1 }]`.
- [ ] A virtio-gpio driver (virtio device 41) serving the line contract: the line names read at bind with
  the request queue's `GET_NAMES`, `SET_IRQ_TYPE` for each armed line, and one event-queue buffer per armed
  line that is queued again only when the consumer acknowledges - which is the device's own mask. Manifest
  row as above, with `virtio-type = 41` and `gpio-lines`.

## P02M0195a - The fixture and the bus half's verification

- [ ] `vhost-i2c-gpio.py`: ONE harness program serving both vhost-user sockets, in Python like the harness's
  other peers (the memory table's descriptors arrive by `SCM_RIGHTS` and are mapped with `mmap`), started and
  reaped by the harness the way `vsock-echo.py` is. DEVICE MODELS sit behind it - here a register device at
  0x50 (writable registers, SMBus block answers carrying a device count, a PEC byte on request) and the lines
  it can raise; P02M0195b adds the touchpad and P02M0202 its TCPCI port controller - and a CONTROL SOCKET
  through which a gate or another fixture raises and lowers a line and picks a script (P02M0196d raises its
  `_AEI` line through it). The two devices are attached only when a run asks with `I2C_FIXTURE=bus` or
  `I2C_FIXTURE=hid`, the pattern `DMA_FIXTURE=1` already follows, at pinned slots, with the guest's RAM on a
  shared `memory-backend-memfd` on that run only.
  THE IOMMU DECISION: THE BACKEND SERVES THE vhost-user IOTLB, AND NO GATE NEEDS `--no-iommu`. The devices
  take their options from `qemu_virtio_opts` like every other endpoint, so they carry `iommu_platform=on`
  exactly when the machine has the virtio-iommu - every ordinary run and guest gate - and not in the
  untranslated suite. For that the backend offers `VIRTIO_F_ACCESS_PLATFORM` together with the reply-ack and
  backend-request protocol features (this QEMU refuses an IOMMU device whose backend lacks them: "IOMMU
  support requires reply-ack and backend-req protocol features."), translates every ring and descriptor
  address through the IOTLB entries QEMU sends (each update and invalidation acknowledged), and on a miss
  sends the request on the backend channel and serves the main channel until the update arrives. `ats`
  stays off: this QEMU's virtio-iommu refuses the device-IOTLB notifier ATS would select ("Virtio-iommu does
  not support dev-iotlb yet"). CHOSEN OVER RUNNING THESE GATES `--no-iommu` because P02M0196d's fixture and
  the gates of P02M0197 to P02M0202 ride this GPIO line, and each of them would otherwise move onto the
  degraded machine, with its second signed image and no network.
- [ ] Kernel hardware tests (tag `i2c`, run with `I2C_FIXTURE=bus`), the test playing DeviceManager's part as
  the existing driver tests do: both drivers bound; a scoped `CONNECT` for 0x50 writing, reading and
  write-then-reading its registers; every SMBus transaction virtio-i2c declares, with PEC right and wrong,
  and the block read with the device's count answered `unsupported`; an unoccupied address failing; a
  transfer over the bound refused; 0x51 refused on the 0x50 connection, a second connection for 0x50
  refused, and 0x50 served again after the first disconnects; the line names read; a line armed for each
  trigger, raised through the control socket, delivered once and silent until acknowledged, and a level
  line still asserted delivered again after the acknowledgement; and a controller driver killed with its
  scoped connections closed. x86_64 in the suite; aarch64 and riscv64 in their emulated sweep.
- [ ] Gate `i2c-bus`: the same selection under `IOMMU=1`, the variable a gate that owns its profile sets
  (as `qemu-virtio-iommu-x86_64` does), so the IOTLB path is proved on x86_64 on the translated machine that
  the gates of this milestone and of the six that ride its line boot.
- [ ] Host suites: both contracts' codecs; the scope checks; SMBus composed from I2C messages and PEC's CRC-8
  against the SMBus specification's own examples; virtio-i2c request building; virtio-gpio's per-line event
  state; the backend's memory-table and IOTLB translation, misses and invalidations included. Each test and
  gate registered in the verification model in the same change.

## P02M0195b - HID over I2C

- [ ] THE CHILD BINDING - how a device on another driver's bus gets a driver of its own. The `PNP0C50` node
  (or the tree's `hid-over-i2c` node) is a platform device of its own, claimed by the `i2c_hid` driver: the
  method-only platform device P02M0196's companion join describes for a node below a companion that names
  no registers of it. Its connections, which the join resolves to the controllers' bindings and providers,
  become REQUIREMENTS of the node beside its entry's `requires`, and each names the controller's FUNCTION,
  so a controller rebound under a new generation satisfies it again. DeviceManager's existing machinery
  does the rest: wait-then-bind keeps the node in `DependencyPending` until both providers are published,
  and the dependency-lost stop (`StopIntent::DependencyLost`, dependents first) stops it when EITHER
  parent's binding ends, spending none of its restart budget; it binds again when both are back. At bind
  DeviceManager mints the address-scoped connection (the `I2cSerialBusV2` address; ten-bit addressing
  refused) and the line-scoped connection (the `GpioInt` line, trigger and polarity) and hands them over as
  `RESOURCE` frames with the claim and P02M0196b's node-scoped ACPI channel; the child's binding ending
  closes both and frees the address and the line. A line the ACPI service holds for `_AEI` is not granted
  to a child. On a device tree the same device is a `hid-over-i2c` node (`reg`, `hid-descr-addr`,
  `interrupts-extended`) under the virtio-i2c function's `virtio,device22` node, its interrupt in the
  virtio-gpio function's `virtio,device29` node - the bindings Linux documents.
  WHY NOT A MODULE INSIDE THE CONTROLLER'S PROCESS, as the USB classes are: the interrupt is a line on
  ANOTHER controller, which a module inside the I2C controller would have to be granted across processes
  anyway; the device has a firmware identity of its own, which a USB interface does not; and SSIF and TCPCI
  have the same shape, so one mechanism serves all three.
- [ ] THE DRIVER, over the protocol layer that ALREADY EXISTS. `src/user/libs/driver/hid-i2c` - the HID
  descriptor and its refusals, the commands, input reports checked in both directions, `Device` - is closed
  under P02M0099 with `host.hid-i2c`'s 13 tests and is not planned again. The driver adds what the library
  deliberately leaves to its caller: the descriptor register from the node's `_DSM` (the HID-over-I2C GUID
  `3CDFF6F7-4267-4555-AD05-B30A3D8938DE`, function 1) or the tree's `hid-descr-addr`; the node's `_PS0`
  before power-on and `_PS3` after sleep where it has them; power on, RESET, and the reset indication
  awaited within a stated bound, retried once and then refused; the report descriptor handed to the generic
  HID parser `drivers::hid`; the report loop - on the line's event, read input reports until the line is
  quiet, then acknowledge - with an interrupt storm bounded (a line asserted with no report behind it
  resets the device once, and then fails the binding); and SET_POWER sleep on `STOP`. It links `hid-i2c`
  statically, so the library stays a source row; that row's manifest comment and the library's module
  comment are corrected in the same change, to name this consumer and to say that SSIF uses the contract's
  SMBus transactions and not `I2cBus`.
- [ ] Its reports published by THIS binding as `pointer` (a touchpad) and `touch` (a touchpad's multi-touch
  collection, a touchscreen) - the vocabulary P02M0099's HID expansion already delivers for USB - so
  InputService consumes them exactly as it consumes xHCI's.

## P02M0195b - The fixture and the gates

- [ ] The backend's touchpad model at 0x2C (`I2C_FIXTURE=hid`): a precision touchpad's HID and report
  descriptors, a report script - moves, a two-finger contact, a click - signalled on its GPIO line (level,
  active low), the reset indication, and a malformed descriptor on request.
- [ ] The SSDT, with NO `iasl`: bytes from the in-tree AML emitter P02M0196 builds for its own fixtures, to
  which this milestone adds, with host tests against the specification's encodings, whatever of
  `I2cSerialBusV2`, `GpioInt` and a `_DSM` returning an integer it lacks. It describes the two functions at
  their pinned slots under `\_SB.PCI0` - by `Scope` into the node QEMU's own DSDT has for a present function
  where there is one, since two nodes with one `_ADR` would give the companion join two candidates - and,
  below the I2C controller's node, the touchpad: `_HID` a vendor id with `_CID` `PNP0C50` (the form laptops
  use, so the `_CID` match is what is proved), `I2cSerialBusV2` at 0x2C, `GpioInt` (level, active low)
  naming the GPIO controller's node, and the `_DSM` answering 0x20. Loaded with `-acpitable`.
- [ ] The device tree, with NO `dtc`: the harness's pure-Python tree editor (`dma-mode-record.py`'s)
  extended to add the two PCI function nodes under the host bridge (`reg` from the pinned slots), their
  `virtio,device22` and `virtio,device29` children and the touchpad node with its phandle reference. The
  ports boot through UEFI and the loader passes on the tree the firmware publishes, so the tree is dumped,
  edited and handed to QEMU with `-dtb` - the round trip the harness's independent-producer fixture already
  makes on both ports. The dump is taken without the two vhost-user devices, which add no node to QEMU's
  generated tree.
- [ ] Gate `i2c-hid`, a guest gate on the DEFAULT translated machine and the development image: the device
  found through the firmware's description and bound as a child; its descriptors read; the scripted moves,
  contact and click arriving in order at a development probe that is a live pointer and contact client of
  InputService (as the Bluetooth gate's `btcheck` is a live pointer client); the malformed descriptor
  refused, the binding failed and nothing published; the device surviving a RESET; and the virtio-i2c
  binding disabled and enabled through the device policy's verbs, the HID binding stopped as a lost
  dependency with both connections closed, then bound again with the moves arriving again. x86_64 through
  the SSDT; aarch64 and riscv64 through the tree, in their emulated sweep.
- [ ] Host suites for the driver's pure parts: `_CRS` and tree resources turned into scopes, the reset bound,
  the storm rule; the emitter's and the tree editor's additions. The protocol's suite is `host.hid-i2c` as it
  stands, extended only where the driver changes the library. Each registered in the verification model.

EXCLUDES: the DesignWare controllers of real laptops (see above), and with them a HID device whose interrupt is
a wired interrupt instead of a GPIO line, which only such a laptop can show; I2C devices other than HID -
sensors, EEPROMs, battery gauges - which each get an item when something consumes them (SSIF is P02M0201's and
TCPCI P02M0202's, both over this contract and this child binding); SMBus alert and host notify (the consumers
poll); GPIO output lines, and with them reset lines a device tree names (`reset-gpios`); ten-bit addresses and
a second bus master.

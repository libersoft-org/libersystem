# P02M0195 - I2C: the bus, its controllers and HID over I2C (touchpads and touchscreens)

Status: OPEN. It closes when every item below is done. NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for the plan
only (2026-09-25).

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-09-25): "yes, if we can emulate it". WE CAN, and this is how, checked against the
QEMU on this machine (10.0.11) rather than assumed:
  - QEMU has no HID-over-I2C device and none of the I2C controllers laptops actually carry.
  - It DOES have `vhost-user-i2c-pci` - a virtio-i2c controller whose DEVICE SIDE is an external process
    speaking the vhost-user protocol - and `vhost-user-gpio-pci`, the same for GPIO lines. That is the
    usbredir arrangement again: whatever answers on the other end of the socket IS the device as far as the
    guest can tell. So a harness program can play a HID-over-I2C touchpad at an I2C address AND the GPIO line
    it raises when it has a report, with nothing loaded into the host's kernel.
  - WHAT CANNOT BE EMULATED: the controllers real laptops use - Intel's and AMD's DesignWare-based I2C blocks.
    A driver for them could only be proved on a real laptop, so it is NOT in this milestone; it is a
    follow-up that needs hardware, put to the owner when there is a machine to prove it on.

WHY IT DEPENDS ON THE FIRMWARE-DESCRIBED DEVICES. An I2C device announces nothing: a laptop's firmware says,
in ACPI's AML, "a HID-over-I2C device (`PNP0C50`) at address 0x2C on controller I2C1, its descriptor register
at 0x20, its interrupt on GPIO pin 17". Reading that needs P02M0196's AML half. In the guest the same
description is supplied the standard way - an extra ACPI table QEMU loads with `-acpitable` - so the VM
exercises exactly the path a laptop will, not a shortcut beside it.

## P02M0195 - The bus

- [ ] A bounded I2C bus contract: a controller provider offers transfers - write, read, and write-then-read
  as one transaction with a repeated start - to one address at a time, with the bus speed and the largest
  transfer it supports; a consumer holds ONE device address on ONE bus, granted through the claim of the
  firmware-described device, never the whole bus.
- [ ] A virtio-i2c controller driver (virtio device 34) serving that contract - the controller the guest can
  have.
- [ ] A GPIO line contract of the same shape - one line, its level, an edge or level interrupt - and a
  virtio-gpio driver serving it, because a HID-over-I2C device says "I have a report" on a GPIO line and the
  host has to hear it.

## P02M0195 - HID over I2C

- [ ] The HID-over-I2C protocol (Microsoft's specification, version 1.0): the HID descriptor read from the
  register the firmware names, the report descriptor, input reports on the interrupt line, output and
  feature reports, reset and power commands, and every length checked against the descriptor before a byte
  of a report is believed.
- [ ] Its reports into the shared report-descriptor parser and on to InputService: a touchpad as the pointer
  it is and, with its multi-touch collection, as contacts; a touchscreen as contacts - the vocabulary
  P02M0099's HID expansion already delivers for USB.

## P02M0195 - The fixture and the gates

- [ ] `vhost-i2c-hid.py` (or a small program, if the vhost-user protocol's shared memory is better served by
  one): a vhost-user backend playing a precision touchpad at 0x2C and its GPIO interrupt, with a report
  script - moves, a two-finger contact, a click - and a malformed descriptor on request; the guest RAM shared
  with it the way vhost-user requires, on this run's QEMU only.
- [ ] An SSDT fixture describing the device the way a laptop's firmware does, loaded with `-acpitable`.
- [ ] A kernel hardware test: the device found through ACPI, its descriptors read, the scripted moves and
  contacts arriving as pointer and contact events in order, the malformed descriptor refused, and the device
  surviving a reset command.
- [ ] Host suites for the protocol's pure layer against the specification's shapes and hostile variants; the
  cross-builds (aarch64's `virt` machine takes the same two vhost-user devices, with a device-tree overlay in
  place of the SSDT).

EXCLUDES: the DesignWare controllers of real laptops (see above); I2C devices other than HID - sensors,
EEPROMs, battery gauges - which each get an item when something consumes them; SMBus's host-notify and alert
protocols.

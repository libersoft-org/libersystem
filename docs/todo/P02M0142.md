# P02M0142 - One boot tail for three architectures, and a HAL surface that means what it says

Status: OPEN. PORTABILITY. NOT A PHASE-2 COMPLETION GATE.

## Goal

Make the portable half of bringing userspace up portable in fact and not only in intent, and shrink
the declared hardware-abstraction surface to what something actually calls.

This is not a tidy-up. The three architectures diverge at exactly one point that carries policy
rather than machinery - the moment the machine is up and the kernel decides what to do about
userspace - and the divergence means the ownership and recovery rules P02M0141 built exist on ONE
target of three.

## What is there now

Measured on 2026-08-22: the line and stub counts by reading the tree, the test rows by DIFFING THE
THREE RUN LOGS against each other. The first draft of this table counted `cfg(target_arch)`
attributes instead and got the last row wrong by a factor of two - a test written once per
architecture under one shared id carries three of those attributes and is not locked to anything.

|  | x86_64 | aarch64 | riscv64 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lines under `arch/` | 4572 | 5001 | 3917 |
| `todo!()` entries in the HAL contract | 0 | 16 | 11 |
| tests in the suite | 305 | 292 | 290 |
| tests that run ONLY here | 19 | 5 | 4 |

WHAT IS ALREADY SHARED IS MORE THAN IT LOOKS. `arch/common` is 2345 lines - PCI enumeration, the
MSI-X slot registry, boot memory, fw_cfg, the portable clock, the paging flags - and each backend
wires its few real primitives into it. The loader splits the same way, a per-architecture prologue
around a shared body.

USERSPACE IS NOT ARCHITECTURE-NEUTRAL, WHICH THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS SECTION CLAIMED IT WAS. Around
twenty-six files outside the kernel crate mention `target_arch`, and eleven of them are the
`*-client-provider` crates, each carrying a per-architecture forwarding trampoline. That is a real
per-target surface; it is simply not one this milestone touches.

So the split is broadly right, and this milestone does not reopen it.

**THE THREE BOOTS END DIFFERENTLY, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM ENDS CORRECTLY.** x86_64 arrives through
`kmain` and calls `boot_userspace_with_recovery()`. aarch64 and riscv64 enter at their own
`aarch64_main` / `riscv64_main` and hand-roll a settle loop - run the scheduler, drain the boot
reports, wait for the shell - then call `console_shell_loop()` directly.

What the two hand-rolled tails therefore do not have:

- `supervise()`, so a SystemManager that faults during boot is never restarted,
- the crash-notify channel, so nothing observes a fault at all,
- the ended-is-as-gone-as-faulted check P02M0141 added,
- the rule that the retry ladder stops once a control-plane branch exists,
- the scheduler idle hook, and with it `SYSTEM_IS_UP` and `resident_manager_lost()` - so a
  SystemManager lost AFTER boot leaves the control plane ownerless and nothing notices.

And the two tails are otherwise the same text. They differ in the prefix they log with and in the
settle cap (400 rounds against 4000). That is not an architecture difference; it is a copy that
drifted.

**THE MACHINERY IS ALREADY PORTABLE. WHAT IS MISSING IS THE WIRING.** `supervise`, `crash_seen`,
`control_plane_lost` and the branch count carry no `cfg` at all: they compile on every target, and
`kernel.boot.system_manager_recovery_escalates_after_repeated_crashes` runs in all three suites
today. Nothing has to be written twice or ported. Two boot paths simply do not call what is sitting
there compiled beside them.

WHY NO TEST NOTICED, EXACTLY. `boot_userspace_with_recovery` is `#[cfg(not(test))]`, and so are the
two hand-rolled tails. No test on ANY architecture executes a boot tail - what the suites cover is
the pieces, called directly. So this is not a hole a missing assertion left; it is a hole in a place
the harness structurally cannot reach, which is the kind that stays open longest.

`arch/mod.rs` predicted it in writing: *"nothing type-checks the per-arch boot sequences against
each other, so a step added to one is silently absent from the others until a test notices."* A step
was added to one. No test noticed.

## Scope

IN: the tail of the boot sequence - the part that runs once the machine is up - the honesty of the
HAL contract's own description, and two properties asserted on one target instead of three.

OUT, and deliberately: paging, exceptions and interrupt controllers, the per-CPU register, context
switching, secondary-core bring-up, the loader's per-architecture prologue, and polled versus
interrupt-driven serial input. Those are the machine. A trait over them buys indirection and nothing
else, and this milestone is not an excuse to write one.

ALSO OUT: the machine-specific PROLOGUE of each boot. Bringing a machine up is the least portable
thing a kernel does and the hand-off genuinely differs: the UEFI loader builds a `BootInfo` for all
three, but what that structure carries and what state the firmware leaves behind is not the same,
and aarch64 and riscv64 also support a direct device-tree entry. What moves is the end, not the
beginning.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - One boot tail, called by all three entries.**
  - THE SPLIT HAS TO BE DRAWN PROPERLY, and drawing it wrong is how this item fails. Today's
    `boot_userspace_with_recovery` is NOT portable: it registers the COM1 receive handler, routes
    ISA IRQ 4 through the IOAPIC and enables the UART receive interrupt. `arch::ioapic::route` is a
    `todo!()` on both other backends, so calling today's function from them would panic on the way
    up rather than unify anything.
  - What each architecture KEEPS in its own prologue: firmware hand-off, memory, per-CPU, interrupt
    controller, timer, device init, and its own console arrangement - the interrupt-driven serial on
    x86_64, polling on the other two.
  - What MOVES into the one shared tail: spawn SystemManager under the recovery ladder, drain the
    boot reports, wait for the system to be ready, register the idle hook that carries the
    resident-manager watch, run the shell, and escalate when the ladder runs out.
  - THE READINESS WAIT EXISTS ON ALL THREE AND IS IN THE WRONG PLACE ON ALL THREE. An earlier draft
    said x86_64 waits for nothing; it does wait, inside `console_shell_loop`, for up to three
    hundred rounds - but that is AFTER `supervise` has already returned success and after
    `SYSTEM_IS_UP` is set. aarch64 and riscv64 wait in their own tails, equally outside the attempt.
  - SO THE WAIT MOVES INSIDE THE ATTEMPT. A round of the recovery ladder is not "the manager did not
    fault in one pass of the scheduler"; it is "the shell is listening". That makes the three
    outcomes distinct and each one honest:
    - the shell attaches - the attempt succeeded, and `SYSTEM_IS_UP` is set THEN and not before;
    - the manager faults or ends while the wait runs - the attempt failed, and the ladder retries;
    - the budget runs out - the attempt failed too. It is not a boot that "settled", which is what
      two of the three print today.
  - The manager is checked for a fault and for having ended during the wait, not only after it. That
    is one condition inside a loop that already exists, not a supervision mechanism.
  - The settle budget differs by target and becomes a parameter - riscv64 under TCG needs an order
    of magnitude more rounds than the other two, which is why its cap is 4000 against 400. A number
    that differs by target is a number the caller passes, not a reason for three functions.
  - AARCH64 CANNOT REBOOT TODAY, so the item includes the fix. `aarch64::reset()` is `halt_loop()`;
    x86_64 resets through the hardware and riscv64 through `sbi_system_reset`. Without a real reset
    the whole point of the shared tail - a lost control plane reaches a declared reboot - silently
    does not happen on one of the three. The boot records which conduit the device tree named, the
    same one `psci.rs` already picks for `CPU_ON`, and `reset` issues `SYSTEM_RESET` over it. With
    no usable conduit it logs the reason and halts, which is what it does today minus the silence.
  - `SYSTEM_OFF` COMES WITH IT, though recovery does not need it. It is one more function id over
    the conduit that is already there, and leaving `poweroff` a halt while `reset` works would be an
    inconsistency created on purpose to save four lines.
  - IT IS WIRING, NOT NEW MECHANISM, and that is what makes it small. `supervise`, `crash_seen` and
    `control_plane_lost` carry no `cfg`, compile on all three targets, and their tests already run
    on all three. What changes is which function the two non-x86 entries call at the end.
  - THIS CHANGES BOOT BEHAVIOUR ON ALL THREE TARGETS - two gain the ladder, one gains a readiness
    wait - so it lands while the suites are green and is measured on each.
  - HOW IT IS VERIFIED, HONESTLY. The suites cannot execute a boot tail on any target, so the
    evidence is a booted image on each: the chain reaches the shell and every expected report
    arrives. NOT the same order on every target - independent services may report in any
    deterministic tie order, which the boot test already says in as many words.
  - NOT IN THIS ITEM: making `supervise` wait for the terminal `SystemManager: online` marker rather
    than for readiness, and watching the manager across the whole wait. Both are changes to what the
    ladder MEANS, and this item is about who calls it.

- [ ] **M2 - The HAL contract has to describe itself truthfully.**
  - Twenty-seven `todo!()` bodies - sixteen on aarch64, eleven on riscv64 - stand for entries two of
    three backends never reach: `init`, `init_interrupts`, `init_syscalls`, `init_tsc`, the two
    per-CPU entries, and the x86-shaped SMP and interrupt-routing helpers (`apic::send_init`,
    `send_startup`, `ioapic::route`, `apboot::install`).
  - DELETING THEM IS NOT AVAILABLE, AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS ITEM SAID IT WAS. `kmain` carries no
    `cfg` - it is compiled on every target - and calls four of them; `smp/mod.rs` calls the other
    two. The stubs have static callers from portable code on all three targets, so removing them
    breaks the build rather than revealing anything. Making them removable means gating `kmain` and
    part of the SMP bring-up to x86_64, which is a refactor of the very thing this milestone is
    trying not to disturb.
  - SO THE DELIVERABLE IS THE TEXT, NOT THE CODE. The doc comment at the top of `arch/mod.rs` IS the
    contract - it is the only place the surface is written down - and it currently reads as one list
    that every backend owes. It should name which entries belong to the x86_64 bootloader hand-off
    and are answered by a panic elsewhere, so a fourth architecture reads what it actually has to
    provide.
  - A COMMENT IS A SMALL DELIVERABLE AND THAT IS THE POINT. The value here was never the thousand
    bytes of `todo!()`; it was that the contract promises twenty-seven things it does not require.
    Fixing the promise costs nothing and does not put the SMP path at risk to do it.

- [ ] **M3 - Two portable properties are asserted on one target. Assert them on three.**
  - Nineteen tests run only on x86_64. Ten of them are `kernel.arch.x86_64.*` and name hardware that
    exists nowhere else - APIC, IDT, the RTC registers, port-mapped PCI - and stay where they are.
    Most of the rest have an equivalent on the other two under an architecture's own name: the
    driver-crash rows against `hardware.driver_survives_crash_and_restart`, interrupt binding
    against the GICv2m and IMSIC dispatch tests, the direct map against
    `arch.the_direct_map_has_no_writable_executable_alias`.
  - TWO HAVE NO EQUIVALENT ANYWHERE:
    - `kernel.sched.scheduler_preserves_xmm_state` - a context switch does not lose the
      floating-point state it is responsible for. Silent data corruption if it is wrong. Each
      backend saves a DIFFERENT set and the test must check the set that backend actually saves:
      FPU/XMM through `fxsave` on x86_64, the callee-saved `d8..d15` on aarch64, the callee-saved
      `fs0..fs11` on riscv64. Neither aarch64 nor riscv64 saves vector registers or the FP control
      word, so a test asking about those would be asking about something the kernel never promised.
      The id loses `xmm` with it - `scheduler_preserves_saved_fp_state` says what is being asserted
      on all three. This one is otherwise a straight port.
    - `kernel.kernel.kernel_access_to_user_memory_is_refused_outside_the_window` - the supervisor
      may not touch user memory outside an explicit window. On riscv64 this is real and unasserted:
      `user_access` sets `sstatus.SUM` and `clac_on_entry` closes it on trap entry, so the test can
      be written and should pass. **ON AARCH64 IT CANNOT BE**: the tested CPU is a cortex-a72, which
      is ARMv8.0 and has no PAN, and `aarch64::paging::user_access` is a passthrough that says so at
      the call site. There is nothing to assert there, and what belongs beside that gate is that
      sentence - not a test that would have to be disabled.
  - THAT ASYMMETRY IS THE FINDING, not an inconvenience. Two of three targets refuse stray kernel
    reads of user memory and one does not, and until this item nothing in the tree said so where an
    operator would see it.
  - THE PATTERN TO COPY IS ALREADY IN THE FILE. `writable_pages_are_not_executable` is declared
    three times in `test_suites/kernel.rs` under ONE id, each body gated to its architecture, and it
    runs everywhere. This item is that pattern applied twice.
  - THE SUM HALF COSTS MORE THAN THE FP HALF, and whoever picks this up should know before starting.
    It is not a port of the x86 test: the fault it provokes is a SUPERVISOR fault, and one of those
    stops the kernel today. It needs a small test-only hook that records the fault and ends the
    probing thread instead. The ring-3 `usermode::program_*_bytes` helpers are no use here - those
    are user-mode probes and this is about a kernel access. If that hook turns out to be more than
    small, the honest outcome is the written reason beside the gate, not a disabled test.
  - AND THE FIRST DRAFT OF THIS ITEM WAS WRONG, which is why the paragraph above names the file.
    It listed W^X as an x86-only gap. W^X has run on all three targets for as long as it has
    existed; counting `cfg` attributes made one test look like three.

## Dependency order

```text
M1 --> M2        the contract cannot be described honestly until the tails stop diverging
M3               independent
```

M1 IS THE MILESTONE. M2 is a comment M1 makes writable, and M3 is unrelated work of similar size
that happens to have been found by the same reading. An earlier draft had M1 blocking M3; it does
not - a portable tail has nothing to do with whether a context switch keeps its FP registers.

A FOURTH ITEM WAS DROPPED. It proposed folding `dtb.rs` and `usercopy.rs` into `arch/common` on the
strength of their being near-copies. They are not: the DTB parser is already the shared `fdt` crate
and what remains is per-target addresses, and `usercopy.rs` is hand-written assembly with exception
table entries on both backends. Merging them would need macros or generics to produce nothing
genuinely shared, which is the kind of unification this milestone opens by refusing.

## Definition of done

- All three architectures reach userspace through ONE portable tail. The recovery ladder, the crash
  channel, the readiness wait and the resident-manager watch behave the same on each, and each is
  measured on its own target rather than inferred from x86_64.
- A SystemManager lost after boot reaches a declared reboot on all three - which means `aarch64`
  actually resets rather than halting.
- Every expected boot report arrives on every target. NOT in an identical order: independent
  services may report in any deterministic tie order, and the boot test already says so.
- The HAL contract in `arch/mod.rs` says which of its entries belong to the x86_64 hand-off and are
  answered by a panic elsewhere. It no longer reads as one list every backend owes.
- The two properties in M3 are asserted on every target under one id each, or the reason a
  particular target cannot assert one is written down beside its gate - which is already known to be
  the answer for the user-memory window on aarch64.
- Nothing under `arch/` grew a trait, a dispatch table or a generic parameter to reach any of the
  above. If a change needs one, it was the wrong change.

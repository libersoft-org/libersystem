# P02M0142 - One boot tail for three architectures, and a HAL surface that means what it says

Status: OPEN. PORTABILITY. NOT A PHASE-2 COMPLETION GATE.

## Goal

Make the portable half of bringing userspace up portable in fact and not only in intent, and shrink
the declared hardware-abstraction surface to what something actually calls.

This is not a tidy-up. The three architectures diverge at exactly one point that carries policy
rather than machinery - the moment the machine is up and the kernel decides what to do about
userspace - and the divergence means the ownership and recovery rules P02M0141 built exist on ONE
target of three.

## What is there now

Measured on 2026-08-22, by reading the tree.

|  | x86_64 | aarch64 | riscv64 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lines under `arch/` | 4572 | 5001 | 3917 |
| `todo!()` entries in the HAL contract | 0 | 17 | 12 |
| tests in the suite | 305 | 291 | 290 |
| tests locked to this architecture | 21 | 10 | 10 |

WHAT IS ALREADY SHARED IS MORE THAN IT LOOKS. `arch/common` is 2345 lines - PCI enumeration, the
MSI-X slot registry, boot memory, fw_cfg, the portable clock, the paging flags - and each backend
wires its few real primitives into it. Userspace is architecture-neutral outright: four files in the
whole tree outside the kernel crate mention `target_arch`, and two of those are the SDK. The loader
splits the same way, a per-architecture prologue around a shared body.

So the split is broadly right, and this milestone does not reopen it.

**THE THREE BOOTS END DIFFERENTLY, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM ENDS CORRECTLY.** x86_64 arrives through
`kmain` and calls `boot_userspace_with_recovery()`. aarch64 and riscv64 enter at their own
`aarch64_main` / `riscv64_main` and hand-roll a settle loop - run the scheduler, drain the boot
reports, wait for the shell - then call `console_shell_loop()` directly.

What the two hand-rolled tails therefore do not have:

- `supervise()`, so a SystemManager that faults during boot is never restarted,
- the crash-notify channel, so nothing observes a fault at all,
- the ended-is-as-gone-as-faulted check P02M0141 added,
- the rule that the retry ladder stops once a control-plane branch exists,
- the scheduler idle hook, and with it `SYSTEM_IS_UP` and `resident_manager_lost()` - so a
  SystemManager lost AFTER boot leaves the control plane ownerless and nothing notices.

And the two tails are otherwise the same text. They differ in the prefix they log with and in the
settle cap (400 rounds against 4000). That is not an architecture difference; it is a copy that
drifted.

`arch/mod.rs` predicted this in writing: *"nothing type-checks the per-arch boot sequences against
each other, so a step added to one is silently absent from the others until a test notices."* A step
was added to one. No test noticed.

## Scope

IN: the tail of the boot sequence - the part that runs once the machine is up - the unused half of
the HAL contract, four tests whose property is portable, and two shim files that are duplicates.

OUT, and deliberately: paging, exceptions and interrupt controllers, the per-CPU register, context
switching, secondary-core bring-up, the loader's per-architecture prologue, and polled versus
interrupt-driven serial input. Those are the machine. A trait over them buys indirection and nothing
else, and this milestone is not an excuse to write one.

ALSO OUT: the machine-specific PROLOGUE of each boot. Bringing a machine up is the least portable
thing a kernel does and the hand-off genuinely differs - UEFI and a loader on one, a device tree and
firmware already in supervisor mode on the other two. What moves is the end, not the beginning.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - One boot tail, called by all three entries.**
  - Extract the portable end of `boot_userspace_with_recovery` into one function in `main.rs` that
    every architecture's boot path calls once its machine is up.
  - aarch64 and riscv64 thereby gain the recovery ladder, the crash channel, the ended-manager
    check, the branch rule and the resident-manager watch. That is the whole point of the item; the
    deleted duplication is a side effect.
  - THE SETTLE LOOP IS THE ONE REAL DIFFERENCE AND IT BECOMES A PARAMETER. riscv64 under TCG settles
    the interrupt-driven chain more slowly and variably than the other two, which is why its cap is
    ten times aarch64's. A number that differs by target is a number the caller passes, not a reason
    for three functions.
  - The log prefix differs today and stops differing. A boot line saying which architecture wrote it
    is information the reader already has from the image they booted.
  - THIS CHANGES BOOT BEHAVIOUR ON TWO TARGETS, so it lands while all three suites are green and is
    measured on all three before it closes. A portability fix that is only built on the target it
    was written on is the same defect in a new place.

- [ ] **M2 - Delete the HAL entries nothing calls.**
  - Twenty-nine `todo!()` bodies - seventeen on aarch64, twelve on riscv64 - satisfy a contract two
    of three backends never reach, because they bring their machine up inline instead of through
    `arch::init*`.
  - DELETE THEM RATHER THAN IMPLEMENT THEM. A contract entry that one backend implements and two
    answer with a panic is not a contract; it is a list. What the surface should say is what a
    fourth architecture would actually have to provide.
  - The doc comment at the top of `arch/mod.rs` is updated in the same change, because that comment
    IS the contract - it is the only place the surface is written down.
  - Anything that turns out to have a real caller stays and is implemented. The point is to find out
    which, and the compiler answers that question for free once the stubs are gone.

- [ ] **M3 - Port the tests whose property is portable and whose probe is not.**
  - Twenty-one kernel tests are locked to x86_64. Most name a mechanism that only exists there -
    GDT, IDT, APIC, SMAP/SMEP, the TSC - and stay locked.
  - These do not: `kernel.kernel.writable_pages_are_not_executable`,
    `kernel.kernel.kernel_access_to_user_memory_is_refused_outside_the_window`,
    `kernel.kernel.an_ordinary_ring_three_exception_kills_the_process_and_not_the_machine`. Every
    one is a security property every target either has or does not, tested on one.
  - THE PROBE IS WHAT IS ARCHITECTURE-SPECIFIC, AND IT ALREADY EXISTS. Each backend supplies
    `usermode::program_*_bytes`; the tests are gated because they assemble their ring-3 probe by
    hand, not because the property is unavailable.
  - Four more are worth looking at and not worth promising: the two driver-crash rows, interrupt
    binding, and PCI enumeration behind a bridge. The last of these runs on `arch/common` code that
    is already shared, which makes its gate the most suspicious of the twenty-one.

- [ ] **M4 - Fold the two duplicated shims into `arch/common`.**
  - `dtb.rs` and `usercopy.rs` differ between aarch64 and riscv64 by 55 and 38 lines once the
    architecture name is normalised away. Both are thin wrappers with a couple of per-target
    constants.
  - SMALLEST ITEM HERE AND IT SAYS SO. This is worth doing when somebody is in those files anyway;
    it is not worth a change of its own, and it must not delay M1.

## Dependency order

```text
M1 --> M2        the stubs are easier to judge once the tails converge
M1 --> M3        a portable tail is what makes a portable probe worth writing
M4               independent, and last
```

M1 is the item. M2 and M3 are what M1 makes possible, and M4 is housekeeping that can wait for a
change that is already touching those files.

## Definition of done

- All three architectures reach userspace through ONE portable tail. The recovery ladder, the crash
  channel and the resident-manager watch behave the same on each, and each is measured on its own
  target rather than inferred from x86_64.
- A SystemManager lost after boot reaches a declared reboot on aarch64 and riscv64, as it already
  does on x86_64.
- The HAL contract in `arch/mod.rs` lists what a backend must provide, and every entry in it has a
  caller. No entry is satisfied by a panic.
- The three portable security properties are asserted on every target, or the reason a particular
  target cannot assert one is written down beside its gate.
- Nothing under `arch/` grew a trait, a dispatch table or a generic parameter to reach any of the
  above. If a change needs one, it was the wrong change.

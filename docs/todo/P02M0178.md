# P02M0178 - Narrow the userspace runtime's unsafe surface to the contracts callers actually hold

Status: PLANNED. CORRECTNESS/HARDENING FOLLOW-UP FROM THE UNSAFE-BOUNDARY AUDIT. NOT A PHASE-2 COMPLETION GATE.

## Goal

Make `unsafe` in userspace mean a caller obligation again. The runtime declares one hundred and
thirty typed syscall wrappers `unsafe fn` although their parameters are kernel handles, slices and
plain values and their bodies uphold the raw call's contract themselves; every service, driver,
library and tool then wraps each call in an `unsafe {}` of its own. The 2026-09-09 unsafe-boundary
audit counted the consequence: of the 4775 boundaries reachable in a shipping configuration, 2734
are this propagation - blocks and functions that dereference nothing and whose only reason to be
unsafe is the signature they call. A reader cannot tell the dozen places userspace really touches
raw memory from the thousands that do not.

## What is there now, and what it forces

- `rt::syscall` is inline assembly and is rightly `unsafe`: every argument that is a pointer must
  be valid for the kernel's copy. That contract is discharged INSIDE wrappers such as
  `random_get(&mut [u8])`, `recv_blocking(chan, &mut [u8])` and `send_blocking(chan, &[u8], handle)`,
  which pass a slice's own pointer and length. `close(handle)`, `yield_now()`, `clock()` and
  `wait_any(&[u64], deadline)` pass no pointer at all. None of these can violate memory safety from
  the caller's side, and all of them are `unsafe fn`.
- A minority genuinely hands the caller a raw address to dereference - `map_object`,
  `dma_buffer_map`, `dma_buffer_phys`, `framebuffer_map`, `memmap_get`, the process loader's module
  placement - and those stay `unsafe`, because the obligation (the mapping's length and lifetime)
  is the caller's.
- The drivers' shared handshake layer (`common.rs`), the client libraries and every `__user_main`
  are `unsafe fn` only by propagation. The kernel side has one analogous shape: the syscall layer's
  safe generic `read_user<T>`, sound for every plain-data `T` it is instantiated with today and
  unable to say so in its signature.

## Work

- [ ] **M1 - the runtime's wrappers get the signature their contract has.** Every wrapper whose
  body upholds the raw call's pointer contract becomes a safe `fn`; the address-returning wrappers
  keep `unsafe fn` and gain a written `# Safety` line naming the mapping's length and lifetime. The
  raw `syscall` stays `unsafe`. No wrapper changes what it does.
- [ ] **M2 - the propagation is removed where it was only propagation.** Services, drivers,
  libraries and tools drop the `unsafe {}` blocks and `unsafe fn` declarations that existed only to
  call M1's wrappers; what remains `unsafe` in userspace is a raw-memory or device access with a
  contract. `__user_main` stays `extern "C"` and `#[unsafe(no_mangle)]` - that is the entry ABI.
- [ ] **M3 - the kernel's user-pointer read says what it needs.** `read_user<T>` is bounded to
  plain-old-data types (a sealed marker trait implemented for the ABI records it reads) or becomes
  `unsafe fn` with the contract written; either way a future `T` with invalid bit patterns is
  refused at compile time or declared at the call site.
- [ ] **M4 - the inventory proves the narrowing.** `unsafe-inventory` is regenerated after the
  change; the `rt-propagated-wrappers`, `drivers-propagated-wrappers`, `services-wrapped-runtime-
  calls`, `apps-wrapped-runtime-calls` and `libs-wrapped-runtime-calls` groups go to zero or to the
  named residue with a stated contract each, and the kernel's `read_user` group is reclassified. The
  scoped guest suites for the affected crates pass unchanged.

## Definition of done

- No userspace `unsafe fn` or `unsafe {}` exists whose only reason is a runtime wrapper's signature.
- Every remaining userspace `unsafe` names its contract.
- `read_user<T>` cannot be instantiated with a type that has invalid bit patterns without an
  `unsafe` at the call site.

## What this milestone refuses

- Changing any syscall's behaviour, the entry ABI, or the shared-image provider ABI.
- `forbid(unsafe_code)` anywhere, or a target count.

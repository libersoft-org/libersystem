# P02M0197 - Sleep and resume: suspend to idle, to RAM and to disk, and what wakes the machine

Status: OPEN. Created 2026-09-25 under the owner's approval of P02M0196 - "yes, do all of it; where a
milestone is missing, create it". NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for plans only until he says
otherwise, and no part starts without its own written plan. The milestone closes when all five parts are
done; suspend to idle and S3 (parts a to d) do not wait for hibernation (part e), which is a part of its own.

WHY IT IS A MILESTONE OF ITS OWN. P02M0196's step 3 supplies the PLATFORM half of a sleep: which sleep states
the firmware offers, the methods it runs around them. Everything else about a sleep belongs to the SYSTEM, and
nothing owns it: P02M0181 says at its head that "suspend, device quiescence/wake, fan curves and vendor policy
remain outside this milestone", `liber:process@1/system-power` has exactly two operations - reboot and
power-off - and no driver in this tree has a suspend or a resume path.

WHAT EXISTS, read in the tree rather than assumed:
  - Power-off and reset. On x86_64 `poweroff` writes SLP_EN with a sleep type of zero to the PM1a control
    ports QEMU's two chipsets decode (0x604 and 0xB004) - "real-hardware ACPI comes later", in its own
    comment - and `reset` uses 0xCF9 and the 8042. aarch64 uses PSCI SYSTEM_OFF and SYSTEM_RESET, riscv64
    the SBI System Reset extension. `src/acpi` already parses the FADT's PM1 control blocks, GPE0 and reset
    register; nothing uses them for this. `sys_system_power` is the only power entry: reboot or off, for the
    holder of MANAGE on the root Domain, which is SystemManager alone.
  - The power and sleep buttons arrive as platform events (`kernel/platform_event.rs`) on DeviceManager's
    channel; the system acts on the power button only. The SCI handler arms PWRBTN and SLPBTN in PM1 enable
    and nothing else: it "arms no general-purpose event, enters no sleep state".
  - Every core takes a periodic tick (`TICK_HZ`) and idles in `hlt` or `wfi`. The tick counter is gated on
    the free-running counter and claims a whole backlog in one step; `SYS_CLOCK_MONO_NS` reads the raw
    counter. Every Timer and every wait deadline is an absolute value on the tick counter.
  - The only pause the kernel has is job control: `SIG_STOP` sets a per-process flag that parks each thread
    at its next kernel exit, and `SIG_CONT` - which `fg` sends - clears it.
  - Every service runs in the one control-plane Domain; DeviceManager has a Domain of its own with a child
    Domain per driver binding; a program ProcessService launches for a client lands in ProcessService's own
    Domain - the control plane - unless it asked for limits. No Domain separates applications from services,
    and no launch operation does either: ServiceManager raw-spawns only the pinned services and loads every
    other one - 24 of the 34, VT 1's shell, TimeService and the power-state service among them - through
    ProcessService's `launch`, or `launch-prepared-limited` for a service with limits, and restarts them the
    same way; `start` is the `run` tool's.
  - The driver wire (`driver-protocol`) has one quiesce verb, `STOP`, whose answer `STOPPED` ends the
    binding. DeviceManager pings every Online binding and scales its deadlines by the port's boot window
    (`machine_scale`). USB class devices are bound inside the xHCI driver's own process.
  - TimeService seeds the wall clock from the kernel's RTC read once, when it starts, and counts it forward
    on the tick counter; StorageService stamps files from the same kernel read.

## P02M0197a - The suspend transaction

- [ ] A VOCABULARY BESIDE `system-power`, NOT INSIDE IT: a `system-sleep` interface in `liber:process@1`
  with suspend (to idle or to RAM, with an optional timed wake), hibernate (part e), a bounded inhibit for
  IDLE sleeps, a scheduled wake (part c) and the last sleep's record - because the authority to put the
  machine to sleep is not the authority to stop it, and `system-power`'s own text says its client "can stop
  the machine and can do NOTHING ELSE". Clients reach it through manifest roles and PermissionManager
  grants; the requesters are the policy (part c), the sleep buttons, P02M0198d's ProcessorPowerService for
  `_HOT` (hibernation where part e is set up, P02M0198's `_CRT` sequence otherwise) and `sleepctl`.
  `suspend` AND `hibernate` ANSWER WHEN SERVICEMANAGER ACCEPTS OR REFUSES THE REQUEST - before any step
  runs and without waiting on any participant - NEVER AT THE RESUME: two requesters are participants the
  transaction waits on - DeviceManager, asking for the fixed sleep button, and the control-method sleep
  button's driver both answer `SUSPEND` in step 4 - and a requester blocked in its own request could not. A
  refusal then names its reason (a transaction already running, a request the grant does not carry);
  everything after acceptance - a sleep refused before anything was frozen, one that slept and woke, one
  that unwound and where - is read from the last sleep's record.
  SERVICEMANAGER KEEPS SERVING WHILE A TRANSACTION RUNS, which is what lets that refusal be sent: the
  transaction is driven from its standing loop's wait set - each step's requests sent and their answers
  awaited there, under the step's deadline - never run as a blocking sequence. Meanwhile:
  - a `system-sleep` request is refused at once as a transaction already running, so a second press of
    either sleep button never blocks DeviceManager or the button's driver, both of which step 4 waits on;
  - a `system-shutdown` `power-off` is answered at once and ENDS THE TRANSACTION at its next step - unwound
    as a failed step is, or resumed if the machine has already slept - and the orderly power-off follows,
    so a person's shutdown or a critical battery's never waits out a sleep; P02M0198d's forced deadline,
    which the critical paths arm first, refuses the kernel's entry anyway (part b);
  - a request that would start, stop or restart a service is refused as a transaction already running;
  - no `alive` is answered until the transaction ends, which P02M0200's watchdog step allows for;
  - every other request is answered as usual.
- [ ] WHO DOES WHAT. SERVICEMANAGER ORCHESTRATES: it serves `system-sleep`, runs one transaction at a time,
  and already holds what the order needs - the services' dependency graph it starts and stops them by, and a
  control channel to each. SYSTEMMANAGER ONLY ENTERS: it is the one holder of MANAGE on the root Domain,
  which the kernel's sleep entry requires (part b), and it makes that call when ServiceManager asks over the
  control channel they already share. No service, driver or application policy moves into SystemManager, as
  P02M0141 requires; the sleep policy lives in the power-state service (part c).
- [ ] THE FROZEN SET AND THE PARTICIPANTS ARE DIFFERENT PROCESSES. PARTICIPANTS are NEVER frozen, and those
  whose manifest entry declares the sleep notice are asked to quiesce: every service ServiceManager starts -
  the control-plane services, every StorageService instance, DeviceManager, the ACPI service, TimeService, the
  power-state service, the watchdog service, VT 1's shell - and every driver DeviceManager binds: user
  processes the sleep needs in order to finish. THE FROZEN SET is the applications, and it gets the boundary
  it lacks today, KEYED TO WHO ASKS AND NOT TO THE OPERATION, because ServiceManager and a client call the
  same operations: ProcessService gains a second serve root that ServiceManager alone holds - no manifest
  role is minted from it - and every launch arriving on it, a first start or a restart, keeps the
  control-plane Domain as today (a limited service's own Domain stays the child of it that it is) and is
  NEVER held. Every launch on the client root - `start`, `launch`, `launch_prepared`, and the bounded and
  limited forms - lands in ONE applications Domain ProcessService creates at start, the per-launch Domains
  becoming its children through an additive parent argument on `SYS_DOMAIN_CREATE` (MANAGE on the parent;
  zero keeps today's "the caller's Domain"). That takes in what `run` starts, the shells ConsoleService
  starts for its other virtual terminals, what PermissionManager launches and every job; a process an
  application creates itself lands in its own Domain, inside the same subtree. VT 1's shell, launched by
  ServiceManager, is not frozen, and it sits waiting for input.
- [ ] A KERNEL FREEZE, NOT JOB CONTROL: `SYS_DOMAIN_FREEZE(domain, on)`, requiring MANAGE on the Domain - the
  right that already lets its holder kill the whole subtree, so freezing it is no new authority. It parks
  every thread of the subtree at the kernel exit where `SIG_STOP` is enforced, on a FROZEN flag separate from
  the job-control stopped flag: `SIG_CONT` does not clear it and the thaw does not clear `stopped`, so a job
  a person stopped before the sleep is still stopped after it, and a `SIG_CONT` sent during the freeze takes
  effect at the thaw. A process created in a frozen subtree starts frozen. The call returns only when no
  thread of the subtree is executing user code, within its bound, and fails otherwise. ProcessService holds
  the applications Domain and freezes and thaws it when ServiceManager asks; a client's launch during the
  freeze is not refused - its process, created in the frozen subtree, runs at the thaw. SERVICEMANAGER
  RESTARTS NOTHING WHILE THE DRIVERS ARE SUSPENDED, because every launch reads the volume: a participant that
  dies during the transaction is restarted once the drivers run again - after the unwind where its death
  failed a step, after the resume's driver step otherwise.
- [ ] THE ORDER, EVERY STEP BOUNDED, run by ServiceManager:
  1. ANNOUNCE the sleep, with a deadline, to every service whose manifest entry declares the sleep notice -
     the ACPI service and the watchdog service among them - on the control channel ServiceManager holds for
     it (the one LogService's `FLUSH` travels on); honour inhibitors - a holder may delay an IDLE sleep (a
     download, a call) by a bounded amount, and nothing delays a lid, sleep-button or critical-battery sleep
     past its bound;
  2. FREEZE the applications (ProcessService, above);
  3. FLUSH: every StorageService instance writes back what it holds for its writable volumes and HOLDS new
     writes until the resume, so a battery that dies during the sleep loses nothing written before it;
  4. SUSPEND THE DRIVERS: DeviceManager sends `SUSPEND` to its bindings in reverse bind order, a binding
     that consumes another binding's provider always before that provider; the xHCI driver suspends the USB
     devices bound inside its own process before its controller; a storage driver flushes the device's
     write cache in its step, and the TPM driver saves the TPM's state in its (below); every binding that
     publishes a `watchdog` goes LAST (its step below);
  5. PREPARE THE PLATFORM, on an ACPI machine, through `platform-sleep`, an interface in `liber:process@1`
     beside `system-sleep` that the ACPI service serves on the control channel ServiceManager holds for it -
     the one its sleep notice travels on: `prepare(state, wake nodes)`, the nodes being the companions of
     the bindings whose `SUSPENDED` said wake armed, evaluates `_PRW` and `_DSW` or `_PSW` on them, sets
     their wake GPEs through P02M0196b's GPE interface (part c), then runs `_PTS` and `_SST` - P02M0196c's
     side of the step. The node-scoped channel still refuses `_PTS` and `_WAK`;
  6. ENTER: ServiceManager asks SystemManager, which makes the kernel call of part b; it returns on resume
     with what woke the machine.
  RESUME IS THE REVERSE: `platform-sleep`'s `wake(state)` - `_WAK` and `_SST`, the wake GPEs cleared;
  DeviceManager resumes every binding that publishes a `watchdog` FIRST and the rest in bind order;
  StorageService releases the held writes; the services receive the resume notice (TimeService re-reads its
  clock on it, part b; the watchdog service restores its configured timeout); ProcessService thaws the
  applications LAST. Each step's bound is ServiceManager's number scaled by the port's boot window the
  way DeviceManager's `machine_scale` scales its deadlines - ServiceManager holds the same window - so an
  emulated port does not abort a healthy sleep for being slow.
- [ ] A DRIVER CONTRACT FOR SUSPEND AND RESUME, IN THE DRIVER WIRE: four opcodes in `driver-protocol`, sent
  and answered only between DeviceManager and a driver, and NOT terminal - the binding, its generation, its
  publications and its consumers' connections survive, which is what `STOP` cannot give. `SUSPEND` carries
  the target state, whether the device is to arm wake, and the sleep's timed wake if there is one; the driver
  takes no new work, finishes or holds what it accepted, stops DMA, masks its interrupts, puts the device in
  its lowest state, arms wake if asked, and answers `SUSPENDED` with the outcome - done, done with wake
  armed, or refused with a `DriverFailureCode` - and, for a device that cannot be stopped, the latest time
  the machine must be awake by. `RESUME` says whether the device may have lost power (S3 and hibernation:
  yes); the driver reprograms from its saved state and answers `RESUMED`, or reports a device that did not
  come back, which DeviceManager tears down and rebinds through the route it already has. The opcodes are
  additive and the wire's revision number does not change (nothing is versioned before the first release).
  A driver declares support with a `suspend-deadline` in its registry entry beside `heartbeat-deadline`:
  ticks, multiplied by `machine_scale()` exactly as the bind deadlines are. `binding-state` in
  `liber:device@1` gains an appended `suspended` value so the binding list shows it; that is the only change
  to that package.
- [ ] THE HEARTBEAT ACROSS A SLEEP: supervision pauses for a node that is not Online - the rule
  `tick_heartbeats` already applies to a `Stopping` node - so a suspending or suspended binding is not
  pinged, its channel is still drained, and its heartbeat resumes (`beat.resume`) when `RESUMED` returns it
  to Online. A suspended driver's control path stays live to answer `RESUME`.
- [ ] NO DRIVER IS SKIPPED: while a binding with no `suspend-deadline` is Online the sleep is refused before
  anything is frozen, and the binding named; a driver that does not answer within its scaled bound, or
  refuses, ABORTS the suspend. A device still doing DMA into memory that is about to stop being refreshed is
  the failure this transaction exists to prevent. The part implements the exchange in every driver the image
  ships, those the gate machines bind first, and A DRIVER ADDED AFTER IT CARRIES THE EXCHANGE ITSELF: the
  plans written beside this one say so for theirs - P02M0190's TPM driver, P02M0198's fan driver, P02M0199's
  `acpi_backlight`, `acpi_als` and xhci class module, P02M0201's `ipmi` and `smbus_ich9`, P02M0202's
  `ucsi-acpi` and `tcpci` - each carried by whichever of the two milestones lands second.
- [ ] THE WATCHDOG'S STEP (P02M0200 defines the behaviour; this contract calls it). THE SERVICE'S HALF, AT
  THE ANNOUNCEMENT: the watchdog service's manifest entry declares the sleep notice, and on it the service
  sets the longest timeout on every running timer it holds and pets each once - a disarmed device is left
  alone, since setting a timeout would start it - because ServiceManager, running the transaction, answers
  no `alive` until the resume notice, on which the service restores the configured timeout. THE DRIVER'S
  HALF applies to EVERY binding that publishes a `watchdog`: suspended LAST, it disarms the device where the
  device allows it, and otherwise sets the longest timeout, pets it a last time and returns its "awake by"
  bound, which caps the sleep's timed wake below that timeout. A device that stops counting in the
  requested state returns no bound: WDAT's `STOPPED` flag covers S3 and deeper and never
  suspend to idle, so a WDAT that cannot be disarmed answers its bound for suspend to idle, and a chipset
  timer that loses power in S3 answers none for S3. On resume every such binding is re-armed FIRST, with a
  timeout that also catches a resume that hangs.
- [ ] THE TPM DRIVER'S STEP: its `SUSPEND` for S3 sends `TPM2_Shutdown(TPM_SU_STATE)`, and its `RESUME` sends
  no `Startup` when the firmware has already started the TPM with the saved state. Without the shutdown the
  firmware's `Startup(STATE)` at resume fails and it falls back to `Startup(CLEAR)`, extending an error
  separator into PCRs 0 to 7 (edk2's S3 path does exactly this): after the first S3 of a boot PCR 4 no
  longer holds the loader's measurement, part e's key does not unseal, and every secret sealed to PCRs 0 to
  7 stays shut until the next boot. P02M0190's driver implements the exchange with this step, carried by
  whichever of P02M0190 and P02M0197 lands second.
- [ ] A suspend that fails at any step unwinds to a running system - the steps already taken, in reverse -
  and says, in the system graph and in the last sleep's record, which step and which driver or service.

## P02M0197b - The sleep states, the kernel's entry, and the clocks across a sleep

ORDER: P02M0198a - idle without a tick, and the clock's single read path - lands BEFORE this part.
P02M0196c's platform half of sleep lands TOGETHER with this part and part a: the sleep-type registration call
below is this part's, and `platform-sleep` (part a, step 5) is the ACPI service's.

- [ ] THE KERNEL'S SLEEP ENTRY, one privileged operation: `SYS_SYSTEM_SLEEP(root Domain, state, timed wake)`,
  requiring MANAGE on the root Domain as `sys_system_power` does; the state is suspend to idle, S3, or the
  hibernation states of part e, and the timed wake is a deadline on the boot-time clock (below) or none. In
  order: arm the kernel's part of the wake set (part c) and clear PM1 and GPE status; write the kernel's
  `sleep: entered` line to the wire synchronously under P02M0191c's SLEEP-ENTRY RULE - NOT through the
  terminal-path writer `poweroff` enters, which would leave COM1 terminal after the resume: while the
  kernel owns COM1 the entry drains the ring and stays the owner, and while a driver holds it - suspended
  by then, step 4 having run - the entry lends the port to the kernel until it returns and then gives it
  back; either way the line is on the wire before the machine sleeps, and so is every kernel line until
  the entry returns, `sleep: resumed` among them (until P02M0191c lands the kernel always owns COM1, and
  this is today's `flush_sync`); hold the monotonic clock (below); for suspend to idle, park every core in
  its idle state (below); for S3, stop the secondary cores, save the kernel's own devices - interrupt
  controllers, timers, the IOMMU, every PCI function's configuration header the kernel recorded at its scan
  (BARs, bridge windows, command register, the MSI-X capability) and the MSI-X table entries the kernel
  itself programmed, which live in a BAR and not in the header, because S3 resets every device (QEMU's
  wakeup reset included) and no driver knows the message the kernel wrote there. THE RULE IS EVERY SETTING
  THE KERNEL MADE ON A DEVICE, AT BOOT OR SINCE, THAT NO DRIVER OWNS, so the list also holds each hot-plug
  slot's Slot Control - the presence-change, hot-plug-interrupt and attention-button enables, the slot's
  power and its indicator, which QEMU's root-port reset clears, powering an empty slot off - PM1's enable
  bits, the configuration registers the kernel writes for a claim (P02M0201's HOSTC), and, while the
  kernel owns it, the console UART's initialisation with its receive interrupt, which QEMU's UART reset
  turns off. Then write its resume trampoline's address into the FACS waking vector, save the boot core's
  context, flush the caches, and write SLP_TYPa and SLP_TYPb, then SLP_EN, into the FADT's PM1a and PM1b
  control blocks. On an S3 resume the boot core comes from real mode back into the kernel through the
  real-mode trampoline boot uses for x86_64's secondary cores (`src/kernel/arch/x86_64/apboot.rs`, which
  derives its own base from CS); everything on that list is restored BEFORE any driver runs again, and the
  secondary cores come back through the path boot uses. On every resume the
  clocks are rebased (below), the GPE runtime mask is restored, and the call returns what woke the machine,
  read from PM1 and GPE status or from the interrupt that ended the idle. The fixed-button status that woke
  it is cleared and reported as the wake reason, NOT delivered as a press - QEMU's `system_wakeup` itself
  sets the power button's status, and a delivered press would power the machine off. A state that did not
  enter, a wake event already pending, or a FORCED POWER-OFF DEADLINE ARMED (P02M0198d - a sleep's held
  clock would postpone it) returns an error and the transaction unwinds; whichever of this part and
  P02M0198d lands second implements that last refusal.
- [ ] THE SLEEP TYPES REACH THE KERNEL FROM THE ACPI SERVICE: when it starts it evaluates `\_S3`, `\_S4` and
  `\_S5` (P02M0196c) and registers each sleep-type pair with the kernel through a call this part introduces,
  admitted by P02M0196b's `FirmwareInterpreter` privilege. A restarted service re-registers the same values,
  and a registered value outlives the service - it is firmware data, so a service that dies after
  registering leaves its values in force. The entry refuses a state nothing registered.
- [ ] THE PER-CORE RESUME PATH, ONE OPERATION: a core that lost its context re-enters the kernel through the
  path boot uses for that architecture's secondary cores (the real-mode trampoline in
  `src/kernel/arch/x86_64/apboot.rs` on x86_64, the PSCI or SBI HSM entry point on the other two) and
  restores its saved per-core state - its interrupt controller interface, its timer, its page tables and
  per-CPU block. S3 uses it for every core, and P02M0198b's idle states that lose the core's context use it
  too, which is why those states wait for this item.
- [ ] SUSPEND TO IDLE on all three architectures: the transaction with no firmware transition - every core
  parked in its idle state through P02M0198a's tickless idle (this part does not stop the tick itself), the
  one-shot timer programmed from the timed wake alone - never from a monotonic deadline, which a sleep does
  not advance, and never capped by P02M0198a's housekeeping bound or its one-tick caps, which the sleep
  suspends with the rest - and every device interrupt masked except the wake set's. An interrupt outside the
  wake set is handled and the cores return to idle without resuming. THE PARKED INTERVAL IS MEASURED: the
  entry takes each core's P02M0198a per-core idle record as the core parks and as it leaves, and the last
  sleep's record keeps the difference - each core's wakeups by cause while parked. It is the state most
  current laptops offer instead of S3, and the one every QEMU target can run.
- [ ] SUSPEND TO RAM (S3) on x86_64 through the entry above, with `_PTS`, `_SST` and `_WAK` run through
  `platform-sleep` in the transaction (part a).
- [ ] SYSTEM SUSPEND ON THE OTHER TWO - PSCI SYSTEM_SUSPEND on aarch64, the SBI System Suspend extension on
  riscv64 - WHERE THE PLATFORM OFFERS IT, as further states of the same entry. Whether QEMU's `virt` with
  this firmware offers either is checked first (PSCI_FEATURES, the SBI probe); where it does not, suspend to
  idle is what those targets prove in the guest, and that is written down rather than assumed.
- [ ] SOFT-OFF AND RESET FROM THE FIRMWARE'S DESCRIPTION: power-off writes the registered `\_S5` pair to the
  FADT's PM1a and PM1b control blocks, and reset uses the FADT's reset register before the 0xCF9 and 8042
  fallbacks - so power-off works on a machine whose chipset is not one of QEMU's two. UNTIL a `\_S5` is
  registered - the ACPI service absent, not yet started, or dead before it registered - today's fixed ports
  (0x604, 0xB004, 0x600), PSCI and SBI stay the path, so power-off never depends on AML; a service that dies
  after registering leaves its `\_S5` in force. POWER-OFF NAMES ITS PATH on the serial log - the registered
  `\_S5` or the fixed ports - among the last words it already flushes, because on q35 the two write the same
  value to the same port: QEMU's `\_S5` is (0, 0), and the FADT's PM1a control block is 0x604.
- [ ] THE CLOCKS ACROSS A SLEEP. P02M0198a computes the tick counter and `SYS_CLOCK_MONO_NS` when read, from
  the free-running counter less ONE sleep-offset term, and provides the one rebase operation and the
  suspended state that holds the clock; this item gives them their meaning and CALLS them:
  - THE MONOTONIC CLOCK EXCLUDES THE SLEEP: on every resume the kernel's entry calls P02M0198a's rebase with
    the counter read at suspend and at resume, so neither clock jumps. Ticks stay the ABI unit, and every
    Timer and every wait deadline stays on this clock - so a sleep makes no deadline pass: a five-second
    timeout armed before a night's sleep has the rest of its five seconds after resume, and the supervisors'
    deadlines do not all fire at once.
  - HELD FROM THE ENTRY TO THE REBASE: from the entry's counter reading at suspend until the rebase, every
    reading of the tick counter and of `SYS_CLOCK_MONO_NS` answers the monotonic value at suspend -
    P02M0198a's suspended state - and the rebase sets the offset and the atomic maximum from that value
    together. The suspend-to-idle wait runs no deadline check, the rebase is the first act on every exit
    from that wait, and an interrupt handled during the sleep sees the held clock - so no reading taken
    while the counter runs on raises the maximum past the suspend value, and no deadline fires in the sleep.
  - REBASED AFTER A COUNTER RESET: S3 resets the TSC. The rebase's arithmetic - the offset becomes the
    counter at resume less the monotonic value at suspend - is the same for a counter that kept running
    (suspend to idle) and one that restarted (S3), so there is no negative distance that stalls the ticks and
    no backlog claimed in one step.
  - A BOOT-TIME CLOCK that INCLUDES the sleep: `SYS_CLOCK_BOOT_NS`, nanoseconds since boot, read-only, with
    no timers on it (`clock_boot_ns()` in `rt`). A sleep's length is the counter difference where the
    counter kept running (suspend to idle) and the RTC difference where it did not (S3) - on a machine whose
    RTC is the Time and Alarm Device's (part c), known once its driver hands the base again in its `RESUME`
    step, when the boot-time clock takes the sleep in one step; nothing waits on that clock.
  - "FIRES ONCE ON RESUME" APPLIES ONLY TO WALL-CLOCK ALARMS - the scheduled wake of part c: one whose time
    passed during the sleep fires once, not once per missed period.
  - TimeService counts the wall clock forward on the boot-time clock instead of the tick counter, so a sleep
    is counted without anyone telling it, and re-reads `SYS_CLOCK_RTC` on the resume notice to correct the
    drift.

## P02M0197c - What wakes the machine, the devices that ask for sleep, and the policy

- [ ] THE WAKE SET, one list with its state, each source armed by its one owner:
  - the fixed buttons: PWRBTN always, SLPBTN where the machine has one - the kernel's PM1 enable;
  - the timed wake: on suspend to idle, the kernel's one-shot timer; on S3, the CMOS alarm with RTC_EN in
    PM1 enable, both set by the kernel from the entry's timed wake, within the alarm's reach (a day, further
    only with the FADT's day-of-month alarm field); on a machine with no CMOS RTC, the Time and Alarm
    Device's timers through its driver;
  - devices: a driver asked to arm wake does it on the device in its `SUSPEND` step (USB remote wakeup
    through the controller, PCI PME, a network device's wake) and marks its interrupt as a wake source
    through the Interrupt it holds, which the kernel leaves unmasked in suspend to idle; on an ACPI machine
    `platform-sleep`'s `prepare` (part a, step 5) has the ACPI service evaluate `_PRW` and `_DSW` or `_PSW`
    on the device's companion node (P02M0196's companion join) and set that GPE for wake through
    P02M0196b's GPE interface, the kernel owning the runtime and wake masks; its `wake` clears it.
- [ ] THE LID AND THE CONTROL-METHOD BUTTONS, as drivers: bound to the `PNP0C0D`, `PNP0C0C` and `PNP0C0E`
  platform devices (P02M0196a) and reaching `_LID` and their `Notify` values through the node-scoped channel
  P02M0196b mints with the claim. The lid's driver publishes a `platform-switch` provider - open or closed,
  and each change - which the policy consumes. The control-method power button is handed the system-power
  resource the Power-key drivers are handed (P02M0141's delivery path), so it powers off exactly as the fixed
  one does; the control-method sleep button is handed a `system-sleep` client by the same route and asks for
  a suspend, as the fixed sleep button's event does through DeviceManager, which holds a `system-sleep`
  client beside its SystemPower one.
- [ ] THE ACPI TIME AND ALARM DEVICE (`ACPI000E`), WHOLE - one device, one driver, on the same channel: its
  wake timers (`_GCP`, `_STV`, `_TIV`, `_GWS`, `_CWS`) programmed in its `SUSPEND` step from the sleep's timed
  wake; and its clock (`_GRT`) REGISTERED BEHIND THE KERNEL'S RTC READ on a machine where the kernel drives no
  RTC of its own (the FADT says the CMOS RTC is absent, or part d's development switch does): the driver
  reads `_GRT` at bind and in its `RESUME` step and hands the kernel the time as a wall-clock base, through a
  `clock-source` resource DeviceManager hands it at bind as it hands the Power-key drivers theirs;
  `SYS_CLOCK_RTC` then answers that base plus the boot-time clock, and 0 before it - as it answers an
  implausible date today. So TimeService, StorageService's timestamps and every other reader keep the one
  kernel read, none of them waits for another service at boot, and TimeService's re-read on the resume
  notice sees the TAD's clock; a TimeService that read 0 at its start reads again at each request until the
  kernel answers a time, so a base registered after it started reaches it without waiting for a resume.
  `_SRT` stays unused: nothing in this system writes a hardware clock.
- [ ] A SCHEDULED WAKE for a program granted it - "wake the machine at 03:00" - as a capability of its own in
  `system-sleep`, because a program that can wake the machine can empty its battery. It becomes the timed
  wake of the next sleep, and it is a wall-clock alarm (part b).
- [ ] THE POLICY LIVES IN THE POWER-STATE SERVICE (P02M0181's), not in SystemManager: it already holds the
  power sources and the battery level; it consumes the lid's `platform-switch` provider, asks DisplayService
  whether an external display is in use, and takes idleness from InputService's `input-activity` root - the
  `watch(idle-after)` stream of `idle` and `active` edges P02M0199c specifies for its dimming, NOT a second
  activity signal; whichever of P02M0197 and P02M0199 lands first builds that root as P02M0199c specifies and
  the other consumes it. Each is a role its manifest entry declares, and it requests through its own
  `system-sleep` client. Defaults the owner confirms when the part starts: closing the lid suspends unless an
  external display is in use; the sleep buttons suspend; the power buttons keep powering off; an idle timeout
  suspends on battery; a critical battery hibernates where part e is set up and otherwise - always, until
  part e exists, and whenever a hibernation is refused - powers off in order: the power-state service first
  arms the kernel's forced deadline with `system-power`'s `power-off-within`, then asks for ServiceManager's
  orderly sequence through `system-shutdown` (both P02M0198d's), never an immediate `system-power` call; for
  these two it gains a `system-power` and a `system-shutdown` client role. `_HOT` is not this policy's: it is
  P02M0198d's, whose ProcessorPowerService asks `system-sleep` to hibernate where part e is set up.
- [ ] `sleepctl`, a shipping tool beside `btctl`: suspend now (to idle or to RAM, with an optional timed
  wake) and hibernate now (part e), each answered at acceptance and followed, once `sleepctl` is thawed, by
  the record of how that sleep ended; the wake sources and their state, the inhibitors present, the cap a
  watchdog puts on a sleep, and the last sleep - how long it lasted, what woke it, each core's wakeups while
  parked, and where a failed one stopped.

## P02M0197d - Verification of parts a to c

- [ ] QEMU x86_64 with S3 offered (`-global ICH9-LPC.disable_s3=0`, set explicitly rather than trusted as a
  default). THE HOST'S ORACLE IS QEMU'S RUN STATE, WHICH IS DURABLE: the harness keeps no standing QMP
  connection - its helper connects per command and skips events, and QMP sends an event only to a client
  connected at that moment - so `query-status`, polled, answers `suspended` while the guest is in S3 and
  `running` after the wake, beside the kernel's `sleep: entered` and `sleep: resumed` lines. Wake by
  `system_wakeup`, and by an RTC alarm the guest armed through `sleepctl` - QEMU wakes a suspended guest on
  the RTC only with RTC_EN set, so this case also proves the kernel's arming. A KEY WAKE FROM S3 IS CHECKED
  FIRST, NOT ASSUMED: the harness's keyboards are `usb-kbd` behind `qemu-xhci` and `virtio-keyboard-pci`; QEMU
  10.0 requests a system wakeup from its PS/2 keyboard, which this system does not drive, and delivers a USB
  key to a suspended controller only as a port change. The outcome is written here, and until a key is shown
  to wake the guest the gate does not require it. After each wake: the network answers a ping, the disk reads
  back a file written before, the display presents a frame, a counter program shows it did not run while the
  machine slept, a line typed at the serial console is answered without any other wake, and - on a boot with
  an empty native hot-plug port, as the interactive profile has - a function `device_add`-ed into it after the
  wake is seen by the kernel, which proves the restored Slot Control. With `swtpm` behind the CRB front-end:
  PCRs 0 to 7, read before the sleep, read back unchanged after it - the firmware's fallback would have
  extended an error separator into each - and a secret sealed to PCR 4 before the sleep unseals after it. PCRs
  16 and 23 are not among what a `Shutdown(STATE)` saves - the PC Client profile saves PCRs 0 to 15 - so they
  read zero after any resume from S3, however the driver behaves, and the case asserts that as well.
- [ ] Suspend to idle on all three targets, with a wake the guest arms itself - the timed wake, through
  `sleepctl` - because QEMU never leaves its running state for it: `query-status` stays `running`, and
  `system_wakeup` is refused. THE HOST'S ORACLE IS THE SERIAL LOG'S TIMING, the entry having put its
  `sleep: entered` line on the wire before the cores park and its `sleep: resumed` line before any driver
  resumed - the same in both of P02M0191c's cases, so the three targets read both: aarch64 and riscv64 with
  the kernel owning its console, x86_64 with the 16550 driver holding COM1 once P02M0191c has landed. The
  harness timestamps each line as it arrives; the counter program that prints every 100 ms is SILENT for the
  whole host-measured interval between the kernel's `sleep: entered` and `sleep: resumed` lines; that interval
  is at least the armed wake time within a stated margin; and the counter resumes at its next value rather
  than with a catch-up burst. Those three hold for the freeze alone, so THE PARKING IS CHECKED TOO, in the
  last sleep's record read through `sleepctl`: the only wakeups any core took while parked are the timed wake
  and the IPIs that end the other cores' park - no periodic tick, no housekeeping bound, no device interrupt
  outside the wake set. The same checks after it as for S3.
- [ ] The transaction's edges: a driver made to refuse (a test hook) aborting the suspend with the system
  still running and the refusal named; a job stopped with Ctrl+Z before a sleep still stopped after the
  thaw, and resumed by `fg`; a Timer armed before a sleep firing after its remaining awake time and not at
  resume; after an S3, a wait armed after the resume completing on time (the rebased tick); the boot-time
  clock advanced by at least the sleep and the monotonic clock by less; the wall clock within two seconds of
  the host's after each.
- [ ] Soft-off from `\_S5`, each half proven by QEMU's exit and by the kernel's line naming the path it took:
  with the ACPI service running, the registered `\_S5`; with nothing registered - the development switch
  below refusing the registration, never the ACPI service killed after it registered, whose value stays in
  force - the fixed ports.
- [ ] THE WATCHDOG ACROSS A SLEEP, with P02M0200's device and `-action watchdog=pause`, read back with
  `query-status` (the harness keeps no standing QMP listener for an event): a suspend to idle longer than the
  watchdog's timeout, and the status `running` after it; `watchdog` after a resume made to hang (a test
  hook). THIS CASE IS CARRIED BY WHICHEVER OF P02M0197 AND P02M0200 LANDS SECOND; the first to land

- [ ] The lid and buttons through P02M0196d's SSDT fixture: the harness closing and opening the lid and
  pressing the control-method buttons. THE TIME AND ALARM DEVICE through the same fixture, on a boot whose
  development switch (below) makes the kernel treat the CMOS RTC as absent - q35's FADT always declares one,

## P02M0197e - Hibernation, a part of its own

Suspend to idle and S3 do not depend on it; until it exists a critical battery powers the machine off
through the policy's orderly power-off (part c) and `_HOT` through P02M0198's `_CRT` sequence. It starts after
part b, from these CONSTRAINTS, read in this tree:
  - THE KERNEL HAS NO STORAGE PATH: the disk is served by a driver process and StorageService, so the image
    must be written by processes the transaction otherwise stops;
  - THE KEY: the TPM is reachable only through P02M0190's driver process and TpmService - on aarch64 and
    riscv64 through a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` node and P02M0196a's device-tree half, which QEMU's
    `tpm-tis-device` provides on both; P02M0190 excludes measured boot of the loader and kernel (PCRs 8-15
    are reserved for it), so no PCR policy can tie a key to the kernel being resumed; TpmService seals to ONE
    PCR's present value, binds every sealed secret to the COMPONENT that sealed it (an `unseal` by another
    answers `other-component`) and admits at most 96 bytes of secret;
  - THE SPACE: the LiberFS container is a partition or the whole device and reserves no area.
THE CHOSEN APPROACH:
- [ ] A SNAPSHOT IN THE KERNEL, WRITTEN BY USERSPACE. The transaction runs as for S3 up to the drivers'
  suspend; the kernel's entry is then called with a SNAPSHOT state, which copies every in-use page into free
  memory - refused, and named, when free memory cannot hold the copy - and RETURNS TWICE: once in the running
  machine after the copy, and once in the restored machine, saying which. After the first return
  ServiceManager resumes only the disk's driver, the TPM driver and an image writer; StorageService stays
  holding writes, so nothing changes a filesystem after the snapshot. The writer seals the key, encrypts and
  writes the copy, and the machine enters `\_S4` where the firmware offers it and otherwise powers off
  (through `\_S5` on an ACPI machine).
- [ ] THE SPACE: a dedicated GPT partition of a LiberSystem hibernation type, at least the size of memory,
  created where hibernation is set up - never a region inside a LiberFS volume, whose format stays as it is.
  A machine without one has hibernation "not set up", and the policy powers off.
- [ ] RESTORE BY A FRESH BOOT, BEFORE ANY WRITABLE MOUNT. The loader stays out of it: storage, the TPM and the
  cryptography already exist above the kernel, and the loader would need all three again. Early in the boot
  chain nothing is mounted writable until the partition's header has been checked: the system volume is
  mounted READ-ONLY until then, which is enough to load TpmService and the image component. A valid image is
  read, its key unsealed, the image authenticated and decrypted, every binding the fresh boot started is
  STOPPED, and the kernel is handed a whole-memory replacement: a privileged call that copies the
  pages to their frames from a trampoline in frames the image does not use and jumps into the image's
  kernel, which continues along the resume path of a machine whose devices lost power - part b's S3 path on
  x86_64, the same restore of the kernel's own devices and part b's per-core resume path on the other two -
  and its drivers, suspended, get `RESUME`. The header is invalidated before the jump, so an image is
  resumed at most once.
- [ ] THE IMAGE IS ENCRYPTED AND AUTHENTICATED, because it holds every secret that was in memory, and its key
  is never on disk in the clear. A fresh image key encrypts the image and is stored in the header WRAPPED by
  a 32-byte key-encryption key; only the key-encryption key is sealed through P02M0190's TpmService - within
  its 96 bytes - to PCR 4's present value, the loader as the firmware measured it. ONE COMPONENT SEALS AND
  UNSEALS IT: the image writer and the restorer are the same program, holding a `tpm-seal` grant, because
  TpmService refuses an `unseal` by any other component. That ties the image to this machine's TPM and this
  loader and NOT to the kernel - a limit written down rather than hidden. Where TpmService cannot seal (no
  TPM bound, or `owner-hierarchy-unavailable`) hibernation is not set up. Whether a passphrase typed
  at resume is offered instead is ASKED OF THE OWNER WHEN THIS PART STARTS.
- [ ] AN IMAGE REFUSED, and the machine booted fresh, when it was modified (its authentication fails), when
  its key-encryption key does not unseal (`policy-refused`: the loader changed), when the system image was
  updated since (the system image's digest is recorded in the authenticated header), or when the hardware it
  describes is not the hardware present (the memory map, the PCI functions and the core count, recorded
  there too).
- [ ] HYBRID SLEEP once both halves exist: the image written, then S3 instead of the power-off, and the image
  invalidated on an S3 resume - so a battery that dies in the night loses nothing.
- [ ] Verification: QEMU x86_64 with S4 offered (`-global ICH9-LPC.disable_s4=0`) and `swtpm` behind the CRB
  front-end. QEMU exits on S4 exactly as on a soft-off, so THIS GATE HOLDS ITS OWN QMP CONNECTION open from
  before the request until QEMU exits and reads `SUSPEND_DISK` on it - the one evidence that tells the two
  apart; where S4 is not offered, QEMU's exit and the kernel's line naming the power-off path. Then a new
  QEMU on the same disk resuming the image - the same program, the same counter; a modified image, an image
  from another system image and an image from another machine each refused, and the machine booted fresh;
  the boot after a resume sees no image left to resume. aarch64 and riscv64 alike, with `swtpm` behind
  `tpm-tis-device`, once P02M0190's driver binds it through the device tree; a machine with no TPM reports
  "not set up" rather than passing.

EXCLUDES: vendor sleep controls reached through WMI; "connected standby", in which the machine keeps network
connections alive while it sleeps - it needs a network device that can do it and a policy for what may run
while the screen is dark, and is asked for separately if it is wanted.

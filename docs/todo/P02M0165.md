# P02M0165 - A driver that stops answering looks exactly like one that is busy

Status: COMPLETE (2026-08-29).

Previously: Status: COMPLETE (2026-08-27). SUPERVISION, PLANNED STOP AND ACCOUNTING. SIXTH OF SEVEN - see P02M0098.

Proved by the boot - every driver answering a sequenced ping in its own loop, a clean shutdown in
reverse dependency order, per-binding Domain counters in the incident capture - by thirty-six
`driver-binding` tests, twenty-three `driver-protocol` tests, and by four things watched to fail: a
driver made to stop answering while staying alive, the release latch removed, the deadline bounds
removed, and a planted late completion.

## Goal

Notice a driver that has stopped making progress, stop one on purpose without losing its in-flight
work, and keep the resource counters honest across every crash and restart.

## What is there now

- **AN EXIT IS NOTICED AND A HANG IS NOT.** P02M0162 supervises a driver that exits. A driver that is
  alive and wedged - a control queue that never completes, a request outstanding forever - is
  indistinguishable from an idle one, and an idle device is not a fault.
- **THERE IS NO PLANNED STOP.** Shutdown revokes; nothing asks a driver to drain first. Storage
  flushes because `StorageService` flushes, not because the driver was told to.
- **A HEARTBEAT EXISTS AND ACCEPTS ANYTHING AS A PONG.** `rt::heartbeat` sends the reserved opcode
  and returns true for `Polled::Message { .. }` whatever came back, so a driver answering with
  unrelated traffic reads as responsive.
- **THE MECHANISM FOR ACCOUNTING EXISTS AND IS NOT BEING USED.** This item said "a child `Domain` per
  binding already accounts for memory, handles, threads, IPC, DMA and stack", and that is wrong.
  `Domain` does account for all of those, and `spawn_in(elf, bootstrap, domain)` is how a manager puts
  a child under one - but `launch_one` calls `spawn(elf, driver_side)`, which is `spawn_in(.., 0)`,
  and domain 0 means the SPAWNER'S OWN Domain. Every driver in the system is therefore charged to
  DeviceManager, and a driver that exhausts memory exhausts the manager's budget rather than its own.
  So the sub-Domain is a change, not a description. What is genuinely outside `Domain` either way is
  device-specific: MMIO windows, IRQ vectors, IOMMU grants.
- **DEVICEMANAGER'S OWN CRASH IS UNHANDLED.** It holds every claim in the system.
- **AND THE SYSTEM VOLUME'S IDENTITY IS P02M0164'S, ONE MILESTONE EARLIER.** It was an item here
  until the audit of 2026-08-26 put it where its first consumer is: P02M0164 deletes the four block
  slots, and the moment they go, "which disk is the root" has to have an answer. This milestone needs
  the same answer for a different reason - a cold reconstruction must find the volume the boot found -
  and it consumes what P02M0164 built rather than building a second one.

## Work

- [x] **M1 - `PING`/`PONG { sequence }`, answered by the control loop.** The payload carries the
  sequence and nothing else: P02M0161 put the generation in the frame HEADER, on every frame in both
  directions, so a second copy in the body is a second thing that can disagree with the first. The existing
  `rt::heartbeat` counts any message as a pong, which makes a busy driver and a wedged one look the
  same. On a driver channel the reply is the typed frame, handled by the same loop as every other
  event rather than by a second one. A deadline is declared by the registry entry and is about the
  driver's control path, not whether its device is busy: a driver may not pet its watchdog through an
  unrelated child.

  THE CADENCE COMES OUT OF THE DEADLINE rather than being a second number to keep in step with it:
  the manager pings at half the entry's declared deadline ROUNDED UP, because half of a deadline of
  1 is 0 and a period of zero is either a busy loop or, through the same `wait_any` rule, no timeout
  at all - the two failures this bound exists to prevent, reached by arithmetic. So an entry that asks for a longer
  deadline is pinged less often and never the other way round, and a driver always has one whole
  period to answer in before the deadline it declared.

  The registry requires `0 < deadline <= MAX_HEARTBEAT_DEADLINE`, and that maximum is not a new
  number: ServiceManager already carries a justified one - 100 ticks, *"~1s at the 100-ticks-per-
  second monotonic clock"*, with `service.watchdog-ticks` as the operator's override. A driver
  deadline is the same question about a different subject, so it takes the same default and the same
  ceiling rather than a second constant that drifts from it. The lower bound is not pedantry: `wait_any` reads a deadline of 0 as NO TIMEOUT, so an entry declaring zero would not be
  supervised strictly - it would not be supervised at all, and would look like the most responsive
  driver in the machine. The upper bound is one shared policy constant, for the same reason
  P02M0162's bind budget has one: an entry that can name any deadline it likes is an entry that can
  opt out.

  BUILT 2026-08-27, and it cost three defects that are worth more than the feature.

  ONE: `wait_any` ANSWERS WITH THE FIRST READY INDEX, so a handle that is always ready starves
  everything after it. The first helper put the driver's service channel ahead of the manager's
  channel in the set, and every virtio-blk in the machine was declared wedged while serving
  StorageService as fast as it could - a watchdog killing precisely the drivers doing work. The
  manager's channel is DRAINED first, every pass, before anything is waited on.

  TWO: A NODE THAT IS ALREADY `Online` IS NOT IN THE BRING-UP WAIT SET, so nothing was reading its
  channel and its `PONG` sat there unread. Every driver was declared wedged at SEQUENCE 1 - which is
  the shape of a supervisor listening on the wrong end, not of a driver that stopped. The answer is
  read where the question is asked: `tick_heartbeats` drains the channel of every supervised node.

  THREE: THE MANAGER WOKE EXACTLY AT ITS OWN DEADLINE, drained the channel, and THEN asked whether
  the driver had answered - with the answer sitting unread in the node's queue. So a driver that
  replied on time was declared wedged and its own pong then arrived to a manager that had stopped
  waiting, reported as "a ping nobody asked". The heartbeat is control-path state, not a lifecycle
  event, and it is settled where it is read. A MISMATCH still becomes an event, because that is the
  case worth reporting.

  Every serving driver answers in ITS OWN loop - `serve_or_answer` for the ones parked on a single
  channel, `wait_or_answer` for the ones already waiting on several - because a second loop answering
  pings would keep answering after the first had stopped working.

  WATCHED TO FAIL: a driver made to stop answering while staying alive is caught -
  `virtio_snd stopped answering its control path inside the deadline its registry entry declares`.
  The three refusals `rt::heartbeat` accepts have a test each, and the deadline's bounds are watched
  to refuse both 0 and 101.

- [x] **M2 - a wedged driver is torn down like a crashed one.** The teardown is P02M0162's
  transaction and the retry and quarantine counter is P02M0162's too; what this adds is the reason
  for starting it, and a bounded diagnostic capture taken BEFORE the process is gone - which is what
  P02M0166 later displays. Bounded means a fixed list, written here so it cannot grow into a
  diagnostic subsystem: the binding id and generation, the state it was in, the cause, the last
  opcode received and how long ago, the restart count, and the Domain's counters at that moment.
  No device payload, no memory dump, no log excerpt.

  BUILT 2026-08-27. The teardown needed nothing new: a `Wedged` event carries `handshake-timeout`
  into the same `advance` a crash goes through, so the transaction, the retry counter and the
  quarantine rule are P02M0162's unchanged - only the reason for starting differs.

  THE CAPTURE IS TAKEN BEFORE ANYTHING IS GIVEN BACK, which is the half that is easy to get wrong:
  the Domain's counters cannot be read once the Domain is killed and the process cannot be asked
  once it is signalled, so a capture taken after the rollback is a capture OF the rollback.

  It is also PRINTED, not only stored. A capture nobody can read is a capture nobody takes
  seriously, and this program had no formatter - every other line it prints is a fixed byte string.
  Measured, on a driver made to stop answering while staying alive:

      incident virtio_snd at 0:9.0 generation 1, online, last opcode 4 557 tick(s) ago,
      attempt 1, domain memory 61440 peak 61440, handles 12, threads 1, dma 32768
        cause: it did not answer inside the window

  "never sent one" is a DIFFERENT FACT from "a long time ago" and is carried as one: a binding whose
  driver said nothing at all is a different failure from one that went quiet. So is "no domain
  counters were readable" against zeros, which would say the driver used nothing.

- [x] **M3 - one `STOP`, and it means all of it - including that it is not a crash.** MET AGAIN (2026-08-29) - the acknowledgement follows the drain, an unsolicited STOPPED is refused, and a clean-stop claim is not made before the teardown answers; see the audit sections. Take no new
  work, finish or explicitly abandon what was accepted, flush, call the existing `device_quiesced`,
  answer `STOPPED`. The node carries a stop INTENT into `Stopping`, because P02M0162's table sends a
  confirmed teardown on to `Backoff` and then back to `Binding` - which is right for a driver that
  died and exactly wrong for one that was asked to stop: the operator stops it and it starts again.
  The intent is what `Stopping` resolves against, and there are only four of them:

  | intent | teardown confirmed | teardown NOT confirmed |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | crash or hang | `Backoff`, or `Failed` when the budget is spent | `Quarantined` |
  | dependency lost | `DependencyPending` | `Quarantined` |
  | operator disable | `Disabled` (P02M0166) | `Quarantined` |
  | shutdown | no further state is entered at all - the manager is going away, so there is no next binding to describe | `Quarantined`, and the report says so |

  An unconfirmed teardown ends in `Quarantined` whatever the intent was, because what is unknown is
  whether the device is still live, and no intent changes that. A separate `QUIESCE`
  followed by `STOP` is two round trips for one transition and the second has nothing left to do. On
  a planned stop the provider is withdrawn and new connections refused FIRST, so nothing arrives
  during the drain. A deadline that expires forces the revocation and says it was forced - never that
  a clean flush happened.

- [x] **M4 - shutdown traverses dependents in reverse.** A controller being stopped stops its children
  first. NOT bus removal: P02M0163 establishes that the scan runs once and no removal event exists, so
  the only ways a controller goes away are a shutdown and a teardown, and both are asked for from
  inside. The panic path may force revoke and must not claim otherwise.

  M3 AND M4 BUILT 2026-08-27, together, because a stop that does not order its dependents is a
  forced revocation with a courteous name. `Opcode::Stop` / `Opcode::Stopped`, one round trip;
  `StopIntent` with the four the table names, and `confirmed_lands_at` is the one place that decides.
  `Shutdown` answers `None` deliberately - the manager is going away, so there is no next binding for
  a state to be about, and the boot log shows exactly that: `the node is stopping`.

  THE ORDER IS BY HOW MANY OTHER NODES' KINDS A NODE REQUIRES, dependents first. A cycle cannot
  appear - `system-manifest` refuses one at registry-build time - so one ordering pass is enough and
  there is no fixed point to chase. The provider is withdrawn and its connections refused BEFORE the
  drain, so nothing arrives while a driver is being asked to finish.

  A PLANNED STOP IS NEVER RETRIED, whatever the cause reads as: the whole point of asking a driver
  to stop is that it stays stopped. And a deadline that expires forces the revocation and SAYS it
  was forced - `nothing here says its work was flushed` - rather than reporting a clean flush.

  Measured on a real shutdown: `virtio_blk stopped cleanly: the machine is going down`.

- [x] **M5 - one child `Domain` per binding, plus a small device-specific ledger.** MET AGAIN (2026-08-29) - the snapshot carries the device-specific holdings and the release order is fixed; see the audit sections. DeviceManager
  creates a sub-Domain per binding and launches the driver with `spawn_in` rather than `spawn`, which
  is the change: today every driver is charged to the manager's own Domain. The Domain then accounts
  for memory, handles, threads, IPC, DMA and stack and `DomainStats` already reports them - a second
  set of books for the same resources is work that can only disagree with the first.
  What it does not cover is MMIO windows, IRQ vectors and IOMMU grants, and that ledger is the only
  new accounting - and it cannot live only in DeviceManager, because M6 kills that process and
  expects the next one to know what is still quarantined. The kernel's claim record already tracks
  these resources in order to revoke them, so the ledger is RECONSTRUCTED from it rather than kept a
  second time: one source of truth, and the one that outlives the manager. A failed bind or a restart
  returns both to baseline ONLY where the teardown was
  confirmed. A `Quarantined` node keeps its resources charged and out of circulation, which is the
  whole point of quarantining them - P02M0098 M5 makes the confirmation the precondition for reuse,
  and an accounting that zeroes on the way past would say the frames and vectors are available while
  the device may still be writing to them. Exhaustion is a typed bind failure rather than a
  half-online driver.

  THE DOMAIN HALF IS BUILT 2026-08-27. Every driver was launched with `spawn`, which is
  `spawn_in(.., 0)`, and domain 0 means the SPAWNER'S OWN Domain - so every driver in the system was
  charged to DeviceManager and one that exhausted memory exhausted the manager's budget rather than
  its own. Each binding now creates a child Domain and launches into it; the Domain is rolled back
  LAST, after the process is gone and the claim is given back, because killing it first would take
  the process out from under a teardown still reading its handles.

  Not resource-bounded here: limits are ResourceManager's subject and a number invented at that line
  would be a policy nobody declared. What the Domain buys without one is ATTRIBUTION, and the capture
  proves it is real - `domain memory 61440 peak 61440, handles 12, threads 1, dma 32768` is the sound
  driver's own accounting, which before this was the manager's.

  THE DEVICE LEDGER IS M6's, because it is the same read: the privileged snapshot returns the
  generation, the claim state and the resources still held, so polling the state and rebuilding the
  ledger are one call rather than two sources that can disagree.

- [x] **M6 - DeviceManager can crash and be rebuilt.** MET AGAIN (2026-08-29) - see the audit sections at the end of this file. ServiceManager owns its `Domain` and the driver
  subtree under it; if it dies, ServiceManager withdraws the provider roots, kills that Domain and
  only THEN starts a cold reconstruction - otherwise the old drivers still hold claims the new
  manager is about to hand out.

  AND KILLING A DOMAIN IS NOT INSTANT, so the reconstruction reads each claim's state before binding
  rather than assuming it is free. `Domain::kill()` marks the subtree and terminates its processes,
  and `Process::terminate()` closes each handle table SYNCHRONOUSLY - so the claim handle's last
  logical close, and with it the start of P02M0098's forced teardown, happens promptly rather than
  whenever a reference is finally dropped. What is asynchronous is the rest: the terminated threads
  observe the kill at their next scheduling point, and the teardown itself has to confirm the device
  is quiet. This item first blamed the delay on the last references, which is the wrong half. Either
  way a new manager can legitimately arrive at a device that is `Releasing`. Treating that as a refusal would be a permanent `Failed` for a state that was about
  to clear on its own, because P02M0162 classifies `claim-refused` as not retryable - a transient
  condition promoted to a terminal one by two correct rules meeting. So, and "waits and then binds"
  is not one of the branches - a bind is attempted only on an OBSERVED `Free`, never on a deadline
  having merely passed:

  | state read | what the reconstruction does |
  | --- | --- |
  | `Free` | bind normally |
  | `Releasing` | re-read until `Free`, bounded by the incident deadline this milestone already has |
  | `Releasing` still, at that deadline | do NOT acquire: `Quarantined` with `teardown-unconfirmed`, which is the conservative answer and the true one - nothing observed the device go quiet |
  | | AND THE KERNEL LATCHES IT TOO. A binding marked `Quarantined` in userspace while the kernel's claim stays `Releasing` is two authorities again: a late teardown reaching `Free` would put the frames and vectors back into circulation against a state already declared terminal. The claim latches `Releasing -> Quarantined` itself, atomically; a completion after that is recorded as late and releases nothing |
  | `Quarantined` | adopt it; it is already terminal |
  | `Claimed` | an invariant violation, reported as one - a correct `domain_kill` cannot leave it here, and a manager that quietly rebound over it would be handing out a device somebody still holds |

  THE DEADLINE IS THE CLAIM'S OWN AND IT IS MINTED AT THE RELEASE, not inherited from the bind. "The
  same hard deadline" was the wrong contract and it fails in both directions: a driver that ran an
  hour in `Online` has a bind deadline long past, so a release would be born already expired; and
  when DEVICEMANAGER dies, the last logical close starts the release before any new manager exists to
  supply one. So the claim record holds a bounded teardown DURATION - a constant of the kernel's, not
  a value handed in - and every `Claimed -> Releasing` stamps a fresh immutable absolute deadline
  from it. Completion before it: `Free`. The deadline first: `Quarantined`, permanently, and a late
  completion releases nothing. The snapshot returns that deadline along with the state, so a new
  manager knows how long it is reasonable to wait rather than inventing a bound of its own. One race
  test, both outcomes.

  The new manager has no claim handle to be woken on, because the old one died with its Domain, so
  this is a bounded poll by device index from the wait loop it already runs - not a new mechanism and
  not an unbounded one. ONE READ ANSWERS BOTH QUESTIONS: the privileged snapshot returns the
  generation, the claim state and the resources still held for that device, so polling the state and
  rebuilding the ledger are the same call rather than two sources that can disagree. It never inherits a live claim it cannot account for, and it finds
  the same system volume again because P02M0164 made that a question with an answer.

  BUILT 2026-08-27, in three parts, and one of them is a kernel change this item is the reason for.

  THE OWNERSHIP. ServiceManager creates a Domain for DeviceManager and keeps the handle; every
  driver lives in a child Domain of that one, so killing it takes the drivers with it. When
  DeviceManager dies the subtree is killed BEFORE anything else is decided - a manager gone while
  its drivers keep running leaves device claims held by processes nobody supervises, and whatever
  happens next must not happen with those live.

  THE CLAIM'S OWN DEADLINE, IN THE KERNEL. `Claimed -> Releasing` stamps a fresh immutable absolute
  deadline from `RELEASE_DEADLINE_TICKS`, a constant of the kernel's - because the party that would
  hand one in is exactly the party that may have just died, and a bind deadline inherited from an
  hour ago would be born already expired. `device::snapshot` LATCHES `Releasing -> Quarantined` at
  that deadline, atomically, under the same lock every other claim decision is taken under; a
  completion after the latch is recorded as late and releases nothing.

  WATCHED TO FAIL: with the late-completion rule removed, a teardown that finishes after the latch
  reaches `Free` - the frames and vectors back in circulation against a state already declared
  terminal.

  THE READ. `SYS_DEVICE_CLAIM_SNAPSHOT` answers by device INDEX, gated on the DeviceManager
  privilege, because a reconstruction holds no claim handle - the old one died with its Domain.
  `observe_claim` implements all five branches, and a bind is attempted only on an OBSERVED `Free`:
  `Releasing` comes back rather than acquiring, `Quarantined` is adopted, and `Claimed` is reported
  as the invariant violation it is rather than bound over.

  WHAT IS NOT HERE, AND IT IS NOT AN OVERSIGHT. ServiceManager does not RELAUNCH DeviceManager:
  its restart policy is `escalate`, and a cold reconstruction would need to load volume drivers from
  the system volume that the dead manager's own drivers were serving. That circularity is real and
  this milestone does not resolve it; what it resolves is that the reconstruction, whenever it is
  built, cannot bind over a device somebody still holds or one nothing observed go quiet.

- [x] **M7 - a named table of the races that are reachable.** **MET AGAIN (2026-08-29)** - each named race now asserts the post-race baseline through the real teardown ledger: one claim owner, every handle closed once, the Domain killed last. Reachable in THIS tree, which is what
  makes it a table and not a wish: a driver answering `READY` after its deadline, a crash between
  publish and subscribe, a crash with DMA in flight, a watchdog expiry racing a clean exit, a
  DeviceManager restart with drivers still live, and a `STOPPED` that arrives after the deadline it
  was supposed to beat - which must NOT turn a forced teardown back into a clean one. Once the forced path has been
  entered the outcome is decided; a late answer is recorded as late and changes nothing, because the
  alternative is a report that says a driver flushed when the manager had already stopped waiting. A device removed mid-bind is NOT on it - there is no
  removal event to race with. A cartesian product of every event against
  every other is a way of never finishing; a named list is a plan. After each: at most one claim
  owner, no stale provider, no leaked process, handle, vector, mapping or counter.

  BUILT 2026-08-27, six of them, in `driver-binding` where they can be DRIVEN rather than argued
  about. What every one of them turns out to rest on is the same two mechanisms, which is the
  useful finding: the transition TABLE refuses the late answer, and the GENERATION on every event
  refuses the one from a binding that is over. Neither needed a flag anybody has to remember to set.

  | race | what refuses it |
  | --- | --- |
  | a `READY` after its deadline | no `Backoff -> Online` edge; late is not up |
  | a crash between publish and subscribe | the provider id carries its binding's generation, so the next binding's publications are distinguishable |
  | a watchdog expiry racing a clean exit | the queue orders them; the second finds a record that has already moved |
  | a manager restart with drivers still live | a node holding no binding drains every event from the previous one |
  | a `STOPPED` after the deadline it was to beat | no edge out of `Quarantined`, and the cause still says the teardown was not confirmed |

  AND WHAT AN EMPTIED PUBLICATION IS OWED IS NOW DRIVEN TOO (2026-09-02). The crash-between-publish-
  and-subscribe row proved the SELECTION and the TRANSFER - which slots a binding's end takes, and
  that every one of them comes back to the caller. The two effects that make a withdrawal mean
  anything, the channel closed and the withdrawal announced, sat in a loop in DeviceManager where no
  host test could reach them: deleting either from that loop's body left every race test in this
  table green while a consumer held a channel whose server was gone, or a subscriber kept metadata
  for a publication that no longer exists. That is this item's own "no stale provider, no leaked
  handle", unproved. `driver_binding::apply_withdrawal` is that loop now, with the order it has to
  keep - closed before announced, so a consumer told its provider is gone cannot then find the
  channel open - and the race test drives it against a recorder. What is left in the manager is a
  syscall and a send, neither of which is a choice.

  A CRASH WITH DMA IN FLIGHT is not in this file because it is already tested where it belongs:
  `kernel.object.dma_buffer.a_dead_drivers_dma_frames_wait_for_its_device_to_be_reset` and
  `a_full_hold_table_leaks_a_dead_drivers_frames_rather_than_recycling_them` are the kernel's, on the

## Definition of done

- A driver that stops making progress is detected and torn down, and an idle driver is not. Where it
  lands is the table's answer and not a choice this line gets to restate: a CONFIRMED teardown goes
  to `Backoff` and then a rebind, or to `Failed` when the attempts are spent; `Quarantined` is for a
  teardown that was NOT confirmed, and for nothing else.
- A frame with the wrong opcode, a stale generation or an out-of-order sequence does NOT reset the
  watchdog, with a test per case. This is the whole of what `rt::heartbeat` gets wrong today - it
  returns true for any `Polled::Message` - so a `PING`/`PONG` that was only added, and never proved
  to refuse the three things the old one accepted, would leave a wedged driver looking healthy for
  the same reason it does now.
- A planned stop that completes leaves the binding at rest and does not restart the driver.
- One `STOP` drains or cancels in-flight work and reaches `STOPPED` before revocation, with the
  provider withdrawn first; a forced teardown says it was forced.
- Every driver runs in its own sub-Domain rather than the manager's, and every resource is charged to
  a binding through that Domain plus the device ledger. A failed bind or a restart whose teardown was
  CONFIRMED returns the counters to baseline, asserted rather than assumed; one that was not leaves
  them charged, which is what `Quarantined` means. Not every field: `DomainStats::memory_peak` is a
  high-water mark and is meant never to fall, so an assertion that "every counter returns to
  baseline" would be asserting against a diagnostic doing its job.
- DeviceManager can be killed and reconstructed without a claim being left owned by nothing, and the
  reconstruction finds the same system volume.
- The named race table is registered as a gate and has been watched to fail.

## What this milestone refuses, and why

- **Suspend and resume of the whole machine.** Planned stop is what a driver needs to be taken away
  safely. System power states are a separate subject with their own firmware surface, and nothing
  here needs them.
- **A general watchdog service.** The deadline belongs to the binding, beside the thing it is
  watching.
- **Predicting hardware failure.** A declared number of failed rebinds ending in `Failed` is a policy
  anyone can read. Anything cleverer is guessing.

## What the 2026-08-28 audit found

Eight findings. Two fixed, six open.

FIXED:
- **The development configuration did not compile.** `check-development-build.sh` failed with
  `E0425: cannot find value \`bind\`` in `dev_channel.rs` plus an unused `bind` refused by the
  deny-warnings policy. And the driver never serviced the heartbeat in its normal loop: `pump` waited
  on `[irq, bytes]` with the manager's channel in neither the polling nor the wait path, while its
  registry entry declares `heartbeat-deadline = 100`. The only handling was in `adopt`, where `bind`
  was out of scope, into a 16-byte buffer - smaller than the 20-byte header, so it could not have
  decoded a 24-byte PING even once the name error was fixed. New `heartbeat(bind, bootstrap)` drains
  and answers without blocking, `pump` waits on `[irq, bytes, bootstrap]`, and `adopt` takes `bind`
  with a 64-byte buffer. The gate now passes.
- **Shutdown order was by requirement COUNT, not reverse dependency order.** `Reverse(requires.len())`
  is not depth: in A requires B requires C, A and B both declare one requirement and B can be stopped
  before its dependent A. `stop_all` now relaxes a depth over the provider graph and sorts deepest
  first. The eight shipping entries declare no `requires`, so every node ties and the old behaviour
  was indistinguishable from correct - which is why nothing caught it.

OPEN: driver-side STOP acknowledges before the drain it certifies (no driver calls `device_quiesced`
on STOP; `virtio_blk` skips `flush_request`, `virtio_snd` skips its stream cleanup, two drivers never
send STOPPED); a valid STOPPED is recorded as `DriverExited` and `stopped cleanly` can print before a
quarantine; the M5 device ledger does not exist and `release_claim` frees the MSI vector before the
IOMMU detach confirms; ServiceManager never kills DeviceManager's subtree on the `escalate` path its
manifest selects; a reconstruction seeing `Releasing` never re-reads; and the named race tests assert
on enum values rather than driving the decisions.

## What the 2026-08-30 audit found

Two findings, both accepted and both fixed.

1. **`STOPPED` certified hardware quiescence that no driver established.** The acknowledgement is
   defined as "everything I accepted is finished or abandoned and my device is quiet", and the kernel
   explicitly cannot verify the claim - it relies on the caller having just stopped the device. No
   driver was stopping one: the virtio reset ran at BRING-UP and a planned stop left the queues live,
   virtio-gpu and xHCI passed no device capability at all, and the xHCI controller was left RUNNING
   with its command, event and transfer rings all DMA.

   The transport's own reset - status zero, and the device reads back zero - is what a virtio driver
   now performs on the stop path. It is reached through the device recorded at bring-up rather than
   threaded through four loop signatures, so every virtio driver gets it by construction rather than
   by remembering to. The xHCI clears Run/Stop and waits for HCHalted, which is the specification's
   own handshake and the same pair its reset performs.

   And the acknowledgement is REFUSED when quiescence is not established: the finishing helper takes

2. **The publish/crash/subscribe race never executed the catalogue decision.** The named test
   compared two provider identities and said "whatever the catalogue does next". The decision now has
   a host-testable form the production path SHARES rather than mirrors: the identity rule - the same
   function AND the same generation - is one function, called both by the catalogue's withdrawal and
   by the model, and the test drives the sequence in order. Publish before anyone asks; the binding
   ends and the publication goes with it; a subscriber arriving then finds nothing rather than a
   server that is gone; the device binds again and publishes; the subscriber reaches the replacement;
   and withdrawing the previous binding a second time takes nothing.

   The model carries the identity rules and not the channel bookkeeping, so closing a withdrawn
   provider's handle is still only exercised in the guest. That is stated rather than implied.

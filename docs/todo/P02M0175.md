# P02M0175 - Dual-stack transports, DNS and NetworkService

Status: PLANNED AFTER P02M0174. FUTURE-TRACK NETWORKING. NOT A PHASE-2 COMPLETION GATE.

## Goal

Carry P02M0174's IPv6 addresses through UDP, TCP, DNS, the public NetworkService contract and the
network tools, producing one coherent IPv4/IPv6 dual-stack appliance rather than a private L3 feature
which applications cannot name.

TLS is not part of this milestone. It authenticates and protects application transports and should
be designed after the address/socket contract is correct, not hidden inside an IPv6 rollout.

**The UDP deliverable is decided here rather than left to implementation.** UDP in this milestone
means the named INTERNAL operations: DHCPv4, and DNS and SNTP over BOTH address families. "Plus
their IPv6 equivalents" was the previous wording and it was wrong about DHCP: this file refuses
DHCPv6 by name and M0174 assigns IPv6 configuration to SLAAC and RDNSS, so there is no IPv6
equivalent of the DHCP client and the test matrix must not imply one (corrected 2026-08-30).

There is NO public datagram bind/send/receive API. Adding one would be a second unplanned
public contract on top of a transmit-side TCP implementation this milestone already has to build,
and the tools this milestone migrates do not need it. If an application ever needs datagrams, it is
its own reviewed requirement with scoped endpoints, conflict rules and queue budgets.

## What is there now, and what it forces

- There is NO transmit-side TCP. `TcpConn` holds sequence counters and a receive buffer; it stores
  no unacknowledged bytes, no peer MSS and no send window. `tcp_build_data` advances `snd_nxt` past
  a one-shot frame and retains nothing, and `socket.send` truncates the caller's buffer to one
  segment, transmits once, closes the buffer and reports success. Only SYN has a retransmission
  loop. So "resegment and retransmit outstanding data after a PTB" cannot be implemented as an
  addition - there is no outstanding data to resegment, and loss is currently unrecoverable.
- DNS is uncorrelated. Any UDP datagram whose SOURCE port is 53 is handed to the parser; the
  destination port, the server address, the UDP length and checksum are unchecked, and the parser
  ignores the transaction ID, QR/opcode/rcode, the question section, the answer owner and CNAME
  chain, returning the first A record. `resolve` collapses every failure to `NotFound`.
- An inbound SYN allocates a TCB BEFORE any socket channel exists. `tcp_alloc` reuses a free slot or
  unconditionally pushes a new one - the pool has no cap - and every fresh TCB allocates 65,535
  receive bytes, growing to 262,140 when the peer offers window scaling. P02M0080's claim that the
  domain handle budget is the true ceiling does not hold for this path, because a half-open TCB
  consumes no handle.
- The public contract is IPv4-shaped and unbounded: `endpoint` is an `ipv4-addr` and a port,
  `net-info` carries one address and one gateway with an unbounded neighbour list, `listen` takes a
  bare `u16`, and `network.lsidl` contains no `@bound` at all - so the schema advertises requests
  larger than the service's 1024-byte request and 4096-byte reply buffers can receive.
- Boot blocks on DHCPv4 before entering the serve loop and then installs a hard-coded IPv4 fallback.
  The loop schedules only the lease deadline, and the synchronous helpers discard unrelated events.
  An IPv6-only profile would still wait for DHCPv4 and silently acquire v4 state.
- `service-logic` is the host-testable boundary; the `services` binary links `rt` and cannot be
  host-tested. Every pure decision added here goes there.

    AND THE FETCH BODY'S OWN BUDGET IS A NUMBER, NOT A CROSS-REFERENCE (added 2026-09-01). The stream
    clause above bounds a body by "the per-flow receive budget below" and no such budget followed:
    the 256 KiB is UNACKNOWLEDGED TRANSMIT data, which is a different direction and a different
    question, and M4's 2 MiB is an aggregate across inbound state rather than a per-flow receive
    bound. So the one number the truncation rule depends on did not exist. It is:

      MAX_FETCH_BODY_BYTES   262144 bytes (256 kB) per fetch

    The cumulative response cap is the same order as the per-flow unacknowledged transmit budget.
    Streaming does not preallocate that whole body: M4 charges receive buffers and retained chunks
    as they are held and releases those charges as they are drained. The cumulative delivery limit
    and aggregate receive-storage cap remain separate checks.

    Reaching the bound alone does not end the stream. A response of exactly 262144 bytes followed
    by orderly EOF is COMPLETE; TRUNCATED requires evidence of a response byte beyond the bound.
    Deliver no more than the cap, retain no extra response body, and inspect the next in-order byte
    in bounded, already-accounted TCP/frame storage. That byte proves truncation and is not delivered.
    At the cap, continue servicing the receive path long enough to distinguish EOF, another byte,
    reset or the existing receive timeout. Preserve bounded receive credit in already-charged storage
    so the peer can supply that evidence, honoring the negotiated window scale and credit previously
    advertised. The first extra byte is sufficient, even if a larger segment arrives; do not deliver
    or retain its extra body. A timeout or reset without EOF or an over-bound byte is FAILED,
    never inferred completion or truncation. Reaching the cap must not create a zero-window deadlock.

    AND THE STREAM SAYS WHICH OF THE THREE ENDINGS IT REACHED. `fetch` returns
    `result<stream<chunk>, error>`: OPENING is guarded, so a refusal before any body exists - an
    unresolvable name, a budget already spent, a connection refused - is the typed error every other
    operation here uses, and not a zero-length stream the caller has to interpret. Once open, the
    stream ends in exactly one of three ways, and they are distinguishable because a caller that
    cannot tell them apart cannot know whether it holds the whole resource:
      - COMPLETE: orderly TCP EOF after at most `MAX_FETCH_BODY_BYTES` response bytes
      - TRUNCATED: an in-order response byte beyond `MAX_FETCH_BODY_BYTES` was observed
      - FAILED: reset, receive timeout or another transport failure before either terminal above
    `chunk` carries only data today, so carrying the outcome is a field this milestone adds; whether
    it rides on the final element or on a terminal frame is IDL spelling and is the generator's, but
    the THREE OUTCOMES and the guarded open are semantics and are frozen here. `fetch` remains the
    existing raw TCP exchange: its request is an endpoint plus request bytes, with no response-length
    declaration. EOF is therefore the transport boundary; application framing such as HTTP length
    validation remains with the caller. No new application parser is required for this correction.
    M10 tests 262144 bytes followed by FIN as COMPLETE and 262145 as TRUNCATED with exactly the first
    262144 delivered, both when FIN/data share a segment and when the decisive event arrives later.
    Exact-bound silence and reset both produce FAILED. All cases assert one terminal and bounded
    receive storage, including while waiting for the decisive event.

- [ ] **M2 - TCP acquires a bounded transmit side.** This is new implementation, not a family
  parameter added to existing code, and everything else in this milestone that mentions
  retransmission depends on it:

  - an owned per-connection queue of UNACKNOWLEDGED bytes, with a hard per-flow and aggregate byte
    budget;
  - peer MSS from the SYN, an effective MSS derived from interface and path MTU, and a tracked send
    window;
  - segmentation of a caller's buffer across several segments, and partial-write backpressure. The
    current silent truncation to one segment is a data-loss bug reported as success.
    AND `send` REPORTS BYTES ACCEPTED, DECIDED HERE RATHER THAN LEFT TO THE IMPLEMENTATION
    (corrected 2026-09-03). The previous wording asked for "a defined contract for whether `send`
    reports bytes ACCEPTED or bytes acknowledged", which names the question and answers nothing -
    and the two are observably different client contracts, buffer lifetimes and failure results over
    a public operation that already returns a byte count (`socket.send -> result<u32, error>`). The
    contract is:
      THE COUNT IS BYTES ACCEPTED INTO THE TRANSMIT QUEUE, and `send` returns as soon as they are
        queued. It may be FEWER than offered - that is the partial-write backpressure above, and a
        caller resends the remainder - and zero accepted is a typed `Again` rather than a success.
      THE BYTES ARE COPIED before `send` returns, so the caller may reuse or free its buffer
        immediately. The buffer is a shared-memory handle and holding it across retransmission would
        pin caller memory for the length of the retry schedule - up to the ten minutes the retry
        limit permits.
      ACKNOWLEDGEMENT IS NOT OBSERVABLE THROUGH `send`. A peer that stops acknowledging surfaces as a
        typed error on a LATER `send` (the queue is full and the connection is failing) or on
        `close`, which is the operation that means "I am finished, flush what is queued": it
        completes when the closing handshake completes and fails with a typed error when the retry
        limit expires with data or FIN outstanding.
      WHY NOT ACKNOWLEDGED BYTES: an RPC held open until the peer acknowledges keeps a caller blocked
        across the whole retransmission schedule, which would put one blocked call per connection
        against this milestone's own no-starvation scheduler and make "close with data in flight" a
        state no caller can reach. Accepted-bytes is the only one of the two that is consistent with
        the rest of this item.
    Its fixture: a `send` of more than the queue budget returns the accepted prefix, the caller
    overwrites its buffer immediately, and the bytes that arrive at the peer are the ones that were
    accepted - not what the buffer held afterwards;
  - ACK retirement with wraparound handling;
  - resegmentation and retransmission of outstanding data within a smaller MSS after a validated
    Packet Too Big; and
  - FIN IS SEQUENCE SPACE AND IS RETRANSMITTED LIKE DATA (restored 2026-08-31). This was accepted and
    promised in an earlier round - "an RTO for data and FIN" - and then went missing from this item,
    which describes only unacknowledged data and segments. It is not a detail: FIN consumes a
    sequence number, so the connection owns it exactly as it owns a byte, and the current teardown
    shows what its absence costs - it builds one FIN, sends it once, pumps briefly for the peer's
    FIN and then frees the control block, so a lost FIN or a lost final ACK ends the connection
    half-open on the peer with nothing left to retransmit from. The transmit side therefore RETAINS
    the FIN's sequence position in the same unacknowledged queue as data, retransmits it under the
    same RTO and backoff, closes under the same retry limit, and the control block survives until the
    closing handshake is acknowledged or that limit is reached - a typed expiry rather than a fixed
    brief wait.
    AND RETAINING THE FIN IN THE QUEUE CANNOT DELIVER THE LOST-FINAL-ACK CASE THIS ITEM PROMISES,
    WHICH NEEDS TIME-WAIT (corrected 2026-09-03). The rule above frees the control block once the
    closing handshake is acknowledged - and on the side that closed ACTIVELY the last thing sent is
    the final ACK, which is never itself acknowledged. So an implementation can free the TCB the
    moment its own FIN is ACKed, satisfy every word of the queue rule, and have nothing left to
    answer the peer's retransmitted FIN with; the fixture this item names as a required case is not
    reachable from the mechanism it names. TCP's answer is a STATE, not a queue entry:
      TIME-WAIT is entered by a side that has SENT a FIN and had it acknowledged, AND has
        acknowledged the peer's FIN. The TCB is retained - not freed - with the four-tuple reserved
        against reuse.
        STATED LOCALLY, BECAUSE "THE SIDE THAT SENT THE FIRST FIN" IS NOT A LOCAL FACT (corrected
        2026-09-03). Under a simultaneous close neither endpoint can observe a global first: both
        send a FIN from ESTABLISHED and each receives the other's before its own is acknowledged, so
        a rule phrased on "first" leaves both sides able to conclude they are not it and free the
        TCB immediately - which loses the lost-final-ACK recovery on BOTH. The condition above is
        decidable by each endpoint from what it has sent and received, and it is the same condition
        RFC 9293 section 3.6 Figure 13 draws.
      ITS LIFETIME IS 2 MSL, and this profile fixes MSL at 30 seconds, so TIME-WAIT is 60 seconds.
        RFC 9293 section 3.4.2 names 2 minutes as the MSL the specification assumes and permits an
        implementation to choose a smaller one; 30 seconds is what this milestone's fixtures can wait
        out, and it is written here rather than left to the implementation for the reason every other
        number in this profile is.
      A RETRANSMITTED FIN ARRIVING IN TIME-WAIT IS ACKNOWLEDGED AND RESTARTS THE 2 MSL TIMER, which
        is the lost-final-ACK recovery, and is why the timer restarts rather than running once.
      THE TUPLE IS ACCOUNTED, because a retained TCB is a TCB: a connection in TIME-WAIT holds its
        M4 budget entry and its one scheduler deadline until the timer expires, and an admission that
        cannot be given one is refused at the point that has a caller. This is the honest cost of the
        state and it is stated rather than discovered when the budget will not close.
      THE PASSIVE SIDE DOES NOT ENTER IT: a side that sent no FIN until it had received one, and
        whose own FIN is the LAST segment of the exchange, is acknowledged by the peer's final ACK
        and closes on it - CLOSE-WAIT, LAST-ACK, closed - which the queue rule above already covers.
        That is the correct statement of the exemption and it does not reach a simultaneous close,
        where neither side is passive.
      THE CROSSED-FIN PATH IS NAMED, because the states between are where an implementation can lose
        it: a side whose FIN is still unacknowledged when the peer's FIN arrives acknowledges that
        FIN and moves to CLOSING; when its own FIN is then acknowledged it moves to TIME-WAIT. So a
        simultaneous close is ESTABLISHED, FIN-WAIT-1, CLOSING, TIME-WAIT on BOTH endpoints, and both
        retain the TCB, the four-tuple, the M4 budget entry and the one scheduler deadline for 2 MSL.
        The cost is stated with the rest: a simultaneous close holds TWO retained TCBs where an
        ordinary close holds one, and M4's budget is what refuses at admission if they will not fit.
    Its fixtures: a lost final ACK, where the peer's retransmitted FIN is answered from TIME-WAIT and
    the timer restarts; an EXPIRY fixture, where nothing further arrives and the TCB and its budget
    entry are released after exactly 2 MSL and not before; and a SIMULTANEOUS CLOSE, where the two
    FINs cross, BOTH endpoints pass through CLOSING into TIME-WAIT, and both retain their TCB, budget
    entry and scheduler deadline for the full 2 MSL - the case a rule phrased on "the first FIN"
    lets an implementation free early on one side or on both.

  sender: it collapses under a changed path and it can congest one. These are BASIC requirements and
  are distinct from the advanced congestion control this milestone refuses. The profile is:

                            candidate the two coincide, which is the case M2's oracle tests.

                        before 180 whatever N is.

  DEPRECATED IS A PREFERENCE, NOT AN EXCLUSION (corrected 2026-08-31). A previous version said "never
  choose a deprecated source for a new connection", which contradicts the RFC 6724 profile pinned in
  the same paragraph and the caller override named beside it. RFC 6724 section 5 rule 3 PREFERS a
  non-deprecated source between two candidates; it does not remove a still-valid deprecated address
  from the candidate set, and the specification expressly does not override a caller's legal explicit
  choice. Implemented as an exclusion, new connectivity ends at PREFERRED-lifetime expiry instead of
  at VALID-lifetime expiry - so an appliance whose only remaining address is deprecated stops being
  able to open connections while its address is still usable, which is a self-inflicted outage rather
  than a policy. The rule is therefore: prefer a non-deprecated candidate; use a deprecated but still
  valid one when it is the only candidate; and honour an explicit caller choice of one. Only
  TENTATIVE addresses are excluded from the candidate set outright, as the same section requires.

  scope, and a candidate with no usable route - plus the two cases the old exclusion rule would have
  failed: a deprecated address that is the ONLY candidate, which must be selected and must carry
  traffic, and an explicit caller override naming a deprecated address while a preferred one exists,
  which must be honoured.

             Plus the case neither generic set reaches on its own: a BROADCAST NAK answering a

      finished query - because matching is worth having whatever the randomness is;

## Definition of done

- First-party applications can resolve, connect, listen, inspect and diagnose IPv4 and IPv6 through
  one typed capability-scoped API whose records, bind modes and bounds are stated in the schema.
- The TCP sender measures RTT, backs off, bounds its flight and probes a zero window, so a changed
  path or a lost window update does not deadlock or flood it.
- Every internal UDP operation - DHCPv4, DNS and SNTP - correlates its replies to its own requests
  through the full tuple. ON EVERY PROFILE: a datagram whose tuple does not match a live request is
  rejected, and one arriving after its request completed or expired matches nothing, so a MISMATCHED,
  STALE or REPLAYED datagram can neither resolve a name, set the clock, nor complete or invalidate a
  lease. ON PROFILES WITH A SECURE ENTROPY SOURCE, and only there: an OFF-PATH attacker cannot
  produce a matching tuple either, so a spoofed datagram is rejected as well; on the others the
  service says out loud that its query identities are predictable, and an off-path forgery carrying
  the predicted tuple would be accepted.
  (This clause was absolute until 2026-09-01 - "a spoofed or replayed datagram can neither..." - and
  the sentence after it carved out the very case that makes it false. A profile whose transaction IDs
  and source ports are predictable can be handed a forged datagram bearing the predicted tuple, and
  that datagram IS spoofed and DOES resolve a name. What every profile can promise is rejection of
  what does not match; what only a seeded profile can promise is that an off-path attacker cannot
  make one match.) See the entropy dependency: this
  is narrowed because two of three architectures have no secure source, not because guessability is
  acceptable.
- TCP owns its outstanding data AND ITS FIN: loss, a lost FIN, a lost final ACK and a reduced path
  MTU are recovered rather than silently dropped, and `send` reports what it actually accepted.
- DNS answers are correlated and validated, distinct errors survive, and a truncated answer is
  retried over TCP.
- Inbound TCP state is charged against declared budgets before publication, and a SYN or backlog
  flood in either family cannot grow the service heap past them.
- IPv4-only, IPv6-only and dual-stack QEMU profiles have positive traffic oracles and hostile parser
  coverage against a controllable peer, with bounded resources and no family aliasing.
- IPv4 behavior remains available; IPv6 support is not represented as TLS or general Internet
  hardening.

## What this milestone refuses

- TLS/HTTPS, certificate validation, QUIC, IPsec, VPN, firewall/NAT and advanced congestion control.
  M2 builds reliable delivery, not a congestion-control implementation beyond what a bounded
  appliance needs.
- A public datagram socket API. UDP here is the named internal DHCP, DNS and SNTP operations.
- DHCPv6 or privacy addresses unless P02M0174's SLAAC/RDNSS QEMU profile proves insufficient and a
  separate reviewed requirement is opened.
- Zone identifiers in URLs or in any transmitted authority.
- POSIX raw sockets or ambient packet authority for tools.

# P02M0190 - TPM 2.0 for applications: a provider, a typed API and a demonstration tool

Status: OPEN. It closes when every item below is done.

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-09-25), IN HIS OWN TERMS: who consumes the TPM does not matter; what matters is
that the resource EXISTS in LiberSystem and is ready for third-party developers to use - shown by a small
tool that demonstrates it working. That replaces the position P02M0099 had taken, which was to wait for a
consuming service (measured boot, key storage) before exposing anything.

WHAT EXISTS. Everything under the binding is in, from P02M0099: `src/tpm` carries discovery from the ACPI
`TPM2` table, the FIFO/TIS and CRB transports over a `Registers` trait, bounded marshaling with every
response header validated, and the typed operations - Startup, SelfTest, GetRandom, PCR_Read and PCR_Extend
on the SHA-256 bank, a secret sealed to a PCR policy and unsealed, and a quote by a restricted ECDSA P-256
key. `host.tpm` holds it against models and against `swtpm` - the TCG reference implementation - with the
quote's signature verified by OpenSSL; the kernel's hardware suite drives it through QEMU's `tpm-crb` and
`tpm-tis` front-ends over `swtpm`.

WHAT IS MISSING IS EVERYTHING ABOVE THE LIBRARY, one prerequisite below it, and two things inside it.
  - NO DRIVER PROCESS CAN BIND THE TPM. It is not a PCI function. On x86_64 the firmware describes it by the
    static ACPI `TPM2` table (and a second time by an `MSFT0101` namespace node); on aarch64 and riscv64,
    whose loaders hand the kernel a device tree and no ACPI tables, by a device-tree node - QEMU's
    `tpm-tis-device`, which this machine's aarch64 and riscv64 QEMUs both offer, emits `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio`.
    This system has no identity for a firmware-described device - no name, no claim, no MMIO capability
    for one. That is the firmware-node device identity - P02M0196, which the owner approved whole on
    2026-09-25. ONLY ITS FIRST STEP IS NEEDED HERE, P02M0196a: its static-table identity on x86_64 and its
    device-tree half on aarch64 and riscv64, with no AML interpreter. This milestone REQUIRES that step and
    does not build it.
  - WHAT THE `TPM2` TABLE CAN AND CANNOT MAKE EXIST. The table names an interface by its start method.
    Method 6 (the FIFO interface) carries no address, and the library puts it at the PC Client profile's
    fixed base `0xFED40000` - right on a PC and on nothing else, which is why the table path is x86_64's
    alone. Method 7 (CRB) carries its control area's address. The methods that need AML to start a command
    (2, ACPI start; 8, CRB with ACPI start), an Arm secure-monitor call (11) or an I2C bus (12) are refused
    by name in the library, and on a machine whose TPM uses one of them this milestone makes no TPM exist.
  - NO SERVICE AND NO CONTRACT: nothing an application can call, and nothing PermissionManager can mint a
    TPM grant from. In this tree a driver publishes a provider of a closed kind to ONE consuming service,
    and every device-backed application grant is a connection that service mints at PermissionManager's
    request, bound to the launched task.
  - NO TOOL: nothing a person can run to see it work.
  - AND TWO THINGS THE LIBRARY LACKS: `TPM2_GetCapability`, without which neither the TPM's identity nor what
    a killed predecessor left loaded can be read; and the recovery from those leftovers.

## P02M0190 - The library's additions

- [ ] `GetCapability`, TYPED AND BOUNDED like every operation in `src/tpm`: TPM properties
  (`TPM_CAP_TPM_PROPERTIES`) for the fixed identity - family, manufacturer, vendor string, firmware version -
  and for the two attribute words `TPM_PT_PERMANENT` and `TPM_PT_STARTUP_CLEAR`; and handles
  (`TPM_CAP_HANDLES`) for transient objects and loaded sessions. At most 16 properties and 64 handles per
  answer, `moreData` followed a bounded number of times, and an answer listing more than was asked for, or a
  property that was not asked for, refused as `Malformed`. Still no passthrough: it reads, and it sends
  nothing a caller chose.
- [ ] LEFTOVER RECOVERY: `flush_leftovers` lists the transient objects and loaded sessions and flushes each,
  answering how many it flushed. A PC Client TPM need hold only three of each, an unseal needs two objects
  and a session, and a driver killed between a `CreatePrimary`, `Load` or `StartAuthSession` and its
  `FlushContext` leaves them loaded - where `Startup` cannot clear them, because the TPM has already
  started. The driver runs it at every start: it is the TPM's one owner, so whatever is loaded when it
  starts belongs to no one.
- [ ] THE HIERARCHY STATE, read from those two attribute words: whether the owner hierarchy has an
  authorization value (`ownerAuthSet`) and whether it is enabled (`shEnable`). Seal, unseal and quote each
  begin with a `CreatePrimary` under the owner hierarchy with the empty password, so on a TPM that another
  system or the firmware provisioned they would answer an authorization or hierarchy code that explains
  nothing to the developer who receives it.

## P02M0190 - The driver and its provider

- [ ] A TPM DRIVER PROCESS (`tpm_driver`), restartable like every driver here, bound through P02M0196a's
  platform-device claim. It matches two identities: the `TPM2` table's static-table identity form on x86_64,
  and a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` device-tree node on aarch64 and riscv64. THE RESOURCE RULE below holds on all
  three targets, and P02M0196a implements it on BOTH paths: in its `TPM2` row, and in the row its device-tree
  item gives a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` node - cut to the page as the `TPM2` row is, not the node's translated
  `reg` whole:
    - ONE MMIO RANGE, THE LOCALITY-0 REGISTER PAGE: 4 KiB at the interface's base. For method 6 on x86_64
      that base is the fixed `0xFED40000`; for method 7 it is the control area's address less its offset in
      the page (the library refuses a control area anywhere but offset 0x40); on the device tree it is the
      base of the node's `reg`, translated through its parents' `ranges`, and the page is all of that `reg`
      the row carries - QEMU's node names the whole five-locality 0x5000 region, and a board's may too.
      Localities above 0 are excluded, and a window over their registers would be an authority this driver
      never uses. A CRB that names its command or response buffer outside the page is refused by the
      transport by name (`Buffer`) - QEMU's and the PC Client profile's buffers are inside it.
    - NO INTERRUPT: both transports poll. Neither the table nor QEMU's nodes name one, and the row carries
      none even where a board's tree node or an `MSFT0101` node's `_CRS` does.
    - NO DMA: the TPM is not a bus master, which is P02M0196a's non-mastering default for a platform device.
    - ONLY METHODS 6 AND 7 MAKE A DEVICE. A `TPM2` table naming any other start method yields no claimable
      device, and the boot log names the method by its number.
  ONE DEVICE, NOT TWO: when P02M0196b enumerates the namespace, the `MSFT0101` node that describes the same
  TPM resolves to the table's device under P02M0196a's reconciliation rule - its `_CRS` memory range starts at
  the same base and contains the page (QEMU's CRB node names exactly that page, its TIS node the whole
  five-locality 0x5000 region, and a PC's firmware may name either) - and the claim stays the page, with no
  interrupt. An `MSFT0101` node with no `TPM2` device beside it (a TPM with a start method refused above)
  matches no driver.
  ACROSS A SLEEP the driver implements P02M0197's `SUSPEND`/`RESUME` exchange, with a `suspend-deadline` in
  its registry entry, carried by whichever of P02M0197 and this milestone lands second: its `SUSPEND` finishes
  the operation in hand and reads no other until its `RESUME`, and for S3 sends `TPM2_Shutdown(TPM_SU_STATE)` -
  a typed operation `src/tpm` gains with it; its `RESUME` sends no `Startup` when the firmware has already
  started the TPM with the saved state.
- [ ] ITS START, in order: Startup(CLEAR) when firmware left that to the OS; SelfTest once; `flush_leftovers`;
  the hierarchy state and the fixed identity read; one boot-log line with the interface, the manufacturer,
  the firmware version, the owner hierarchy's state and how many leftovers were flushed; then ONE provider
  of the `tpm` kind published. A failure before the publication is a failed bind, named in the log and
  retried by DeviceManager as any driver's is.
- [ ] THE PROVIDER CONTRACT `tpm-device` (in `liber:tpm@1`), served to TpmService alone: `describe` (the
  interface, the fixed identity and the owner hierarchy's state, read afresh) and the typed operations -
  random, PCR read, PCR extend, seal, unseal, quote - with the library's own bounds. It EXECUTES ONE
  OPERATION AT A TIME, WHOLE, in the order received: a TPM runs one command at a time, and an operation
  interleaved between another's `CreatePrimary` and its `FlushContext` would exhaust the objects the first
  one needs. It decides nothing about who may do what; that is the service's.
- [ ] THE `tpm` PROVIDER KIND, appended after the last kind in each of the three closed mappings - the IDL's
  `provider-kind` in `src/idl/device.lsidl`, `ProviderKindName` in `src/tools/system-manifest`, and
  DeviceManager's wire mapping both ways with its `driver_protocol` provider constant - never inserted, so
  no kind's number moves. Its doc comment says what the others say: a TPM 2.0's typed operations, which
  TpmService alone consumes; applications reach the TPM through minted `tpm` connections and never through
  this kind.

## P02M0190 - The service, the contract and the authority

- [ ] TPMSERVICE (`tpm_service`), a declared service in SmartcardService's shape: a CATALOGUE role minted for
  the `tpm` kind alone (presence optional, so a machine with no TPM runs it idle) and ONE root, ADMIN
  (`liber:tpm@1/tpm-admin`), the minting endpoint PermissionManager alone resolves through the broker. No
  public root: every application connection is minted there. No device claim and no storage;
  `restart = "transparent"`, `state_class = "reconstructible"`. It serves one TPM provider; a second
  publication - which the one-device rule above makes a firmware or reconciliation error - is logged and not
  used. EVERY DECISION IS IN `service_logic::tpm`, host-tested: which operations a grant carries, the PCR
  sets, the bounds, the component tag, the queue and the provider's comings and goings.
- [ ] THE QUEUE: one request outstanding at the driver; one call outstanding per application connection (a
  second answers `busy`); at most 16 calls waiting in all (past that, `busy`).
- [ ] WHAT A CLIENT SEES WHEN SOMETHING RESTARTS:
    - THE DRIVER: its provider is withdrawn and later republished. Application connections STAY - the TPM's
      state is in the chip, and a driver restart does not reset it. The operation in flight answers
      `interrupted` and is NEVER REPLAYED: an extend may have reached the TPM before the driver died, and a
      replay would extend twice; a client that must know reads the PCR. Queued calls, and calls made while
      no provider is present, answer `unavailable`.
    - THE SERVICE: every application connection dies with the old instance, and the grants are minted again
      at the component's next launch, as SmartcardService's are.
- [ ] A TYPED CONTRACT `liber:tpm@1` (`src/idl/tpm.lsidl`), three interfaces and nothing raw:
    - `tpm`, THE APPLICATION'S CONNECTION, carrying the operations of the one grant it was minted for:
      `info` (the interface, the manufacturer, the vendor string, the firmware version, and whether seal,
      unseal and quote are available); `random` (at most 1024 bytes a call); `pcr-read` (any PCR, 0-23);
      `pcr-extend` with a SHA-256 digest - PCRs 16 AND 23 ONLY, the PC Client profile's debug and
      application PCRs, both extendable at locality 0; 0-7 are the firmware's, 8-15 are RESERVED for this
      system's own measured boot, 17-22 are the dynamic root of trust's; `seal` to one PCR's present value
      and `unseal` (any PCR 0-23); and `quote` of one PCR (any, 0-23) over a caller's nonce of at most 64
      bytes, answering the attestation, the ECDSA signature and the key's public point for verification
      elsewhere.
    - `tpm-admin`, PermissionManager's alone: `mint(kind, component, owner)` - a connection carrying one
      grant's operations for the named component, alive as long as the `owner` task and no longer, however
      it is copied.
    - `tpm-device`, the provider contract above.
  NAMED REFUSALS: `not-granted`, `pcr-not-allowed`, `bounds`, `busy`, `policy-refused` (the unseal's PCR no
  longer holds its value), `owner-hierarchy-unavailable`, `other-component`, `interrupted`, `unavailable`,
  and the TPM's own response code for anything else. No command passthrough: a raw TPM command interface is
  an authority over every session and every hierarchy, and it is exactly what a third-party API must not be.
- [ ] A SEALED OBJECT IS BOUND TO THE COMPONENT THAT SEALED IT. The TPM binds it to this TPM's owner seed and
  to the PCR's value, and nothing more: the storage primary it is created under needs no authorization, so
  nothing in the TPM tells one application from another. TpmService does: the first 32 bytes of every sealed
  secret are the SHA-256 of the sealing component's name as the grant was minted for it, an `unseal` whose
  tag is not the caller's answers `other-component` and returns nothing, and the application's secret is at
  most 96 bytes (the library's 128 less the tag). It holds because TpmService is the only process that
  reaches the TPM's `Unseal` - the driver's one consumer.
- [ ] THE OWNER HIERARCHY. When the driver reports an owner authorization set or the owner hierarchy disabled,
  `seal`, `unseal` and `quote` answer `owner-hierarchy-unavailable` without a command reaching the TPM, and
  `info` says so; random, PCR read and extend keep working. No path supplies the owner authorization in this
  milestone: such a machine is a documented limit, and its remedy is a TPM clear from the firmware's setup -
  which changes the owner seed and makes every earlier sealed object unopenable.
- [ ] THE AUTHORITY IS SPLIT BY WHAT IT CAN HURT: `tpm` (info, random, PCR read, quote - observation),
  `tpm-measure` (PCR extend, which changes what every later quote and unseal answers) and `tpm-seal` (seal and
  unseal) - three capabilities APPENDED to the vocabulary in `src/idl/security.lsidl`, never inserted, so no
  capability's number moves. PermissionManager mints each separately through ONE `grant_for_task` arm in the
  smart-card and modem shape: the admin root resolved over the broker by a new name, `TPMADMIN` (in
  `capability_names.rs`, and in ServiceManager's three rows: the names PermissionManager may resolve, the
  service serving each, the root each resolves to), the task duplicated with wait and transfer, `mint` with
  the grant's kind and the component, and the minted connection narrowed to the grant rights. There is one
  TPM and no alias to resolve, so the component row IS the policy: a component whose row lists a TPM
  capability gets that grant, and one whose row does not gets nothing.
- [ ] THE DEFAULT ROWS: the `tpm` tool (shipping) holds all three, and `volumes` for its files - the one
  shipping row, as `btctl` is the one for the Bluetooth operator; `tpmprobe` (development only, not staged in
  the shipping image) holds `tpm`, `tpm-seal` and `volumes` (to read the tool's sealed file) and not
  `tpm-measure`. Nothing else holds any of them, and a third-party program receives them the way every device
  grant is received: a row naming it.
- [ ] A CLIENT LIBRARY, `tpm-client` with its provider half, in `src/user/libs/clients` beside the others, so a
  third-party program links the contract the way the system's own tools do: it takes whichever of the three
  grants the component received and sends each call on the connection whose grant carries it, answering
  `not-granted` itself for a call no held grant carries. That answer is a convenience, not the boundary:
  TpmService itself answers `not-granted` to any operation sent on a connection whose grant does not carry
  it, which is what a program that does not link `tpm-client` meets. The contract's generated documentation
  says what each call costs (a seal, an unseal and a quote each generate a primary key) and what it refuses.

## P02M0190 - The demonstration tool

- [ ] `tpm`, a small text tool: `tpm info` (the interface, the manufacturer, the firmware version, and whether
  seal, unseal and quote are available), `tpm random N`, `tpm pcr N`, `tpm extend N TEXT` (the digest
  extended is the SHA-256 of TEXT), `tpm seal N TEXT FILE` (sealed to PCR N), `tpm unseal FILE` and
  `tpm quote N NONCE FILE`. It prints what the TPM answered and nothing it computed itself - the quote's
  attestation, signature and public point in hex as well as in FILE, for whoever verifies them - and every
  refusal by its name, so what a person reads is the device's answer.
- [ ] A cold development scenario gate, `qemu-tpm-tool`, with `swtpm` behind QEMU's CRB front-end, in this
  order: `lsdev` lists the TPM as one platform row whose resources are one 4 KiB MMIO range at `0xFED40000`
  and no interrupt; `tpm info` names the CRB interface, `swtpm`'s manufacturer and firmware version, and
  seal, unseal and quote available; two `tpm random` answers differ; `extend 16` then `pcr 16` shows the
  SHA-256 chain the GATE computes on the host from the text it typed; `extend 8` is refused `pcr-not-allowed`
  and PCR 8 is unchanged; `seal 16` then `unseal` returns the text; `tpmprobe` is refused `not-granted` TWICE
  on an extend of PCR 23 - a PCR `pcr-extend` accepts, so only the missing grant can refuse it - and PCR 23
  is unchanged: through `tpm-client`, which answers in the probe's own process because PermissionManager
  minted it no `tpm-measure` grant, and through the contract's generated client on its `tpm` connection, the
  way `cardread` sends `acquire` on its read-only smart-card connection, which only TpmService can answer;
  `tpmprobe` is refused `other-component` on the tool's sealed file, and seals and unseals its own; the
  DRIVER RESTARTED with `lsdev --disable` and `--enable`, with `tpmprobe hold`, started in the background
  before it, holding ONE `tpm` connection across it: `random` on that connection answers a value before the
  disable, `unavailable` while the driver is down (an `interrupted` admitted for the one call the withdrawal
  overtook) and a value after the enable, the probe printing each change of answer and the scenario waiting
  for it before its next step; while the driver is down a newly launched `tpm random` answers `unavailable`
  too; the driver's start line is logged again, and the tool's sealed file still unseals after it; TpmService
  stopped and started, and a new `tpm random` is answered (grants are minted per launch); an `extend 16` makes
  the next `unseal` refuse `policy-refused`; and the quote's signature verifies with OpenSSL on the host
  against the public point it carries, over this nonce and PCR 16's digest. The same scenario on `tpm-tis` in
  a second run.

## P02M0190 - Verification

- [ ] Host tests: `service_logic::tpm` - every grant against every operation, the PCR sets, the bounds, the
  component tag both ways, the queue and `busy`, `interrupted` never replayed, `unavailable` while no
  provider is present, the owner-hierarchy refusal; `GetCapability` against hostile answers; and, against
  `swtpm` in `host.tpm`: a killed predecessor's leftovers - transient objects and sessions loaded and never
  flushed, up to the TPM's limit - make an unseal fail with the TPM's object-memory warning, and after
  `flush_leftovers` the same unseal returns the secret; an owner authorization set on `swtpm` (the test sends
  `HierarchyChangeAuth` itself) and the owner hierarchy disabled (`HierarchyControl` under the platform
  hierarchy's empty authorization) are each reported by the hierarchy state, while random, PCR read and
  extend still work. THAT HOST TEST IS WHERE A KILL IN THE MIDDLE OF AN UNSEAL IS PROVEN: in the guest a
  driver stop is a planned one - `lsdev --disable` withdraws the provider, asks the driver to stop and
  forces a teardown only past its deadline - and an operation on `swtpm` completes in milliseconds, so no
  step can place a kill inside one; the recovery that kill needs runs against the same `swtpm`. The three
  cross-builds; the dynamic report refreshed for the new library, service and tool.
- [ ] Runtime on x86_64 with both front-ends. aarch64 and riscv64 alike: the `tpm-tis` run's scenario, with
  QEMU's `tpm-tis-device` on the `virt` machine over `swtpm`, bound through the row P02M0196a's device-tree
  item gives a `tcg,tpm-tis-mmio` node once it exists (the node sits under QEMU's platform bus, so its `reg` is
  read through that bus's `ranges`) - its first step showing that row as one 4 KiB MMIO range at the node's
  translated base and no interrupt, though the node's `reg` names 0x5000.
- [ ] `docs/` gains a page for third-party developers: what the contract offers, which grant each call needs,
  the PCR sets and why; that PCRs are SHARED by the whole machine - any `tpm-measure` holder can move 16 or
  23, and a secret sealed to one of them then stays shut until the next boot; that a resume from S3 resets
  16 and 23 as well, because the PC Client profile saves only PCRs 0 to 15 across a sleep, so a secret
  sealed to either stays shut after a sleep too, while one sealed to PCRs 0 to 15 survives it; that a
  sealed object opens only on this TPM, only while its PCR holds its value, only for the component that
  sealed it, and never after a TPM clear; that a machine whose owner hierarchy is provisioned offers no
  seal, unseal or quote; that the quote key is not certified by the TPM's endorsement key, so a verifier
  learns it on first use; that a TPM whose start method needs AML is not driven; and what this TPM is NOT -
  not a key store with backup, not a measured-boot chain until firmware measures the loader.

EXCLUDES: measured boot of this system's own loader and kernel (it needs firmware and loader work of its own;
PCRs 8-15 are kept for it); localities above 0, whose PCRs belong to firmware; RSA keys; HMAC and
parameter-encryption sessions; supplying an owner-hierarchy authorization; certifying the quote key by the
endorsement key; TPMs whose start method needs AML (2, 8) - drivable later through P02M0196b's node-scoped
channel to the ACPI service - an Arm secure-monitor call (11) or an I2C bus (12, and on a device tree a
`tcg,tpm-tis-i2c` node); any vendor security processor outside TPM 2.0.

# P02M0202 - USB Type-C connectors: UCSI, a port controller with Power Delivery, and what they feed

Status: OPEN. Created 2026-09-25 under the owner's approval of P02M0196 - "yes, do all of it; where a
milestone is missing, create it". NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for plans only until he says
otherwise, and no part starts without its own written plan.

WHY. A Type-C connector negotiates who powers whom and at what voltage and current (USB Power Delivery), which
side is the USB host, and whether the cable carries DisplayPort in place of some of its USB lanes. On most
laptops an embedded controller runs all of that and reports it through UCSI - on x86 an ACPI device
(`PNP0CA0`) whose mailbox is a memory region and whose doorbell is a `_DSM` - and the operating system reads the
state, configures what the platform may accept and asks for changes. On boards without such a controller, the
operating system runs Power Delivery itself over a port controller (TCPCI) on I2C; here that engine is a SINK
in the Standard Power Range and nothing more, until a board with a port controller is named. P02M0099 lists
UCSI with NO destination, and nothing in the system has a notion of a connector, a power contract or an
alternate mode.

ORDER. P02M0202a needs only what has landed - P02M0180's kind-scoped catalogue client and P02M0181's
PowerService - and lands first, proved by host suites until a driver publishes. P02M0202b needs P02M0196a's
platform-device claim (several MMIO ranges), P02M0196b's `_DSM`, `Notify` and node-scoped ACPI channel, and
P02M0196d's fixture. P02M0202c needs P02M0195a (the I2C contract with plain I2C in its functionality set, the
GPIO line contract, the scoped connections, and `vhost-i2c-gpio.py` serving the vhost-user IOTLB), P02M0195b's
child binding, and P02M0196's companion join, through which the port controller's resource sources resolve to
the controllers that serve them.

## P02M0202a - The processes, the vocabulary, and where each fact goes

- [ ] THE PROCESSES. Only a driver holding a claim publishes; the service that serves readers holds none.
  - `ucsi-acpi`, a driver bound to the `PNP0CA0` platform device (P02M0196a, matched by `_HID`). Its claim
    mints the mailbox range its `_CRS` names; with the claim comes the node-scoped ACPI channel (P02M0196b)
    through which it evaluates that node's `_DSM` and receives its `Notify` values. The platform's PPM runs
    Power Delivery; this driver is UCSI's OPM.
  - `tcpci`, a driver bound to the port controller's node as a CHILD BINDING, exactly as P02M0195b binds a
    HID-over-I2C device: the node (matched by the `compatible` `tcpci` in a device tree, or by `PRP0001` with
    that `compatible` in `_DSD` under ACPI) is a platform device of its own below the I2C controller's
    companion. At bind DeviceManager mints, through each controller driver's scoped `CONNECT`, an
    ADDRESS-SCOPED I2C connection (the `I2cSerialBusV2` address or `reg`) and a LINE-SCOPED GPIO connection
    (the alert's `GpioInt` or `interrupts-extended`), which the controllers enforce and which arrive with the
    claim. Both are requirements naming the controllers' functions, so the driver binds after both parents
    and is stopped as a lost dependency (`DependencyLost`) when either ends. The Type-C sink state machine and
    the sink policy engine (P02M0202c) run INSIDE this process, so the path from the alert to the answer
    crosses no process but the two controllers'.
  - Each driver publishes TWO providers from its binding: `power-source` (P02M0181's kind; one local source
    per connector that can sink, the local id the connector's number, at most 16 as P02M0181 bounds a
    provider) and `typec-connector` (below; every connector). Manifest entries declare
    `{ kind = "power-source", most = 1, consumers = 1 }` and `{ kind = "typec-connector", most = 1,
    consumers = 1 }`. A PPM that reports more than 16 connectors is served for the first 16 and the rest are
    logged as unserved.
  - TypeCService (`typec_service`), which holds no claim: it consumes every `typec-connector` publication
    through a catalogue connection minted for that kind alone (the `open-consumer(kinds)` path P02M0180
    landed) and serves two roots, `liber:typec@1/typec` (read) and `liber:typec@1/typec-control`
    (operator). Its manifest row has the shape of `power_service`'s: a CATALOGUE factory role with
    `kinds = ["typec-connector"]`, the two serve-root roles, restart transparent, state reconstructible - a
    restarted instance takes a fresh snapshot from every provider and its subscribers subscribe again.
- [ ] `typec-connector`, appended to `provider-kind` in `liber:device@1` (the next free value when it lands,
  never renumbered; its one consumer TypeCService), and a new package `liber:typec@1`:
  - the PROVIDER CONTRACT a driver serves to TypeCService alone: a stream that opens with an atomic snapshot
    and a monotonic provider revision, then connector added, updated and removed (the shape of
    `power-provider`), and `request`;
  - the CONNECTOR RECORD: its number; what it can do (power roles, data roles, Power Delivery, the transport -
    UCSI or TCPCI - and on UCSI the PPM's optional features, alternate-mode override among them); the partner
    (none, a charger, a device, a host, a powered cable, an audio or a debug accessory) and the plug's
    orientation; the power role and the data role; the power operation mode (default USB, BC 1.2, Type-C
    1.5 A, Type-C 3 A, Power Delivery); THE CONTRACT - the selected offer's position and object, the
    operating and maximum current the request named and its capability-mismatch flag, or the Type-C current
    level where there is no Power Delivery contract; the partner's source offers (at most 11: Power
    Delivery's seven standard positions and four extended ones); the cable where it is known; the partner's
    alternate modes (SVID and mode VDO, at most 16), the entered ones, and DisplayPort's pin assignment and
    hot-plug state where the transport reports them; the last refused request and its reason; and whether
    the transport is currently answering;
  - the READ interface: enumerate, and subscribe - an atomic snapshot at (epoch, revision), then changes; a
    subscriber that does not drain is closed and subscribes again, as `liber:power@1`'s are;
  - the OPERATOR interface: data-role swap, power-role swap, enter a mode, exit a mode - one outstanding
    request per connector, a five-second deadline, answered `done`, `refused` with a reason (the connector
    cannot, the platform does not offer the command, the platform enters modes itself, the PPM refused with
    its error, the transport does not do it) or `indeterminate`, which is never retried (P02M0181's rule for
    controls).
  Nothing is versioned: `liber:device@1` gains one enum value and `liber:typec@1` starts at 1.
- [ ] POWER. `liber:power@1` gains `usb-c`, appended to `source-kind`: the partner on one connector as a
  supply. `present` - a partner is attached; `online` - this machine sinks from it. `voltage`, `current` and
  `power` carry ONLY what is measured - a UCSI 2.1 PPM's VBUS voltage and average current readings when its
  reading-ready bit is set, a port controller's VBUS voltage register where it has one - signed as the record
  already defines (negative delivered out of the partner into this machine, positive into the partner while
  this machine supplies it), and are unsupported otherwise. A port controller's VBUS fault is a reported
  `source-fault` alarm. A `usb-c` source has no capacity, charge state, runtime, load, temperature, trips or
  controls. THE NEGOTIATED CONTRACT IS A LIMIT, NOT A MEASUREMENT, and is never written into those fields: it
  and the partner's offers are `typec`'s. `power-model` gains a `usbc` adapter both drivers call before
  publishing, and `canon::validate` refuses a `usb-c` record that carries a field the kind does not have or a
  known voltage above 48 V; host tests for both. The enum value is the only wire change - no record changes
  shape.
  THE ACPI AC ADAPTER AND A `usb-c` SOURCE ARE TWO REPORTS, NOT ONE SOURCE. On a laptop `_PSR` (P02M0099's ACPI
  item, delivered to P02M0181) says the machine is on line power, whichever jack it comes through; the UCSI
  connector says which partner, on which connector, under which contract. Firmware describes no join between
  them, so none is invented: PowerService publishes both as the devices reported them and merges nothing, and
  the kind's documentation says a reader must not add them. A board without an ACPI AC adapter - a TCPCI
  board - has the `usb-c` source alone.
- [ ] THE DATA ROLE AND DISPLAYPORT ARE REPORTED IN `typec` AND NOWHERE ELSE. The USB drivers have no notion of
  a data role, nothing maps a Type-C connector to a root-hub port, DisplayService drives one surface and its
  device contract carries only resizes and replaced backings, and a display controller that reaches a
  monitor's bus is unowned (P02M0099). Delivering these facts waits for the milestones that create a
  connector-to-port map and a connector-aware display controller. A connector whose data role became device,
  on a machine with no device-mode controller, is shown as such rather than hidden.
- [ ] POLICY, BY TRANSPORT, with defaults the owner confirms when the part starts:
  - UCSI: the PPM negotiates contracts, answers the partner's swaps and - unless it grants override - enters
    alternate modes itself. The system CONFIGURES, after every PPM reset: swap acceptance through the accept
    bits of `SET_UOR` and `SET_PDR`, and the connector's operation mode (`SET_CCOM`, `SET_UOM` before UCSI
    2.0) and power direction mode (`SET_PDM`), each only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises it. PROPOSED
    DEFAULT, asked when the part starts: accept both swap directions and leave the operation modes as the PPM
    reports them. The system ASKS on an operator's request: a role through `SET_UOR`/`SET_PDR`; a mode entered
    or left through `SET_NEW_CAM` ONLY where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises alternate-mode override - otherwise
    the request is refused before any command is sent and `typec` says the platform enters modes itself. The
    system never enters a mode on its own: nothing here consumes DisplayPort.
  - TCPCI: the sink engine's selection rule (P02M0202c) is the system's own policy, PROPOSED DEFAULT asked
    when the part starts; every swap, every structured VDM and every vendor mode is answered `Not_Supported`
    (from a revision 2.0 partner a swap is answered `Reject` and a VDM ignored).
- [ ] `typec`, a shipping tool: connectors, partners, cables, roles, contracts, offers, modes and the last
  refusal; and the four requests. The read grant goes to the tool; the requests need the operator grant, which
  PermissionManager's policy gives only to an administrative session, as `power-control`'s is.

## P02M0202b - UCSI over ACPI

- [ ] THE TRANSPORT. The mailbox is the claim's `_CRS` memory range, admitted by the kernel's check on
  `_CRS`-derived resources (P02M0196b) - on hardware, firmware-reserved memory. VERSION is read first and fixes
  the layout: before 2.0, CCI at 4, CONTROL at 8, a 16-byte MESSAGE_IN at 16 and MESSAGE_OUT at 32; from 2.0, a
  256-byte MESSAGE_IN at 16 and MESSAGE_OUT at 272. A range shorter than the layout its version needs, or a
  major version outside 1 to 3, refuses the bind. The `_DSM` is UCSI's (`6f8398c2-7ca4-11e4-ad36-631042b5008f`,
  revision 1), function 0's mask checked for functions 1 and 2: FUNCTION 1 after every write of CONTROL and
  MESSAGE_OUT, so the platform takes them; FUNCTION 2 before every read of CCI and MESSAGE_IN, so the platform
  refreshes them. `Notify(0x80)` on the node is the PPM's signal.
- [ ] THE COMMAND DISCIPLINE. One command outstanding at a time. After a completion, CCI's length is checked
  against MESSAGE_IN's size and against the command's own answer size, and a connector number against the
  count `GET_CAPABILITY` gave, before a byte of an answer is believed; reserved values are refused. Every
  command completion and every connector change is acknowledged with `ACK_CC_CI`, and its acknowledgement
  completion waited for, before the next command. BUSY is waited on; ERROR is followed by `GET_ERROR_STATUS`,
  whose error goes to `typec` with the refused request; NOT SUPPORTED is a refusal.
- [ ] THE COMMANDS. Always: `PPM_RESET` (completion polled, notifications off), `SET_NOTIFICATION_ENABLE`,
  `GET_CAPABILITY`, `GET_CONNECTOR_CAPABILITY`, `GET_CONNECTOR_STATUS`, `GET_ERROR_STATUS`, `ACK_CC_CI`, and
  `SET_UOR`/`SET_PDR` for acceptance and role requests. Each only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises it:
  `GET_PDOS` (the partner's source offers and the connector's own), `GET_CABLE_PROPERTY`,
  `GET_ALTERNATE_MODES`, `GET_CAM_SUPPORTED`, `GET_CURRENT_CAM`, `GET_ATTENTION_VDO` (DisplayPort's hot-plug
  state), `SET_NEW_CAM` (override; for DisplayPort with the pin assignment the partner's mode VDO allows, D
  first because it keeps USB 3, then C, then E), `SET_CCOM`/`SET_UOM` and `SET_PDM`. A fact whose command the
  platform does not offer is reported as not offered, never guessed. NO command that changes power
  (`SET_POWER_LEVEL`, `SET_SINK_PATH`) is issued: on UCSI the platform owns power.
- [ ] RECOVERY. A command or an acknowledgement not completed within five seconds is a silent PPM: `PPM_RESET`,
  its completion polled for five seconds, then the configuration, the notifications and every connector read
  again. Meanwhile each connector is reported as not answering in `typec` and its `usb-c` source's `online`
  is unknown. Two failed resets in a row end the binding with a driver-reported failure, and DeviceManager's
  restart policy takes over.

## P02M0202c - A port controller, and a sink-only Power Delivery engine run by the system

- [ ] TCPCI (revision 2.0) over the address-scoped I2C connection, whose controller must declare plain I2C
  (register write-then-read with a repeated start; a controller without it is refused at bind); the alert on
  the line-scoped GPIO connection; vendor, product and revision registers read, and POWER_STATUS's
  uninitialised bit waited on for at most one second. A BIND THAT FINDS VBUS ALREADY PRESENT - a first start
  on a powered connector, or a rebind after a lost dependency - trusts no contract it did not see made: the
  contract is reported unknown, the sink path is left as the controller's registers show it, and Soft_Reset
  makes the source advertise again, so the contract is negotiated anew without VBUS dropping. The connector's
  description - the `usb-c-connector` child with `power-role`, `sink-pdos` and `op-sink-microwatt` - is read
  from the device tree or `_DSD`. A connector described as a source or dual-role is REFUSED at bind (nothing
  here can source); a sink whose description is missing or unreadable runs no Power Delivery at all and sinks
  at the Type-C current only.
- [ ] THE TYPE-C SINK STATE MACHINE: Unattached.SNK, AttachWait.SNK, and Attached.SNK once Rp is stable for
  tCCDebounce and VBUS is present; the Rp level reported as the Type-C current; detach on VBUS removal or on CC
  open for tPDDebounce; error recovery with CC open for tErrorRecovery.
- [ ] THE SINK PROTOCOL AND POLICY ENGINES, USB Power Delivery 3.1 in the Standard Power Range, revision 2.0
  partners accepted: messages on SOP only; the header, the object count and the byte count checked before a
  message is believed; a repeated MessageID discarded; Source_Capabilities evaluated, Request, then Accept,
  Reject or Wait, then PS_RDY; Get_Sink_Cap answered with the described sink offers; Soft_Reset and Hard_Reset
  in both directions; `Not_Supported` for everything else. The PROPOSED SELECTION RULE: the Fixed offer giving
  the most power at or below the board's highest described sink voltage, requesting the lesser of its current
  and the board's sink current, ties to the lower voltage; when no offer reaches `op-sink-microwatt`, position
  1 at 5 V with capability mismatch set. Pure, host-tested leaves - the message codec, the timer table, and the
  state machines as transition functions of events and time - driven by the driver with the clock and the bus.
- [ ] THE SAFETY INVARIANTS, each an acceptance criterion with a host test and a gate case:
  1. No request names a voltage above the board's highest described sink voltage, or a current above the
     offer's maximum or the board's sink current.
  2. Only Fixed offers are requested; Variable, Battery and augmented (PPS, AVS) offers are reported and never
     selected.
  3. A request names only a position of the LATEST Source_Capabilities; a new one voids every earlier offer.
  4. No contract above vSafe5V is reported until its PS_RDY arrives; between Accept and PS_RDY the contract is
     reported as in transition.
  5. The engine enables the sink path (`SinkVbus`) only in Attached.SNK with VBUS present (a bind leaves it
     as it finds it, above), and on detach, Hard Reset, error recovery or a VBUS alarm it is disabled and the
     contract voided FIRST, before any message is sent or any state published; the next contract is
     negotiated from nothing.
  6. Where the port controller measures VBUS, its alarms are set around the contract's voltage; VBUS outside
     them disables the sink path, sends Hard Reset and reports `source-fault`.
  7. A message that fails a check is not believed and nothing is inferred from it.
- [ ] THE TIMING BUDGET, an acceptance criterion:
  - TIMERS never expire before the specification's minimum. A timer is armed for ceil(minimum / granule) + 1
    granules of the kernel's deadline resolution - one tick, 10 ms, today; finer once P02M0198a lands - and
    the timer table lists every timer whose maximum that exceeds: only SenderResponseTimer (24 to 30 ms, which
    fires at 30 to 40 ms), whose late expiry delays nothing but the sink's own recovery.
  - RESPONSE: from the alert for a partner's Source_Capabilities to the Request's TRANSMIT write, NEVER 24 ms
    or more (the shortest SenderResponseTimer a source may run, after which it sends Hard Reset) and at most
    15 ms (tReceiverResponse) at the 99th percentile, over 200 negotiations measured by the fixture on x86_64
    under KVM. GoodCRC is the port controller's own and outside the budget.
  - A missed budget leaves the part open: the path is measured and fixed, and the bound is not relaxed.

## P02M0202d - Verification

- [ ] UCSI through P02M0196d's fixture, on x86_64. The SSDT's `PNP0CA0` has a `_CRS` method returning the
  mailbox inside the fixture's ivshmem region (located as the fixture's other methods locate it), minted to the
  UCSI claim under P02M0196's authorisation for that region. Its `_DSM` behaves as firmware does: function 1
  copies CONTROL and MESSAGE_OUT into an outbound staging area and rings a doorbell; function 2 copies an
  inbound staging area into CCI and MESSAGE_IN; `_AEI` on the fixture's GPIO line runs `Notify(0x80)`. The
  harness PPM answers from the staging areas only, so a driver that skips either function reads stale data.
  Two PPM profiles: VERSION 1.2 with alternate-mode override and the detail features, and VERSION 2.1
  without override, with power readings, refusing `SET_PDR`. Cases: a charger attached with its offers and a
  contract made, shown in `typec` and as an online `usb-c` source in P02M0181's service (measured VBUS under
  2.1) beside the fixture's ACPI AC adapter, which stays its own source; the configuration commands after
  every reset; a data-role swap requested through `typec` and accepted; a power-role swap refused, with the
  PPM's error in `typec`; a DisplayPort partner - entered by request under override, refused before any
  command without it, and entered by the PPM on its own and reported; a detach; a malformed answer (a length
  past MESSAGE_IN or the command's size, a connector out of range) refused; a PPM gone silent recovered
  within the bound and reported. The PPM's command log fails the gate on a second command while one is
  outstanding, a completion left unacknowledged, a read not preceded by function 2, or a write not followed
  by function 1.
- [ ] TCPCI through `tcpc-partner`, a device model added to P02M0195's `vhost-i2c-gpio.py` and selected the
  way that program selects its models (`I2C_FIXTURE=tcpc`), on the DEFAULT translated machine with the
  vhost-user IOTLB P02M0195a adds: a TCPCI register file at an I2C address, its alert on a virtio-gpio line,
  the PHY-level GoodCRC and retries a port controller does itself, and a Power Delivery SOURCE partner written
  from the specification's source-side state machines and message tables - sharing no code with the sink
  engine, so the two ends of every exchange are different machines. The gate picks the partner's script
  through the program's control socket. The SSDT describes the controller below the virtio-i2c controller's
  companion node with `PRP0001`, `I2cSerialBusV2`, `GpioInt` and the connector's `_DSD`; the device tree,
  through P02M0195's tree editor, as a `tcpci` node under the `virtio,device22` node with
  `interrupts-extended` into the `virtio,device29` node and a `usb-c-connector` child. Cases: a revision 3.1
  charger offering 5, 9, 15 and 20 V and a PPS offer, against a board described up to 15 V - the contract at
  15 V, 20 V and PPS never requested; a revision 2.0 charger; non-PD chargers at 1.5 A and 3 A; Wait then
  Accept; Reject; an offer below the operating power (5 V, capability mismatch); no Source_Capabilities (Hard
  Reset not before 310 ms, then the Type-C current after the hard-reset count); Accept without PS_RDY (Hard
  Reset not before 450 ms); no answer to a Request (not before 24 ms); malformed messages (an object count
  past the byte count, a reserved type, an extended message); PR_Swap, DR_Swap, VCONN_Swap and
  Discover_Identity answered `Not_Supported` with the contract kept; VBUS driven past the alarm (the sink path
  off before the Hard Reset, `source-fault` reported); a detach mid-negotiation; a new Source_Capabilities
  with less power (a new request); the virtio-i2c binding disabled and enabled through the device policy's
  verbs during a 15 V contract - the TCPCI driver stopped as a lost dependency with both connections closed,
  bound again, and the contract made anew through Soft_Reset while the partner records VBUS never dropping.
  The timing run: 200 negotiations, the response distribution in the gate's log. x86_64 through the SSDT;
  aarch64 and riscv64 through the tree in their emulated sweep, as P02M0195's gates run, where the timing is
  recorded and the budget is not gated - it is gated only under KVM.
- [ ] Host suites: every UCSI answer decoder, CCI and both mailbox layouts against the specification's
  structures and hostile lengths; the Power Delivery codec and the sink engine under a virtual clock - every
  safety invariant, a start with VBUS present, and every timer's minimum at 10 ms and at 1 ms granules; the
  `usbc` adapter and its validation; the connector record. Each gate and suite registered in the verification
  model as the other milestones' are.
- [ ] A recorded run on real hardware where the owner provides a board, never in place of the fixtures.

EXCLUDES: USB4 and Thunderbolt - PCIe tunnels and their DMA authorisation are a subsystem of their own, not a
connector's; vendor UCSI transports (Qualcomm's GLINK, Cypress's CCGx over I2C); a USB device-mode controller
for the port that became a device - no such controller is emulated; delivery of the data role to the USB host
controllers and of DisplayPort to a display controller (P02M0202a says why). ON A PORT CONTROLLER, UNTIL A
BOARD WITH ONE IS NAMED: Extended Power Range, the source and dual-role roles and VCONN sourcing, power-role,
data-role, VCONN and fast role swaps, structured VDMs as initiator (identity, SVID and mode discovery) and the
DisplayPort alternate mode, and augmented offers (PPS, AVS); and a charger driver that sets its input limit
from the contract - no charger is emulated, and the board's described sink capabilities are what bound a
request here.

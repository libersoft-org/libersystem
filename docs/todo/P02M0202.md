# P02M0202 - USB Type-C connectors: UCSI, a port controller with Power Delivery, and what they feed

Status: OPEN. Created 2026-09-25 under the owner's approval of P02M0196 - "yes, do all of it; where a
milestone is missing, create it". NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for plans only until he says
otherwise, and no part starts without its own written plan.

WHY. A Type-C connector negotiates who powers whom and at what voltage and current (USB Power Delivery), which
side is the USB host, and whether the cable carries DisplayPort in place of some of its USB lanes. On most
laptops an embedded controller runs all of that and reports it through UCSI - on x86 an ACPI device
(`PNP0CA0`) whose mailbox is a memory region and whose doorbell is a `_DSM` - and the operating system reads the
state, configures what the platform may accept and asks for changes. On boards without such a controller, the
operating system runs Power Delivery itself over a port controller (TCPCI) on I2C; here that engine is a SINK
in the Standard Power Range and nothing more, until a board with a port controller is named. P02M0099 lists
UCSI with NO destination, and nothing in the system has a notion of a connector, a power contract or an
alternate mode.

ORDER. P02M0202a needs only what has landed - P02M0180's kind-scoped catalogue client and P02M0181's
PowerService - and lands first, proved by host suites until a driver publishes. P02M0202b needs P02M0196a's
platform-device claim (several MMIO ranges) and its `cid` match, P02M0196b's `_DSM`, `Notify` and node-scoped
ACPI channel with its admission of a mailbox the claimed node's own AML shares (P02M0202b's transport item
names the two admissions), and P02M0196d's fixture. P02M0202c needs P02M0195a (the I2C contract with plain I2C
in its functionality set, the GPIO line contract, the scoped connections, and `vhost-i2c-gpio.py` serving the
vhost-user IOTLB), P02M0195b's child binding, and P02M0196's companion join, through which the port
controller's resource sources resolve to the controllers that serve them. Two pieces are carried by whichever
lands second: the drivers' sleep exchange with P02M0197 (P02M0202a's SUSPEND AND RESUME item, each half with
its driver's part), and the first UCSI case's ACPI AC adapter with P02M0099's ACPI AC adapter driver
(P02M0202d).

## P02M0202a - The processes, the vocabulary, and where each fact goes

- [ ] THE PROCESSES. Only a driver holding a claim publishes; the service that serves readers holds none.
  - `ucsi-acpi`, a driver bound to the platform device (P02M0196a) whose `_HID` OR `_CID` is `PNP0CA0` - one
    match row with the `hid` predicate and one with `cid`, as Linux's driver matches it - because firmware
    built on Intel's reference design gives the device its own `_HID`, `USBC000`, and names UCSI by `_CID`
    `PNP0CA0`, as shipping laptops do. Its claim mints the mailbox range its `_CRS` names; with the claim
    comes the node-scoped ACPI channel (P02M0196b) through which it evaluates that node's `_DSM` and receives
    its `Notify` values. The platform's PPM runs Power Delivery; this driver is UCSI's OPM.
  - `tcpci`, a driver bound to the port controller's node as a CHILD BINDING, exactly as P02M0195b binds a
    HID-over-I2C device: the node (matched by the `compatible` `tcpci` in a device tree, or by `PRP0001` with
    that `compatible` in `_DSD` under ACPI) is a platform device of its own below the I2C controller's
    companion. At bind DeviceManager mints, through each controller driver's scoped `CONNECT`, an
    ADDRESS-SCOPED I2C connection (the `I2cSerialBusV2` address or `reg`) and a LINE-SCOPED GPIO connection
    (the alert's `GpioInt` or `interrupts-extended`), which the controllers enforce and which arrive with the
    claim. Both are requirements naming the controllers' functions, so the driver binds after both parents
    and is stopped as a lost dependency (`DependencyLost`) when either ends. The Type-C sink state machine and
    the sink policy engine (P02M0202c) run INSIDE this process, so the path from the alert to the answer
    crosses no process but the two controllers'.
  - Each driver publishes TWO providers from its binding: `power-source` (P02M0181's kind; one local source
    per connector that can sink) and `typec-connector` (below; every connector). Manifest entries declare
    `{ kind = "power-source", most = 1, consumers = 1 }` and `{ kind = "typec-connector", most = 1,
    consumers = 1 }`. A SOURCE'S LOCAL ID IS ITS CONNECTOR'S NUMBER LESS ONE: connectors are numbered from 1,
    and PowerService takes local ids 0 to 15 and ends a provider that sends 16 or more, so connectors 1 to 16
    are ids 0 to 15, and a PPM that reports more than 16 connectors is served for the first 16 and the rest
    are logged as unserved. A connector's source lives as long as the binding - an attach or a detach changes
    `present` and never removes it - because PowerService also ends a provider that publishes a local id
    again after removing it.
  - TypeCService (`typec_service`), which holds no claim: it consumes every `typec-connector` publication
    through a catalogue connection minted for that kind alone (the `open-consumer(kinds)` path P02M0180
    landed) and serves two roots, `liber:typec@1/typec` (read) and `liber:typec@1/typec-control`
    (operator). Its manifest row has the shape of `power_service`'s: a CATALOGUE factory role with
    `kinds = ["typec-connector"]`, the two serve-root roles, restart transparent, state reconstructible - a
    restarted instance takes a fresh snapshot from every provider and its subscribers subscribe again.
- [ ] `typec-connector`, appended to `provider-kind` in `liber:device@1` (the next free value when it lands,
  never renumbered; its one consumer TypeCService), and a new package `liber:typec@1`:
  - the PROVIDER CONTRACT a driver serves to TypeCService alone: a stream that opens with an atomic snapshot
    and a monotonic provider revision, then connector added, updated and removed (the shape of
    `power-provider`), and `request`;
  - the CONNECTOR RECORD: its number; what it can do (power roles, data roles, Power Delivery, the transport -
    UCSI or TCPCI - and on UCSI the PPM's optional features, alternate-mode override among them); the partner
    (none, a charger, a device, a host, a powered cable, an audio or a debug accessory) and the plug's
    orientation; the power role and the data role; the power operation mode (default USB, BC 1.2, Type-C
    1.5 A, Type-C 3 A, Power Delivery); THE CONTRACT - the selected offer's position and object, the
    operating and maximum current the request named and its capability-mismatch flag, or the Type-C current
    level where there is no Power Delivery contract; the partner's source offers (at most 11: Power
    Delivery's seven standard positions and four extended ones); the cable where it is known; the partner's
    alternate modes (SVID and mode VDO, at most 16), the entered ones, and DisplayPort's pin assignment and
    hot-plug state where the transport reports them; the last refused request and its reason; and whether
    the transport is currently answering;
  - the READ interface: enumerate, and subscribe - an atomic snapshot at (epoch, revision), then changes; a
    subscriber that does not drain is closed and subscribes again, as `liber:power@1`'s are;
  - the OPERATOR interface: data-role swap, power-role swap, enter a mode, exit a mode - one outstanding
    request per connector, a fifteen-second deadline (UCSI's ten-second command bound, P02M0202b, and five
    seconds for the connector change that reports the result, as Linux's driver waits for a swap), answered
    `done`, `refused` with a reason (the connector cannot, the platform does not offer the command, the
    platform enters modes itself, the PPM refused with its error, the transport does not do it) or
    `indeterminate`, which is never retried (P02M0181's rule for controls).
  Nothing is versioned: `liber:device@1` gains one enum value and `liber:typec@1` starts at 1.
- [ ] POWER. `liber:power@1` gains `usb-c`, appended to `source-kind`: the partner on one connector as a
  supply. `present` - a partner is attached; `online` - this machine sinks from it. `voltage`, `current` and
  `power` carry ONLY what is measured - a UCSI 2.1 PPM's VBUS voltage and average current readings when its
  reading-ready bit is set, a port controller's VBUS voltage register where it has one - signed as the record
  already defines (negative delivered out of the partner into this machine, positive into the partner while
  this machine supplies it), and are unsupported otherwise. WHILE `present` IS NO, NOTHING IS KNOWN: VBUS on
  an empty connector is not a partner's, and `canon::validate` refuses an absent source that carries a known
  value - a refusal that ends the provider, and every connector's source with it - so the `usbc` adapter
  publishes a measurement the transport has as `unknown` while no partner is attached, in an unplugged
  connector's first snapshot and after every detach, and one the transport lacks stays unsupported. A port
  controller's VBUS fault is a reported `source-fault` alarm. A `usb-c` source has no capacity, charge state,
  runtime, load, temperature, trips or controls. THE NEGOTIATED CONTRACT IS A LIMIT, NOT A MEASUREMENT, and is
  never written into those fields: it and the partner's offers are `typec`'s. `power-model` gains a `usbc`
  adapter both drivers call before publishing, and `canon::validate` refuses a `usb-c` record that carries a
  field the kind does not have or a KNOWN voltage above 60 V. That bound stands well clear of a correct
  reading - Extended Power Range's highest contract, 48 V, reads up to 50.9 V within tolerance, 5% and 0.5 V
  over its voltage as the TCPCI specification computes a 20 V contract's upper bound, and a measurement adds
  its own error - and the adapter publishes a reading past it as INVALID (range), never as known, because a
  refused record ends the provider and with it every connector's source. Host tests: a 48 V contract reading
  50.9 V accepted as known, a reading past 60 V invalid from the adapter and refused when a record claims it
  known, a field the kind lacks refused, and an unplugged connector's first snapshot and a detach - each with
  the controller's VBUS register reading about 0 V - published canonical, with no known measurement. The enum
  value is the only wire change - no record changes shape.
  THE ACPI AC ADAPTER AND A `usb-c` SOURCE ARE TWO REPORTS, NOT ONE SOURCE. On a laptop `_PSR` (P02M0099's ACPI
  item, delivered to P02M0181) says the machine is on line power, whichever jack it comes through; the UCSI
  connector says which partner, on which connector, under which contract. Firmware describes no join between
  them, so none is invented: PowerService publishes both as the devices reported them and merges nothing, and
  the kind's documentation says a reader must not add them. A board without an ACPI AC adapter - a TCPCI
  board - has the `usb-c` source alone.
- [ ] THE DATA ROLE AND DISPLAYPORT ARE REPORTED IN `typec` AND NOWHERE ELSE. The USB drivers have no notion of
  a data role, nothing maps a Type-C connector to a root-hub port, DisplayService drives one surface and its
  device contract carries only resizes and replaced backings, and a display controller that reaches a
  monitor's bus is unowned (P02M0099). Delivering these facts waits for the milestones that create a
  connector-to-port map and a connector-aware display controller. A connector whose data role became device,
  on a machine with no device-mode controller, is shown as such rather than hidden.
- [ ] POLICY, BY TRANSPORT, with defaults the owner confirms when the part starts:
  - UCSI: the PPM negotiates contracts, answers the partner's swaps and - unless it grants override - enters
    alternate modes itself. The system CONFIGURES, after every PPM reset: swap acceptance through the accept
    bits of `SET_UOR` and `SET_PDR`, and the connector's operation mode (`SET_CCOM`, `SET_UOM` before UCSI
    2.0) and power direction mode (`SET_PDM`), each only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises it. PROPOSED
    DEFAULT, asked when the part starts: accept both swap directions and leave the operation modes as the PPM
    reports them. The system ASKS on an operator's request: a role through `SET_UOR`/`SET_PDR`; a mode entered
    or left through `SET_NEW_CAM` ONLY where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises alternate-mode override - otherwise
    the request is refused before any command is sent and `typec` says the platform enters modes itself. The
    system never enters a mode on its own: nothing here consumes DisplayPort.
  - TCPCI: the sink engine's selection rule (P02M0202c) is the system's own policy, PROPOSED DEFAULT asked
    when the part starts; every swap, every structured VDM and every vendor mode is answered `Not_Supported`
    (from a revision 2.0 partner a swap is answered `Reject` and a VDM ignored).
- [ ] SUSPEND AND RESUME, P02M0197's exchange in both drivers, each half landing with its driver's part (b or
  c) and each driver declaring its `suspend-deadline`, carried by whichever of P02M0197 and this milestone
  lands second - P02M0197 refuses every sleep while a binding without one is Online, `ucsi-acpi` binds on
  every UCSI laptop through the `_CID` match, and P02M0197's own gates boot the fixture SSDT this plan's UCSI
  device is added to:
  - `ucsi-acpi`: `SUSPEND` finishes the command in flight - its deadline covers the ten-second command bound,
    since a UCSI command is finished, never abandoned - and sends none until `RESUME`; a notification that
    arrives meanwhile waits on the node channel. `RESUME` enables notifications again and reads every
    connector again, as RECOVERY does after a reset, because the PPM may have changed anything while the host
    slept; each connector is reported as not answering until its read completes.
  - `tcpci`: THE ENGINE DOES NOT RUN WHILE ITS BINDING IS SUSPENDED - its alert reaches it through the GPIO
    controller's binding, suspended after its consumers - so while a partner is attached nothing would keep
    invariants 5 and 6: nothing would turn the sink path off on a VBUS alarm or a Hard Reset. So its
    `SUSPEND` is REFUSED, naming the connector, while the sink path is enabled; with no partner attached it
    suspends with the sink path off and the alert unwatched, and a partner that attaches while the machine
    sleeps finds the sink path off, which only the engine turns on. Its `RESUME` reads the port afresh as a
    bind does: a partner found with VBUS present has its contract reported unknown and negotiated anew
    through Soft_Reset. Sleeping with a partner attached waits for an alert kept as a wake source through
    its GPIO controller across the sleep (EXCLUDES).
- [ ] `typec`, a shipping tool: connectors, partners, cables, roles, contracts, offers, modes and the last
  refusal. IT HOLDS THE READ GRANT ONLY. The OPERATOR grant - the four requests - goes to no shipping program:
  PermissionManager's policy gives it to `typeccheck`, the development probe that drives the gate's requests,
  as the power gate's `powercheck` is the one holder of `power-control`. Whether the shipping tool also
  receives it, and with it the four requests, as `btctl` holds Bluetooth's operator authority, is asked of the
  owner when the part starts: it would be the second shipping row that grants an operator authority.

## P02M0202b - UCSI over ACPI

- [ ] THE TRANSPORT. The mailbox is the claim's `_CRS` memory range, and IT IS SHARED: the claim maps it for
  the driver, and the node's own AML reads and writes the same range through a SystemMemory region the node
  declares - inside `_DSM`, copying between the mailbox and the embedded controller, and before its
  `Notify` - as Intel's reference design and shipping laptops do. Both mappings use ONE memory type:
  write-back where the range is firmware-reserved or ACPI NVS memory, as a mailbox in main memory is on
  hardware, and uncached where it is MMIO, as the fixture's is. So this transport REQUIRES two admissions of
  P02M0196b, and P02M0196b carries both: a namespace device's `_CRS` range over firmware-reserved or ACPI
  NVS memory (usable RAM stays refused), and a SystemMemory region that the claimed node ITSELF declares
  inside its own `_CRS` range, which the ACPI service may still map while the device is claimed - a region
  any other node declares over a claimed range stays refused; in the fixture, the harness-played range
  inside the firmware-held ivshmem BAR is admitted for the claim and stays reachable by the node's own AML.
  VERSION is read first and fixes the layout: before 2.0, CCI at 4, CONTROL at 8, a 16-byte MESSAGE_IN at 16
  and MESSAGE_OUT at 32; from 2.0, a 256-byte MESSAGE_IN at 16 and MESSAGE_OUT at 272. A range shorter than
  the layout its version needs, or a major version outside 1 to 3, refuses the bind. The `_DSM` is UCSI's
  (`6f8398c2-7ca4-11e4-ad36-631042b5008f`, revision 1), function 0's mask checked for functions 1 and 2:
  FUNCTION 1 after every write of CONTROL and MESSAGE_OUT, so the platform takes them. FUNCTION 2, which has
  the platform refresh the mailbox, ONLY where no notification has refreshed it: to read VERSION at bind, and
  to poll CCI while notifications are off (the `PPM_RESET` completion). NEVER AFTER A NOTIFICATION: the
  firmware refreshes CCI and MESSAGE_IN before its `Notify(0x80)` - a shipping laptop's notification method
  runs the same copy its function 2 makes, then notifies - so the driver reads them as they are, and a
  function 2 after a notification breaks real firmware, which may spoil its own copy once it has notified.
  `Notify(0x80)` on the node is the PPM's signal.
- [ ] THE COMMAND DISCIPLINE. One command outstanding at a time. After a completion, CCI's length is checked
  against MESSAGE_IN's size and against the command's own answer size, and a connector number against the
  count `GET_CAPABILITY` gave, before a byte of an answer is believed; reserved values are refused. Every
  command completion is acknowledged with `ACK_CC_CI`, and its acknowledgement completion waited for, before
  the next command; a connector change is acknowledged in the same `ACK_CC_CI` as the completion of the
  `GET_CONNECTOR_STATUS` that reads it, never alone, since some PPMs refuse a lone connector-change
  acknowledgement. BUSY is waited on; ERROR is followed by `GET_ERROR_STATUS`, whose error goes to `typec`
  with the refused request; NOT SUPPORTED is a refusal.
- [ ] THE COMMANDS. Always: `PPM_RESET` (completion polled, notifications off), `SET_NOTIFICATION_ENABLE`,
  `GET_CAPABILITY`, `GET_CONNECTOR_CAPABILITY`, `GET_CONNECTOR_STATUS`, `GET_ERROR_STATUS`, `ACK_CC_CI`, and
  `SET_UOR`/`SET_PDR` for acceptance and role requests. Each only where `GET_CAPABILITY` advertises it:
  `GET_PDOS` (the partner's source offers and the connector's own), `GET_CABLE_PROPERTY`,
  `GET_ALTERNATE_MODES`, `GET_CAM_SUPPORTED`, `GET_CURRENT_CAM`, `GET_ATTENTION_VDO` (DisplayPort's hot-plug
  state), `SET_NEW_CAM` (override; for DisplayPort with the pin assignment the partner's mode VDO allows, D
  first because it keeps USB 3, then C, then E), `SET_CCOM`/`SET_UOM` and `SET_PDM`. A fact whose command the
  platform does not offer is reported as not offered, never guessed. NO command that changes power
  (`SET_POWER_LEVEL`, `SET_SINK_PATH`) is issued: on UCSI the platform owns power.
- [ ] RECOVERY, WITH THE BOUNDS REAL FIRMWARE NEEDS. Every bound is TEN SECONDS - Linux's driver raised its own
  from five after shipping laptops took eight to ten seconds for the first `PPM_RESET` and more than five for
  the commands after it (`SET_NOTIFICATION_ENABLE`, `GET_CAPABILITY`): a command or an acknowledgement not
  completed within ten seconds is a silent PPM, and a reset's completion is polled for ten seconds. A silent
  PPM gets `PPM_RESET`, then the configuration, the notifications and every connector read again. Meanwhile
  each connector is reported as not answering in `typec` and its `usb-c` source's `online` is unknown. Two
  failed resets in a row end the binding with a driver-reported failure, and DeviceManager's restart policy
  takes over. A SLOW FIRST RESET DOES NOT HOLD THE BIND: the driver reports ready to DeviceManager, whose
  ready deadline is two seconds, once the mailbox is mapped and VERSION checked, and runs the first reset and
  the first reads after that; its two providers are offered when every connector has been read.

## P02M0202c - A port controller, and a sink-only Power Delivery engine run by the system

- [ ] TCPCI (revision 2.0) over the address-scoped I2C connection, whose controller must declare plain I2C
  (register write-then-read with a repeated start; a controller without it is refused at bind); the alert on
  the line-scoped GPIO connection; vendor, product and revision registers read, and POWER_STATUS's
  uninitialised bit waited on for at most one second. A BIND THAT FINDS VBUS ALREADY PRESENT - a first start
  on a powered connector, or a rebind after a lost dependency - trusts no contract it did not see made: the
  contract is reported unknown, the sink path is left as the controller's registers show it, and Soft_Reset
  makes the source advertise again, so the contract is negotiated anew without VBUS dropping. The connector's
  description - the `usb-c-connector` child with `power-role`, `sink-pdos` and `op-sink-microwatt` - is read
  from the device tree or `_DSD`. A connector described as a source or dual-role is REFUSED at bind (nothing
  here can source); a sink whose description is missing or unreadable runs no Power Delivery at all and sinks
  at the Type-C current only.
- [ ] THE TYPE-C SINK STATE MACHINE: Unattached.SNK, AttachWait.SNK, and Attached.SNK once Rp is stable for
  tCCDebounce and VBUS is present; the Rp level reported as the Type-C current; detach on CC open for
  tPDDebounce, and on VBUS removal OUTSIDE A HARD RESET only, as the Type-C specification's exit from
  Attached.SNK says - a source answers a Hard Reset by taking VBUS to vSafe0V and back. From a Hard Reset,
  sent or received, the port controller's automatic discharge on disconnect is held off, and VBUS is expected
  to fall within tSafe0V (some sources keep it up) and to return within tSrcRecover and tSrcTurnOn at their
  maximums; the engine detaches only if it does not return, the partner stays attached in `typec`
  throughout, and the hard-reset count survives the reset, cleared only by a contract made or a detach.
  Error recovery with CC open for tErrorRecovery.
- [ ] THE SINK PROTOCOL AND POLICY ENGINES, USB Power Delivery 3.1 in the Standard Power Range, revision 2.0
  partners accepted: messages on SOP only; the header, the object count and the byte count checked before a
  message is believed; a repeated MessageID discarded; Source_Capabilities evaluated, Request, then Accept,
  Reject or Wait, then PS_RDY; Get_Sink_Cap answered with the described sink offers; Soft_Reset and Hard_Reset
  in both directions; `Not_Supported` for everything else. The PROPOSED SELECTION RULE: among the Fixed offers
  whose voltage lies inside a described sink PDO, the one giving the most power with its current limited to
  that sink PDO's, requesting that limited current, ties to the lower voltage; when no offer reaches
  `op-sink-microwatt`, position 1 at 5 V, its current limited the same way, with capability mismatch set.
  Pure, host-tested leaves - the message codec, the timer table, and the state machines as transition
  functions of events and time - driven by the driver with the clock and the bus.
- [ ] THE SAFETY INVARIANTS, each an acceptance criterion with a host test and a gate case:
  1. A request's voltage lies inside ONE described sink PDO - equal to a Fixed sink PDO's voltage, or within
     a Variable or Battery sink PDO's range - and its operating and maximum currents are at or below both the
     offer's maximum and that sink PDO's current (for a Battery sink PDO, its power divided by the voltage).
     A board describing Fixed 5 V and 15 V never requests a 9 V or a 12 V offer.
  2. Only Fixed offers are requested; Variable, Battery and augmented (PPS, AVS) offers are reported and never
     selected.
  3. A request names only a position of the LATEST Source_Capabilities; a new one voids every earlier offer.
  4. No contract above vSafe5V is reported until its PS_RDY arrives; between Accept and PS_RDY the contract is
     reported as in transition.
  5. The engine enables the sink path (`SinkVbus`) only in Attached.SNK with VBUS present (a bind leaves it
     as it finds it, above), and on detach, Hard Reset, error recovery or a VBUS alarm it is disabled and the
     contract voided FIRST, before any message is sent or any state published; the next contract is
     negotiated from nothing.
  6. Where the port controller measures VBUS, its alarms bound VBUS to what the contract allows, with the
     tolerance the specification gives a source's output and a cable's drop: vSafe5V's range from attach;
     from Accept to PS_RDY a span from the lower of the old and the new contract's lower bounds to the higher
     of their upper bounds, narrowed to the new contract's at PS_RDY; after a bind that finds VBUS present
     and trusts no contract, from vSafe5V's lower bound to the upper bound of the highest described sink
     voltage, until the first PS_RDY; and off from a Hard Reset until VBUS is back at vSafe5V, while the
     sink path is off. VBUS outside them disables the sink path, sends Hard Reset and reports `source-fault`.
  7. A message that fails a check is not believed and nothing is inferred from it.
- [ ] THE TIMING BUDGET, an acceptance criterion:
  - TIMERS never expire before the specification's minimum. A timer is armed for ceil(minimum / 10 ms) + 1
    ticks: the kernel's deadline resolution is the ABI's 10 ms tick, which no planned milestone makes finer
    (P02M0198a keeps every deadline an absolute tick). The timer table lists every timer whose maximum that
    exceeds: only SenderResponseTimer (24 to 30 ms, which fires at 30 to 40 ms), whose late expiry delays
    nothing but the sink's own recovery.
  - RESPONSE: from the alert for a partner's Source_Capabilities to the Request's TRANSMIT write, NEVER 24 ms
    or more (the shortest SenderResponseTimer a source may run, after which it sends Hard Reset) and at most
    15 ms (tReceiverResponse) at the 99th percentile, over 200 negotiations measured by the fixture on x86_64
    under KVM. GoodCRC is the port controller's own and outside the budget.
  - A missed budget leaves the part open: the path is measured and fixed, and the bound is not relaxed.

## P02M0202d - Verification

- [ ] UCSI through P02M0196d's fixture, on x86_64. The SSDT's UCSI device has `_HID` `USBC000` and `_CID`
  `PNP0CA0`, the form shipping laptops use, so the `_CID` match is what is proved. Its `_CRS` method returns
  the mailbox inside the fixture's ivshmem region (located as the fixture's other methods locate it), admitted
  for the UCSI claim; the device declares its own SystemMemory region over that range, and its `_DSM` behaves
  as firmware does: function 1 copies CONTROL and MESSAGE_OUT into an outbound staging area and rings a
  doorbell; function 2 copies an inbound staging area into CCI and MESSAGE_IN and logs its evaluation in the
  region, where the harness reads it; `_AEI` on the fixture's GPIO line makes the same copy and then runs
  `Notify(0x80)`, as a shipping laptop's notification method does. The staging areas and the log lie outside
  the mailbox range, in the region the ivshmem function's companion declares, so no other node's region covers
  the claimed range. The harness PPM answers only through the staging areas, and completes `PPM_RESET` in the
  inbound staging area alone - its notifications are off, so nothing else copies it - so a driver that polls
  without function 2 never sees the reset complete and never publishes. Four PPM profiles: VERSION 1.2 with
  alternate-mode override and the detail features; VERSION 2.1 without override, with power readings, refusing
  `SET_PDR`; a SLOW one, whose first reset and the two commands after it each take longer than five seconds
  and less than ten; and a SPOILING one, which spoils its inbound staging area once it has notified, as some
  shipping firmware spoils its copy, so a function 2 after a notification is also caught by its effect. Cases:
  a charger attached with its offers and a contract made, shown in `typec` and as an online `usb-c` source in
  P02M0181's service (measured VBUS under 2.1) - and BESIDE AN ACPI AC ADAPTER, WHICH STAYS ITS OWN SOURCE, a
  half carried by whichever of this milestone and P02M0099's ACPI AC adapter driver lands second, since no
  plan adds that device and QEMU has none: that change adds an `ACPI0003` device to the gate's SSDT, its
  `_PSR` read from the harness region, and shows it in P02M0181's service as a source of its own, merged with
  nothing; the configuration commands after every reset; a data-role swap requested by `typeccheck` and
  accepted; a power-role swap requested by it and refused, with the PPM's error in `typec`; a DisplayPort

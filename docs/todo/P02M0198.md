# P02M0198 - Processor and thermal power: idle without a tick, idle and performance states, cooling

Status: OPEN. Created 2026-09-25 under the owner's approval of P02M0196 - "yes, do all of it; where a
milestone is missing, create it". NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for plans only until he says
otherwise, and no part starts without its own written plan.

WHY IT IS A MILESTONE OF ITS OWN. P02M0196's step 3 evaluates what the firmware says about the processors and
the thermal zones. What the system DOES with it is this milestone, together with the part of processor power
that needs no firmware at all. P02M0181 owns thermal STATE - a zone's temperature and trip points as a reader
sees them - and leaves "fan curves" outside; nothing owns the processor's power.

WHAT EXISTS, read in the tree: every core, busy or idle, takes a periodic interrupt at `TICK_HZ` (100 Hz) - the
LAPIC timer on x86_64, CNTP on aarch64, the SBI timer on riscv64 - and the monotonic clock is that tick, advanced
by whichever core's interrupt first sees a whole period of the free-running counter elapsed (`advance_ticks`,
`TickClock`). The tick is THE ABI UNIT: `SYS_CLOCK_GET` answers it, every deadline argument (`SYS_WAIT`,
`SYS_WAIT_ANY`, `SYS_WAITSET_WAIT`, `SYS_TIMER_SET`) is an absolute tick, and `TICKS_PER_SECOND` is in the ABI;
`SYS_CLOCK_MONO_NS` reads the counter itself. The tick also carries duties besides preemption, listed in part a.
An idle core executes `hlt` or `wfi` and nothing deeper. No core ever changes its clock.

## P02M0198a - Idle without a tick (needs no firmware, and is what a VM gains)

- [ ] TICKS STAY THE ABI UNIT, AND NOTHING IN USERSPACE CHANGES. `TICKS_PER_SECOND` stays 100, `SYS_CLOCK_GET`
  still answers ticks and every deadline is still an absolute tick, so TimeService's and NetworkService's private
  copies of the rate stay right. What changes is where a tick comes from: `ticks()` is COMPUTED WHEN READ from the
  free-running counter (the TSC, CNTVCT, the `time` CSR) - the counter less the sleep offset, less the anchor set
  once the counter's frequency is known, divided by the cycles in one tick - instead of being advanced by an
  interrupt. A reading is kept non-decreasing across cores by one atomic maximum, as the counter-gated clock is
  today; a test build's harness skew stays an added term; `SYS_CLOCK_MONO_NS` reads the same counter less the same
  offset.
- [ ] ONE SLEEP-OFFSET TERM, the whole of this part's share of a sleep: one kernel value subtracted from every
  counter reading, zero until the first sleep, and one rebase operation - given the counter at suspend and at
  resume, the offset becomes the counter at resume less the monotonic value at suspend - which covers a counter
  that kept running (suspend to idle) and one that restarted (S3) with the same arithmetic. Every hardware compare
  value is converted from a tick deadline through the offset. P02M0197 owns what a sleep MEANS for the clocks (the
  monotonic clock excludes it, its boot-time clock includes it) and calls the rebase. ORDER: this part lands
  before P02M0197b, and suspend to idle relies on this tickless idle rather than stopping the tick itself.
- [ ] A BUSY CORE KEEPS ITS PERIODIC TICK. While a core runs a thread its tick still carries the time slice, the
  kill point for a ring-3 loop, `SIG_STOP` enforcement, the TLB-shootdown answer (`on_timer_preempt`) and, on
  x86, the serial transmit drain (`on_timer_tick`). Only an IDLE core stops it: on entering idle it programs a
  one-shot instead - the TSC-deadline MSR where CPUID offers it, otherwise a one-shot LAPIC count re-armed in
  steps; CNTP_CVAL; `stimecmp` where the hart has Sstc, otherwise `sbi_set_timer` - and it restarts the periodic
  tick when it leaves idle with a thread to run.
- [ ] EVERY OTHER DUTY OF THE TICK, WITH ITS REPLACEMENT:
  - DEADLINE EXPIRY stays on the BSP, the one core that runs `check_deadlines`. Every halt the BSP makes - the
    deadline wait inside `run_until_idle_until` and the console loop's settled halt - first programs its one-shot
    for the GLOBAL EARLIEST deadline: progress waits, housekeeping (`WAIT_PERIODIC`) waits and part d's forced
    power-off deadline, capped by the housekeeping bound below. `min_deadline` still leaves housekeeping waits out
    of what counts as PROGRESS; it no longer decides when the BSP wakes.
  - A DEADLINE ARMED ON ANOTHER CORE earlier than the wake the BSP has published sends the BSP a wake IPI, and the
    BSP re-programs. So does every change the BSP's idle hook must notice without an interrupt of its own: a lost
    SystemManager, and the shell starting to listen.
  - WORK ENQUEUED ON AN IDLE CORE from another core always comes with a wake IPI; the tick is no longer the
    backstop that picks it up.
  - SERIAL RECEIVE. x86 already takes COM1's IRQ4. On aarch64 and riscv64 the kernel arms its own console UART's
    receive interrupt, read from the device tree's node for that UART: the PL011's SPI through the kernel's
    `gic::enable_spi`, the 16550's APLIC source through the wired-EID path hot-plug slots already use. A board
    that routes it to a controller the kernel does not drive keeps today's poll, and its BSP's longest idle is
    one tick. The line is released if a userspace driver later takes the port, as P02M0191 does for IRQ4.
  - THE POLLED HOUSEKEEPING - hot-plug ports with no routed slot interrupt, PCI error reporting and the IOMMU
    fault queue (`service_faults_if_due`) - runs on the BSP at a HOUSEKEEPING BOUND of ten ticks (100 ms), the
    idle BSP's longest sleep while any of them exists on the machine. A routed slot interrupt and the SCI are
    routed to the BSP and end its halt at once, as today. A busy BSP keeps its one-tick slice, so the IOMMU bound
    is unchanged while it works.
  - HELD CONSOLE INPUT (`console_input::drain`) and, on x86, a NON-EMPTY SERIAL TRANSMIT RING each cap an idle
    core's sleep at one tick until they are empty - the latency they have today.
- [ ] Per-core idle accounting in the system graph - time idle, wakeups per second and what caused them (the
  timer, an IPI, each device vector, the housekeeping bound) - through a new FREE READ SYSCALL answering one
  record per core, as `SYS_IRQ_INFO` answers one per vector; parts b and c add their fields to the same record.
  Because "the processor rests when there is nothing to do" is a number or it is only a claim.

## P02M0198 - Where each half runs (decided before parts b to d)

THE KERNEL holds what runs at the scheduler's rate or needs ring 0: the idle governor and the entry into an idle
state, the performance governor and the register writes that carry out its choice, idle injection, and the
latency bound. USERSPACE holds what is AML, policy or a person's choice: evaluating the firmware's objects (the
ACPI service, P02M0196b and P02M0196c), the profiles, the thermal policy and the fans. One privileged interface
joins them.

- [ ] ONE USERSPACE HOLDER: ProcessorPowerService (`processor_power_service`), a new service - restart
  transparent, state reconstructible - alone holds the kernel's processor-power authority: a new `ProcessorPower`
  privilege kind, delivered as a manifest `privilege` role from the kernel, as DeviceManager's DEVPRIV is. It
  receives each processor's evaluated `_PSS`, `_PCT`, `_PPC`, `_PSD`, `_CPC`, `_CST`, `_LPI`, `_PTC`, `_TSS`,
  `_TPC` and `_TSD`, and the processor's `Notify` values (0x80 performance, 0x81 idle, 0x82 throttling), over a
  contract the ACPI service serves it through a manifest client role (P02M0196c's side). Each processor is keyed
  by its namespace path and its `_UID`, and the `_UID` matched against the MADT's processor UID names the
  kernel's core. The processor capabilities the ACPI service declares to the firmware (`_OSC`, and `_PDC` where
  there is no `_OSC`) are exactly the forms the kernel executes, so the firmware never hands over a table the
  kernel refuses. What it installed stays in the kernel across its own restart, and a restarted instance installs
  it again from a fresh evaluation.
- [ ] THE KERNEL INTERFACE, every operation requiring that privilege: install a core's IDLE-STATE TABLE (at most
  eight states - entry method, exit latency and target residency in microseconds, whether the state loses the
  core's context or stops its timer); install a core's PERFORMANCE TABLE (`_PSS` states with `_PCT`'s registers
  and `_PSD`'s coordination domain, or CPPC's performance levels with `_CPC`'s desired, minimum and maximum
  registers, and the throttling states with `_PTC`'s register - at most 32 states each); set a core's
  performance WINDOW, a floor and a cap the kernel's governor never leaves, which is where `_PPC`, the thermal
  limits and the profile meet; set a core's IDLE-INJECTION share, at most one half. The device tree's
  `idle-states` need no interpreter: the kernel reads them itself at boot on aarch64 and riscv64, and a table
  installed later replaces them.
- [ ] EVERY REGISTER A TABLE NAMES IS CHECKED AT INSTALL, and a table with one refused register is refused whole.
  A SystemIO register is refused if it is in P02M0191's reserved set or a claim holds it; otherwise it becomes
  kernel-held, added to that reserved set so no later claim can take it. A SystemMemory register is refused if it
  is RAM, a range the kernel holds or inside a claim; otherwise it is admitted under the memory-class policy the
  kernel enforces for the ACPI service's SystemMemory regions (P02M0196b), mapped uncached by the kernel, and no
  claim may take it while the table stands. A fixed-function register is admitted only as an MWAIT hint on x86
  (in `_CST` and `_LPI`); every model-specific-register form is refused (EXCLUDES), and so is the platform
  communications channel. So THE KERNEL WRITES EVERY PROCESSOR REGISTER, the fixture's included, and no process
  changes a processor's state on its behalf.
- [ ] WHAT LEAVES THE KERNEL is part a's free read syscall, which parts b and c extend with each idle state's
  entries and residency, the performance level now and the time at each, the window, the injected idle and the
  live latency requests. Utilisation itself stays inside the kernel's governor. The system graph and
  ProcessorPowerService both read the record; nothing is pushed.
- [ ] THE LATENCY BOUND IS A KERNEL OBJECT: a `LatencyRequest`, created against a new `IdleLatency` privilege with
  a bound in microseconds. The idle governor enters no state whose exit latency exceeds the smallest live
  request, and a request ends when its last handle closes, so a holder that dies releases it. Bounded: four live
  requests per process and 64 in the system, and the smallest bound means the shallowest state (`hlt`, `wfi`),
  never a core that spins. A SERVICE receives the privilege through its manifest row, the path CONSOLE, DEVPRIV
  and DISPLAYCTL take today - AudioService first, for playback; a launched component only through
  PermissionManager, whose typed grant vocabulary gains the entry when the first component needs it (none in this
  milestone).
- [ ] THE THERMAL POLICY'S INPUT IS THE ZONE DRIVER, NOT POWERSERVICE. PowerService's zone state stays what
  P02M0181 made it: observations for readers, coalesced to 100 ms per source and closed on overflow. The policy
  needs more - `_PSL`, `_TC1`, `_TC2`, `_TSP`, `_ACx` with `_ALx`, `_SCP` - and must have `_TMP` sampled every
  `_TSP` while passive cooling is engaged. So the thermal-zone driver, the one binding on the zone's node
  (P02M0099's ACPI item owns its state half), also publishes a `thermal-zone` provider kind - the COOLING HALF,
  built by this milestone into the same program - carrying those objects, the temperature and a sampling-period
  control. A `PNP0C0B` fan driver, this milestone's, publishes a `cooling-device` kind; both kinds are
  append-only values in the shared provider-kind vocabulary, as `power-source` was. ProcessorPowerService
  consumes both through a kind-scoped catalogue connection, as PowerService consumes `power-source`; `_PSL` and
  `_ALx` entries arrive as namespace paths, matched against the processor contract's paths and the fan
  publications' node paths. It learns the power source, for the default profile, as an ordinary PowerService
  state subscriber.
- [ ] FANS ARE ACTUATED THROUGH THE ACPI SERVICE: the fan driver evaluates `_FSL` and `_FST`, or switches an ACPI
  1.0 fan by its device power state, over its node-scoped channel (P02M0196b), on ProcessorPowerService's command
  through the `cooling-device` contract.

## P02M0198b - Idle states

- [ ] Each core's idle states with their exit latency and target residency: ACPI's `_LPI` or `_CST`, installed by
  ProcessorPowerService; the device tree's `idle-states`, read by the kernel; and with no table, what the CPU
  offers without one - on x86 `hlt`, or MWAIT's C1 hint where CPUID offers MONITOR/MWAIT, and `wfi` elsewhere.
  Deeper MWAIT hints come only from `_CST` or `_LPI`.
- [ ] THEIR ENTRY IS THE KERNEL IDLE LOOP'S LAST ACT, in ring 0: MWAIT with the state's hint; a `_CST` or `_LPI`
  register access at the port or address checked at install, in whichever of SystemIO and SystemMemory the
  register names; PSCI CPU_SUSPEND with the state's parameter; the SBI HSM suspend with
  `riscv,sbi-suspend-param`. Retention states first; states that lose the core's context after, on P02M0197's
  per-core resume path. A state that stops the core's timer is entered only where the timer keeps running anyway -
  ARAT on x86, no `local-timer-stop` in the tree - because this milestone builds no broadcast timer; on x86 a
  state deeper than C1 also needs an invariant TSC, because the clock is the counter. A `_CST` type-3 state that
  needs bus-master arbitration control is refused.
- [ ] A KERNEL GOVERNOR choosing the state from the predicted idle time - the core's own next one-shot and its
  recent history - against target residency and the latency bound (the smallest live `LatencyRequest`).

## P02M0198c - Performance states

- [ ] ACPI's `_PSS`, `_PCT`, `_PPC` and `_PSD` (the states and their coordination across cores: software-all,
  software-any, hardware-all), CPPC's `_CPC`, and the throttling states (`_PTC`, `_TSS`, `_TPC`, `_TSD`), in their
  SystemIO and SystemMemory register forms, installed by ProcessorPowerService after the checks above. A `_PPC`
  notification is re-evaluated in userspace and becomes the window's cap, acknowledged through `_OST` where the
  processor has one.
- [ ] A KERNEL GOVERNOR that follows utilisation, measured at the scheduler's own points - a busy core's tick,
  entry to and exit from idle - and never by a wakeup of its own, choosing a level inside the core's window and
  changing it no faster than the table's transition latency allows. The profiles a person chooses between -
  performance, balanced, power saving - are ProcessorPowerService's: each sets the windows' floors and caps, and
  CPPC's energy-performance preference where `_CPC` has one, with the power source from P02M0181 choosing the
  default; the defaults per source are confirmed by the owner when the part starts. `_PPC` and the thermal limits
  are obeyed above any profile.

## P02M0198d - Thermal policy and fans (ProcessorPowerService)

- [ ] PASSIVE COOLING: past a zone's `_PSV`, the processors it lists (`_PSL`) held down by the `_TC1`, `_TC2` and
  `_TSP` equation, with the zone driver asked to sample `_TMP` every `_TSP` while it is engaged - the window's cap
  lowered first, then throttling states, and where there are none, idle the scheduler injects.
- [ ] ACTIVE COOLING: the `_ACx` trip points and their device lists (`_ALx`) switching the fans through the
  `cooling-device` contract - ACPI 4.0's `_FIF`, `_FPS`, `_FSL` and `_FST` for a fan with levels, the device power
  state for one without; `_SCP` set from the profile; and where the platform leaves its fans to the operating
  system (`_FIF`'s fine-grain control), a fan curve the owner can change - the "fan curves" P02M0181 left outside.
  The default curve is confirmed by the owner when the part starts.
- [ ] CRITICAL: past `_CRT`, an orderly power-off within a BOUND, and an immediate one when the bound runs out;
  past `_HOT`, hibernation through P02M0197's `system-sleep` where it is set up, and the `_CRT` sequence otherwise
  or when hibernation is refused. NEITHER IS A POLICY ANYONE MAY TURN OFF: no profile, curve or operator verb
  reaches it.
  - THE OWNER is ProcessorPowerService. At `_CRT` it arms the bound first and then asks for the orderly
    power-off. Its grants: a `system-power` client (a manifest client role, as DeviceManager's SYSPOWER is), a
    `system-shutdown` client, and P02M0197's `system-sleep` client for `_HOT`.
  - THE BOUND IS ENFORCED BY THE KERNEL, which outlives every process the orderly power-off stops, and a hung
    ServiceManager or SystemManager alike. `system-power` gains one op, `power-off-within(seconds)`, which
    SystemManager - the authority's one holder - carries out as a new `SYS_SYSTEM_POWER` action arming a FORCED
    POWER-OFF DEADLINE in ticks. The kernel keeps the earliest one armed, never moves it later and never cancels
    it, checks it on the BSP's deadline path, includes it in the BSP's one-shot (part a) and powers off when it
    passes; while one is armed, every path by which the kernel would reset the machine (a lost SystemManager, the
    recovery ladder) powers it off instead. The op only stops the machine, which is all a `system-power` client
    may do.
  - THE ORDERLY POWER-OFF is ServiceManager's existing `!poweroff` sequence - every service stopped in reverse
    dependency order, LogService's last batch flushed, then `system-power` - reached through a new narrow
    `system-shutdown` interface with one op, `power-off`, served by ServiceManager, rather than through the
    shell's admin channel, which can stop any service. A request while the sequence runs is answered as already
    under way.
  - THE FALLBACK, for a ProcessorPowerService that is dead, restarting or stopped: the thermal-zone driver
    compares every `_TMP` it reads with `_CRT` itself - the comparison the power-model leaf already derives - and
    on the crossing calls `power-off-within` with the same bound, on a `system-power` connection DeviceManager
    delivers at bind as it delivers the Power key's to the keyboard drivers. Arming twice is harmless, since the
    earliest wins, and a policy that comes back inside the bound finds the zone past `_CRT` in its first snapshot
    and still asks for the orderly half. With the zone driver gone too, nothing in the system reads the zone, and
    what remains is the platform's own hardware trip - named here, not claimed: QEMU has none.
  - The bound is ten seconds, confirmed by the owner when the part starts.
- [ ] `powerctl`, a shipping operator tool (P02M0181 has none): the power sources P02M0181 reports, the profile,
  each core's states, residency and live latency requests, each zone with its trip points, and each fan; changing
  the profile or a fan curve through ProcessorPowerService's operator root, granted as PowerService's CONTROL is.

## P02M0198e - Verification

- [ ] Idle without a tick on all three targets: an idle guest's wakeups per second measured before and after, in
  the system graph and in `docs/PERF.md`. Kernel tests for each replaced duty: a timeout armed on a secondary core
  expiring on time while the BSP sleeps; a housekeeping wait still firing; the forced power-off deadline firing
  with every core idle; a clock reading that never goes backwards across cores; bytes typed at an idle guest
  reaching the shell on all three; a transmit burst from an idle core reaching the wire whole; a hot-plug arrival
  on an idle machine. The sleep-offset rebase in a host suite, for a counter that kept running and one that
  restarted. And the whole kernel suite green on all three, because this changes the clock under everything - the
  one part of this milestone whose test is not scoped.
- [ ] Through P02M0196d's fixture: an SSDT processor description whose `_PCT` control register and `_CPC`
  desired-performance register lie on a page of their own in the region the harness reads - written by the kernel
  after its install checks - so the harness SEES which state the governor asked for, under load and at rest, and
  a `_PPC` notification capping it; a table naming a model-specific register refused whole; `_LPI` states with
  stated latencies, and the governor's choices - read from the kernel's per-state entry counts, since a register
  read leaves nothing for the harness to see - checked against them under a scripted idle pattern, with and
  without a `LatencyRequest`.
- [ ] A thermal zone the harness heats: past `_PSV` the requested performance falls, past `_ACx` the fan level the
  fixture reads rises, and past `_CRT` the orderly power-off appears on the serial log and QMP's `SHUTDOWN` event
  arrives within the bound. Then again with ProcessorPowerService stopped before the heating: `SHUTDOWN` at the
  bound, within a stated margin, and no orderly power-off.
- [ ] PSCI CPU_SUSPEND and the SBI HSM suspend entered and left on aarch64 and riscv64, where QEMU and this
  OpenSBI run them - checked first, not assumed - from `idle-states` the harness adds with its device-tree editor,
  since QEMU's own tree has none. MWAIT on x86 only where the host lets the guest have it (`-overcommit
  cpu-pm=on`), checked first; `hlt` otherwise, and said so.
- [ ] Host suites for the governors, for the install checks against hostile tables (a register in RAM, in the
  reserved set or inside a claim, a model-specific register, too many states), for the latency-request bounds,
  and for the passive-cooling equation against the specification's worked examples.

EXCLUDES, each a follow-up put to the owner when there is hardware to prove it on: the x86 processor's own
digital thermal sensors, and every model-specific-register form of processor control - IA32_PERF_CTL for a
fixed-function `_PCT`, IA32_CLOCK_MODULATION for a fixed-function `_PTC`, Intel's HWP and AMD's CPPC registers
for a fixed-function `_CPC` - none of which QEMU's CPUID offers, so a table naming one is refused; the device
tree's operating-point tables and `thermal-zones`, which need the per-SoC clock, regulator and sensor drivers
also excluded here; ACPI processor tables on aarch64, which this system boots from the device tree. Also
excluded: a broadcast timer for idle states that stop a core's timer; per-model idle tables; the platform
communications channel; vendor performance profiles reached through WMI; turbo and boost controls beyond what
`_PSS` and CPPC express.

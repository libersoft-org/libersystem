# P02M0204 - Restart policy for services and drivers, and `restartctl` to set and show it

Status: PLANNED (2026-10-14; revised 2026-10-15 after the plan audit) - the owner's decision and his agreement with
this plan are recorded below; nothing is implemented yet, by his instruction ("zatim neimplementuj").

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-10-14). The proposal was one rule for a failed start and a crash, permanent failures not
retried, and a fixed "fast, then slow for ever" schedule. The owner agreed with all of it but the fixed schedule:
"it seems nonsense to try fast and then slow and for ever... make a tool with which this can be set: how many times
to try / for ever, how fast and so on. The tool sets it and also shows the current settings - human-readable, and
with `--json`."

WHAT IS WRONG TODAY (read from the tree on 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15):
  - A SERVICE WHOSE START FAILS IS NEVER TRIED AGAIN, whatever its restart class.
    - ServiceManager prints `FAILED to start` and its reason. The services waiting on it print `never started -
      waiting for`. Nothing asks again (P02M0099 names the gap).
    - Only the bring-up loop ever starts a service. The standing loop never starts one that is `Absent`.
  - A CRASHED `transparent` SERVICE IS RESTARTED AT MOST `service.restart-budget` TIMES (default 3) PER BOOT.
    - The count never goes back down: a service that crashes once a week is dead from the third week until a reboot.
    - An `escalate` service is not restarted at all. That is a limit, not a policy: its clients hold the channels they
      were handed, and a replacement would leave those dead. 24 of the 42 services are `escalate`, StorageService,
      PermissionManager, ProcessService and the shell among them.
  - THE SUPERVISOR BLOCKS.
    - The back-off is a `sleep_ticks` (100, 200, 300 ms).
    - A start or a relaunch waits for the new instance's report with `recv_blocking` and no deadline.
    - While it waits, ServiceManager answers no other crash, no `stop`, no shutdown and no `alive`.
    - WatchdogService pets the hardware only for an `alive` answered by the standing loop. A replacement that hangs
      before it reports therefore ends in a hardware reset.
  - THE POLICY IS ONE NUMBER, READ ONCE PER BOOT, from a key any configuration client can write: seven services hold
    one, and so does the shipping `config` tool (`config set <key> <value>`).
  - DRIVERS (DeviceManager) GET THREE AUTOMATIC ATTEMPTS PER DEVICE FOR THE WHOLE BOOT.
    - The three are counted across all of a device's incidents and candidates. A binding that comes up ends its
      incident's time window but gives nothing back. Only a rebind after a sleep is free.
    - Between attempts the back-off is 100 ms, then 200 ms.
    - A device that has failed three times in a boot stays `failed` until an operator `retry`.
    - None of it can be configured.

WHAT THE TREE ALREADY HAS, AND THIS BUILDS ON:
  - Why a service failed: `BootstrapRefused`, `NoReport` and `Faulted`. A failed start's own words travel through
    `rt::fail_bootstrap` (68 call sites).
  - Each driver failure cause already carries whether it may be retried (`FailureCause`). A quarantined device is out
    of reach for the boot.
  - The orderly reboot: the admin channel's `!reboot`. It tears the services down in reverse dependency order and
    prepares the hardware watchdogs first.
  - The reserved configuration namespace with an owner: `device.policy.`, written only through the `POLICYOWNER`
    pair.
  - The boot window ServiceManager is handed, with its deadline.
  - Two status surfaces that already exist:
    - the `supervisor` interface's `supervisor-stat`, per service; its driver rows are only a snapshot taken after
      bring-up;
    - DeviceManager's `binding-record`, per device and live, which `lsdev` and the system graph read.
  - The sleep transaction's deferred restarts.

THE RULES - fixed, not settable:
  1. TWO KINDS OF FAILURE. A START that never reached `Ready`, and a DEATH after `Ready`.
  2. A FAILED START IS RETRIED UNDER THE POLICY FOR EVERY SERVICE WHOSE START CAN BE RUN AGAIN IN PLACE, the
     `escalate` ones included. A start that never completed handed nothing to anybody, so the reason `escalate`
     services are not restarted does not apply to it.
     - A start cannot be run again when it consumed an input the supervisor cannot produce twice. As the tree stands,
       those services are:
       - the StorageService instances, whose disks are taken, not copied;
       - PermissionManager, which is given the font catalogue's admin root itself;
       - DisplayService and the shell, whose roles are exclusive.
     - For those, the policy's `then` applies at once. The set of services whose start can be run again is declared
       in one place, beside `plan_relaunchable`, and `check-bootstrap-plan` refuses a declared service that has an
       exclusive role or one of the one-shot takes above.
  3. A DEATH AFTER `Ready`. A `transparent` service is restarted under the policy. For an `escalate` service the
     policy's `then` applies at once.
  4. PERMANENT AND TRANSIENT.
     - A failed start is permanent when running it again in place cannot change the answer:
       - a role or capability the supervisor did not deliver;
       - a missing or malformed init package;
       - the hardware the service exists for not being there, for example no framebuffer;
       - the kernel refusing to attach the instance.
     - Everything else is transient: an allocation or a domain refused, a peer that closed (the volume going away
       under a start was the measured case), a crash, no report before the deadline.
     - The carrier: `rt::fail_bootstrap` takes the class, and the failure report carries it as a marker. Every
       existing call site is classified in the same change, by the categories above.
     - A permanent failure is never retried automatically. The `failed` state shows its reason, and it is tried again
       by an operator `start` or by the next boot.
     - Drivers keep their causes' existing retryability. A quarantined device stays outside automatic retry for the
       boot, and a rebind after a sleep stays free.
     - The device that holds `vol://system` takes no policy (P02M0203).
  5. NOTHING WAITS INSIDE THE SUPERVISOR.
     - A back-off is a deadline in the standing loop.
     - A start launches the instance and delivers its roles, then awaits its report with a deadline. In the standing
       loop the wait is an event in the wait set, so `alive` keeps being answered. During bring-up, before the
       watchdog's first answer, it is a bounded wait.
     - The report deadline derives from the boot window, the way DeviceManager's bind deadline does, so the emulated
       ports get proportionally longer.
     - No report by the deadline is `NoReport`, which is transient. The instance is killed and the failure counted.
  6. THE SERVICES THAT WAIT ARE STARTED WHEN THEY CAN BE.
     - When a service reaches `Ready` after bring-up, the standing loop starts every `Absent` service whose
       dependencies are now `Ready`, through the same start path bring-up uses. That path's context moves out of the
       bring-up loop with it.
     - `ServiceManager: online` is sent when the set settles, during bring-up or later.
  7. THE BOOT WINDOW STAYS THE OUTER BOUND. Retries during bring-up are scheduled like any other. If the shell has not
     attached when the window closes, the kernel's recovery ladder acts as it does today. The policy never extends
     the window.
  8. A RETRY DUE DURING A SLEEP TRANSACTION waits for its end, as the restarts it defers do today.
  9. THE LOG says the first failure, and then only when the count doubles, as the heartbeat already does.

THE SETTINGS - one set of parameters, with a default for services, a default for drivers, and an override for any one
service or driver by name:
  - `attempts`     how many automatic retries in a row: a number, or `unlimited`.
  - `delay`        the wait before the first retry.
  - `backoff`      what the wait is multiplied by after each failed attempt (`1` keeps it constant).
  - `max-delay`    the longest the wait may grow to.
  - `reset-after`  how long a service or device must stay up before its count starts again from zero, so
                   `attempts 3` means three in a row, not three per boot.
  - `then`         what happens when the attempts are spent, or at once where rules 2 and 3 say so:
                   - `stop` leaves it failed until an operator starts it or the next boot;
                   - `reboot` is for a service the system cannot run without. Drivers take `stop` only, because a
                     device that fails is not a reason to reboot the machine.
  `then reboot`:
  - It is the orderly reboot, the same sequence as `!reboot`.
  - It is bounded so that it cannot become a loop. As soon as ConfigService is up, ServiceManager records the boot in
    its own namespace as not yet healthy: the count of consecutive such boots, this one included.
    - The count is written at the start of the boot, while the storage works. A death that takes the storage with it,
      StorageService's, therefore needs no write before its reboot.
    - A boot that stays up for `reset-after` with every `then reboot` service `Ready` clears the count.
  - A policy reboot is taken only when:
    - this boot's record was written, so a boot whose storage or configuration never came up never reboots on the
      policy; and
    - the count is at most three.
  - Otherwise `then reboot` acts as `stop`, and the console and the journal say why.
  THE DEFAULTS, AGREED BY THE OWNER WITH THE PLAN (2026-10-14, "OK, souhlasim"):
  - Services: attempts 5, delay 1 s, backoff 2, max-delay 1 min, reset-after 10 min, then stop.
  - Drivers: attempts 3, delay 100 ms, backoff 2, max-delay 1 s, reset-after 10 min, then stop. The attempts and the
    back-off are today's. `reset-after` is the change: today the three are spent once per boot.
  - Which services take `then reboot` by default is named when the item starts. The candidates are StorageService,
    PermissionManager and ConfigService, and rules 2 and 3 now give the two `escalate` ones a `then` to apply. All of
    them can be changed with `restartctl`.

WHERE THE SETTINGS LIVE:
  - In ConfigService, under a second reserved namespace, `service.restart.`. ServiceManager owns it through a second
    seeded owner pair, `RESTARTOWNER`, the `POLICYOWNER` pattern. No other configuration client can write it, and any
    can read it.
  - ServiceManager takes the owner end again at every ConfigService start.
  - `remove` is allowed under each reserved namespace to its owner.
  - `restartctl` changes settings only through ServiceManager. ServiceManager checks the values, writes them through,
    and applies them at once: being the writer, it needs no change notification.
  - The drivers' settings live in the same namespace. ServiceManager hands DeviceManager its effective policy on its
    control channel when ConfigService comes up and after every change. Until then DeviceManager runs the compiled
    defaults: through phase one and the start of phase two.
  - `service.restart-budget` and its seeded default are removed. `service.watchdog-ticks`, which the canary uses, stays.

THE TOOL, `restartctl`:
  - `restartctl` and `restartctl show [NAME]` print the defaults and every override, and for each service and driver:
    its effective policy, its state, the attempts spent, the reason of the last failure, and when the next attempt is
    due.
    - It reads the two surfaces that exist: `supervisor-stat` for services and `binding-record` for drivers, both
      extended with those fields and with a state word for "waiting to retry".
    - Human-readable by default. `--json` prints the same as one JSON document, through the shell's `json` sub-form
      as the other tools.
  - `restartctl set [--services | --drivers | --service NAME | --driver NAME] PARAMETER VALUE ...` sets the default
    for services or drivers, or an override for one. Values are checked before anything is stored, and a refusal says
    which value and why. Some settings cannot apply and are refused with the reason:
    - any policy for a service whose start cannot be run again and that is `escalate`, beyond `then`;
    - `then reboot` for a driver;
    - any policy for the device that holds `vol://system`.
  - `restartctl reset [NAME]` removes an override, or the changed defaults, and returns to what is shipped.
  - The existing `start` command also accepts a `failed` service whose start can be run again, and starts its count
    afresh.
  - Reading needs only what `lssvc` and `lsdev` already hold. Changing needs the operator root `restart-policy`,
    served by ServiceManager and minted by PermissionManager like the other operator roots.

- [ ] 1. POLICY CORE, a host-tested library beside `driver_binding`:
  - the schedule (delay, backoff, cap), `unlimited` and `reset-after`;
  - `then`, with the reboot guard's arithmetic;
  - the permanent and transient classification of the services' start failures, and the drivers' causes;
  - the choice of which `Absent` services a newly `Ready` one releases.
- [ ] 2. THE CARRIER.
  - `rt::fail_bootstrap` takes the class and the report carries it.
  - ServiceManager records it beside the reason.
  - Every existing call site is classified.
- [ ] 3. SERVICEMANAGER.
  - The start splits into launch-and-deliver and awaiting the report with a deadline: an event in the standing loop,
    a bounded wait in bring-up.
  - Failed starts retried for the declared set, and `then` at once for the rest.
  - `Absent` dependents started from the standing loop, and `online` sent when the set settles.
  - Deaths after `Ready` under the policy for `transparent` services, and `then` for `escalate` ones.
  - `then reboot` through the orderly sequence, with the guard.
  - The sleep deferral.
  - The policy from its namespace, re-applied as `restartctl` changes it.
  - `start` accepting a `failed` service.
- [ ] 4. CONFIGSERVICE.
  - The second reserved namespace and its owner pair, with ServiceManager taking the owner end again at every
    ConfigService start.
  - `remove` allowed under each reserved namespace to its owner.
  - The seeded `service.restart-budget` removed.
  - Its host tests, and the kernel test of the reserved namespace extended to the second one.
- [ ] 5. DEVICEMANAGER.
  - The policy replaces `MAX_AUTOMATIC_ATTEMPTS` and `BACKOFF_TICKS`, and the spent count resets after
    `reset-after`.
  - The per-attempt bind deadline, which doubles for patience, stays as it is.
  - An attempt is counted as today, by `admit_attempt`: each admitted bind, across a device's candidates. A
    candidate whose artifact is missing, or whose dependency is parked, spends nothing.
  - `then stop` only, quarantine and the sleep rebind as rule 4 says, and the volume's device untouched.
  - The policy taken from ServiceManager, with the compiled defaults until then.
- [ ] 6. THE WIRE.
  - The `restart-policy` interface (`set`, `reset`) on an operator root minted by PermissionManager.
  - `supervisor-stat` and `binding-record` extended: the policy in force, the attempts spent, the next attempt due,
    and the "waiting to retry" state word.
  - The generated docs.
- [ ] 7. `restartctl`, with `--json`.
- [ ] 8. THE ORACLE, in QEMU: x86_64 first, the ports at the end of the job. It uses development fixtures:
  - a service that fails its first N starts and then comes up, `escalate` and `transparent` alike: it comes up
    after the configured delays, and the services that depend on it start then;
  - a service whose start fails permanently: never retried, and started by an operator `start`;
  - a service that never reports, with a hardware watchdog armed: it is counted `NoReport` at the deadline and the
    machine is not reset;
  - a service that crashes in a loop:
    - the schedule is measured on the guest's clock;
    - `attempts` is respected;
    - `unlimited` never gives up;
    - the log is rate-limited;
    - a crash after a long healthy run starts again from attempt one;
  - a `then reboot` service that dies after `Ready`:
    - the orderly reboot happens;
    - after the bound, the next death stops it instead of rebooting, and says so;
    - a healthy boot clears the count;
  - a driver fixture failing its bind the same ways, and the device that holds `vol://system` refusing a policy;
  - `restartctl show --json` parsed by the gate;
  - `restartctl set` refused without the grant;
  - an ordinary configuration client refused a write under `service.restart.`;
  - `restartctl reset` returning to the defaults;
  - the settings surviving a reboot, and applying without one.

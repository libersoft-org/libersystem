# P02M0203 - Boot-capable storage drivers: the system volume's criticality belongs to its binding

Status: PLANNED (2026-10-14) - the owner's decision is recorded below; nothing is implemented yet, by his instruction
("zatim neimplementuj").

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-10-14), on the question P02M0099 left when NVMe and AHCI became able to host the
system volume: "souhlasim s doporucenim" - accept the bootstrap-set cost, and move the criticality from the driver
class to the one binding that holds the system volume.

WHY. `lifecycle = "boot-critical"` in `src/user/services/manifest.toml` says two different things at once:
  - WHERE THE DRIVER LIVES - in `init.pkg`, so it can bind before any volume is mounted. That is a fact about the
    DRIVER: a driver that might have to make the system volume reachable cannot be loaded from it.
  - WHAT AN OPERATOR MAY DO - a boot-critical binding takes no stored policy and no operator retry or disable
    (`driver_binding::decide_retry`, `device_manager::decide_policy`), because that policy lives on the volume the
    binding may be needed to mount. That is a fact about ONE BINDING - the one whose disk is `vol://system` - and it
    holds only until the volume is mounted.
Since NVMe and AHCI joined `virtio_blk` in the bootstrap set (2026-10-04), every controller of those three kinds
pays the second price, a data disk included: `lsdev --disable` on a second NVMe answers `refused`, and a stored
policy for it is never read. DeviceManager does not know which binding holds the system volume; it decides by the
driver's class.

WHAT STAYS. NVMe, AHCI and virtio-blk remain in the bootstrap set on every architecture (about 400 KB). One image
must boot from any standard controller; the alternatives - an image per machine, or a loader choosing drivers - are
more complex and more fragile, and a general-purpose initramfs carries its storage drivers the same way.

- [ ] Split the manifest's meaning: the bootstrap staging (`stage = "pinned"`, a driver that can bind before the
  volume exists - "boot-capable") is the driver's; "holds the system volume" is a property of a binding, decided at
  run time. `system-manifest` keeps refusing a boot-capable driver staged on the volume.
- [ ] DeviceManager marks the ONE binding whose block provider StorageService chose as `vol://system` (by the
  medium's pairing uuid) as the volume's. Only that binding refuses `disable`, `retry` and stored policy, and the
  refusal names the reason (`holds vol://system`), not a class. Every other binding of a boot-capable driver is
  ordinary once the volume is mounted.
- [ ] Stored policy for the other bindings is read after the volume is mounted and applied then: a data disk the
  operator disabled is bound during the boot until the volume is mounted, and stopped right after. (Keeping the
  policy outside the volume - on the ESP or in firmware variables - would remove that window and add a second source
  of truth; not done.)
- [ ] Host tests over the decision (the volume's binding refused with its reason, a sibling binding of the same
  driver accepted, a binding made before the mount picking up its stored disable after it).
- [ ] THE ORACLE, in QEMU: `boot-volume-controllers` grows a case with two NVMe disks - the system one and a data
  one. `lsdev --disable` on the data disk is accepted and survives a reboot (the disk is stopped after the mount);
  on the system disk it is refused with the reason. The same with AHCI, and with a second virtio-blk disk. x86_64
  first; the ports in the end-of-job runs.

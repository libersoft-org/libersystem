# P02M0194 - Bluetooth, complete: classic and LE, every desktop profile, audio and voice

Status: OPEN. It closes when every part below is done. NOTHING HERE IS STARTED: the owner asked for the plan
only (2026-09-25).

THE OWNER'S DECISION (2026-09-25), IN HIS WORDS: a new milestone that finishes the service COMPLETELY, for the
whole of Bluetooth. It follows his "yes" to Bluetooth voice: the SCO transport is planned in P02M0099, and he
was told that voice is useless without a host stack that can set a headset's call up - which P02M0180, scoped
to one LE mouse, does not have. So this milestone takes the service from "one encrypted LE boot mouse" to what
a desktop operating system's Bluetooth does.

WHAT EXISTS, and this milestone builds on it rather than beside it:
  - THE TRANSPORT (P02M0099): HCI over USB - commands, events and ACL with their bounds, stall and unplug
    recovery, epochs across a reset - and, planned there, SCO over the isochronous alternates. ISO is a
    reserved kind nothing carries, and no P02M0099 item owns carrying it: part h does, both halves.
  - THE SERVICE (P02M0180): a sandboxed process with no device authority; LE scanning, one LE central
    connection, LE Secure Connections pairing with Just Works, a durable bond store, ATT/GATT discovery for
    the HID-over-GATT boot mouse, the read / operator / profile scopes, bounded tables, and `btctl`.
  - THE FIXTURES: an in-guest HCI controller-and-peer provider on the production wire, a FunctionFS
    controller in the harness, and `usbredir_device.py`, which has the isochronous endpoints a gadget lacks.
  - THE CONSUMERS, each built for less than this milestone hands them: InputService opens one mouse stream from
    the first enabled peer; AudioService drives one device at a fixed 48 kHz stereo with one recorder and no
    inventory, volume, latency or voice; NetworkService runs one uplink, an Ethernet publication or a raw-IP
    link ModemService installs; and no application-facing serial port exists.

WHAT "COMPLETE" MEANS HERE, stated so it can be checked: every profile a desktop user meets - input devices,
music, calls, serial links, file push, tethering and LE Audio - on both radios, with the pairing models the
specification defines (Out Of Band excepted: this machine has no out-of-band channel to carry its data) and the
security it requires. EXCLUDED as not a desktop's: Bluetooth Mesh, the peripheral (server) role beyond what the
profiles need, proprietary codecs (aptX, LDAC) and AAC, whose use is licensed.

HOW WHAT THE STACK PRODUCES REACHES ITS OWNERS (decided 2026-09-26). ONLY A DRIVER BINDING PUBLISHES INTO THE
CATALOGUE, and BluetoothService is no driver: it publishes nothing, and no path for publishers without a device
is built - P02M0180's rule against a pretend provider stands. Every delivery is a NAMED CONTRACT on a root of
BluetoothService's own, which its one consumer reaches BY NAME THROUGH THE BROKER, exactly as InputService
reaches `bluetooth-profile` today, and not through a role: a role is a dependency, a stop takes the reverse
closure, and stopping the radio's stack must stop no audio, network or input service - nor may any of them wait
for a radio to start. Each is a new root in the manifest, a broker name in `capability_names.rs` and one row in
ServiceManager's grant table, additive in `liber:bluetooth@1` at version 1:

    contract            consumer            carries
    bluetooth-profile   InputService        decoded pointer, key, consumer-control and gamepad reports from
                                            every peer trusted for input (part c; grown from `open-mouse`)
    bluetooth-audio     AudioService        audio endpoints as they come and go, each opened as a PCM channel
                                            with its format and latency; volume both ways; the voice link and
                                            the call relay (part d)
    bluetooth-network   NetworkService      PAN links as they come and go, each opened as a frame channel
                                            speaking a NIC provider's frame-plus-link wire (part g)
    bluetooth-admin     PermissionManager   the minting root for per-launch application grants: an SPP byte
                                            stream (`bluetooth-serial`, part g) and a GATT client
                                            (`bluetooth-gatt`, part b), each for one component and one peer

LOSS AND RESTART, ONE RULE FOR ALL FOUR. A consumer's open channels end with the BluetoothService instance that
served them and the consumer re-resolves, as InputService's slot already does; the new instance offers again
what its devices bring back and revives nothing. A restarted consumer re-resolves and reopens what is still
offered. When a channel ends, InputService releases every held key and button, AudioService moves the device's
streams by its routing rule, NetworkService withdraws the link as it withdraws a NIC (a new interface
generation), and a granted application sees its stream end.

ORDER: part i's oracle is built first; then a, b and c (c's gamepad half after P02M0192's vocabulary); d before
e, f and h; P02M0099's SCO transport item before part f's gate; part h's own ISO transport item before part h's
gate.

## P02M0194a - Classic (BR/EDR) foundations, bounds and pairing

- [ ] THE BOUNDS, STATED BEFORE ANY CODE, replacing P02M0180's one-link table. The Domain is derived from them;
  part i's re-measurement checks the derivation and is not its source.

    links        two controllers, as before; at most 8 ACL links per controller across both radios, at most 7
                 of them BR/EDR (a piconet's active members); the controller's own smaller count wins
    per link     16 L2CAP channels, at most 2 of them in enhanced retransmission mode; 8 RFCOMM channels in its
                 one RFCOMM session; 32 queued ACL packets
    SDU / MTU    1691 bytes on every BR/EDR dynamic channel - BNEP's minimum MTU, and above what AVDTP, AVCTP,
                 HID, RFCOMM and OBEX need; OBEX's maximum packet is offered as the same 1691. ATT MTU 247 on
                 LE - above BAP's minimum of 64, and one Data Length Extension PDU with its L2CAP header. One
                 incomplete SDU per channel and the one-second assembly deadline, as before
    ERTM         for AVRCP browsing and OBEX over L2CAP: a transmit window of 8 and the specification's default
                 2-second retransmission and 12-second monitor time-outs; streaming mode is not used
    synchronous  one SCO/eSCO link per controller; 4 connected isochronous streams and one broadcast group of
                 at most 2 streams; ISO SDUs within the controller's buffer and the 4100-byte transport ceiling
    storage      1 MB aggregate packet storage, against worst cases of 8 x 16 x 1691 = 216 kB of reassembly,
                 8 x 2 x 8 x 1691 = 216 kB of ERTM windows and 8 x 32 x 1028 = 263 kB of queued ACL; 16 client
                 connections and 32 minted grants; bonds 64 records in 64 kB, unchanged - a record grows by keys
                 and trust bits to about 200 bytes
    Domain       memory 64 MB, 256 handles, 4 threads and 2 MB stacks stay, every table above being well inside
                 them; IPC queues 2 MB -> 4 MB for the new standing queues - a PAN link's 64 frames each way
                 and four audio endpoints' 16 periods each way, about 0.7 MB

- [ ] Inquiry with extended inquiry results (names, classes of device, service UUIDs), paging, and links up to
  those bounds; encryption required on every link that carries user data; role switch; sniff mode with its
  parameters bounded, so an idle keyboard does not hold the radio awake.
- [ ] THE PAIRING INTERACTION, ONE RULE FOR BOTH RADIOS. Every model that needs a person - Numeric Comparison,
  Passkey Entry in either direction, legacy PIN entry, and consent to an incoming pairing - is a PROMPT THE
  SERVICE ORIGINATES on the operator authority: a typed record (controller, peer, model, the six-digit value or
  the passkey to show, keypress notifications as they arrive, a deadline) answered with yes or no, a passkey or
  a PIN. `btctl` holds it, as it holds every operator verb. THE DEADLINE IS THE PROTOCOL'S: 25 seconds, five
  inside the 30-second Security Manager and LMP response timers, after which the service answers no itself.
  This host declares KeyboardDisplay only while a prompt watcher is attached (a running `btctl pair` or `btctl
  pairable`, one per controller) and NoInputNoOutput otherwise, so no model is offered that nobody can answer.
  NOT P02M0188'S PROTECTED SCREEN: it confirms an operation a requester asked for and an executor froze, not a
  value a protocol produces midway that nobody requested; it waits 60 seconds against a 30-second timer; it has
  no entry; and it exists only with a USB or virtio keyboard, so a machine whose only keyboard is Bluetooth
  could never pair one. What an answer proves is stated rather than implied: the holder of the operator
  authority confirmed, on an ordinary terminal another local program could overdraw - a limit
  `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` states, beside the fact that Numeric Comparison and Passkey Entry still defeat an
  attacker on the radio alone. A first Bluetooth keyboard is paired from whatever terminal the machine has (a
  USB or virtio keyboard, the serial shell); once bonded it reconnects with nobody present.
- [ ] Secure Simple Pairing through those prompts; link keys in the bond store beside the LE keys, with the key
  type, whether it is authenticated and the model that produced it, and the other radio's key derived across
  transports where both sides support it; the record grows additively and its version stays 1. Legacy PIN
  pairing only when the operator asks for it for a device that cannot do better, at a security level of its own
  below both Secure Connections levels; a peer bonded with Secure Connections that later offers only legacy is
  refused, never downgraded. Out Of Band is excluded: no out-of-band channel exists here, and this host always
  reports none.
- [ ] L2CAP for BR/EDR - basic mode, and enhanced retransmission where a profile needs it - and RFCOMM over it,
  with its credit-based flow control.
- [ ] SDP ON BOTH SIDES: the client every classic profile is found through, and a SERVER holding a record for
  each role this system offers while that role is enabled - HFP and HSP Audio Gateway, A2DP source and sink,
  AVRCP controller and target, the OPP server. SPP, the PAN user and the HID host are client roles and publish
  none.
- [ ] INBOUND POLICY, stated per radio. BR/EDR is CONNECTABLE (page scan) while the radio is on and a bonded
  peer is trusted for an inbound profile, or while pairable; DISCOVERABLE (inquiry scan) only under `btctl
  discoverable`, for at most 180 seconds; PAIRABLE only while a prompt watcher is attached - otherwise an
  incoming pairing is refused. On LE this host is always the central and never advertises; bonded peripherals
  reconnect through the controller's filter accept list. TRUST IS PER PEER AND PER PROFILE (input, audio,
  voice, PAN, SPP, OPP, GATT), kept in the bond record and set by the operator; an inbound connection for a
  profile the peer is not trusted for is refused at L2CAP. P02M0180's `enable` becomes trust for input.

## P02M0194b - LE, beyond one mouse

- [ ] Several LE connections within part a's bounds; LE privacy - peers' resolvable private addresses resolved
  with their identity keys, kept in the bond record, and this host's own address rotated; `peer-kind` gains the
  resolvable kind.
- [ ] Every LE pairing model through part a's prompts; LE legacy pairing only when the operator asks for it for
  a peer that has nothing better, at the legacy level, and never for a peer bonded with Secure Connections.
- [ ] A GATT SERVER holding what every LE device must expose - the GAP and GATT services - and PACS for part
  h's Broadcast Sink. Nothing outside the stack adds to it.
- [ ] A general GATT client - discovery, read, write, notify and indicate - for APPLICATIONS through
  `bluetooth-gatt`: a grant PermissionManager mints per launch from `bluetooth-admin` for one component, one
  peer ALIAS (a name the operator gives a bonded peer with `btctl alias`) and a set of service UUIDs - the route
  a camera grant takes, with a policy row naming the component and the alias. Refused for an alias that names
  no bonded peer trusted for GATT; never covering a service the stack drives itself (HID, GAP, GATT and the LE
  Audio services); ended with the launch. PermissionManager refuses every runtime request today, so until it has
  an interactive path "a third-party program" means one a policy row names.

## P02M0194c - Input

- [ ] `bluetooth-profile` grows from one mouse to every peer trusted for input: `open-input` answers a stream of
  decoded reports - pointer, key transitions (Keyboard page usages), consumer-control keys and P02M0192's
  gamepad record - and `open-mouse` folds into it. Still decoded and never a HID report, so InputService does
  not become a second HID implementation.
- [ ] Classic HID (control and interrupt channels, boot and report protocol) and HID over GATT with arbitrary
  report maps, both through the shared report-descriptor parser, mapped onto those shapes - a gamepad
  collection the way P02M0192's USB half maps it. A headset's AVRCP play, pause, next and previous (part e) are
  delivered as the same consumer-control keys: a headset's button is a media key like a keyboard's.
- [ ] InputService's Bluetooth slot grows from one stream to one per input peer, at most 8, on its one profile
  connection: pointers into the existing pointer path; keys into the ordinary key stream and NEVER the trusted
  sink (a Bluetooth keyboard's Ctrl+Alt+F12 stays reserved, and is neither secure attention nor delivered);
  gamepads into P02M0192's gamepad set as one more source, InputService giving each its id at arrival and a
  `departed` when its stream ends; every held key and button released when a stream ends.
- [ ] Reconnection by the device after a restart of either side, with the bond and its input trust the only
  things that let it in.

ORDER: P02M0192's vocabulary before the gamepad half. Consumer-control keys use the vocabulary P02M0099's HID
expansion plans for them; whichever of the two is implemented first defines it and the other consumes it.

## P02M0194d - AudioService: devices, routing, voice and calls

AudioService today drives one device at a fixed 48 kHz stereo with one recorder, and has no inventory, volume,
latency or voice. This part gives it the device model parts e, f and h stand on. It is also the multi-provider
migration P02M0099's destination table owes AudioService for a second `audio` provider: whichever of the two
lands first owns the inventory and the device-side PCM contract, and the other consumes it.

- [ ] A DEVICE INVENTORY: every catalogue `audio` provider and every Bluetooth endpoint, each with an identity,
  a label, its directions, its own PCM format (rate and channels, no longer the fixed 48 kHz stereo), its
  latency and its volume. The device-side PCM contract grows the format and the latency, so a Bluetooth
  endpoint is driven exactly as a sound card is, its period acknowledgment its clock.
- [ ] A ROUTING RULE: one default output and one default input; a device that arrives becomes the default
  (headphones connected take the music) and the previous default returns when it leaves; `audioctl` may choose
  instead. A stream follows the default unless it named a device, and NEVER ENDS because its device left: it
  moves, and with no device at all it keeps accepting and the gap is silence, counted - the owner's playback
  choice of 2026-09-25 applied to every device.
- [ ] PACING, for devices whose clock is not the host's: an A2DP output by the service's timer at the codec's
  rate; a voice link by the SCO packets the controller delivers, one out per one in; an isochronous stream by
  its interval against the controller's completions; a phone's A2DP stream through a jitter buffer bounded at
  200 ms, whose overflow drops the oldest period and whose underrun is silence, both counted.
- [ ] LATENCY STATED: each device's own (codec frame, queue, the sink's AVDTP delay report, BAP's presentation
  delay) in the inventory, and a stream's - its queue and its device's - on the stream.
- [ ] VOLUME PER DEVICE: a device that controls its own volume (AVRCP absolute volume, HFP speaker and
  microphone gain, LE Volume Control) is sent the level and its own changes are taken back, and its samples are
  not scaled; any other device is scaled in software.
- [ ] VOICE: `open-voice(rate)` - a duplex session at 8 or 16 kHz mono, converted to and from the device's own
  rate, minted only from a connection allowed both to play and to record (`audio-admin.open-voices`, a grant of
  its own) and routed to the default voice device: a headset, a pair of LE Audio earbuds, or otherwise the
  default output and input. OPENING A SESSION IS WHAT BRINGS THE AUDIO LINK UP (SCO/eSCO, or the bidirectional
  isochronous streams) and closing it takes it down; a link a headset opens while no session exists is refused.
- [ ] CALLS: a voice session carries a call state its holder sets - none, incoming, outgoing, active, held -
  and receives the headset's call commands (answer, hang up, reject, redial) as events; AudioService relays
  both to the voice device. With no session, or none declaring a call, the Audio Gateway reports no call and no
  network service, and answers every call command with ERROR.
- [ ] THE A2DP SINK IS ROUTED, NOT CAPTURED: a phone playing to this system is a stream AudioService plays to
  the default output itself, never an input a holder of `audio-capture` can read.
- [ ] `audio-control`, an operator authority on AudioService (the inventory, the default devices, volume), held
  by `audioctl`, a shipping tool, as `btctl` holds Bluetooth's.
- [ ] `bluetooth-audio`, resolved through the broker: endpoints offered as they come and go - output, input,
  voice and the A2DP-sink route - each opened as a PCM channel on the device-side contract above, with volume
  both ways and the call relay. An endpoint that closes is a device leaving, and the routing rule applies.

## P02M0194f - Calls: HFP and HSP over SCO

- [ ] THE SERVICE HALF OF THE SCO TRANSPORT, OWNED HERE. P02M0099's SCO item plans the transport and the
  contract - the `sco` kind, the attachment's SCO fields, the `voice` operation - and leaves the half above it
  to the Bluetooth service, whose milestone P02M0180 is closed. This part sets the synchronous connection up
  (eSCO, SCO where the peer has nothing newer), hands the transport the voice setting it negotiated through
  `voice`, and carries SCO packets both ways with their flow control; CVSD air coding is the controller's (the
  host carries 8 kHz linear PCM) and mSBC's framing, its H2 header included, is the host's.
- [ ] The Hands-Free Profile in the Audio Gateway role - this system is what a headset connects to - over
  RFCOMM: the AT command set's service level connection, indicators, codec negotiation, CVSD (narrowband) and
  mSBC (wideband); the Headset Profile for devices that have nothing newer. The audio connection follows part
  d's voice session, the call state and commands part d's call relay.
- [ ] The headset's battery level, where it reports one (HFP's indicators; the Battery Service on LE), in the
  device's status on the read authority.

ORDER: P02M0099's SCO transport item lands before this part's gate.

## P02M0194g - Serial, file push and tethering

- [ ] SPP over RFCOMM, as the client of a device's serial port, delivered to APPLICATIONS as
  `bluetooth-serial`: a byte stream - bounded writes answered with what was accepted, RFCOMM credits as their
  backpressure, reads as a bounded stream, close - minted per launch by PermissionManager from
  `bluetooth-admin` for one component and one peer alias, like part b's GATT grant. Not a `console-bytes`
  publication: that kind is a device's, and its one consumer is the development agent.
- [ ] OBEX Object Push over RFCOMM and, where the peer's record offers it, over L2CAP. SENDING: `btctl send
  <peer> <name> < file` - the shell's input redirection reads the file the user named, `btctl` holds only that
  stream and forwards it on the operator connection, and the service holds no storage authority. RECEIVING:
  only while `btctl receive <peer> > file` waits; the offered object - its name as the peer's words, escaped,
  its size and type - is shown on `btctl`'s terminal and accepted there within part a's prompt deadline, and its
  bytes go to `btctl`'s output, which the shell's redirection writes where the user chose. The peer's name for
  it is never a path, nothing here opens it, and with no receiver waiting a push is refused.
- [ ] PAN (BNEP): this system as a PAN user tethered to a phone, delivered through `bluetooth-network`.
  NetworkService resolves it through the broker and opens each offered link as a frame channel speaking a NIC
  provider's frame-plus-link wire - the greeting with the local controller's address as its MAC and a 1500-byte
  MTU, then one Ethernet frame per message - so its own ARP, DHCP and IPv6 run on it unchanged. THE UPLINK RULE,
  since NetworkService runs one: the operator's `btctl connect <peer> pan` says whether the link may replace a
  selected uplink, as a modem data grant does; a link that may not is held unselected while another is
  selected, one that may is selected under a new interface generation, and when it goes `uplink`'s existing
  fallback applies.

## P02M0194h - LE Audio

- [ ] THE ISO TRANSPORT, BOTH HALVES, OWNED HERE: no P02M0099 item carries ISO, and its USB item names carrying
  it a change to the service's contract. Over USB, ISO shares the ACL bulk pair and only the connection handle
  tells them apart, and the handles are the service's - so THE SERVICE CLASSIFIES. With ISO negotiated in the
  attachment, the transport (`drivers::bt_usb` and the Bluetooth class module) carries outbound `iso` packets
  on the bulk OUT pipe and delivers every inbound bulk packet as `acl`, reassembled by the four-byte header both
  kinds share and bounded by the larger ceiling; the service reads the handle against its own table of
  connected and broadcast stream handles and applies that kind's ceiling after classifying, refusing an ACL
  packet above the ACL one. A packet for a handle not yet in the table - data can overtake, on another pipe,
  the event that created its stream - is dropped and counted. Connected (CIS) and broadcast (BIS) streams both.
- [ ] Extended scanning and periodic advertising sync, the isochronous setup for both stream kinds, and the LC3
  codec, pure and host-tested against part i's codec oracles.
- [ ] The Basic Audio Profile's unicast client - PACS and ASCS clients, stream configuration and QoS - with
  CAP's coordinated set (a pair of earbuds found and driven as one device through CSIP) and volume control
  (VCP), offered to AudioService as part d's endpoints, so LE Audio earbuds play, and their microphones record,
  like any other device; part d's call relay and part c's media keys reach them through the Telephony Bearer
  and Media Control services in part b's GATT server (TMAP's Call Gateway and Unicast Media Sender).
- [ ] Broadcast (Auracast) reception as the last item of the part: a Broadcast Sink with PACS in part b's GATT
  server, the broadcast chosen by the operator (`btctl broadcast`) with its Broadcast Code given there when it
  is encrypted, and played by AudioService to the default output as the A2DP-sink route is.

ORDER: this part's ISO transport item before its gate; part b's GATT server before the Broadcast Sink.

## P02M0194i - Operator, verification and completion

- [ ] `btctl` grows with the stack: classic and LE devices in one list with their status, battery included;
  `pair` and `pairable` as part a's prompt watchers; `discoverable`; `connect` and `disconnect` per profile;
  `trust` per profile; `alias`; `forget`; `media`; `send` and `receive`; `broadcast`. The read authority's scan
  and device records carry both radios.
- [ ] THE ORACLE, BUILT FIRST: AN INDEPENDENT RADIO AND INDEPENDENT PEERS ON THE PRODUCTION PATH. The peers are
  Bumble's (Apache-2.0, written elsewhere) - a HID keyboard and a gamepad, a headset (HFP hands-free, A2DP sink,
  AVRCP), a phone (A2DP source, AVRCP target), a serial device, an LE Audio earbud pair and a broadcast source -
  on RootCanal (Apache-2.0), a virtual BR/EDR and LE controller that serves HCI over TCP. The guest's
  controller is `usbredir_device.py --emulate bt-bridge`: a USB Bluetooth controller with the voice alternates
  of P02M0099's `bt-sco` whose HCI is RootCanal's, so every gate crosses the real USB transport - SCO on the
  isochronous alternates, ISO on the bulk pair. TEST-ONLY AND NEVER LINKED: they run in the harness and nothing
  of theirs is staged into an image. WHERE THEY STOP, THE ORACLE IS THIS TEAM'S AND SAYS SO: Bumble has no BNEP
  and no OBEX, so the PAN access point and the OPP peer are harness peers written here on Bumble's L2CAP and
  RFCOMM and recorded as same-team oracles - the access point bridged to a host network where `dnsmasq` answers
  DHCP, so the addressing a PAN user receives is not ours; and a RootCanal feature a part needs and lacks
  (checked when part a starts: BR/EDR Secure Simple Pairing, eSCO, CIS, BIS) makes that part's oracle the
  in-guest fixture, said so in that part. THE IN-GUEST FIXTURE STAYS for what an independent peer cannot
  script: bad fragments, exhausted credits, resets and unplug mid-stream. Real hardware is recorded where it
  was used and never substituted for an oracle.
- [ ] THE CODEC ORACLES, with their sources and terms. SBC and mSBC from the A2DP and HFP specifications' own
  definitions, differentially against the Android Open Source Project's SBC encoder and decoder (Apache-2.0);
  LC3 from its specification, differentially against Google's liblc3 (Apache-2.0) within the specification's
  conformance thresholds. Each oracle runs on the host beside the suite, never linked into anything staged. The
  SIG's own conformance material - the A2DP bitstreams and the LC3 test software - is member-only and is used
  only if the owner obtains it under its terms.
- [ ] Host suites for every pure leaf - SDP, RFCOMM, AVDTP, AVCTP, AT parsing, OBEX, BNEP, the ISO classifier,
  SBC, mSBC, LC3, the pairing state machines against the specification's sample data - and a guest gate per
  part on the oracle above, each registered like P02M0180's.
- [ ] `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` gains Bluetooth: the pairing prompt and its stated limit, the application grants,
  and that no Bluetooth input reaches the trusted sink.
- [ ] The three cross-builds; the dynamic report; the Domain limits derived in part a re-measured under load
  and restated.

OWNER QUESTIONS, RECORDED AND ASKED WHEN THE PART STARTS - not answered here:
  - WHEN PART a STARTS, since the oracle is built first: adopting RootCanal and Bumble, and for parts e and h
    the Android Open Source Project's SBC and Google's liblc3 - all Apache-2.0 - as test-only host dependencies,
    run by the harness or beside the host suites and never staged into an image. Without them every oracle is
    the in-guest fixture, written by the same team and stated as such.
  - WHEN PART e STARTS, once for all three codecs: shipping SBC and mSBC, and LC3 in part h, on a product that
    is not SIG-qualified. The SIG's royalty-free patent licence covers the compliant portions of a QUALIFIED
    product, which is the question already answered for AAC by excluding it. And whether the SIG's member-only
    conformance material is obtained under its terms.

EXCLUDES: Mesh; the peripheral role beyond what the profiles need - no LE advertising and no broadcast scan
delegator; Out Of Band pairing, for want of an out-of-band channel; aptX, LDAC and AAC; the Bluetooth SIG's
qualification, which needs its proprietary tester - what this milestone claims is its own oracles.
